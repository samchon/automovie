This design owns the material texture and reflection the user requested. Following [Surface decomposition](../settings/003-spatial-basis.md#surface-decomposition), elevations, rooms, and storeys handle architectural faces, models object part/faces, and instances placement. The material language comes from the bright stone-family opaque faces, dark metal frames, and continuous wood floors and cabinetry of [Envelope and openings](../settings/003-spatial-basis.md#envelope-and-privacy), and the colour relation from the [visual grammar](../settings/001-production.md#production-visual-grammar). materials provides reusable material responses and coordinate conditions. New glazing sections and shading are left to spaces, furniture appearance to models, and planting positions to the corresponding placement owner. The object meshes and finish strings of the current room sources are temporary consumption during the handover and are not approved as permanent binding addresses. Materials change neither the division of measurement and verdict roles, the room and storey graphs and division, the characters and living density of the resident settings, the accessibility outputs, nor the passage cylinder check, and consume the actual faces of the individual room, wall, door, window, and elevation H2s and the living program. The verification target is the actual surfaces of the same house, and a separate sample does not replace the house.

It assumes the [current delivery limits](../../README.md#current-material-delivery-scope) and `src/house/assembly.ts`, `src/viewer/payload.ts`, and `src/viewer/scene.mjs`. Only native baseColor colour and the baseColorTexture of the primary UV, scalar roughness, metallic, opacity, transmission, ior, thickness, and clearcoat, emissive colour, and alphaMode, alphaCutoff, and doubleSided are used. normalTexture, metallicRoughnessTexture, occlusionTexture, emissiveTexture, displacement, new shaders, and post-processing material substitution are not in this design. It is not claimed that colour grain makes the actual shadows of fibre relief or stone protrusion. Mirrors are an approximation of environment reflection, and exact mirror images of the room interior are unverified.

Below, colours are sRGB `#RRGGBB`, lengths are m of the [coordinate basis](../settings/003-spatial-basis.md#coordinate-datum), and roughness and metallic are dimensionless render values. Every finish H2 states roughness. Unless stated otherwise, metallic=0, opacity=1, alphaMode=opaque, doubleSided=false, transmission=0, emissive=null, thickness=0, clearcoat=0, and ior=1.5. alphaCutoff is used only with alphaMode=mask and defaults to 0.5 when unstated, and a finish without a baseColorTexture uses only colour without texture. Render thickness=0 does not mean that an object has no thickness. Member thickness is owned by the existing geometry of spaces, and the nominal thickness of surface paint and veneer by each finish H2. The values are authoring choices, not values measured from reference pixels or certified physical values.

The current fallback that replaces unspecified material names with plaster is not allowed on the new binding path. If an owner, surface, material, or coordinates is unresolved, it fails at that address. The [delivery check](007-observation.md#binding-census) checks whether the native materials, texture resources, current models, and uploader consume the same values. This path and source are implemented in materialSources after the materials evidence content closes.

The stable face addresses of architecture and objects are provided by the space owner and the model owner of [Surface decomposition](../settings/003-spatial-basis.md#surface-decomposition) respectively. materials decides the finish ID, grain axis, and material response for the two-sided addresses, and the generating owner carries that binding. Whole `oak` or `white` strings are not replaced in bulk. The white of a bed pillow is textile, and the white of a basin rim is ceramic. The oak of a tree trunk is not furniture wood. These roles are typed inputs at generation time, and the viewer does not paint by guessing names. The existing plan, wall cuts, openings, and connectors continue to be generated by spaces, object members by models, and placement and per-state positions by instances. The `*-bed-pillow-*` and `*-rim` strings of the earlier room sources are temporary addresses before the handover, and the confirmation of model part ids is reflected after the models re-verdict.

Models of independent elements can be shared per material, actual scale, and grain axis. materialSources provides variants with the same shape and different metric UVs or material responses and stable model part/face bindings, and does not change object dimensions or members. instances keeps the existing `set.id`, explicit member `id`, count, seed, order, and translation/rotation/scale, and selects variants with the public type `IAutoMovieInstancePrototypeDesign` and an explicit `prototype`. It keeps the address `instance:<set.id>:<explicit.id>` returned by `instanceSlot` of `@automovie/engine` and does no manual member duplication or set splitting. The weight of each variant is 1, and every member states its prototype. The original prototypeBounds must equal the actual world bounds.

`IAutoMovieInstancePrototypeDesign` is not a callable function or a new production helper but an input record `{ id: string; modelRecipe: string; weight: number }`. The [definition](../../../../packages/interface/src/production/IAutoMovieInstancePrototypeDesign.ts) is re-exported from the [production index](../../../../packages/interface/src/production/index.ts) and the [package root index](../../../../packages/interface/src/index.ts). The actual import path is `@automovie/interface`, and internal repository paths are links for reading the basis. `id` is a non-empty string unique within the set, `modelRecipe` is the ID of a registered variant model, and `weight` is positive, fixed at 1 in this design. `default` is the ID the engine gives the default modelRecipe and is therefore not used as an authored variant ID. This type itself has no return value or runtime check.

At implementation, modelSources provides the shape and part/faces and materialSources the finish and UV variants of those addresses, after which `buildHouse()` of the neutral assembly boundary [src/house/build.ts](../../src/house/build.ts) registers them in `environment.models`. That function currently calls the temporary room fit-out directly, and the transition replacing it with later branch consumption is not yet complete. The population authoring boundary of instanceSources consumes `import type { IAutoMovieInstancePrototypeDesign, IAutoMovieExplicitInstanceTransform } from "@automovie/interface"` and puts `IAutoMovieInstancePrototypeDesign[]` into `set.prototypes`. It keeps the id, translation (m), rotation (quaternion), scale, and optional palette of [IAutoMovieExplicitInstanceTransform](../../../../packages/interface/src/production/IAutoMovieExplicitInstanceTransform.ts), puts the selected variant id into `prototype`, and passes `set.layout = { kind: "explicit", transforms }`. The temporary population of the current [Assembly.repeat](../../src/house/assembly.ts) retires after the handover, and the final repetition authoring is handled by the instances owner. Duplicate variant IDs, empty IDs, `default` collisions, and unregistered modelRecipes are rejected at the instanceSources authoring boundary with an `Error` carrying the set/member address and are not replaced with another model.

The actual consumer is the existing `@automovie/engine` root import of [src/viewer/payload.ts](../../src/viewer/payload.ts). After `lowerBuiltEnvironment(environment)`, `materializeCompiledInstanceSet({ instanceSet: design, world: { routes: [] } })` returns an `IAutoMovieCompiledInstanceSet`, and `instanceSlot(compiled, index)` returns an `IAutoMovieInstanceSlot` with the selected `prototype`, `modelRecipe`, `node`, and position, rotation, scale3, and palette. The basis is the [compiler](../../../../packages/engine/src/populationRuntime/materializeCompiledInstanceSet.ts) and the [slot reader](../../../../packages/engine/src/populationRuntime/instanceSlot.ts). A missing explicit prototype is the native `Error` thrown by the [prototype selection](../../../../packages/engine/src/populationRuntime/selectInstancePrototype.ts) the compiler uses (`Instance set "<id>" slot <n> references missing prototype "<prototype>".`), and an out-of-range slot is a `RangeError`. These exceptions are not caught and turned into a default model or an empty population. This import and call already exist in payload.ts today and materialize every population set with the modelRecipe of the default prototype (`default`). materialSources supplies bindable variants without editing repeated members, and instanceSources writes the `set.prototypes` and `transforms[].prototype` of those variants. The browser [client](../../src/viewer/client.mjs) does not import the engine and consumes the JSON payload sent by the [server](../../src/viewer/server.ts).

The palette is used in the engine as absolute sRGB colours. materialSources defines the new finish base colours and the per-board variation each H2 allows, and instanceSources passes only those values as the explicit palette. For an object with several finishes, the unchanged reference is the first material baseColor of the selected native model converted to sRGB, and that set states the `palette-material-index` trait as 0. The payload carries the linear colour of that material of the actually selected model as `paletteReference`, and the viewer applies the palette/reference ratio once to every part. On this basis, the different colours and textures of wood, textile, and ceramic are kept, and only the allowed palette variation is applied at the same ratio. Existing populations without the trait reproduce the absolute palette as before, using each part's baseColor as the denominator. The trait must be a finite integer and a registered material index, and the RGB of the reference must be finite values greater than 0 and at most 1. A wrong declaration is an Error at the member/model address and is not replaced with white or the first material. The palettes of the remaining populations stay as they are. A new material source corresponds to one public owner per document file, and `buildHouse()` of `src/house/build.ts` binds it to architectural and object surfaces. The final library export and the viewer must receive the native materials and models of the same neutral assembly result. The current registration of the whole temporary environment in `src/spaces/citizen-house.ts` is separated into the architectural spaces output after the handover.

The check is the [full binding census](007-observation.md#binding-census). An increase in existing model IDs is not itself a geometry increase, but the per-model member selection, the same world bounds, member identity, and every existing topology reference must be compared. An implementation in which several after-the-fact painting owners share one complete surface does not fit this design.

The length unit m and the +Y up and +Z rear axes follow the [coordinate basis](../settings/003-spatial-basis.md#coordinate-datum), and the decision not to use the five reference images as textures follows the [delivery scope](../settings/001-production.md#delivery-scope). Colour textures are tileable RGBA that the typed source makes with a fixed seed=2080 and integer texel coordinates. Photographs, references, billboards, baked lighting, and baked junction shadows are not used. Achromatic grain assets are bound with sRGB colorSpace so the uploader decodes them into linear lighting space, with alpha=255 and a mean tolerance of ±.005 in linear value. Each texture carries the document's ID, resolution, and physical repeat length into the resource as is, and the primary UV is delivered with `coordinateSource: surface-metres`, transform.scale=(1/tileU,1/tileV), offset=(0,0), and rotationDeg=0. The sampler is wrapS/T=repeat, minFilter=linearMipmapLinear, magFilter=linear. The binding record has no anisotropy field, and the [uploader](../../src/viewer/scene.mjs) applies anisotropy 8 to every texture. The existing PV binding is an exception stated by its owner. The [floor-band metal cassette and seal](002-exterior-solids.md#opaque-floor-band) follow this metric repeat rule.

UVs are written from the surface length after non-uniform scale. The positions, normals, and triangles of the existing public tessellateToMesh or the existing mesh are preserved, and only vertex attributes are duplicated where UVs differ at face seams. Each box face uses two tangent axes among XY, XZ, and ZY according to its local axes, flipped to match the normal sign. Wide surfaces with a grain direction put V on the document's grain axis. End faces whose grain axis is parallel to the normal follow [wood end faces](004-wood.md#wood-end-faces). The actual scale including the part transform and parents is reflected, but rotation and translation do not fix the texture to the world and make it slide. The UV specialization key is the original model/part, the original finite numbers of the scale, the finish, the grain axis, and the phase, and different sizes are not merged by eyeballed rounding.

When a per-board phase is needed, the Unicode code points of the existing stable member ID are accumulated in order with 32-bit FNV-1a, and seed2080 is XORed with the initial hash. The lowest 2 bits and the next 1 bit, divided by 4 and 2 respectively, are used as the U/V phases, limiting it to at most 8 kinds per size and grain axis. The per-board sRGB factor is the next 16 bits divided by 65535, linearly interpolated within each H2's range. Time, execution order, and Math.random are not used. Isotropic stone also uses the surface's local coordinates but may break the phase at panel joints. Door leaf vertical grain moves with the door when it rotates, and floor grain follows each board's +Z. seed=2080, the phase hash and its bit assignment, the texture resolution of each finish H2, and the UV specialization key are this layer's authoring choices, not values taken from the engine or settings.

The same texture asset and sampling binding share one GPU texture in the viewer. The textureCache of the [scene uploader](../../src/viewer/scene.mjs) makes one source per asset and, for each binding, returns a clone sharing that source with the binding's sampler, colorSpace, and UV transform set. The texture cache of three 0.186, declared in package.json, keeps a GPU texture per source keyed by wrap, filter, anisotropy, colorSpace, and pixel format values and does not put the UV transform in the key, so the same asset, sampler, and colorSpace is uploaded once. Part placements with the same native material record are drawn as one static batch. Even as material variants grow, this sharing and batching are kept, and the path that re-uploads RGBA per variant is not revived. Scene disposal releases all of a batch's textures, and a shared GPU texture is let go once when its last user is released. This is resource lifetime management of the existing supported delivery, not a new render channel or shader. The actual native model variant count and the texture resource count and bytes are recorded together in the [census](007-observation.md#binding-census), and without performance measurement the frame rate is not guaranteed.

The method of keeping one 0..1 UV on the existing unit primitive and only enlarging the scale is not adopted. The [scale check and call contract](007-observation.md#scale-and-junction-samples) passes the current native models to `validateTextureScale({ models })` of `@automovie/engine`. This API of the [original](../../../../packages/engine/src/validation/validateTextureScale.ts) returns only `IAutoMovieValidation` and neither applies transforms nor writes a census. The length comparison of whether UVs reflect the actual scale and the tally of the checkable scope are recorded separately by the linked production check owner. Unmeasured surfaces such as primitives or UV-less faces and a native result without violations are not read as passing the whole. Normal tile repetition must show no seams, and the boundary between two faces with different UV directions changes only at actual member edges.
