# Exterior and interior finishes

## Envelope assembly {#envelope-assembly}

Exterior walls read as a layered build-up of painted gypsum or sheathing behind an air gap, white lap siding above a 0.75 m brick skirt, white trim at openings, and dark roof shingles over roof sheathing. The front porch carries the same trim and brick language. The chimney is real red-brown brick with a dark cap. The garage uses the same siding and trim but remains visually subordinate to the main gabled mass.

## Interior finish map {#interior-finish-map}

Ground entry, living, stair, and the rear common room use warm medium oak planks with a consistent direction. Bedrooms and the upper corridor use low-pile warm-gray carpet. Pantry and bathrooms use pale matte tile. The garage and driveway use cool-gray concrete. Walls and ceilings are warm off-white paint. Kitchen cabinets are muted taupe with pale stone counters and a white subway-tile backsplash. These assignments follow room ownership and are not applied as one global texture.

## Repeated module laws {#repeated-module-laws}

Siding is emitted from a measured facade length at 0.203 m board course spacing with a small stagger at corners. Brick is emitted as running-bond modules from the measured skirt perimeter at 0.203 m course height and 0.406 m nominal bed length, with alternating half-module offsets and explicit corner returns. Roof shingles are emitted from the measured slope extents at one course per 0.305 m rise and one module per 0.305 m run. These are laws with measured count and stable source ids; they are not manually copied per-face records.

## Finish limit {#finish-limit}

The model promises material distinction, grain direction, trim hierarchy, and readable surface repetition at review distance. Fine manufacturer marks, physically certified assemblies, hidden membranes, exact product provenance, and photoreal microtexture are unverified and outside this blocking-to-fit-out delivery. A surface that cannot be measured or inspected remains explicitly unverified.
