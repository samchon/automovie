# Modern suburban house settings

## Delivery scope {#delivery-scope}

This production is a reusable deterministic building-and-interior library, not a film or timed brief. It delivers one ordinary American two-storey detached house, its right-attached empty two-car garage, the site interface, complete room topology, envelope elements, repeatable exterior modules, and lived-in but middle-class fit-out. It ends at a reviewable source graph and its current compiled geometry; it does not promise photoreal material capture, vehicles, occupants, or a finished architectural construction set.

## Governing aim {#governing-aim}

At inspection distance the work must read first as one plausible, unexceptional American suburban home and second as the named room or envelope surface being inspected. When detail conflicts with the fixed graph, preserving direct circulation and room identity wins. When finish conflicts with legible structure, the structural edge and opening win.

## Scale and coordinate convention {#scale-and-coordinate-convention}

All source distances are metres in a right-handed Y-up frame. X increases toward the house's east/right side, Y is vertical, and Z increases from the street-facing front toward the rear yard; the front facade is the negative-Z boundary. The main conditioned footprint is 11.0 m by 9.6 m. Its two 105.6 m² storeys plus the 5.8 m by 6.2 m attached garage and small porch target approximately 246 m² of gross constructed area, with the discrepancy treated as a design-range consequence rather than false survey precision. Ground clear height is 2.65 m, upper clear height is 2.55 m, the floor build-up is 0.28 m, exterior walls are 0.18 m, and partitions are 0.12 m.

## Visual grammar {#visual-grammar}

The shared language is white warm-gray horizontal lap siding, a dark charcoal asphalt-shingle roof, a restrained red-brown brick skirt and chimney, white trim, black-framed divided-light windows, a medium walnut front door, warm oak on the principal ground floor, quiet carpet in bedrooms and hall, pale tile in wet rooms, and matte painted walls. Interior furniture is common and durable: oatmeal upholstery, walnut or white-oak wood, brushed metal, black accents, and a few plants. Variation is limited to lighting and small furnishing placement; massing, openings, room graph, and material ownership do not drift.

## Delivery fidelity and review condition {#delivery-fidelity-and-review-condition}

The promised representation is a deterministic blocking-to-fit-out model. It may use primitive and generated module geometry, but every visible construction surface and operable opening required by this brief must be represented by a named element or generated population. Review uses the current compiled topology and derived neutral views at a consistent 16:9 raster, with the building exterior at street distance, the interior at room distance, and section axonometric views used only for inspection. A view may establish readability and material distinction; it may not be used to claim code compliance, structural engineering, photorealism, or measurement accuracy beyond the authored dimensions.

## Subject breakdown and production scope {#subject-breakdown-production-scope}

The required built subject is one building, two storeys, one L-shaped stair, a single right-attached empty two-car garage, its garage door and storage, site slab and paths, complete exterior envelope, all stated rooms, room openings, kitchen equipment, bathroom fixtures, laundry equipment, mudroom storage, and ordinary furnishings. Vehicles, people, hidden corridors, second stairs, a double-height void, detached outbuildings, and decorative architecture that changes the graph are explicitly outside scope. A reference image may inform proportion, light, or material but is never instantiated as an image surface.

## Operative subjects and services {#operative-subjects-and-services}

The building envelope, floors, roofs, openings, stair, kitchen appliances, plumbing fixtures, laundry pair, garage storage, lighting fixtures, and planted site elements are the operative populations because removing or moving any of them changes the delivered reading. The garage has no vehicle population. Plumbing, drainage, electrical service, and HVAC are represented as bounded visible fixtures and service-zone relationships only; their hidden engineering networks remain outside this blocking library and are recorded as unverified rather than invented.

## Reference basis and uncertainty {#reference-basis-and-uncertainty}

The five supplied images are visual references for exterior character, cutaway spatial relationship, rear common-room atmosphere, entry/living/stair relationship, and upper-floor privacy. They are not dimensional sources. The dimensions above are production inventions chosen to keep two storeys, a middle-class scale, furniture clearances, a garage connection, and a compact circulation graph coherent. No claim of measured existing-house accuracy is made.
