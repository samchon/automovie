With the user brief as the authority, author as a deterministic library one ordinary middle-class two-storey detached family house in a contemporary American suburb, its whole interior, an attached two-car garage on the right, and the site needed to read the house. The final deliverable is the public TypeScript space, geometry, and material output, a source-based viewer that shows it in real 3D, and the author's self-reading of the complete observations against the five references. By the latest user instruction this closing scope is the building itself, and no separate individual object is started. The design drafts for unbuilt furniture, planting, and household props are preserved, and completing the building does not claim those objects are complete too. A time-ordered film, shot, voice, person, or car is not part of this deliverable. Cutaways are provided only as an inspection feature. A reproduction made by pasting photographs onto the background or surfaces is not allowed.

The user-specified aim is that, at review distance, the exterior and each interior read with the structure, members, materials, and household function of the same house as the five reference images. A box that matches only the mass, or one well-dressed view, does not offset what other rooms lack. When two choices conflict, the continuity of the fixed space graph is preserved first, and the reference envelope and interior identity are realized inside it. A detail that could be expressed only by changing the graph is not chosen arbitrarily but raised for the coordinator's judgment.

As an authoring decision, the operator is a desktop reviewer using a mouse and keyboard. The viewer provides exterior orbit, pan, zoom in and out, perspective viewpoint movement inside real rooms, storey, space, and observation selection, and return to the default viewpoint. Observation selection moves the camera position and view direction to a real viewpoint inside that space. The reviewer can see each room with an intact envelope and then check cutaways and structure in a separate inspection mode. Labels, observation markers, dimensions, and cutaways are off by default and belong only to the inspection mode. A free camera's ability to pass through is not treated as verification of human circulation.

The production choice is right-handed Y-up, length in m, angle in rad, area in ㎡, and time in s where needed. +X is the direction of the garage on the right as seen from the front, +Y is up, and +Z points out of the house toward the front walk. The rear garden is at -Z, with no claim that it is geographic north. The origin is set at the front center of the main block's ground-storey finished-floor height. Space design derives storey reference heights and the difference between structural and finished surfaces from these coordinates. Local transforms of doors, windows, and furniture must convert to this world reference, and cameras and observations use the same reference.

The mandatory alternative access of this library is English documents, named keyboard controls, camera return and observation selection, and inspection results that do not distinguish state by color alone. The source/viewer implements them. Subtitle, dialogue transcript, and audio description tracks are deliberately not provided because there is no audio or video program. Documents describing the spatial structure are mandatory, but there is no guarantee that a non-visual user can perform material or shadow judgments equally. Per the latest user instruction, that visual judgment needs the modeler's own reading of real frames. Barrier-free certification of the house itself is outside the delivery scope and is not confused with the accessibility documents.

The authoring assumption for reviewing structural and household feasibility is a house used by two adults and two children, requiring no circulation for special medical equipment or a resident caregiver. The family's build is not claimed to be a real population average. The test shapes of space design are a person occupancy body on the floor 0.60 m wide, 0.45 m deep, and 1.90 m tall, and a 0.75 m width including a hand-carried laundry basket. Main interior walking routes take as authoring targets a clear door width of at least 0.80 m, a continuous passage width of at least 0.90 m, a clear stair width of at least 0.95 m, and headroom of at least 2.00 m. These values are design inspection choices and do not mean U.S. code compliance or accessibility for all users. On a dimensional collision, the boundaries and placement are corrected instead of shrinking the occupancy body to pass. Furniture doors and drawers and the use space in front of the washer are additionally inspected for the real operating range of that design.

The house exterior, each room, the stair, doors, windows, the garage, furniture, equipment, props, and planting are built inside the production. By user prohibition, the references are adopted as input to compare by eye and are not copied as asset geometry or textures. The site and exterior access are owned by maps, room boundaries, envelope, and circulation by spaces, real members, furniture, and tree prototypes by models, surface expression by materials, exterior repetition and furniture and planting instances by instances, and lighting by systems. Repetition results and observations are derived from this design. Each matching source branch and the viewer consume the same output and keep no copied demo house. Variation without an explicit random seed and external image-generation services are not adopted. After reviewing the installed public engine features, resource shortfalls are handled by expression-preserving means such as batch/instance, and rooms, faces, and questions are not omitted for performance. When the installed public engine lacks a needed feature, the actual limitation is recorded and raised to the coordinator, and a needed external package is declared and installed through the procedure of [editing and dependency boundary](20-verification.md#implementation-boundary).
