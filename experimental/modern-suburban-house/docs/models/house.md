This model owns the deterministic bounded representation of one two-storey suburban house and its attached empty two-car garage. It supplies rigid envelope, roof, chimney, partition, opening-fill, stair, and room-surface receiver geometry to the library. The representation ceiling is a reviewable blocking pass that preserves identity, extents, openings, surface zones, repeated-module hosts, and room-contact interfaces; fine architectural fabrication, code compliance, and photoreal microtexture are outside the model promise and remain unverified.

The model source is `src/house.ts`. Its public `Building`, `Element`, `Part`, `Opening`, `Space`, `Storey`, `SurfaceOwner`, and `ModuleLaw` records are the typed representation boundary, and `buildModernSuburbanHouse()` emits the complete deterministic library record. `src/spaces/modern-suburban-house.ts` is a consuming lowering owner, not a second model definition.

The coordinate convention is metres with X across the frontage, Y upward from the ground floor, and Z running from the front street edge at negative Z to the rear at positive Z. The model origin `(0, 0, 0)` is the main-house plan centre on the ground reference plane, so the garage extends in positive X and ground contact is Y 0. `MAIN` and `GARAGE` define plan-basis extents of X -5.5..5.5 and Z -4.8..4.8 for the main house, and X 5.5..11.3 and Z -4.8..1.4 for the garage. The compiled `building.envelope` is a separate derived content bound over all emitted building parts, including transformed roof and module parts. Upper elements begin at elevation 2.93 m.

Roof slabs and their repeated shingle parts use the same box basis and a Z-axis roll for cross-X pitch. Their module centres come from the measured host span after the same transform, so the roof module population remains a bounded surface treatment input rather than a second roof shape. Wall and roof dimensions are authored values or source-derived measurements; no reference-image pixel ratio is a model input.

The stable source element boundaries are the building and storey envelope elements under `ground/wall/*` and `upper/wall/*`, garage walls under `ground/garage-wall/*`, partitions under `ground/partition/*` and `upper/partition/*`, the structural and ceiling elements `ground/floor-slab`, `ground/ceiling`, `upper/floor-slab`, and `upper/ceiling`, `chimney/front-left`, the roof elements `roof/main-north`, `roof/main-south`, and `roof/garage`, the opening population under `opening/*`, the single stair `stair/single-l-turn`, and the generated module laws and elements. There is no separately named garage structural-floor element: `ground/floor-slab` is the main-house storey slab, while `floor-finish/ground/garage` is the garage room's downstream floor realization. `building/root` is the lowering adapter's compiled parent for these source elements, not an additional authored geometry owner.

An `Element` carries its source id, storey id, space id, surface owner id, layer, and part list. A `Part` carries its own id, primitive shape, centre, size, material binding token, fixed rotations, and tags. A part does not carry a separate room or surface owner; those relationships are read from its parent element. No part is shared between semantic element owners. This distinction keeps the model addressable without claiming fields that the source does not emit.

Openings are represented as bounded host-wall cuts with lower, side, and head solid spans around each opening. The opening record owns the axis, centre, width, sill, height, host element, and endpoint space ids; the opening element owns the frame, glazing, or door-panel parts. The spatial graph owns the endpoint topology and routes, while this model owns the bounded geometry that realizes those already named interfaces.

The stair geometry is rigid construction geometry for the one authored L-turn connector. It has no bones, morphs, animated pivots, or motion-writable interface. Its fixed transforms are construction parameters, not a hidden second connector; a future change that needs articulation would require a separately reviewed model interface.

The model exposes one stable receiver for each complete exposed elevation, roof family, storey plane, and room surface through the `surface.elevation.*`, `surface.roof.*`, `surface.storey.*`, and `surface.space.*` ids. Elements bind to exactly one of these owners. A model surface is complete at its named boundary and is not split by a decorative detail or a neighbouring room owner.

`MaterialId` on a part is a binding token for the material branch, not the material response itself. Construction, finish, texture scale, optical response, and material state belong to `docs/materials/finishes.md`. Room boundaries, storeys, adjacency, openings, and circulation belong to `docs/spaces/house.md`. Placement of furniture and appliances belongs to `docs/instances/fit-out.md`, and visible lighting state belongs to `docs/systems/lighting.md`. The source may emit these consumers' geometry in one library record, but that fan-out does not transfer their design ownership to this model.

The neutral review population is derived by `deriveReviewObservationPopulation()` from the compiled building, not selected from a preferred camera. It contains the setting, four exposed elevations, six building corners, the two main roof slopes, the garage roof and underside, every opening, and for each of the 15 rooms one threshold, four inside corners, and four in-room cardinal directions. The current population therefore has 15 exterior, 31 opening, and 135 room observations, totaling 181 stable observation ids.

Each observation is checked against its model-specific question: the whole-building set tests silhouette, attached-garage proportion, roof direction, and envelope bounds; the opening set tests bounded wall solids and readable frame or panel ownership; and the room set tests stable room boundaries, surface receivers, stair placement, and fit-out clearance without moving the boundaries. A neutral background and consistent review distance are required for comparison. The observation record supplies position and direction, while visual readability, GPU-backed output, hidden HVAC, plumbing, drainage, electrical networks, structural engineering, and photoreal finish remain outside this source-only model claim until independently observed.
