Adopt the low white baseboards in reference 03's common room, 04's entry/stair side and 05's upper corridor. These are separate closed timber/MDF board prototypes attached at the junction of [complete room-interior faces](../spaces/03-surface-owners.md#interior-surface-handoff), rather than wall paint or floor finish itself. [White interior trim](../materials/02-interior-shell.md#interior-trim-white) owns color, gloss and wood grain. Do not measure their photographed height from pixels.

The local origin is the start of an exposed run where a room's finished-floor top meets its interior wall-finish face. +X follows that wall, +Y is up, and +Z points into the room. The YZ section vertices are `(0,0) → (0.10,0) → (0.10,0.005) → (0.09,0.015) → (0,0.015)` m, with Y first and Z second. Height is therefore 0.10 m, maximum roomward projection 0.015 m, and the upper 0.01 m forms a bevel. Extrude the section through actual run length L, closing front, back, top, bottom and start/end faces. Every triangle has the single face id `wall-baseboard`. This prototype does not create structural walls, room finishes or furniture-base `plinth`.

Input L comes from actual exposed straight segments of the [room-wall finishes](../spaces/03-surface-owners.md#interior-surface-handoff). Boards on one room's walls terminate at open door openings, casing outer ends and wall ends of doorless passages. They do not continue under thresholds or across openings. They continue below ordinary windows whose sills do not descend to this height. At interior/exterior room corners, neighboring runs abut exactly through respective 45° miter ends; independently exposed terminations close with vertical end caps. Two rooms do not create the same corner board, and two runs in one room do not duplicate a corner's common volume. Ground/upper painted walls include common areas, bedrooms, corridors, entry and laundry, plus painted sections outside tile in the powder room and two bathrooms. No baseboard is made on shower/tub tiled walls, at tile bases or in the [garage excluded by room-face owners](../spaces/03-surface-owners.md#interior-surface-handoff). At bathroom vanity plinths, runs continue along their rear/side lower recesses and terminate where tile begins. A starting run below the stair ends at the intersection with the first riser and existing stair-mass side; upper-floor runs begin at the new arrival-floor boundary. The [white sloped side skirt of the open stair](04-stair-members.md#stair-side-skirt) is a separate model prototype, and horizontal baseboard is not stretched into a slope board. Existing board/skirt producers do not by themselves prove all those contact lines or current readability in references 03·05; those observations remain unverified here.

UV origin is the finished-floor/wall contact at the run's starting end face. Long front/back faces use metre length along +X as U and metre height Y as V; upper bevel and end caps start new projections at each face's actual boundary at the same physical scale. Corner-miter grain resets to each run's +X, and textures do not continue across casing, stair or tile terminations. There is no articulation. The design source owner is `src/models/interior/baseboard.ts`; current `src/instances/baseboards.ts` derives runs from actual painted wall triangles, cuts them at placed casings and supplies model lengths, miters and world transforms. That implementation exists, but this document does not establish a fresh complete mesh/UV/binding/contact or GPU verdict.

Baseboard runs continue behind wall-adjacent sofas, bookcases, dressers, desks, benches, refrigerators, ranges, washer/dryers, L-shaped lower kitchen cabinets, wall-front sliding wardrobes, [dressing-room hanging sections](13-bedrooms.md#wardrobe-hanging), and [dressing-room shelf sections](13-bedrooms.md#wardrobe-shelves). Under the [empty band below furniture](00-model-frame.md#model-furniture-local-frame), these prototypes leave empty the volume 0.10 m above the floor and 0.015 m from the wall; when touching a side wall, such as the dryer's, bench's or dressing-room shelf's right side panel, they also clear that side's lower 0.015 m. At the laundry–garage door, baseboard stops at the casing's outer end, and the washer, folding counter and upper cabinet clear the casing's actual volume. At the pantry door, the pantry's rear shelf clears the separate casing's actual volume. Each run's termination derives only from compiled wall boundaries and casing outlines, without shortening room reservations or moving furniture forward. At open doors, it terminates at the outer edge of exterior-door `casing` or interior-door `casing-a/b`; at uncased openings, it terminates at the wall end. This decision partitions only exposed baseboard volume without recreating painted walls.

A straight run's closed `wall-baseboard` surface comes from the pentagonal-section extrusion over local X=[0,L] m, Y=[0,0.10] m and roomward Z=[0,0.015] m. The Z=0 back touching the structural wall also closes under this id; at 45° miters abutting neighboring runs, shared internal faces are excluded from exposed faces.

The prototype's [neutral review views](00-model-frame.md#model-review-set) use front orthographic view for the 0.10 m-high band/vertical end caps and side orthographic section for maximum 0.015 m projection/upper bevel. A face-id overlay at the same pose inspects whether front, back, top and cut ends belong to one `wall-baseboard` partition. After placement, inspect board continuity, empty bands below doors and corner duplication from inner corners and centers in 03's common area, 04's entry/stair and 05's upper corridor. At thresholds, compare casing outer ends and baseboard ends in close sections. Actual modelSources meshes and material bindings remain unverified.
