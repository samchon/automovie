# Fit-out population

## Ground-floor fit-out {#ground-floor-fit-out}

The living room contains a three-seat sofa, two armchairs, a low coffee table, a fireplace, a bookcase, a rug, and small plants. The rear common room contains a compact L-shaped kitchen with refrigerator, range, hood, sink, island and three stools, a six-seat dining table, a family sofa, a low table, and a media console. The pantry has shelves; the powder room has a toilet and hand basin; the laundry/mudroom has washer, dryer, bench, hooks, shoe storage, and a utility sink. The garage has wall cabinets and a deep shelf system but no car or vehicle proxy.

## Upper-floor fit-out {#upper-floor-fit-out}

The primary bedroom contains a queen bed, two nightstands, a low dresser, and a closet. Bedrooms two and three each contain a full-size bed, nightstand, dresser, and closet. The two bathrooms contain toilets, vanities, mirrors, and one tub/shower and one shower. Linen storage uses shallow shelves. The corridor remains short and visually legible from the stair landing.

## Population and identity {#population-and-identity}

Furniture is generated from a small set of reusable bounded prototypes and deterministic placements keyed to room ids. Placement changes do not change room boundaries or surface ownership. The population is intentionally ordinary, sparse enough to preserve circulation, and varied through material and orientation rather than luxury styling. No decorative object is allowed to become a hidden route, second room, or implied occupant.
