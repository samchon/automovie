Late 1809. Joséphine, having gained the position of Empress, cannot escape pressure over her failure to bear the child the dynasty needs. Napoleon chooses a marriage that may provide an heir over maintaining the relationship in which he has loved and lived with her. Do not reduce this to punishment for Joséphine's extravagance or a single moment when feeling vanished. The marriage she had protected through anxiety and social influence loses its legal security before the needs of state.

Do not combine notification, ceremony, relocation, and annulment of the church marriage into continuous actions on one night. Joséphine retains family and a life beyond helping the Emperor's next decision and quietly disappearing. Eugène too must protect his mother's safety and the responsibilities entrusted to him. The audience receives the result that the stability Napoleon wants comes to the people closest to him as separation.

## A daughter of an alliance and a wife who is a person {#remarriage}

The 1810 marriage to Marie Louise links the security and dynastic interests of two countries. Metternich's diplomacy and Austria's needs are at work, while the young bride is not introduced as a victor's ornament or a person soon to betray him. A person who must live between her father's country, her new husband, and her own security, not yet secured, enters a new family.

Napoleon gains pride in a connection to a long-established dynasty once hostile to him and the expectation of becoming a father. Keep the relationships continuing in Joséphine's household alongside the new dynasty's promise, without inventing secret remarks as fact. This marriage cannot resolve European hostility forever. The child of the next birth inherits at once a loved body and the position of heir to be used by war and diplomacy.
