## Success is still unknown when he leaves the island {#departure}

On February 26, 1815 Napoleon leaves Elba. Between his judgment that he can return and the result of gaining power on return lie the sea, the army, and people's choices. Introduce Bertrand as a man with both responsibility for following the Emperor again and a family life. The face who will later stand beside the coffin accumulates from here as a person who continues to perform duties and pay a family cost, not only as a symbol of loyalty.

Do not begin a return the audience already knows as a risk-free triumph. Napoleon needs discontent with the Restoration actually to become action supporting his name. That change does not happen automatically because the Emperor arrives. This time a man who does not yet have a state to command must obtain an answer from the army and people.

## Old reasons for loyalty grow stronger than the new king's orders {#regiments}

The choices of regiments that change sides are not an event in which everyone is magically fascinated by the Emperor. Old bonds, recognition of service, and discontent accumulated under the Restoration conflict with the cost of executing present orders. When soldiers change whom they follow, Napoleon regains strength and the monarchy loses troops it can actually use from the same paper rolls. Do not merge those who cheer and those who hesitate into one mind for a nation.

Ney's return does not cancel his demand for abdication in 1814 and turn him into a man unwaveringly loyal from the start. He chooses again amid changed politics and military conditions. The road to Paris creates a base for the new regime, but that base does not immediately give European recognition or a future without war. When cheers for success subside, the question remains what kind of rule the country will accept again.
