# A Victorious Army Trapped by the Sea

## Victory on land and defeat at sea {#nile}

Egypt, 1798. Victory at the Pyramids proves the French army's strength on land, but for the people subjected to it the new army changes daily life and faith, requisition and control. Napoleon seeks to join military power and scholarly inquiry as achievements of the same expedition. Keep the relationship between those who acquire knowledge and those who are governed, so the scientists' work does not absolve the occupation or make it harmless.

Nelson is not an enemy waiting to complete the French general's legend. The fleet's fighting and its sailors' labor defeat the French navy. Before news of the land victory has settled, possibilities for return and supply narrow at sea. Through Nelson's achievement, the audience first understands that occupying land was not victory for the whole expedition. The French command bears the weight of isolation only when the news actually reaches it.

Additional factual parents: [additional historical decisions](../settings/100-history/030-decisions-and-results.md#egypt-purpose), [credit, knowledge, and industry](../settings/030-society/020-credit-knowledge-and-industry.md#survey-knowledge).

Before battle, establish that France chose Egypt as a foothold for eastern trade and an opportunity to put pressure on Britain's position in India. Victory on land does not itself achieve that purpose; loss of the fleet narrows the conditions for communication and continuation. Surveyors measure the width between two walls of a building, compare it with a recorded value, and move to the next measurement. Local people cannot participate on equal terms in the survey and military control. Show actual scholarly work without fixing new specimen names, measurements, or private remarks as historical fact.

## Acre refuses the future {#syria}

In March 1799 the French army advances into Syria, captures Jaffa, and disposes of prisoners. The choice to break isolation through victory on land leaves responsibility for the deaths of people who surrendered. Do not turn the harm into a contest of numbers to prove Napoleon's cruelty. This disposal of prisoners does not occur on the same day as the later problem of the sick.

At Acre, Sidney Smith and the Ottoman defense stop the French. The defense is an opponent's achievement, not an accident interrupting the protagonist's destiny. The desire to advance conflicts with the need to bring back a sick and depleted army; in May the French abandon the siege and withdraw. An army that sought to break the enemy and open a road must first find a way to bring its own men back.

Jaffa, encountered again on the retreat, presents the problem of patients left behind. Do not show the March prisoners' deaths and the May patients' bodies both dealt with before the advance to Acre. Leave accounts of an order to poison and of opium as conflicting testimony; do not resolve them as one established act of mercy. The audience receives the cost in lives abandoned at different moments of advance and retreat. The army can turn around but cannot reverse that responsibility, and a debt accumulates that will separate the general from the army left behind on his return.

## The general returns and the army remains {#departure}

The 1799 land victory at Aboukir does not cancel the earlier Battle of the Nile. Separate Napoleon, who leaves first for France, from Kléber, who takes charge of the Army of the Orient. The former heads toward political opportunity in France; the latter must actually attend to the army and local problems left by the man who departed. Do not combine the assignments and returns of Desaix and the others on one ship.

The audience must not understand the eastern story as finished when the general returns. Bring back the consequences for the army and inhabitants through Kléber's death at the time of Marengo. The political reputation gained by the return carries the debt of those who could not return with him.
