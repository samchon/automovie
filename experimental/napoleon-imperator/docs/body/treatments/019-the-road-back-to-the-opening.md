Trafalgar, October 21. Nelson fights relying on subordinates' discretion and signals, and the actual labor of sailing and gunnery. His death and the fleet's victory are not merged into one sentimental moment. Remaining command and sailors' work continue to secure the result. Do not create a new close scene or dialogue for Collingwood, whose individual performance is not selected here; retain the fact that command of the fleet continues.

News reaches London in the early hours of November 6. The audience knows the battle and its transmission first, while the Emperor on the continent does not yet know. Britain receives the victory as a condition for politics and the next war, and the French army continues on its own road. Until the naval result and information received on the continent meet again, the audience's knowledge runs ahead of the characters'.

## Success at a bridge gives no authority to make an armistice {#bridges}

On the road through Dürnstein, the Vienna bridge, and Schöngrabern, distribute battlefield judgment and danger between Murat and Lannes. The daring that wins a bridge does not also give authority to stop the war on one's own. Murat relies on an armistice he cannot conclude independently, and pursuit is delayed. Russian forces use the time to retreat, postponing the decision the French wanted.

Napoleon judges the armistice a deception and rebukes Murat. Convey deception as his contemporary judgment, not as an objective account confirming the Russian command's secret intent. First let the audience know the expectation beyond Murat's authority and the actual delay, then separately receive how the Emperor interprets the result. The change by which one man's expectation cost the whole army time is clear without leaving only mockery of vanity or showing an unconfirmed secret plan.

Additional factual parent: [additional historical decisions](../settings/100-history/030-decisions-and-results.md#vienna-bridge).

At the Vienna bridge the French must cross a bridge its defenders intend to destroy. Murat and Lannes's side claims an armistice; while Bertrand holds Geringer in conversation, Oudinot's grenadiers cross and cut the fuse. First receive the actual result of securing the bridge and guns, then move separately to the failure in which Murat's expectation of an armistice stops the pursuit. Do not merge the initial success of a false claim and the later delay caused by relying on the enemy's word beyond his own authority into one success.

On November 18 news of Trafalgar reaches Napoleon at Znaim. The belated defeat is not a motive to set the army, already turned, in motion again, but a continuing constraint on later possibilities at sea, in trade, and for invasion. With a separate loss that cannot be repaid by continental victory, the army must concentrate in Moravia.

The advance reaches the unconcentrated state of November 27. Do not repeat the outpost retreat of the 28th, already shown, as a complete scene. The audience now knows the past of the three marshals at the post house and the army's achievements and fatigue, and understands the opening anxiety differently. Connect the gap through the first night of victory to events already seen, and cross to December 3 for the next delivered event. Do not write the same decision twice to celebrate the Emperor's success again.
