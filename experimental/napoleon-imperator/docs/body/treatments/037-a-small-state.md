# Rule Does Not End on an Island

## Remaining authority is used on a smaller land {#elba}

Factual parents: [Elba and the Congress of Vienna](../settings/100-history/020-life-and-memory.md#return-and-waterloo), [the work of governing](../settings/030-society/010-power-and-cost.md#government), [family and authority](../settings/030-society/010-power-and-cost.md#dynasty-and-belief), [the period's scope](../settings/010-work/010-identity.md#scope).

Elba, 1814. Rather than remain a prisoner able to do nothing, Napoleon uses the governing authority left to him on a small island. Street paving and installation of water facilities become achievements that actually change residents' living space. Yet he does not have funds to complete every project begun or planned. In road and villa construction, the desire to build more widely clashes with cost, and halted work reveals the limits of authority. Do not combine facilities that reached residents and stopped work in one list of completed projects.

The historian distinguishes achievements and interruptions reported by Houssaye's later synthesis from plans Campbell recorded in the first week. Do not make work start and finish at once because it appeared in a plan. Do not settle conflicting income totals, mine production, or exact project counts, or add unadopted mine improvements and school reform. Preserve the need to reduce the scale of work because of cost, without turning it into a single cause that all money ran out and therefore he left the island.

Instead of seeing only plans for a comeback after downfall, the audience receives both achievements possible under limited authority that change people's lives and limits beyond which it cannot be expanded at will. Even doing this, he cannot summon his wife and son by his own wish. Satisfaction in retaining some rule and deprivation in not recovering his family and place on the continent press differently on the coming news and choice to return.
