# The Opponents Prepare While War Pauses

## Mediation's deadline closes over the price of reduced domination {#mediation}

Factual parents: [armistice and rebuilding](../settings/100-history/020-life-and-memory.md#russia-and-coalition), [Allied money](../settings/030-society/010-power-and-cost.md#finance), [reform and conscription](../settings/030-society/010-power-and-cost.md#recruitment), [Metternich](../settings/050-people/010-ensemble.md#metternich), [Bernadotte](../settings/050-people/010-ensemble.md#bernadotte).

During the 1813 armistice Napoleon gains time to repair his army, while Austria seeks security and balance using mediation as a lever. Metternich is not a man who sees through the Emperor's true intentions and already knows the outcome; he calculates his country's weakness and the sides available to it. Treat the armistice, British subsidy treaties, and Austria's conditional commitment as separate processes creating different forces; do not finish them all in one meeting.

In the Prague mediation, dissolution of the Confederation of the Rhine and return of Illyria are the price of reducing Napoleon's control through alliances in Germany and territorial authority. After the audience knows these objects, let it follow the conflict between powers he must surrender to gain peace and powers he believes he can defend by fighting again. As he holds out, finding the balance the other side wants hard to reconcile with preserving his regime, no agreement is reached within the deadline. Do not replace this breakdown with a reading of every condition or a witty exchange at a private meeting. Leave detailed words of that meeting in their source layer as Metternich's later self-defense.

In the answer Napoleon writes after the conference closes, he expresses willingness to surrender the Confederation of the Rhine and part of Illyria, among other things. The audience learns of this concession after his earlier resistance, but Austria declares war before the answer is conveyed. A willingness written late cannot turn closed mediation back into agreement. Rather than stubbornness without a single concession from beginning to end, leave the difference between holding out to protect authority and a concession written after the deadline. War resumes with Austria no longer in the same neutral space. The next section reveals the process by which countries have meanwhile prepared money and armies, and the resumed war inherits that preparation.

British subsidy promises and treaties combine with each country's conscription and reforms and Russian and Prussian determination to continue. Do not treat promised money as cash all arriving that day. British taxation and credit make borrowing possible, and that finance gives power to promise the conditions for allies to fight again. Allies prepare recruits and equipment while awaiting support, so a promise alone does not complete an army. Conscripted households surrender workers; the state must turn those bodies into an army through training and equipment. Even revived capacity through reform carries remaining obligations and losses to daily life. Develop the burdens of finance and reorganization together so defeated countries do not become enemies who resurrect endless troops for free.

Bernadotte acts from Sweden's interests rather than helping the Emperor out of affection as a former French marshal. Blücher and Gneisenau's action and calculation, and the army Schwarzenberg must lead under the constraints of several sovereigns, have different capabilities. The coalition holds not because its members trust one another completely, but because they need together to limit Napoleon. The choice to avoid him and strike marshals separately rather than repeatedly confront him changes the next campaign.
