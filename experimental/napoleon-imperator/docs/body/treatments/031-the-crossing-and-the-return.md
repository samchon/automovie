At the Berezina the possibility of the army's escape depends on Éblé and engineers actually building bridges in water and cold. Even with the Emperor's wishes and the will to escape, there is no crossing without tools, time, and bodies able to work. Develop the fighting to defend the bridges alongside the movement across them, so the achievement of military recovery does not conceal the sacrifice of labor.

The survival of people crossing and the loss of people left behind or abandoned are results of the same event. Do not use unsupported survivor counts or a temperature without a scale to measure the tragedy. Bourgogne's experience of surviving is not proof that the whole army was saved in the same order. Leave the audience both relief at escape and the fact that not everyone could be saved. Do not erase either result under one name of victory or defeat.

## The man who returns to the capital demands a new army {#return}

Napoleon's return at the end of 1812 differs from the return of the entire scattered army. Caulaincourt's close companionship carries responsibility for getting the Emperor back safely and the weight of a person who knows the conditions of defeat. In the capital, the ruler who has returned alive can gather the regime and strength for the next war again. For the remaining soldiers, a road on which they must still walk, eat, and survive remains.

Loss becomes the need for new conscription, horses, and equipment and reaches French homes again. The state asks for more people from a society that has already sent sons. Instead of declaring that everything is restored because Napoleon returned, close with the result that a talented commander lives, while the quality of the army and alliances needed to carry out his talent have changed greatly. The next war begins in this imbalance.
