The main narrative continues on December 3, 1805. The audience now meets the same victory knowing the history by which the army came into being. Napoleon must turn the battle's result into diplomatic achievement; the defeated sovereign has reason to preserve the country and army that remain. Distinguish the emperors' meeting and armistice agreement on the 4th, the formal armistice on the 6th, and the Treaty of Pressburg on the 26th as separate results. One handshake does not automatically write the remaining documents.

The defeated side's choice to accept terms is a judgment to avoid greater loss, not eternal submission. After December 3, for Moravian residents the victorious army is a present force demanding food and money. Against requisition for the army's next movement, residents hold out to keep their own provisions and livelihoods. Choosing refusal and resistance rather than simply handing over what is demanded prevents procurement from ending in smooth payment. The army increases coercion to take what it needs; deprivation and resentment accumulate among people left after feeding it. Do not fix the amount ordered, the money actually collected, and what was paid with it as one calculation.

This progression chooses resistance by a group of residents after the battle. Do not move the particular unnamed winegrower from the night of December 2 here, or add a new name, speech, or unrecorded death and injury.
