In renewed war Napoleon remains a threatening opponent. For that reason the Allies do not want to stake the entire war on a single result where he is present. If armies and marshals in other directions come under pressure, the Emperor cannot personally correct every place. French command's orders and movements and Allied avoidance and concentration narrow one another's next choices.

Do not combine Bernadotte's calculations for his country, Blücher's energy, Gneisenau's staff work, and Schwarzenberg's caution into one Allied genius. Political necessity to preserve the coalition remains through each delay and achievement. The audience understands not that Napoleon's ability has suddenly vanished, but that his opponents have learned to avoid it.

Premature demolition of the bridge interrupts it and leaves losses including Poniatowski's death.
