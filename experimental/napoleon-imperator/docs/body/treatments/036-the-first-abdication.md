# People Who Will Fight No Longer in the Emperor's Name

## Remaining loyalty demands an end to war {#abdication}

Factual parents: [abdication and family in 1814](../settings/100-history/020-life-and-memory.md#return-and-waterloo), [Ney](../settings/050-people/010-ensemble.md#ney), [Marie Louise](../settings/050-people/010-ensemble.md#marie-louise), [the son](../settings/050-people/010-ensemble.md#napoleon-ii), [Joséphine](../settings/050-people/010-ensemble.md#josephine), [corps movement and self-justification](../settings/050-people/010-ensemble.md#marmont).

At Fontainebleau, as Napoleon holds to the possibility of fighting further, the need not to consume more of the army and country grows for marshals including Ney. Do not erase their years fighting together and call them traitors from the beginning. Make the difference an actual conflict between a time when loyalty to the Emperor meant continuing to march and this moment when it means refusing to march and demanding a stop.

Napoleon attempts a conditional abdication that can continue the dynasty in his son's name after him. This choice around April 4 is not a final abdication in which he has already abandoned every alternative. Do not fix the document's April 4/5 variants, the list of marshals, or the exchange to one exact clock for a meeting. The audience receives the last possibility in which a man whose authority to continue war is shaken holds to another authority through his son.

Then, on the night of April 4–5, the Essonnes corps moves. Set Marmont's self-justification alongside Houssaye's account, without fixing one interpretation of a secret intention to sell out from the start or a victim unaware of any consequences. News of the defection closes the military possibility on which conditional succession could rely. Once the expectation of gathering troops again to exert pressure is cut off, Napoleon moves to unconditional abdication on the 6th.

Paris's deposition procedure, the Emperor's two different choices, and the later treaty are not completed simultaneously by one declaration. The attempt to preserve his son's name does not become actual succession to power. The audience receives the closing in turn of military authority and dynastic alternatives through which he could exercise his ability, rather than the disappearance of that ability.

Additional factual parent: [additional historical decisions](../settings/100-history/030-decisions-and-results.md#essonnes).

Identify the Essonnes corps movement as a departure from Napoleon's command. Troops available to support negotiation for his son's succession disappear, closing the leverage of the conditional proposal. After that result proceed to unconditional abdication without inventing a confrontation in which Marmont's inner thoughts are known.

Marie Louise and their son become diplomatic figures whom the Emperor's wishes alone cannot bring together in one place. She faces pressure from her father's country, her child, and her own safety; the son has no actual power to inherit his father's state. Do not give a three-year-old words already interpreting his later fate. The family's separation results from security expected of a dynastic marriage being divided by different countries' demands.

Do not turn Joséphine's death on May 29, 1814 into a new scene of farewell and reconciliation with the abdicated Napoleon. Do not invent an exact day the news reached him; convey that her life closed that day. Eugène, with his own responsibilities in Italy, does not become another person who comes to the Emperor and hands him a symbolic object. The audience is left with families whose lives have continued apart from the name of the state.
