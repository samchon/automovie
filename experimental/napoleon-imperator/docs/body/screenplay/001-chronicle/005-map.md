# The People with the Map

## The day after victory {#settlement}

[Event parent 1](../../../body/treatments/020-the-price-of-victory.md#negotiation) · [Event parent 2](../../../body/treatments/020-the-price-of-victory.md#requisition) · [Scene parent](../../../body/scripts/001-chronicle/005-map.md#settlement)

December 3, 1805. On a road where battle has passed, a garrison cart waits for supplies. A villager moving provisions to hand over pulls a share to keep back toward themselves.

RESIDENT: We need this to eat.

PROCURING SOLDIER: We have to eat here too.

As the resident grips the sack, troops close in. Soldiers move even the food the resident meant to keep to the cart. The army leaves with provisions for its next movement. Gathering what remains after the seizure, the resident watches them for a long time.

December 4, the sovereigns' meeting. Show the public distance at which Napoleon and Francis II meet. A caption identifies the meeting and armistice agreement. Move to the formal armistice document on the 6th, then return to garrison and residents still waiting. Again, after the army takes its share, residents gather what is left. The person who earlier held the sack watches the cart leave, then turns away.

December 26, Treaty of Pressburg. Even after the sound of signing stops, wheels carrying food continue. The historian's voice is brief and heard only over the document.

HISTORIAN (voice): Victory became power in negotiation. The day of agreement was not the day the burden of war was actually paid.

## Victory where the Emperor is absent {#two-fields}

[Event parent 1](../../../body/treatments/021-two-battles-and-a-report.md#two-fields) · [Event parent 2](../../../body/treatments/021-two-battles-and-a-report.md#report) · [Scene parent](../../../body/scripts/001-chronicle/005-map.md#two-fields)

1806. Prussia's demand appears: “French troops to withdraw beyond the Rhine.” As the image connects French deployment with Prussia's room for action in Germany, the historian speaks.

HISTORIAN (voice): The place where Prussia sought to exercise its power overlapped the military and alliance order made by France.

Among Prussians stands a queen, upright. Screen text: Louise. Move from her face to the path of her country's demand. It leaves the place of dispatch and is delivered elsewhere.

October 14. Clearly divide the image by two battlefield names. At Jena, French attack breaks the Prussian left and center. A separate corps then counterattacks, but cannot reverse the defeat. Follow the victorious Napoleon, then change the distance and direction of cannon with the caption “Auerstädt.”

The enemy before Davout is nearly twice his size. Cavalry approaches. Anxious soldiers inside a square point muskets at it. Davout speaks low and briefly.

DAVOUT: Hold formation. We will bring in the next division when it arrives.

The leading men must wait longer than those words. Follow the work of loading after firing; when a gap appears, a man beside it steps in. Only after following divisions actually arrive and thicken the line do they counterattack. Davout watches the enemy withdraw, then turns toward his own army's losses.

Next morning, a report reaches Napoleon. He stops the hand pointing at Jena.

NAPOLEON: Davout defeated that army?

He checks the report again. After confirming that Davout defeated an enemy almost twice his own force, he points to the two fields separately.

After defeat Louise conveys a request to preserve her country. Follow her face and voice as she asks without inventing a partner or place for a new private meeting.

LOUISE (reconstructed request): Please preserve Prussia and the dynasty.

The words are not an original quotation from literature.

## An army remains {#eylau-and-tilsit}

[Event parent 1](../../../body/treatments/022-blood-and-tilsit.md#eylau) · [Event parent 2](../../../body/treatments/022-blood-and-tilsit.md#tilsit) · [Event parent 3](../../../body/treatments/022-blood-and-tilsit.md#new-map) · [Scene parent](../../../body/scripts/001-chronicle/005-map.md#eylau-and-tilsit)

EXT. EYLAU — SNOWSTORM, 1807

Infantry emerge from the snow and vanish into it again. A man looks toward the men beside him for direction; their line is already passing out of his sight to the left.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): Augereau's corps lost its direction in the storm and drifted left.

Russian artillery fires into the infantry. The advancing bodies break apart under the fire. Men who have turned to find their place meet others trying to get away. The attack collapses before the camera leaves them.

Beyond the fugitives, cavalry gathers motion. Murat is among the horsemen as they go forward.

We remain low enough to feel horses passing close, then follow their movement into the Russian lines. The riders push through. From farther back, the passage opens and disappears amid men, horses and smoke.

The sound of hooves recedes. Gunfire continues.

EXT. EYLAU — AFTER THE FIGHTING

A wounded man tries to draw his clothing over himself. His fingers fail to hold the cloth. Another wounded body lies beyond his reach.

The wind moves loose fabric against the snow. French soldiers still hold their ground.

Across the field, Russian soldiers are together under their officers. Men move among them carrying the business of an army still in being.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): The French had held. The cavalry's intervention had not destroyed Russia's forces. For the wounded left in winter, surviving the fighting was only the first danger.

EXT. FRENCH ARMY — LATER IN 1807

SCREEN TEXT: Friedland — French victory.

The army moves on from another battle. News of victory is being carried away from it.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): Friedland brought a French victory before the emperors met at Tilsit. It brought Napoleon to the meeting with power. Alexander still had to choose what Russia would do.

INT. NEGOTIATION AT TILSIT — 1807

Napoleon and Alexander face one another across a table. Napoleon sits forward; Alexander rests against the back of his chair.

NAPOLEON: We can end the war here.

ALEXANDER: You have won a battle. Russia will still have interests when we leave this room.

NAPOLEON: I know that.

ALEXANDER: I will not make peace by placing Russia at your disposal.

NAPOLEON: I am asking you to settle with me.

Alexander waits for him to go on. Napoleon lets the answer stand.

ALEXANDER: And afterwards?

NAPOLEON: You will still answer for Russia. I shall have to deal with your government.

ALEXANDER: I can refuse this peace.

NAPOLEON: Yes. Will you agree?

Napoleon waits. Alexander considers the man opposite him, then brings his chair closer to the table.

ALEXANDER: Yes. Russia is better served by an agreement than by continuing this war.

NAPOLEON: Then we are agreed.

Alexander offers his hand. Napoleon takes it. We stay with both men as the handshake ends and they remain together to conclude the settlement.

A separate view of Louise, Queen of Prussia. Hear her request as reconstructed speech without a reverse view establishing a private audience.

LOUISE (reconstructed request): Prussia must survive this. Its dynasty must survive.

Her appeal ends. Remain with her before returning to the agreement made between the two emperors.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): Prussia could not bargain as an equal. Its humiliation did not end the country or the work its people would do.

EXT. TERRITORIES CHANGED BY FRENCH POWER — SUCCESSIVE DAYS

People wait where the business of government is conducted. One is admitted to work that gives him a place he did not have before. Elsewhere, a declaration of changed personal status is read to people who listen, then return to obligations still waiting for them.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): Some gained status, release from personal subordination, or a path upward. Freedom in one relation did not extinguish every obligation.

Money is collected. Men leave those who have come to see them off. The administration that offers advancement also requires payment and bodies for France's wars.

INT. PRUSSIAN WORKING ROOMS — OVER TIME

Officers and civil officials work among papers, interrupt one another, return to unfinished business. A discussion in one room carries into another sitting with other participants.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): Prussian reorganization took collective work over time. The limit on its retained army strength came in 1808, after Tilsit.

The officials continue with the work still unfinished.

## A road to live on and the Emperor's road {#blockade}

[Event parent 1](../../../body/treatments/023-the-blockade-enters-homes.md#blockade) · [Event parent 2](../../../body/treatments/023-the-blockade-enters-homes.md#crowns) · [Scene parent](../../../body/scripts/001-chronicle/005-map.md#blockade)

Screen text: “The Netherlands.”

A port enforcing the Continental System. Incoming goods stop and a merchant looks for a way back. As an inspector examines goods, the line of people waiting lengthens.

MERCHANT: If I cannot sell these, I cannot bring in the next lot.

INSPECTOR: Those goods cannot enter.

Behind hands reloading the goods, a small movement seeks another route. The next inspection becomes stricter.

Screen text: “Italy.”

Other people's goods await inspection. Follow a group taking back loads that did not pass and another seeking to move around enforcement. Their movement stops when an enforcer checks that road again.

Screen text: “Warsaw.”

Another group has incoming goods inspected. Do not reuse faces or goods from the two earlier places. People trying to move blocked goods by another route wait again before stricter checking. In each region, after movement evading inspection, the next inspection becomes stricter. Do not skip the time between.

Naples, 1807. King Joseph, the elder brother, communicates local demands in a letter. In France, younger brother Napoleon answers with imperial authority. The voices reconstruct the letters' meaning, not original quotations. Do not hear the other voice as present in each man's space.

HISTORIAN (voice): Joseph, the elder brother, was king of Naples. Napoleon, the younger, demanded enforcement from him as Emperor.

JOSEPH (reconstructed letter voice): To work as king here, people must be able to live. I cannot govern them by enforcement alone, Sire.

NAPOLEON (reconstructed letter voice): If they trade with Britain, the other side can keep fighting. Enforce it.

Joseph reads the answer that arrives. Unable to write his next reply at once, he lays local demands beside it.

Napoleon at work. Talleyrand comes on diplomatic business and takes his place favoring one leg. As Napoleon points out work to enforce, Talleyrand asks to speak.

TALLEYRAND: Sire, as our rule expands, we have more countries to deal with. We must preserve a balance between their interests.

NAPOLEON: They escape because enforcement is lacking. We must stop it more strictly.

TALLEYRAND: Losing those relationships will not make us secure.

Napoleon draws the document for enforcement toward himself.

NAPOLEON: Enforce it as it stands.

Talleyrand cannot continue. The decided document goes out in a direction different from his advice.

Back at the Dutch port, a person who received goods back ties them up again. Enforcement continues.

## Changing the king does not end a country {#spain}

[Event parent 1](../../../body/treatments/024-spain-will-not-close.md#bayonne) · [Event parent 2](../../../body/treatments/024-spain-will-not-close.md#bailen) · [Event parent 3](../../../body/treatments/024-spain-will-not-close.md#peninsula) · [Scene parent](../../../body/scripts/001-chronicle/005-map.md#spain)

EXT. MADRID — MAY 1808

Residents confront the occupation troops in a street. People press forward from different doorways; others drag those closest to them back toward shelter. The French troops advance into the resistance.

The view is caught among residents trying to get out of the troops' path. A door is pulled almost shut, then opened again to let someone through. Shots sound in the street.

EXT. MADRID — AFTER THE SUPPRESSION

Prisoners stand before armed men. We remain with the waiting bodies until the firing starts.

Later, families search for the people taken away. A woman looks into a group of returning residents, passes them, and goes back to search among the places where the prisoners were held. Someone calls a name out of our hearing. No answer reaches us.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): Madrid resisted before Joseph came. Those who fought did not all want the same politics. They contested foreign occupation, the loss of their authority, and power over their own land. Suppression left families looking for people they would not bring home.

INT. BAYONNE — LATER IN 1808

SCREEN TEXT: Bayonne — abdications and a change of dynasty.

The formal business passes from one set of royal hands to another. The historian's voice carries us from the abdications to Napoleon facing his elder brother.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): Joseph's crown changed from Naples to Spain. The elder brother remained subject to the younger brother's imperial power.

NAPOLEON: Spain is yours to govern.

JOSEPH: With French troops keeping me there.

NAPOLEON: You will need them.

Joseph looks toward the doorway through which the business of his new government will arrive.

JOSEPH: Then every order I give will have to pass through a country that sees your soldiers first.

Napoleon turns to the next business. Joseph remains where his brother has put him.

INT. FRENCH HEADQUARTERS — LATER

A report is delivered. The man reading it reaches the name Bailén and stops to read the passage again.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): At Bailén, a French army was defeated. Reputation had failed to keep possession of Spain.

Napoleon takes the report. The room waits while he reads.

EXT. ERFURT — BEFORE NAPOLEON'S RENEWED INTERVENTION IN SPAIN

Napoleon and Alexander appear together before watching attendants. Their public courtesies continue as the historian names what the meeting actually secured.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): Russia promised to declare war on Austria if Austria declared war on France. Napoleon did not obtain the stronger commitment he sought over the placement of Russian corps. Alexander retained Russia's own judgment about entering the war.

The sovereigns part. Napoleon turns toward the men waiting on his side; Alexander is already attending to those on his.

INT. NAPOLEON'S WORKING ROOM — AFTER ERFURT

The affairs of Spain lie before Napoleon with other military business. An officer waits to know which papers to take.

NAPOLEON: I shall go to Spain.

He returns the Spanish papers separately, then keeps the remaining business before him.

NAPOLEON: These commands must be carried on without me.

The officer takes the work entrusted to him. Napoleon calls for the Spanish papers again.

EXT. IBERIAN COAST — DURING THE WAR

Cargo comes ashore from sea transport. Men carry it off while more waits aboard. The movement continues after the first load has gone inland.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): Sea supply and a base that could be sustained helped Wellington keep an army in the field.

INT. WELLINGTON'S HEADQUARTERS — DAY

Wellington listens to an officer who has come from the coast.

OFFICER: The supplies are coming ashore, sir.

WELLINGTON: Keep them moving to the army. I need to know what the base can maintain.

The officer takes his instructions away. Wellington turns to the needs still waiting before him.

EXT. PORTUGUESE AND SPANISH PREPARATIONS — SUCCESSIVE PLACES

Portuguese men prepare equipment under their own officers. Spanish groups gather and make their own preparations in inhabited country.

French troops waiting for provisions look up at approaching movement. It is their escort returning without the load. An officer goes out to meet them.

ESCORT SOLDIER: We could not bring it through.

See the denied route separately: Spanish resistance holds the passage, while the French withdraw with the supplies they cannot carry onward. No new engagement or injury is shown. Return to the waiting troops. The officer has nothing to distribute; men set down the empty containers they had brought. These unnamed actions reconstruct continuing pressure on supply, not a newly identified historical incident.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): British, Portuguese and Spanish forces had distinct authorities.

EXT. IBERIAN PORT — DAY

Cargo continues to pass from ship to shore as the historian reaches back to 1807.

HISTORIAN (V.O.): In 1807 the Portuguese royal family had moved to Brazil under Sidney Smith's escort. Opening Brazil's ports changed trade across the Atlantic. Smith's escort and his defense of Acre were separate achievements.

INT. JOSEPH'S GOVERNMENT — 1809

Joseph asks an official about the marshals' correspondence.

JOSEPH: Where are their reports?

OFFICIAL: They go directly to the minister of war, Sire.

JOSEPH: In France.

OFFICIAL: Yes, Sire.

Elsewhere, a marshal's dispatch is passed onward for the minister of war. It continues along that route without reaching Joseph's desk.

Back with Joseph, the government business before him requires answers.

JOSEPH: I am expected to govern with an army that reports past me.

The official cannot supply the missing reports. Joseph takes up the government business waiting on his desk.

INT. CÁDIZ — LATER IN THE CONTINUING WAR

The Cortes sits in public political work. A speaker has the floor; others attend to the business before them. Voices and the movement of people in the chamber carry through the cut to the country outside.

EXT. IBERIAN COUNTRY — THE CONTINUING WAR

French troops are still moving through a country in resistance. They pass out of sight while the distant sound of firing continues.

After the screen closes, “Author's Commentary.”

AUTHOR (voice): This time we followed the work of people sharing a map. The time Davout won and the time the Emperor learned of the victory were different. We also separated the Tilsit agreement in time from the later army limit. Conversations by residents and merchants were reconstructed. We wanted to show whom the blockade pressed and who sought another route. We did not place resistance in different countries under one person's command.
