# Scene and performance notation

Choose a consistent readable notation before drafting scripts or final screenplay. [Production layout](../production/layout.md) owns default files and identity conventions; the selected contracts judge the execution and expression.

Scene headings can name the used place, interior or exterior, and story time. Keep action and each speaker's actual words in distinct blocks, in the order they are performed and filmed. Mark an off-screen speaker, voiceover, overlap, visible text, flashback and return, intercut, or montage when used and otherwise ambiguous.

Screenplay prose, audiovisual columns, and shot-oriented text can all carry that meaning. Match a required submission format when the deliverable calls for it; Markdown H1/H2 identities remain navigation and evidence units. Their boundaries need not imply a shot count or uniform duration.

Read the blocks as a continuous sequence of seen and heard events. Follow exchanges with their adjacent action, and compare each transition's entry with the preceding exit. A table read can refine spoken timing when available. Final duration comes from actual action, fixed speech, pauses, and the performed edit, not page or word count.

For a motion hint, direct the simple figure's movement and performance accurately; a coarse representation does not excuse an ambiguous action. Retain the complete screenplay's intended experience even where an irrelevant object's detail stays in text. [Motion](../motion/SKILL.md) programs their performance and staging, and [production](../production/SKILL.md) carries them into the commissioned whole video; still images serve only their actual supporting scope. Use [3D modeling](../3d-modeling/SKILL.md) for representation and [delivery review](../delivery-review/SKILL.md) for observation.
