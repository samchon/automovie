# Adopt and retarget motion

Use this branch for recorded, generated or imported motion, or for changing a character's proportions/rig while preserving performance. Read the accepted source motion, the [rig characterization](../3d-modeling/rigging.md), required screenplay decisions and installed kinematics, motion, import and rendering contracts. A source clip's name or recognizability cannot establish its timing, contacts or semantic correspondence.

## Establish the source and target meanings

Freeze the accepted source bytes and conversion settings with their actual skeleton, rest pose, frame/time convention, root trajectory, body scale and usage terms. Record source-to-target semantic bones, joint axes, rest frames, units and coordinate conversion. Imported joint quaternions are not automatically the clinical joint-angle representation an installed retargeter accepts. Normalize through supported operations or retain a compatible imported playback path; do not relabel an arbitrary clip as a normalized humanoid clip.

Inspect the selected operation's actual admission and result envelope. The installed humanoid retargeter has source/target structural and target range-of-motion checks, explicit rig interpretation and root scaling; its contact warnings can accompany a usable returned motion. Preserve those warnings and nullable/refused results rather than treating a non-null clip as proof of contact fidelity. Never widen canonical joint limits, discard a required bone or invent a reset to conceal an incompatible conversion.

Set root scale from meaningful dimensions and the current API's actual convention. Confirm facing, root translation, bone lengths, foot positions, hand reach and attached props after conversion. Preserve the intended action and result rather than merely the source joint angles. If the target cannot reach a required support or object, repair the rig, staging, path or authorized action at its owner; a different outcome is a narrative change.

## Preserve consequential contacts and time

Identify when feet support the body and when hands or other effectors grasp, push, lean or release. Bind contacts to the actual ground or object frame and declare required contact windows where supported. Check stance sliding, penetration, floating, reach, support transfer, release and subsequent motion, including moving support or objects when promised. Root rescaling alone cannot preserve every contact under changed proportions. Use [physical feedback](physics.md) for supported correction and diagnostics; a kinematic retarget is not proof of whole-body dynamics.

Respect source sampling and the assembled [clock](timeline.md). Recheck interpolation, root trajectory and contacts at the actual output times, critical extrema, cut/trim boundaries and any permitted seeking/restart path. A ground checker that skips absent bones or tests only sampled endpoints cannot prove required bones exist or establish continuous collision freedom. Validate the actual scope and disclose those limits rather than turning an empty check into a negative finding.

## Accept the actual performance

Compare the source and retargeted progression from matched views as well as the actual shot cameras, using the adopted [motion questions](../contracts/motion.md). Keep the entire relevant sequence in the observation round; a still pose can hide foot sliding, velocity spikes or changed exchanges. Measure tolerances from the production promise and physical/control requirements, not from a favorable solver result. The [research references](research.md) motivate these checks without importing robotics benchmarks as film acceptance criteria.

Record the accepted motion, rig interpretation, diagnostics and observations with their actual inputs under [execution traceability](../evidence/execution.md). Hand that performance to production's edit; new takes, rig changes or time fitting invalidate affected observations and synchronization. Do not renew closed screenplay evidence to accept a motion clip.
