## Locked first response, before contracts and parents

The six scripts bodies and six screenplay bodies, with all twelve indexes, were read in their authored sequence. The main line is intelligible: Gaesomun learns that getting there first does not move everyone; the king he kills leaves that same governing burden; Namsaeng learns delegation, denies his brothers a hearing, and knowingly opens the roads he restored to conquest after losing Heonchung. The last surviving son inherits responsibility without absolution. Tactical and administrative difficulties have visible effects rather than serving only as rules.

Part 1: “Did your bow get here first?” (scripts, t003-shared-bow) makes rank and competitive pride funny without a separate joke scene. “Does the work at home wait because he rests?” (scripts, t004-night-shortcut) turns military achievement toward household loss. I followed the delayed understanding. Initial continuity concern C1: in screenplay t003-shared-bow, “At the gyeongdang as the sun falls, the shooting place is empty” leads to the private delayed meeting, but “The next child receives the shared bow from Eulbo” then introduces a group contest without a new time or re-entry. A filmmaker must invent whether this is later that evening or another day. The corresponding scripts private meeting ends with “For now, the boys still argue only over whose turn it is,” which does not require other children to reappear.

Part 2: “We laid down our weapons together too” (screenplay, t011-prisoner-sorting) makes the sorting consequential before the narrator distinguishes the historical execution claim. “I will share the risk of what remains unrepaired” (screenplay, t013-eulbo-after-withdrawal) is limited by actual remaining repair calls. I understood Gaesomun gaining the king's burden while retaining his refusal to say the killing was wrong. Initial concern C2: historian passages sometimes interrupt immediate work to distinguish recorded counts, claims and reconstructions, especially t002-hero-becomes-king, t003-inflated-victory and t012-withdrawal-645. They preserve uncertainty but may ask the audience to decode a source apparatus. This is a concern to test against adopted narrator requirements, not yet a finding.

Part 3: “That does not erase the month gained before it” (screenplay, t018-defeat-and-trust) protects the purpose of the Yalu defense without pretending victory. The two households at the gate share the rule and differ in the practical ability to depart. The physical loads that do not follow the lead's feet make the Silla transport legible. Initial concern C3: repeated confirmations, lists, reports and waiting in t020-local-concentration through t024-handover can seem procedurally alike. Check whether each performs a distinct causal and human task before asking for compression. The wet load, ration shortfall, fire and Chano's damaged hands supply such differences in several places.

Part 4: “The companion ties it for him, then puts it back in his hand” (screenplay, t025-guidol-dies) survives into the cord check at Imjin and the untying before Ajin. Those dependencies make the injury real. The Ren, Sasu and Imjin attacks have distinguishable geometry, defenders' responses and failed connections. “We broke three. We didn't strike all six” (both layers, t029-su-withdrawal) answers the opening boast and protects other contributors. Initial concern C4: headline totals such as 130,000 nonreturners require parent verification because the visible battles do not independently let an audience derive them. This is a provenance check, not a demand to stage every casualty.

Part 5: “Neither younger brother gets another chance to speak” (screenplay, t030-demands-after-victory) makes Namsaeng's later isolation partly his own work. His recovery policy can be sound and his handling of brothers still damaging. “I cannot surrender these three hundred guards on my own authority” (screenplay, t036-heonchung-rejects-waiting) gives Durim a constrained alternative without making Heonchung's death an assassination. Reports arrive on separate days. Initial concern C5: “Three days later the answers stop” (scripts, t037-treatment-fails-on-third-day) could differ from the screenplay's “Third day after the wound”; check earliest parent for the intended count.

Part 6: “I know the country may fall. Even so...” (screenplay, t038-namsaeng-chooses-destructive-aid) states knowledge and destructive intent before the road is handed over. Seokbu's refusal has a home to protect, and Sokro chooses those going home. “Do I live there only helping yours?” (screenplay, t041-four-migrate-to-henan-together) gives Seou work of her own. The family's final shown home remains in Henan after the return order. “I knew when I went too. I asked for aid. I have my share” (screenplay, t043-death-leaves-heonseong-living) denies a comforting exemption to the living son. I did not lose the ending's intent or mistake clemency for forgiveness.

Fragile achievements to preserve: the bow's status joke and repeated physical tests; the injured man's unavailable wages; sorting through interpretation without granting omniscience; Eulbo's actual rest alongside unfinished repairs; gate permission versus usable passage; the month gained before defeat; the knife and persistently damaged hands; defense preserved alongside recovery; brothers denied hearing before the royal bargain; Heonchung's non-assassination and three separately received reports; knowingly destructive defection; Seou's separate tools; the Henan household still together without a promised return; Heonseong alive, implicated and un-absolved at the end. Preservation protects these functions, not every sentence.
