## The naval failure below Geonanseong {#645-naval-failure}

**Definition and scope**

Tang sought to draw Goguryeo forces to Liaodong with its land army and strike Pyongyang directly with its naval force. After attacking Bisaseong, Zhang Liang’s force seeks a base near Geonanseong to connect with land operations. His biography in the New Book of Tang records a surprise Goguryeo attack before the camp is complete, Zhang Liang sitting unable to speak, and his deputy Zhang Jinshu beating a drum to rally troops and counterattack. Distinguish the local counterattack’s success from achieving the whole expedition’s objective.

**Established facts**

The work constructs losses of 10,000 killed or captured from the initial 40,000 through the surprise attack below Geonanseong and subsequent withdrawal. It uses an attack while troops have not yet established the camp and are scattered on work and grazing duties, causing an initial collapse. Zhang Jinshu’s rally preserves the remaining force but does not conquer Geonanseong or establish a land supply connection. The fighting and withdrawal cost the core of the fleet that would have advanced to Pyongyang, frustrating Tang’s plan for a direct naval attack.

Not every expedition ship is destroyed. Goguryeo does not gain the ability to blockade the sea permanently through this success. Zhang Liang’s remaining ships and troops return, and Tang can still use naval forces later. The battle shows that landing a naval force does not immediately create a secure route inland. The work presents it as a major Goguryeo success.
