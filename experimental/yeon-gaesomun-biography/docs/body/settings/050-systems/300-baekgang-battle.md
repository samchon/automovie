## Land and water operations converge at Baekgang {#baekgang-battle}

**Definition and scope**

Baekgang is the decisive battle of 663, treated in the work as a fight for survival by the Baekje restoration forces and Silla. Do not move it earlier as if it were the final battle of Goguryeo’s 661–662 campaign. Wa’s main support force is about 30,000, distinct from the record of 27,000 dispatched in March 663. Do not land all support personnel at one port on the same day.

The battle occurs after [Boksin’s killing](../040-agents/071-boksin.md#boksin). Do not place him alive in command at Baekgang; use the conditions facing Buyeo Pung and the Wa commanders.

**Established facts**

The work uses a situation in which Wa and Baekje ships engage Tang’s navy on sea and river approaches while restoration strongholds face pressure on land. Several ships cannot move at will simultaneously on the waterway, and forces pressing from behind cannot immediately correct confusion ahead. A decision to keep committing fresh forces while damaged ships and people are recovered causes greater losses. Failure at sea and loss of land strongholds together destroy restoration capacity.

Wa’s support is not small aid sent merely from dislike of Silla; it is a decision to avoid losing people and political relationships tied to Baekje. Do not establish the total support or every noble’s origin from this one battle. Ship types and battle routes follow object and place canon; locating Baekgang near the lower Geum River is a choice for the work.
