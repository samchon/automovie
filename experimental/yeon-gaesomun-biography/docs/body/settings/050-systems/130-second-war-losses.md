## Destruction of three organizations and people remaining {#second-war-losses}

**Definition and scope**

Of Tang’s six operational groupings, Nufang, Paegang, and Okjeo are shattered beyond ability to continue. A larger defeat lies behind brief records. The following invented casualty table consistently accounts for mobilization of 350,000 and withdrawal; distinguish organizational destruction from every person dying.

**Established facts**

| Operational grouping | Initial mobilization | Work-specific number unable to return, including killed and captured | Returned or remaining on other duties |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Pyongyang route main force | 70,000 | 10,000 | 60,000 |
| Paegang route main force | 60,000 | 40,000 | 20,000 |
| Okjeo route main force | 50,000 | 45,000 | 5,000 |
| Liaodong route main force | 60,000 | 5,000 | 55,000 |
| Buyeo direction | 60,000 | 2,000 | 58,000 |
| Nufang direction | 50,000 | 28,000 | 22,000 |
| Total | 350,000 | 130,000 | 220,000 |

The 220,000 returning or remaining are not all elite troops immediately fit to fight; they include wounded, sick, exhausted, scattered command structures, and forces diverted elsewhere. Do not fix precise modern ratios of killed and captured within 130,000. When scenes distinguish the two, use subsets of that total, without adding other defeat figures outside it.

Tang’s defeat leaves national aftereffects comparable to Sui’s failed Goguryeo expeditions. Tang stops conquest for more than a count of lost people and ships: repeated field defeats where it thought itself finest under heaven warn the court not to continue. Do not use the work’s 130,000 to prove it exceeds actual Sui deaths in 612.

Keep the nature of records in view. Tang official accounts are brief: Old Book of Tang says Ren Yaxiang, Su Dingfang, and Qibi Heli “all returned without great merit.” [The recorded calendar](../020-world/030-second-war-calendar.md#second-war-calendar) owns Ren and Pang’s deaths; [Zheng Mingzhen](../040-agents/058-zheng-mingzhen.md#zheng-mingzhen) owns his death and record’s nature. Liaodong and Buyeo loss figures are invented for a gap with no recorded Goguryeo-front losses. Zheng Rentai’s 14,000 light cavalry of whom only 800 returned is a separate Tiele expedition outside the table, not added to this front. [Available personnel](040-goguryeo-661-strength.md#goguryeo-661-strength) owns Goguryeo losses; victors’ figures such as 30,000 at Yalu or 10,000 at Imjin are their claims. Goguryeo also retains defensive and mobile losses and destroyed livelihoods. Victory is defined not merely as losing less than the opponent but as stopping large-scale conquest.
