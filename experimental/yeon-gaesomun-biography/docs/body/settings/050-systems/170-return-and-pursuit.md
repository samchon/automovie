## Deception on return and pursuit at the Imjin {#return-and-pursuit}

**Definition and scope**

The work’s baseline for the Silla main body is departure around the seventh day of the second lunar month and arrival on the Imjin’s north bank around the 15th. The supplied main body leaves first. Unlike Silla’s annals linking Tang withdrawal immediately after receipt and Kim Yusin’s departure after that news, the work places Tang withdrawal after the Sasu: the main body cannot leave on news of a withdrawal not yet occurred. [The recorded calendar](../020-world/030-second-war-calendar.md#second-war-calendar) owns this conflict and order. On the same roughly 240-kilometer route, with delivery loads reduced, return averages 27–30 kilometers daily; wounded and rearguard do not all match that stride. Kim Yangdo’s sea detachment is part of the transport total, with different departure and arrival from the land main body.

**Established facts**

Goguryeo’s 10,000 available pursuers comprise 7,000 Liaodong mobile troops and 3,000 southern defenders. Other shares of the 20,000 Liaodong mobile force are 10,000 central and 3,000 remaining in Liaodong. The 7,000 travel south 550 kilometers in 24 days without central assembly, assigned to southern watch and communications; they are already there around day 6 of the second lunar month, not newly departing Liaodong on the day of scouting contact. They do not overlap the central 50,000 counterattack. These 7,000 are light cavalry with 10,500 horses; 4,500 horses for the 3,000 remaining and 15,000 for the 10,000 central are separate. All are work-specific organization. Daily issue limit for the southern force is 180 tons, including 147 tons horse feed and about 9.8 tons for people plus local transport and loss margin. The 24-day move needs 4,320 tons; 60 days of later watch and return response need a separate 10,800-ton dispersed reserve. This southern share is outside [central mobile reserves](100-counterattack-concentration.md#interior-supply-capacity). If routes share a supply zone, separate dates, stocks, and carrying order; do not count the same 850 tons per day twice. Losing next-stage hay or routes prevents promised speed and pursuit.

The 2,000 pursuers killed, seriously wounded, or captured after the return battle are included in 15,000 losses on other campaign fronts. Do not convert recorded large claims into actual deaths. Silla’s record claims 10,000 beheaded and 5,000 captured, possibly exaggerated since only one captured commander is named, a sohyeong (小兄). The Sasu main force does not leave the capital to join this pursuit.

Reference: treatments own actual deception through camp traces, fires, and night movement, pursuit contact, crossing defense, next-day detour, and confirmation of return.
