## The Okjeo-route force ends at the Sasu {#sasu-battle}

**Definition and scope**

The work dates the Sasu battle to the 18th day of the second lunar month in 662, adopting the Tang date without claiming agreement with the first month in Goguryeo’s annals. Tens of thousands die, including Pang Xiaotai and his 13 sons, and the Okjeo-route organization is lost; this outcome is fixed. Sasu’s location is disputed between the Botong River west of Pyongyang (Lee Sang-hun) and the Hapjang River east. The work chooses Botong ([riverside camp](../060-places/100-sasu-ground.md#sasu-ground)), leaving Hapjang as unresolved disagreement. Do not read it as a complete death count excluding every individual escapee.

**Established facts**

Feeding and interior-route limits follow [dispersed reserves](100-counterattack-concentration.md#interior-supply-capacity).

The work sets about 12 kilometers direct from Ren’s camp to Sasu, and 22 by a connecting route avoiding enemy roads and Botong’s bank. Preserve reorganization on the night of the 14th, daytime movement and gathering on the 15th–16th, reconnaissance and interdiction preparation on the 17th, and frontal contact the morning of the 18th. These are not measured distances from a scholarly march diary. [Sasu place](../060-places/100-sasu-ground.md#sasu-ground) owns camp form and Botong identification.

The usual connection from Su Dingfang’s main camp is about 13 kilometers and a winter river crossing. A messenger takes about half a day; assembling and crossing relief takes at least one day. From the 17th the land junction is blocked. Su’s main force must also bear pressure on its own camp and prepare withdrawal, while Pang’s baggage and troops must first pass blocked exits. Knowing each other’s crisis does not itself create relief capacity.

Heavy cavalry suits an open front for formation, infantry a narrow entrance, light cavalry scattered troops and communication interdiction. No force charges identically on all ice, snow, and obstacles. Do not adopt as agreed fact that Pang refused a superior order, or use one son’s death to stop the whole army.

Reference: treatments own reorganization, detour, reconnaissance and relief decisions, actual attack and entry, command collapse, and Su’s receipt and withdrawal.
