## Cross-border trade and submission {#cross-border-ties}

**Definition and scope**

Grain, cloth, iron tools, leather, and animals travel on cross-border routes. Even when states are hostile, these connections sustain people and leave channels for future choices. When great-power war cuts roads, livelihoods of frontier communities also suffer. Volumes and prices differ by region and are not set as a uniform national table.

**Established facts**

Officials who allow trade, carriers who know routes, and households that keep goods do different work. Restricting access to prevent raids deprives some residents of their livelihoods; lifting control may let military information out.

Submission places routes and people under another power; a declaration changing a name does not complete it. Not every resident supporting Namsaeng’s submission shares the same grievance toward the Go royal house. Some nobles accompany him for protection, while some residents stay to escape military service. Malgal communities judge existing promises of protection and Tang’s new orders separately. Even if Tang’s rule later weakens in some areas, not everyone there planned the same resistance from the beginning.

Diplomatic and military routes may overlap, but sending envoys and delivering supplies to an army have different costs. A mountain path used by a few envoys cannot immediately carry grain for a large army. Goguryeo leaders who recognize Kim Yusin’s achievement understand this difference. The actual distances and labor needed to calculate connections follow individual operations and place canon.
