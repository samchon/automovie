## An incorporated community primarily practicing mobile herding {#pastoral-malgal}

**Definition and scope**

One Malgal community moving between forests and grasslands of the northwest frontier relies mainly on mobile herding rather than farming. The work calls this invented group the Grassland Community. It does not assign it the same proportions of farming and hunting as other incorporated Malgal communities or identify it as one of the seven settlements in historical texts. In the main story it appears as a community reporting military service and seasonal movement; no individual chief leads an independent political drama.

**Established facts**

From spring to autumn the community follows livestock to water and pasture, then gathers in winter at a settled base on a forest edge sheltered from river winds. Remaining households tend small fields; when grain is short, they exchange leather and livestock at Goguryeo trading posts. Goguryeo service burdens them by obstructing seasonal routes, but also gives rights to protect livestock and guarantees trade.

The work places a Gyeongdang at the winter base where returning adolescents learn letters, signals, and martial skills. This operation is invented; herding does not itself confer mountain scouting or advanced cavalry training. Those serving as light cavalry come from households with horses and arms. Through joint service they receive independent interdiction missions. Not every household supplies light cavalry.

The community reveals differences in Malgal livelihoods and actual conditions of living incorporated into Goguryeo. Its setting does not statistically reconstruct the share of nomadic life among all ancient Malgal.
