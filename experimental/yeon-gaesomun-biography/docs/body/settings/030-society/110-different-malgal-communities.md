## Different Malgal communities {#different-malgal-communities}

**Definition and scope**

The name Malgal encompasses several communities inside and outside Goguryeo. Among them are people serving in Goguryeo’s military, trading goods beyond the frontier, and connected to Tang. A shared name does not mean a shared political will. The Book of Sui records several settlements and their respective leaders, farming, pig raising, and other practices, but those divisions alone cannot identify each resident’s origin and affiliation. Assessments of living standards and character express the perspective of contemporary Chinese observers.

**Established facts**

In the work the incorporated eastern communities live on narrow farmland supplemented by forests and streams. When hunting and sowing seasons overlap, households argue over whom to send to military service. Even amid lives using much leather and familiar with forests, farming is part of their livelihood. After incorporation into Goguryeo, marriage and trade ties with relatives outside the frontier continue. An officer who treats all residents as spies solely because of these ties damages actual cooperation.

This combination of livelihoods and the detailed operation of incorporated communities are constructed for the work. Communities maintain different livelihoods while participating in Goguryeo military service and education. Their knowledge remains contemporary; they do not already know that Balhae will later be founded.
