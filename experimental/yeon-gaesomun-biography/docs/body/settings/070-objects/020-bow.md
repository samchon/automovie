# Bows and arrows

Canon guide: [Location of this subject](../outline/index.md#070-objects--020-bow).

## Bows and arrows {#bow}

**Definition and scope**

The bow is learned at Gyeongdang for hunting and military service. The work uses composite and simple wooden bows, both curved at the ends and drawn with a string. Poor village children do not all initially possess expensive bows like nobles’; some learn with a shared Gyeongdang bow before using a personal bow supplied by family or community.

**Established facts**

The recurring composite bow is an invented form. Strung, it is about one meter between tips; seen upright from the side, the string and archer side are called inner and the target side outer. Limbs from the grip curve toward the string and the short tips turn back toward the target. This describes side curvature, not the frontal silhouette. The dark brown wooden grip is wrapped in leather; the string is light brown. A shared wooden bow is distinguished by a simple curve about 1.4 meters long.

Arrows have straight shafts, fletching, and points varying with use. The basic service arrow has a wooden shaft about 75 centimeters, three gray-brown feathers, and an iron point about four centimeters. Hunting points change for purpose. Arrows used in hunting and battle cannot be reused forever.

Sokro’s quiver is an 80-centimeter leather pouch, external length including hanging tabs and bottom cover. Effective depth from closed bottom to mouth is 60 centimeters; arrows point down with rear shafts and feathers showing above the mouth. The iron point overlaps the shaft by about two centimeters, giving a basic arrow total length about 77 centimeters. External length and storage depth differ. These shapes are work choices, not equipment specifications recorded by Gyeongdang sources.

Accuracy at a practice target cannot immediately be expected while moving on mountain roads or shooting in cold. Wet strings and hand wounds limit use; the number of arrows carried limits attacks. Effective range is not fixed by ancestry or ethnicity. Before writing a scene, establish from actual placement whether opponents are close enough for arrows and angles at which shields stop them. The arrow hitting Ondal does not pierce every part of armor like paper.
