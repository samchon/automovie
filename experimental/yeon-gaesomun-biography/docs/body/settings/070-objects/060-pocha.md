# The pocha stone thrower

Canon guide: [Location of this subject](../outline/index.md#070-objects--060-pocha).

## The pocha stone thrower {#pocha}

**Definition and scope**

A pocha is a machine using ropes and a wooden structure to hurl stones against fortress walls. It is distinct from a modern gunpowder cannon and needs space for assembly, labor, and stones. Once installed, it cannot be picked up and carried by a soldier during battle.

**Established facts**

The work’s pocha is a human-powered traction design with a 3-meter-high frame on a dark wooden base 4 by 3 meters, a 4-meter arm, and a stone pouch at its end. The arm turns in a vertical plane around a horizontal wooden axle at the top of the frame. The axle divides the arm into a short end of 1 meter and a long end of 3 meters. Several traction ropes attach to the short end, and operators pull from below and behind on either side. The pouch at the long end swings upward and forward from a low rear position and releases the stone. Keep the pulling area separate from the forward path of the stone; stop pulling and secure the arm while lowering it and filling the pouch. It cannot fire if the base rocks or the axle or ropes are damaged. This is the minimum reconstruction needed to realize its direction of operation, not a claim to an exact historical mechanical drawing. These specifications are invented and absent from the 661 account.
