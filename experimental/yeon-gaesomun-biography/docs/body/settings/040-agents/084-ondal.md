# The path of advancement shown by Ondal

Canon guide: [Location of this subject](../outline/index.md#040-agents--084-ondal).

## The path of advancement shown by Ondal {#ondal}

**Definition and scope**

Ondal is a hero used to explain earlier reconstruction and military advancement, without a separate long biography in the main story. Use the Samguk Sagi account that he led in fighting Northern Zhou, killed dozens, contributed to victory, and obtained the rank daehyeong. He counters a country in which only noble eldest sons win names through service.

**Established facts**

His early talent at Gyeongdang and the royal family accepting him as son-in-law to gain commoner support are invented. Do not derive expedition command ability automatically from the rank name daehyeong. The work makes him able to command through achievements and later career, without treating daehyeong and daemodal as the same title.

His invented appearance is 179 centimeters tall, thick-armed and broad-chested, with a broad forehead and long square jaw. He has large brown eyes, thick black eyebrows, a broad nose, large mouth, and sun-darkened tawny skin. His black hair is tied, beard short, and he wears iron lamellar armor over gray-brown underclothes, dark brown outerwear, and a black sash. His whole face moves when he laughs. His voice is thick, bright, and clear below the middle register. Rather than elaborate ceremony to hide his family’s low status, he directly explains what he can do. Poverty did not automatically guarantee training and merit, and his acquired status gave him no power to ignore Silla’s defenses.

Use the recorded ending in which an arrow kills him below Adanseong on an expedition against Silla. Do not establish it as a crossbow bolt. The work chooses Ondalsanseong as the location, distinguishing the Achasan proposal and debate. Sources do not establish whether the expedition failed to advance at all or first gained territory. The main story uses the memory to show wall defense and limits of expedition size, without inventing a numeric territorial gain.
