# A king who won a war and a king seeking to recover the throne

Canon guide: [where to find this subject](../outline/index.md#040-agents--015-geonmu).

## Go Geonmu's courage and experience of reconstruction {#geonmu}

**Definition and scope**

King Yeongnyu, Go Geonmu, is the king Gaesomun worships and ultimately kills. A son of Pyeongwon and half-brother of Yeongyang, he defeated Sui's naval force in 612 as the king's brother. His victory and experience rebuilding the country after repeated invasions both form the character's center. He was born around 585. In 612 he is a 27-year-old royal brother and, as brother of a king with no recorded son, effectively the next king. The work calls him 'royal brother,' not 'crown brother.' Do not fix his birthday; in 642 depict him as a king in his fifty-seventh year.

**Established content**


Among middle-aged generals, a young royal personally leading the picked force makes him the hero boys of the next generation admire most. He ascends when his elder brother Yeongyang dies in 618; after that, the boys' hero is their king.

His fictional appearance is 178 centimeters tall with a thick chest and long arms. His back remains straight in old age. He has a broad forehead, square jaw, thick black eyebrows above dark brown eyes, a high bridge and broad nose tip, and a thin upper lip. Wrinkles gather around his eyes as he ages. His skin is sun-browned yellow, his black hair tied, and his beard short. Gray increases at temples and beard in later life. He wears a dark reddish-brown outer robe with black sash and armor when inspecting troops.

His voice is low and deep, with dryness and roughness in later years. He exhales at length before speaking and delivers important judgments slowly but ends firmly. His speech can identify where his judgment may be wrong while maintaining the decision. He does not attach a long excuse every time he hears opposition.


As king he has less need to display prowess at every opportunity. Instead he continues to hear of people who did not return and the state of farmland even after war ends. Aging or setting down his sword is not evidence that he has lost courage.

What Geonmu fears more than any individual Tang general is the power of a unified Chinese dynasty to invade repeatedly. Knowing Goguryeo can win and still lose farming and livelihoods again, he considers measured diplomatic concessions practical while national strength recovers. Preserve his actual career combining diplomacy and defense. When Tang defeats the Turks and increases pressure, he expands walls and military preparation. Yet even if an opportunity arises he judges there is not enough capacity to launch a large expedition into China first.


His wariness of Gaesomun combines discomfort with the man's character and a real threat to power. Because the king recognizes Gaesomun's ability, he also sees great danger if he moves troops beyond royal command. Distinguish this caution from dullness that fails to recognize a young man's talent.

## The battlefield omitted from the five hundred's heroic story {#geonmu-war-story}

**Definition and scope**

In the work Geonmu's 612 victory circulates as a heroic story of 500 picked men defeating Sui's navy. Book of Sui's biography of Lai Hu'er says Geonmu recruited 500 picked men and attacked when Sui troops broke through the outer fortress, plundered, and lost formation.

**Established content**


Sui's navy crossed the sea, entered the Paesu, and advanced toward Pyongyang.

Zizhi Tongjian gives the later course in detail. Deputy commander Zhou Fashang urged waiting for other forces, but Lai selected 40,000 elite troops and went straight to the fortress. Goguryeo hid troops in an empty temple inside the outer fortress, came out to fight, and pretended to flee. Lai pursued into the outer fortress; Book of Sui says he broke its outer defenses (「破其郛郭」). When Lai released his troops to capture and plunder, their formation collapsed; the ambush rose and Sui's naval force suffered heavy defeat. Lai barely escaped, and only several thousand soldiers returned alive (「士卒還者不過數千人」). Goguryeo pursued to the ships but withdrew when Zhou Fashang waited with an ordered camp. Book of Sui says Lai, driven back by the picked force, camped on the coast to wait for the army and withdrew after hearing of Yuwen Shu's defeat. Five hundred and 40,000 thus come from different records; distinguish their sources even when citing both.


In oral tradition heard by the young, 500 defeat 43,000 naval troops. The 43,000 is a figure attached to the heroic story. Oral tradition inflates the fight into a heavy-cavalry charge led by the royal brother. In actuality, an ambush inside Pyongyang's outer fortress struck elite naval troops whose formation had broken during plunder. Young listeners remember the hero's decision and valor before the condition that the enemy's ranks collapsed through plunder.


Geonmu does not deny the courage he showed in that victory. But winning did not prevent the next invasion or avoid long reconstruction. There is a difference between those who heard the result and those who lived through what followed.

## His defense immediately before the killing {#geonmu-last-defence}

**Definition and scope**

Captured during the 642 coup, Geonmu has an opportunity to face Gaesomun. The meeting and conversation are entirely invented; specific dialogue is not yet fixed in canon. The king explains how his predecessors rebuilt ruins. Sui invaded repeatedly even amid internal disorder, and Geonmu chose both diplomacy and defense for reconstruction. To the charge that he should have foreseen Tang's rapid reunification, he answers that hindsight erases the uncertainty of the time.

**Established content**


Gaesomun feels the king who was once his hero has changed. Geonmu explains how the country looks after allocating resources and cleaning up destruction from the highest position. He does not assert his policy was risk-free or claim every judgment stood outside royal and family interests. His defense has force: Gaesomun recalls it later when he actually governs.


The king does not plead for life. He says in substance that Gaesomun will understand him when he reaches the highest position. The plot by king and great men to kill Gaesomun has already been exposed, and Gaesomun judges diplomacy cannot stop invasion if more time passes. Killing a captured king remains his responsibility for the coup. Treat the chronicles' account of mutilation as exaggeration and do not establish it in the text; [the 642 coup](../050-systems/240-642-coup.md#642-coup) owns the scope of that record.


The specific scene sequence and mobilized forces are established in coup event canon. This conversation is invented, distinct from recorded last words.
