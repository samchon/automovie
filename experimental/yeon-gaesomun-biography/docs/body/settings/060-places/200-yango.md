## The handover site at Yango {#yango}

**Definition and scope**

Yango is where the Silla transport party transfers grain to Tang’s Pyongyang expedition. The work adopts a Yupo-ri-area identification near the Daedong without fixing an exact modern lot as the ancient handover point. It has a riverside yard, Tang guard line behind it, and narrow entrance road. Do not mix grain to be handed over with the party’s own food. The narrow approach and Tang guard line prevent entry of the entire column at once without advance contact and safety confirmation. Treatments own actual contact and entry.

**Established facts**

The yard is not wide enough to line up over 2,000 carts side by side. Individual loads brought over difficult roads and goods still on animals share the area. The receiver needs space to separate and check wet grain and empty room for the next load. A one-day delivery date does not mean tens of thousands of seok move instantly; night unloading and guarding require passages and light. The sequence of checks, handover completion, and return preparation follows the existing event.

The invented yard lies about 100 meters inland from the river’s south bank, 80 meters east–west and 60 north–south. Tang’s road for moving received loads is north, Silla’s arrival road south, each five meters wide. The eastern half holds dry sacks received; the west holds loads under inspection and wet sacks separately. A temporary wooden load cover one meter above ground stands centrally, with an open place before it where Ma Jeong receives checks. Do not use the low space under it as an office. Supplies move north from the unloading area to make room for the next loads. Daylight enters openly from the river; at night torches stand at passages and checking points. Water, animals, and sacks being put down mean messengers must repeat orders across the yard. Kim Yangdo’s return detachment reaches ships at a separate bank about 500 meters along the northern road.
