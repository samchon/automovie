## The palace at Pyongyang {#pyeongyang-palace}

**Definition and scope**

The palace is the capital’s power space from which royal orders and ceremonies issue, separate from [the Yeon residence](020-yeon-residence.md#yeon-residence). All dimensions and layouts below are choices for the work, not an excavated plan. An eight-meter-wide road runs 60 meters north from the south gate to the council hall’s forecourt, 40 meters east–west by 30 north–south. The wooden hall on its northern edge is 28 by 10 meters. Beyond a rear wall and small gate lies an inner courtyard with royal quarters and a private room. Outsiders pass both the south gatekeeper and inner-gate attendant.

**Established facts**

From between the hall’s south pillars one sees courtyard entry; daylight enters through south windows and open doors. The northern private room is five meters east–west by four north–south; with its door closed, forecourt conversation is indistinct. This is where the captured king meets Gaesomun in 642. East-window and lamp light divide low table and faces; the king sits before the north wall, visitor inside the south door. Attendants’ footsteps and brief access checks sound from the outer corridor. One cannot look down on every court troop position from the window.

The large building has dark tiles and reddish-brown pillars; inside are low wooden furnishings and yellow lamplight. Even in war, do not decorate both powerholders’ whole rooms with one huge military map. There is a place to read reports and a door outside which people wait. Map markers are in [war objects](../outline/index.md#070-objects).

Goguryeo murals showing living spaces with wooden pillars, beams, and purlins inform materials and frame. Fifth-century funerary art is not a measured seventh-century palace plan. The two buildings’ divisions and access routes are choices absent from that material.

The hall has one story, 3.6 meters from floor to beam underside, four to eaves, and 6.5 to ridge. The east–west ridge carries dark tiles sloping north–south and projecting 0.8 meters beyond the walls. The private room is in a separate single-story building, 2.8 meters beneath the beam, three to eaves, and 4.5 to east–west ridge. Both expose beams and roof framing; no extra floor occupies that space. This elevation is invented and retains the low furnishings, window light, access checks, and 642 encounter positions above.
