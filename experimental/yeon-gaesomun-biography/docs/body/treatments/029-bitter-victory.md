After Sasu, Su Dingfang uses his camp's rations and preparations to lift the siege and withdraw alive. His army suffers heavy snow and losses, but Kim Yusin's provisions have given him the strength to gather people and equipment. He carries both the failure to save Pang Xiaotai and the decision that saved his own troops. Gaesomun abandons the expectation of destroying Su Dingfang's entire army in pursuit and gives priority to recovery of the capital and his remaining forces ([the campaign's result](../settings/050-systems/130-second-war-losses.md#second-war-losses)).

Gaesomun has won a real victory showing that Tang's elite can be defeated in the field. Yet the northern departures took away enemies he intended to strike, and Silla's transport prevented him from eliminating Su Dingfang. He cannot hide the bitterness of an ending different from the plan he first stated. He specifically recognizes Namsaeng's month, the fortress defense, and the cooperation of local infantry and light cavalry. He does not call all those achievements failure because the final enemy escaped alive.

Back in Silla, Chano gives Ajin Gwidol's small knife. She asks how her son walked on his last journey. Chano tells her about the injuries and death he witnessed and why they could not carry the body, without inventing thoughts he did not see or an exact medical cause of death ([Ajin's loss](../settings/040-agents/078-ajin.md#ajin)). Seeing the hand Chano tries to hide, Ajin offers him a place to sit. The survivor's injury is no compensation for her dead son.

Without either husband or son, Ajin must continue the next cultivation season and rely on seasonal exchanges of labor with kin and neighbors. Chano survives in a body that cannot carry loads as before. The geupchan Kim Yusin granted in the field and the sachan he requested from the king after the return are reported as measures recognizing Yeolgi and Gugeun's service. Do not treat them as changes to bone rank or solutions to every bereaved family's livelihood. From Silla's perspective, close on costs that have not ended the day after victory.

As reports of the transport and return come together in Pyongyang, Gaesomun acknowledges Kim Yusin's ability to move a large quantity along a route they had thought impossible. His belief that Silla would be easily subdued once Tang was defeated is shaken. From the same reports Namsaeng reads the need for a productive and transport base that can sustain the next war, while Namgeon gives greater weight to the power of the field victory ([judgments left by the march](../settings/050-systems/180-winter-human-cost.md#winter-human-cost)).

The historian points out the difference between Tang's brief postwar account and the three organizations actually broken, offering the work's interpretation that the shock was comparable to the failure of Sui's expedition. Do not call all 130,000 who did not return dead, or turn the comparison into a claim that more people were killed than in 612. The country has survived and gained time to prepare for the next spring. Using that time is not an afterword less important than victory.
