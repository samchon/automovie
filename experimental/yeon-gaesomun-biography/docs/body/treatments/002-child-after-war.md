The infant cries and eats without understanding the adults’ war ([age-specific state](../settings/040-agents/001-yeon-gaesomun.md#yeon-body-and-voice)).

Before withdrawal in this birth year, the historian connects earlier hopes for war’s end and their different failures. After diplomacy in 598 people hoped for recovery and peace, but Sui returned in 612 with a vast force proclaimed as two million. That is the enemy’s advertised threat, not a verified count. Defeating it raised hope expeditions would stop, but Sui invaded again in 613. Hope that Sui’s internal rebellion would stop war was broken by its final 614 expedition. These are distinct expectations and outcomes organized for the audience by a later historian, not the infant’s knowledge or identical beliefs held by everyone ([repeated expeditions](../settings/020-world/080-repeated-sui-wars.md#repeated-sui-wars)).

[Lai Hu’er](../settings/040-agents/098-lai-huer.md#lai-huer) urges continued attack because Goguryeo is exhausted, but [Emperor Yang](../settings/040-agents/097-sui-yangdi.md#sui-yangdi), holding final authority, accepts surrender and orders withdrawal. Distinguish the field commander’s wish to press on from the actual order: war does not end because every enemy force is destroyed or the enemy has one shared will. Goguryeo seeks time to stop further exhaustion of country and livelihood.

Do not call the war’s ending year only by its later fame as victory. The historian distinguishes the glory the child will inherit from the absences adults are now settling, then returns to Taejo’s accounting of people and equipment. Do not stage a long battlefield recollection or independent Sui court scene, or have the infant understand the withdrawal.

In autumn 618 news of King Yeongyang’s death and Go Geonmu’s accession reaches Taejo’s home. Four years after birth, Gaesomun hears adults call the new king a war hero and takes it as if a master greater than his own household head has appeared. Do not open Yeongyang’s life as a separate plot. The historian briefly connects the two kings’ genealogy and succession, leaving the child’s attention on Geonmu ([Yeongyang](../settings/040-agents/013-yeongyang.md#yeongyang), [the prince’s achievement](../settings/040-agents/015-geonmu.md#geonmu-war-story)).

To explain what the new king inherits, the historian connects an earlier past. In the mid-sixth-century succession civil war, [Chugun](../settings/040-agents/089-chugun.md#chugun) and [Segun](../settings/040-agents/090-segun.md#segun) fought on opposing sides. The work makes the 2,000 dead on Segun’s side all nobles. Their deaths broke military, marriage, and supply ties among families. Northern rights and supplies passed to Turkic powers; in the south Silla and Baekje took Goguryeo’s base. Some Gungnaeseong military families remember the royal house abandoning people who bled for it; the royal house remembers turmoil in which it could not control nobles’ and in-laws’ armies. Do not make either memory a common national grievance ([different memories of collapse](../settings/020-world/060-earlier-civil-war.md#earlier-civil-war)).

Pyeongwon and Yeongyang’s recovery reconnected that broken cooperation. The historian links reports of western pressure: when Northern Zhou and Northern Qi opposed each other, both sought Turkic support through marriage, friendship, and gifts. Chinese powers sought northern help; Turks alone did not cause Chinese unification ([powers seeking northern support](../settings/020-world/110-tang-and-steppe.md#tang-and-steppe)). After that rivalry, [Emperor Wu of Northern Zhou](../settings/040-agents/093-zhou-wudi.md#zhou-wudi) combined northern China’s forces and people through military and governing ability, and [Emperor Wen of Sui](../settings/040-agents/094-sui-wendi.md#sui-wendi) extended that base south into a unified dynasty. The historian judges both capable rulers who laid foundations for reunification. Goguryeo’s kings faced western recovery and the burden of sustaining conquest after crossing southern fortresses. They widened the base through trade, protection, and education with eastern communities. Counting the east, the narrator judges Yeongyang’s sphere Goguryeo’s largest, but distinguishes that area after loss of the Han basin from its greatest productive and population substance. This later judgment is adopted by the work, not a precise boundary map or a four-year-old’s knowledge. Without reenacting another war, the long view explains why victorious Geonmu asks for recovery time ([eastward reconstruction](../settings/020-world/070-first-restoration.md#first-restoration)).

That winter Seogu and Taejo debate divided China among guests at Taejo’s house. Taejo proposes a single winter strike west of the Liao against strongholds, delaying a new ruler’s next invasion. Seogu cites the 300,000 expedition after the 598 Liaoxi attack and the fields that would be left empty. He connects the king’s need for recovery to the [current belief that the Central Plain has only a third of its former population](../settings/020-world/090-second-restoration-and-tang.md#second-restoration-and-tang). Attribute this to contemporary rumor and judgment, not a verified population total; Taejo considers that weakness an opening, while Seogu fears further losses before recovery. Neither treats the other as cowardly or reckless. Kept outside the door, the boy remembers his father’s offensive argument more strongly. The audience knows Taejo supports the king while prescribing differently; the child cannot yet separate those things ([conditions of the debate](../settings/020-world/090-second-restoration-and-tang.md#great-turmoil-debate)).
