# A war that does not end when the enemy leaves

## Driving people from the fields again and again {#raids-and-recovery}

In 647–648, Tang alternates small forces to interrupt cultivation and drive people inside the fortresses. The historian tells the audience that the Tang court regarded this attrition as policy; Goguryeo's people experience it as having to abandon fields again while repairing them after the enemy has withdrawn. Do not expand every report from Seokseong, Jeokriseong, Yeoksan, and Bakjakseong into a separate battle; retain the accumulation of distinct outcomes, field defeats and fortresses holding out ([the course of individual records](../settings/050-systems/235-field-records-after-645.md#field-records-after-645)).

Gaesomun demands supplies for protection and horses and arms for the next campaign from the same resources. Because he himself does not rest, he expects the same pace of his officials. Gubin reports what remains, and Miran asks whether they can keep seed until the next season. Gaesomun knows their demands but does not cancel preparations for defense. His ability to punish delayed support and the burdens he imposes for the country accumulate under the same rule.

News reaches the center of a defeat by the Khitan at Sinseong in 654 and defeat at Gwidansu in 655. In that same year, a Goguryeo, Baekje, and Malgal coalition takes 33 Silla fortresses in the south. The historian does not convert this achievement into territory held by Goguryeo alone or the conquest of all Silla ([the scope of the 33 fortresses](../settings/020-world/130-655-southern-offensive.md#655-southern-offensive)). Gaesomun sees a real opening to press the south, but cannot erase the failures in the northwestern field.

In examining the 654 northern report, the historian keeps to its scope: Khitan affiliations are reported alongside livestock movements and trade routes. The influence Goguryeo sought to regain included access to those herds and routes and to salt resources in the west. The defeat was not merely a loss of prestige on land without resources. Yet do not treat all Khitan as one wealthy group or a country forever bound to one ruler, and do not invent a salt production site's name or a volume of trade ([the resource motives in the Khitan report](../settings/020-world/140-khitan-resources.md#khitan-resources)).

The southern reports also demand more troops and provisions to hold the positions gained and attack the next fortress. The limit seen earlier in Ondal's death extends beyond one man's heroic story to these gains. Gaesomun cannot count news of captured fortresses as the creation of a spare expeditionary army. Silla's mountain fortresses and relief forces, and the ability to maintain occupation, remain real constraints on conquest in the south ([southern defense and the burden of holding gains](../../instructions/003-era-and-restoration.md#silla-fortresses-and-limits-of-southern-campaigns)).

The historian briefly compares earlier changes in power behind the southern report. King Seong's death at Gwansanseong in 554 marks Baekje's decline, but does not mean that every later king was incompetent or that no recovery took place. Under King Mu its military strength revived and pressed Silla; Gaesomun also received reports of King Uija's attacks on Silla fortresses, including Daeyaseong, in 642. While Goguryeo sought to recover its former advantage, Baekje had also changed the south through its own strength. Do not collapse this broader view into one success in 655 or the achievement of a subordinate state controlled by Goguryeo ([King Seong's death](../settings/040-agents/107-seongwang.md#seongwang), [King Mu's recovery](../settings/040-agents/108-mu-baekje.md#mu-baekje), [King Uija's offensive](../settings/040-agents/109-uija.md#uija)).

The historian handles the difference between a report of a fortress held in 658 and other records of fortress and relief-force losses. In 659, Ons amun's defeat reaches them as a family memory: even Ondal's descendant could not recreate old glory on the same battlefield. Gaesomun repeatedly demands preparation to cut the enemy's connections rather than rely on reputation; for Go Hwi, the string of defeats hardens into grounds for keeping the central army inside the fortresses. The two men prepare different conclusions from the same experience.

Later, separate news arrives that Baekje's capital fell and King Uija's dynasty was destroyed in 660. Connect the king who has now lost his dynasty with the subject of the 642 offensive report, but do not merge him with Buyeo Pung and his restoration forces. This news is a later development, not simultaneous information in the 654–655 reports, before the story returns to the first council in late spring 661. Neither the southern country's growth in strength nor its collapse was something Goguryeo controlled at a time of its choosing ([changes in southern strength](../settings/020-world/120-southern-recoveries.md#southern-recoveries), [King Uija's fall](../settings/040-agents/109-uija.md#uija)).

Across the years, Namsaeng in 661 is already the successor entrusted with state affairs and command. Dealing with troops in Liaodong and Gungnaeseong, he asks about work and routes he does not know, listens to the answers, delegates, and checks results and accountability. His father sees this patience not as incompetence, but as a chance to share the work he has shouldered alone ([Namsaeng's ability](../settings/040-agents/007-namsaeng.md#namsaeng-before-succession)). Seokbu begins to trust a commander who shares the same food and cold and does not shift blame for failure.

In Pyongyang, Namgeon commands a group of heavily armed cavalry and earns recognition for his troops' vigor and his responses in the field; Namsan reads the words and concerns for status of the royal family and nobles at court. Their abilities do not replace their elder brother's position as successor. Gaesomun believes he can keep these different forms of cooperation together under him. When the next news arrives, he must put all that strength at risk. Here the story returns to the first report in late spring 661.
