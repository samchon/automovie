In 667 Tang resumes its pursuit of conquest. Li Shiji combines the difficulties of roads and fortresses experienced in earlier expeditions with the access, storehouses, and command relationships Namsaeng provides. Through cooperating regions he links supplies and movement; where resistance persists, he concentrates the necessary force or cuts connections and bypasses it. Tang's military ability did not disappear and suddenly revive. Civil war and defection supplied the conditions to resume a policy halted after the earlier defeat.

Seokbu experiences the result of roads Namsaeng explored for reconstruction now serving the entry of a foreign army. His choice not to follow Namsaeng does not guarantee he can protect his region. Fortresses and forces that hold out continue fighting, but cannot act as one defensive network when they do not know whether a neighboring fortress will aid or cooperate with the enemy. The cost accumulates as one unit's courageous stand fails to restore the campaign as a whole.

The narrator briefly connects the earlier development in 666 when Jeongto offered surrender with territory as part of this division. Do not transform Jeongto's childhood appearance directly into an adult surrender scene or act out an adult portrait and private motives the work does not own. Namsaeng's defection and Jeongto's surrender are separate departures, not the choice of the entire country. Li Shiji gains more connections to use; Namgeon faces more defensive sectors he cannot connect.

## Namgeon defends; Namsan chooses surrender {#brothers-choose-different-ends}

In 668 Namgeon gathers troops against the immediate crisis and maintains resistance. He does not become an incompetent man who abandons the capital. But the center cannot restore severed supplies and authority to every region, and he cannot by himself bring back defectors who do not obey his orders. His confidence that he could remove his brother and use the victorious army better cannot undo the broken cooperation of that army.

Treated by Tang as a necessary collaborator, Namsaeng continues to help the attack. Even when others urge him to distinguish the regime that caused Heonchung's death from the country, he does not turn back. As his knowledge makes conquest easier, he repeatedly sees that this cooperation is a weapon reaching beyond his family's enemies. He remains involved through the end of the offensive, so later responsibility is not confined to the instant of defection.

As pressure on Pyongyang grows, Namsan chooses surrender. For the man who believed he could coordinate and control royal orders and the army, the way to preserve what strength remains now differs from Namgeon's. Namgeon continues to resist. The audience first knows where the two diverge, then watches where the military duties and access authority Namgeon delegated inside the fortress will go. This is the entry to the next event, which makes Sinseong's cooperation from within possible.
