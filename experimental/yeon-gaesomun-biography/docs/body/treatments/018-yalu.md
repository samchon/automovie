# A month gained, the river lost

## Holding the main crossing {#month-of-delay}

In September 661, Namsaeng blocks Qibi Heli's crossing from the south bank of the Yalu. The stretches of level riverbank where Tang can put boats in are limited, so he concentrates the defense there and on the approaches. Seokbu and the soldiers repeatedly scout, stand watch, and stop actual crossings; Namsaeng endures the same cold and food. He does not cross the river to kill every enemy, but holds to his assignment of blocking the land supply connection ([the front](../settings/060-places/090-yalu-crossing.md#yalu-crossing)).

Qibi Heli waits and looks for conditions favorable to crossing. Instead of repeating the same attack at the same place, he widens his search upstream and downstream. Namsaeng cannot multiply his troops without limit to block every route, so he divides the watch between the front and side paths. The delay constrains supplies to the army before Pyongyang, but the soldiers on the spot do not receive its effects as a victory report. The audience knows the time gained on other fronts; the soldiers live through a confrontation that has not ended.

In the story's timeline, an early freeze in late September or early October opens crossings beyond the existing ferry. Qibi Heli first tests a narrow stretch upstream with men on foot, then sends troops across. He does not assume all the ice is a bridge and push horses and carts onto it first. From Namsaeng's command position, woods and a bend in the river obscure the upstream stretch roughly four kilometers to the east ([conditions of the freeze](../settings/050-systems/080-yalu-delay-and-loss.md#yalu-delay-and-loss)).

By the time the field report of the crossing reaches Namsaeng, the enemy is already on the south bank and on a road leading behind him. He sees that he cannot hold both the frontal block and the new front, and orders a withdrawal to a rendezvous behind them. Orders do not reach every force at the same time; some are surrounded, and Seokbu retreats while choosing which people and objects must be left behind. Tang's maneuver, the river's change, and Goguryeo's limited observation combine to produce defeat. No suddenly created relief force comes to save Namsaeng.

At the gathering at Cheolsan, Namsaeng reorganizes the returning troops and accounts for those lost. Of the original 40,000, an ordered 20,000 have withdrawn; the remainder are not immediately available. He does not conceal this, accepts responsibility for his judgment, and reassigns defenses at the south-bank approaches ([losses](../settings/050-systems/040-goguryeo-661-strength.md#goguryeo-661-strength)). Seokbu still trusts this commander, who does not make the defeat disappear. The achievement of delay does not cancel out lost comrades.

Namsaeng's report reaches the palace in Pyongyang and the mobile army moving north separately. Gaesomun reads both the achievement of buying time and the final defeat; Namgeon, traveling with him, gives greater weight to the failure to stop the enemy at the end. News sent to the capital and the mobile army's local observations do not converge with identical content at the same moment. The historian distinguishes the Tang record's claim of '30,000 beheaded' as the victor's claim and does not equate it with the work's actual losses. Namsaeng does not yet know why Qibi Heli will stop or leave; he learns the next move only through separate observation.
