After the surrender at Jupilsan, Tang separates officers, ordinary troops, and Malgal prisoners. Go Yeonsu and Go Hyejin receive Tang offices, but gain no authority to protect all their men. Watching others released, Nallo looks for a chance to return home, only to be kept with the Malgal troops. His affiliation and the sorting by dress and interpreters determine his fate ([the prisoners' fate](../settings/030-society/150-jupilsan-prisoners.md#jupilsan-prisoners), [Nallo](../settings/040-agents/038-nallo.md#nallo)).

Three thousand three hundred Malgal prisoners are executed. From the perspective of the historical record, the historian points to the connection between the approach to the emperor's camp and this decision, without claiming that everyone executed belonged to the same infiltration party. Nallo's last attempt to survive ends here. The massacre is neither an ingenious punishment nor a black joke: its consequence is that surrender did not guarantee life.

Sokro survives outside the surrendering group and pieces together news of the executions from soldiers returning home. He does not report Nallo's last words, which he did not witness. Ordinary Goguryeo soldiers who fought alongside them have been released, while his own kin and neighbors have not returned; the difference gives rise to survivor's guilt and anger. He has reason both to demand greater protection from Goguryeo's commanders and to refuse the next call to service ([Sokro](../settings/040-agents/029-sokro.md#sokro)).

What reaches Gaesomun is news of the relief army's collapse and the prisoners' fate. He had believed that rebuilding the army after its defeat would be enough to mobilize again; now he must confront the unequal treatment of people who fought as soldiers of the same country. He does not turn all their anger into loyalty with an immediate declaration of sympathy. The war is still under way, and he must separately secure the resources and authority to deliver actual support.

Miran, in the eastern village, learns that Nallo has been executed and will not return. Two-year-old Nuri cannot understand what the battle means and needs Miran's care. The bow Miran has is one left at home, not the arms her husband took to war. Do not add to his effects as though those arms had also come back ([Miran](../settings/040-agents/030-miran.md#miran), [Nuri](../settings/040-agents/039-nuri.md#nuri)).

When Meoru tries to discuss the next call to service and each household's share, Miran first asks about the protection promised to the families of the dead. Sokro agrees that his own anger gives him no right to demand that household's labor again. Tang's violence does not immediately unite the community; its members demand different answers from the state about their families' survival. Their demands persist alongside the defense of Ansi and become a political debt after the war.
