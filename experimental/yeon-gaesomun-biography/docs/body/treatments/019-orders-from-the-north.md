In October, Tang's forces in the Buyeo direction move first, followed by those in Liaodong. Namsaeng's scouts confirm the departure from Buyeo two days later and report it; Namsaeng receives the report another two days later. It takes an additional eight days for that report to reach Pyongyang. The first news gives only the direction of movement and the camps remaining, not the contents of an edict or the full extent of the Tiele rebellion ([the delays in reports and orders](../settings/050-systems/090-northern-diversion.md#northern-diversion)). Namsaeng keeps his Yalu force at the south-bank approaches instead of transferring it to the capital's counterattack army.

In the northern operational area, Gaesomun must decide whether to press ahead with preparations to strike several directions in Liaodong in turn, or verify the gap. If he interprets the enemy as fleeing exhausted, he will choose the wrong target to pursue. Without waiting for a reply from the capital, he compares further local scouting reports and moves cavalry to concentrate on the connections of the remaining Nufang force. Do not make the eight-day journey to Pyongyang a same-day round trip; the locally obtained information does not include the reason for the departure either. The audience, too, does not yet know the exact reason.

After the departures have been observed, the historian looks back at the earlier decision by Tang's center and reveals the reason. To meet the Tiele crisis in the north, three edicts were sent in the story's early dispatch window in mid-September. They reached Xiao Siye, Ashina Zhong, and Qibi Heli at different times; each prepared and departed separately, leaving the Goguryeo front no later than October 20 ([status of the adopted timeline](../settings/020-world/030-second-war-calendar.md#second-war-calendar)). This brief look back gives the audience information that prevents mistaking an empty camp for a victory. Do not retroactively give Gaesomun this knowledge when he first received the report.

Later confirmation also connects the defeat of the Khitan aligned with Goguryeo when they tried to block Ashina Zhong's withdrawal. Do not put Goguryeo troops directly into that battle or make Gaesomun the commander of the entire rebellion. Two enemies he intended to break have been taken away by circumstances elsewhere. Their absence reduces the chance of relief for the isolated army of Zheng Mingzhen. Frustration and a new opportunity arise from the same movement.
