# Five days later the western gate opens

## Sinseong's military duties enable cooperation from within {#sinseong-arranges-collusion}

In the ninth lunar month of 668, Sinseong (信誠), a monk entrusted with military duties by Namgeon, contacts Tang and promises to open a gate. He judges that Tang will win and hopes that stopping the fighting can save people inside the fortress. That hope is not a guarantee of protection for everyone received on the residents' behalf. Reveal Sinseong's choice to the audience before the gate opens, so they do not receive only a result as if the gate collapsed by itself.

Sinseong secures the cooperation of Osa and Yomyo, who can actually move the gate and control the road. Osa handles the western postern's night shifts, locks, and passage; Yomyo handles the junction inside the gate and patrol deployment. Osa weighs the danger of staying to fight against the treatment he may receive for opening it, while Yomyo joins in hope of safety for his people and family. Sinseong's promise is not a system that automatically commands every defender.

For five days after the promise, they align their separate powers. Yomyo first changes the inner patrols and shifts so troops inside cannot gather before the gate; Osa prepares the shift that will actually open it. Tang can approach at the agreed time. Namgeon still believes the commanders inside are continuing his defense, and Haeun's family knows nothing of the bargain. Only the audience knows both the different information and the five days remaining.

## Osa opens the gate and the entering troops burn the tower {#gate-opened-and-tower-burned}

Five days after the promise, the approach and shifts Yomyo has cleared prevent obstructing troops inside from gathering. Osa moves his men to clear room for the gate leaves and shifts the inner crossbar. Tang troops approaching on the western outer road enter through the open postern. Entry requires both the lock and the inner route to change; it is not a secret passage that makes the entire fortress irrelevant.

The entering Tang troops set fire to the gate tower. The relief and treatment Sinseong, Osa, and Yomyo expected did not mean they could control subsequent arson and capture. Distinguish the collaborators' limited command from what happens to everyone else, so the audience does not see their cooperation as approval by all Pyongyang residents. Even strong defenders outside cannot reverse the access rights changed from within.

After the internal cooperation and entry cut off resistance, Namgeon wounds himself but does not die and is captured. Establish both the end of his resistance and his survival. His final stand does not erase responsibility for the coup, but neither does it erase his ability and actions in fighting to defend the country. Pyongyang's national command collapses; for those left, the question becomes survival and where they will be placed.

## Haeun's family refuses an escape that would scatter them {#haeeun-household-stays-together}

For Haeun at the fall, finding a way to keep her family together is more urgent than identifying who opened the gate. Janggeun's knowledge of people through deliveries and transport does not reveal the center's secrets. He weighs the possibility that the family could escape by splitting up against the greater cost of never meeting again afterward. The four give up separate flight and stay together.

Seou and Chamu are not children who only receive protection. Seou separately packs her repair materials and tools; Chamu calculates what baggage can be carried and supports the family's decision with actual labor. Haeun first wants to keep cloth to cover all four and tools for one household, while Janggeun calculates a weight at which all four can travel together. They have not yet agreed whether to protect Seou's separately bundled work kit too. Do not invent a new escape in which one family member leaves first and miraculously reunites with the others.

Their choice to stay together is not rewarded with exemption from relocation. The audience understands that they do not represent every Pyongyang household, while seeing that a home to which repair work returned after victory may now lose both its place and its livelihood. In the next event, the forced-migration order reaches a family that has already decided to avoid separation as the concrete loss of having to leave together.
