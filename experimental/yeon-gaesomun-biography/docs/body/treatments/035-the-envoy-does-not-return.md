In 666, Namsaeng sends Dugyeom from the regions to Pyongyang to investigate developments at the center. Dugyeom waits at Namsan's working residence, trying to learn what has changed between the brothers. His knowledge of family insignia and the language of central military affairs lets him ask to be admitted, but gives him no authority to summon troops or make a settlement on Namsaeng's behalf. Rather than send him back with an explanation, Namsan's side hands him to the commander taking control, who detains him.

The audience learns that Dugyeom is detained after waiting. There is no event in which torture and confession reveal the whole coup. The driver who accompanied him confirms that Dugyeom does not come out and has been detained; he does not see the interrogation or the king's bargain. After waiting, the driver leaves Pyongyang and sends a report back. Deliver it as the source Namsaeng hears, without inventing a face, direct speech, or an independent acted journey for which there is no established canon.

Only after receiving that report does Namsaeng know Dugyeom has been seized. The judgment that the center intends to bring him in and strip him of power takes precedence over his hope that explaining matters would resolve his brothers' discontent. Dugyeom's later fate remains unconfirmed; do not give Namsaeng a rumor of his murder as fact. What Namsaeng has is grounds to believe he cannot return trusting his safety, not knowledge of Heonchung's future or a final royal execution order.

When the royal summons arrives, Namsaeng knows that obeying may cost him both position and safety, while refusal can give them grounds to accuse him of disobeying the king. After confirming Dugyeom's detention, he refuses to return without a guarantee of safety. At this stage his aim is survival and protection of his remaining family, not revenge to destroy his brothers and the royal house. Do not bring forward the decision to call Tang troops.

Namsaeng enters Gungnaeseong and gathers supporters who held the front and took part in reconstruction with him, along with his remaining troops. The yard where provisions gather is distinct from the meeting place in a noble residence; those who rely on him have different motives, including resentment of the royal house, comrades' trust, and the need to protect families from a punitive expedition. The whole city does not become one family's military camp. His regional reputation provides an actual refuge.

The center uses Namsaeng's disobedience as grounds to strip his authority. The remaining Yeon residence in Pyongyang is now both the home of a head of household who has not returned and a target for seizure. The audience knows that the father who has found immediate safety at Gungnaeseong and Heonchung, who bears his own share of protective responsibility in Pyongyang, do not have the same information. The next event is the spread of those separated powers into armed conflict.
