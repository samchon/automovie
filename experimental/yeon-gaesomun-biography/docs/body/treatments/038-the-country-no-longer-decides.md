# A choice the country's fate cannot stop

## Namsaeng chooses aid knowing it will harm the defense {#namsaeng-chooses-destructive-aid}

After Namsaeng confirms Heonchung's death and the king's execution order, Heonseong explains a way to survive by connecting with Tang. Heonseong has lost his elder brother and believes the new regime in Pyongyang will not guarantee the family's safety. He also knows that the foreign court is not acting solely out of goodwill toward Goguryeo people. This is not the clever tale of one boy persuading everyone and changing history: a proposal to protect his living father and his own future meets Namsaeng's desire for revenge.

Namsaeng knows that once a foreign army enters, it cannot remove only the king and two brothers while leaving Goguryeo's defense intact. Handing over fortress approaches, storehouses, and mobilization relationships will endanger people he once protected. He does not defect through ignorance of that risk. If he can gain the force to reach those who caused his son's death, he decides that Goguryeo's survival will not be the standard that stops his choice. His initial refuge becomes an active choice to damage the defense.

He sends Heonseong as an envoy to present the family's position and request aid. Namsaeng's knowledge and actual support at Gungnaeseong give the request value. The envoy approaching Tang speaks of family survival, but the audience knows those words have not secured a promise that the country will survive. Even while waiting for an answer, Namsaeng does not prepare conditions to return to the original reconstruction; he moves to determine which regions will cooperate and which connections he will hand over.

## People who shared his trust choose different sides {#followers-split-over-defection}

Some families in Gungnaeseong follow Namsaeng. They accept defection because of memories of generations of service to the royal house followed by exclusion, trust built together at the front, and the need to protect family and livelihood from a punitive force under the new regime. Namsaeng gains their cooperation, but cannot demand that every resident place Heonchung's death above the country. Those who join him are neither puppets of revenge nor permanent loyalists to Tang.

Unable to discard his memory of trusting Namsaeng, Seokbu nevertheless says he will not follow him in defection. He pays the cost of turning away from a commander with whom he endured the cold, but cannot accept bringing foreign troops into his region. He remains on Goguryeo's side. Sokro, too, protects the troops returning home and their families first and does not join immediately. Keep the achievement of belonging to Goguryeo distinct from any duty to follow the Yeon family anywhere.

Namsaeng does not withdraw the request for aid because of these refusals. As Seokbu leaves and Sokro's troops make their choice, he proceeds with the regional relationships and forces that follow him. The audience understands that long trust was not all false; trust formed while defending the country has split over a choice that harms it. The conditions now exist for both cooperation and resistance within Goguryeo in the next invasion.

## Accepting relief changes the use of fortress and roads {#defensive-connections-handed-over}

Tang receives the request as more than a matter of family survival. Through the defection and contact it confirms Gaesomun's death and Namsaeng's succession and removal, and gains an internal opening through which to pursue conquest again. The response comes that Qibi Heli has been appointed Liaodong pacification commissioner to relieve Namsaeng. Namsaeng accepts the reversal: the commander who defeated him at the Yalu now comes as a force protecting him. Do not invent a private reconciliation or a confirmed historical meeting between them.

Namsaeng changes the use of Gungnaeseong's gate from a defense for his own side alone. He permits cooperation involving the road from the river-facing south gate to the provisions yard and storehouses, and contacts regional commanders so approaches, supplies, and troops can serve Tang's relief and next advance. The causal handover is not a declaration that the gate will be opened, but actual orders received by his supporters who can move the gate and storehouses. A meeting in a noble residence does not automatically grant authority to surrender every fortress.

The information he gives distinguishes those who will cooperate from those who will hold out, allowing Tang to bypass or isolate the latter through the former. Part of the defense network changes from a route blocking foreign troops to one admitting them. Namsaeng gains power to retaliate; his Gungnaeseong supporters gain immediate strength to avoid a punitive attack; Tang gains internal connections absent from earlier expeditions. Their aims produce the same result without being identical.
