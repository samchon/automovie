Pyongyang, late spring 661. The first event is Yeon Gaesomun entering the [council hall](../settings/060-places/010-pyeongyang-palace.md#pyeongyang-palace) and spreading the Liaodong report before those present. Baekje’s capital has fallen, though restoration forces are retaking fortresses. The report says Tang is preparing commanders in six directions and 35 expeditionary armies. From their organization Gaesomun estimates about 350,000; he does not claim to know their actual departure or attack routes ([source and limits](../settings/020-world/030-second-war-calendar.md#second-war-calendar)). King Bojang sits in the royal place, mangniji Namsaeng beside his father, and his two brothers listen. The first explanation tells the audience both the father–son relationship and who decides military matters before the king.

Go Hwi calls for bringing the central field army inside the fortress. Having escaped Jupilsan in 645, he takes no comfort in matching Tang’s numbers. Citing the Liaodong relief force’s initial advantage overturned by Li Shiji’s arrival and the defeat at Gwidansu, he argues that a loss against one column would leave no army to return to the capital. Gaesomun acknowledges the enemy’s strength yet declares he will defeat all six in the field, one by one. Do not show victory or disclose the outcome first.

Go Hwi asks who will recognize enemy exhaustion, how distant local troops can join in time, and whether fortresses can hold until then. He remains doubtful that the other columns can be kept from the capital after even one delay or defeat. Gaesomun answers that while fortresses and rivers hold the enemy, light cavalry will cut its communications and supplies and connect friendly forces. He will move the cavalry himself and receive infantry from regional fortresses. He does not present exact strength tables or operational dates. If an enemy stays through winter and prevents spring plowing, he says, the country will wither even in victory; he will strike the exhausted enemy and finish before spring ([fixed council conditions](../settings/050-systems/035-late-spring-661-decision.md#late-spring-661-decision)).

Nobles with people and land on the northwest frontier and coast accept that they must settle the war this time rather than defend endlessly, though they cannot promise success. Go Hwi maintains his opposition: the trap is real, but the field is a faster road to ruin. Accepting that he risks cavalry he cannot rebuild, Gaesomun decides on battle. Namsaeng receives his father’s order. At the end the audience is left asking what he trusts enough to take this risk, rather than being assured the operation is right.

That night in [the Yeon office](../settings/060-places/020-yeon-residence.md#yeon-residence), Gaesomun sits before the Liaodong report and addresses Go Geonmu inwardly. He has reached the position the king bore, but has not yet reached the same conclusion. Nobody hears him; scripts supplies the wording. No answer or ghost appears. The historian’s first intervention reveals only that this man killed the king he addresses with his own hands, without judging the act’s legitimacy or the war’s outcome for the audience.

From this inward question return to the same house in Pyongyang in 614. The past proceeds chronologically, following how king and boy grow apart as the audience seeks this night’s answer.
