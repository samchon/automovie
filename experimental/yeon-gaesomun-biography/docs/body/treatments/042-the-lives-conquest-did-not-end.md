# People's work does not end with conquest

## Repair work in Henan cannot immediately restore a livelihood {#henan-work-rebuilds-an-incomplete-livelihood}

After 669, Haeun's family makes both workspace and sleeping space in a small house in Henan. They put the cloth bundle brought from Pyongyang beside their newly acquired household goods, but the same skill does not bring the same work. They cannot understand everything nearby neighbors say, and understanding official orders becomes a condition of earning a living. No named Chinese savior appears to solve language and employment at once.

Haeun and Seou continue repair work with the tools they brought, but must divide resources between materials for work and what they need to live. Seou cares for her parents while also taking a share to maintain her own work. Janggeun and Chamu contribute carrying strength, but must newly learn the routes and tasks of transport in an unfamiliar place. The achievement of their choice to remain together holds, while the cost accumulates that their livelihood did not return with it.

Time carries the wider view through 676. Amid repeated language barriers and lost trading relationships, the family continues to live through its tools and one another's labor. The narrator does not conclude that this is a successful settlement story or representative of all displaced people. Leave them in a condition where even an order to return cannot restore their home and livelihood as they were.

## Communities that remain measure Tang's orders against their lives {#remaining-communities-resist-new-demands}

In the east, Miran, Nuri, and Sokro must still weigh food and labor, education, and their families' future after conquest. When the garrison and new rulers demand supplies and labor, they confront whether a relationship in which they once negotiated protection and burdens under Goguryeo now obliges them to accept Tang's demands unchanged. Miran does not surrender all her daughter's labor because the country's name has changed, and Nuri also seeks to decide her own share separately from her mother's protection.

Sokro uses relationships with troops and the region that did not follow Namsaeng to protect families. Through roads and language they share, the community delays communication and mobilization, preventing all the demanded share from being gathered immediately. Do not make this a nationwide simultaneous uprising on a particular date. Some who defected also come to resent Tang rule, while others still see cooperation as a way to survive. A side chosen at the fall is not fixed in one direction afterward.

Seokbu fought on Goguryeo's side and survived disarmed, required to provide labor in his own region. Not following Namsaeng did not guarantee freedom. The people and place he tried to protect remain, and even under coercion they must continue living. The audience sees that losing the center did not erase a community's judgment, but that judgment does not immediately become the founding or victory of a new country either.

## Imperial limits change the task of ruling after conquest {#imperial-limits-change-the-task}

Destroying Goguryeo does not let Tang automatically rule its land and neighbors. In the south, Silla resists Tang's demand for direct rule. In the west, news of Xue Rengui's defeat in the 670 war against Tibet reveals a new limit to Tang's power of conquest, and that front draws its strength in another direction. Convey this through reports and the later historian's wider view. The historian contrasts the failure of a general elevated at Jupilsan with his earlier success, leaving the comparison that valor on one battlefield did not guarantee every conquest. Do not expand new Silla or Tibetan campaigns into direct operations with different protagonists; connect them to the constraint that Tang cannot place as many people and troops as it wants in the Goguryeo region.

For Namsaeng, the difficulties reported in governance reveal that regional connections he handed over do not function with the same meaning after conquest. The position Tang recognizes cannot replace the country's reconstruction he had prepared. Reports of local resentment and the losses of relocated residents reach him, and the consequence accumulates that his name, once able to gain cooperation in the region, is now read as the name conveying Tang's demands. Do not make the established Sokro and Seokbu happen to meet him in another region.

By 676, Tang increasingly needs to use the former royal house and local relationships again to maintain rule. The main story reaches the point where that change takes concrete form in an order in 677. Do not bind communities' survival and resistance into a sign of a future that will redeem Namsaeng; leave them as lives in which people continued to choose on their own terms.
