# When a promise reaches the village

Before spring 646, Gaesomun guarantees the grain and tools assigned to the bereaved, saying that those who died in the same war deserve the same protection. Miran must care for her young daughter without her husband while sowing and preparing hides, so she asks to postpone her turn carrying supplies. Meoru submits a plan for sharing the burden after consulting the other households, and Gaesomun defers Miran's household's one transport turn until after that year's sowing. Other households carry first in the meantime, losing days they could have spent gathering and preparing forest products. Existing grain and supply obligations remain; this creates no right to exemption from all future military service ([household burdens and adjustments](../settings/030-society/070-household-burdens.md#household-burdens)).

Gotak, however, assigns people and animals to other duties first, delaying the support intended for the eastern bereaved. Miran waits with Meoru for the distribution, but the promised goods do not arrive. When she asks the instructor Mujin, he explains that he has no authority over distribution and passes on her demand ([their respective authority](../settings/030-society/140-eastern-incorporation.md#eastern-incorporation)).

At the next discussion of military service, Miran raises the delayed share first. Sokro, too, cannot send his kin out again on the strength of his anger at the massacre alone, and reports the failed support to the center. Gotak explains that the other duties were urgent. His decision had public reasons, but Gaesomun does not count the explanation as fulfillment of the promise ([Gotak](../settings/040-agents/044-gotak.md#gotak)).

Gaesomun removes Gotak and gives Gubin responsibility for transport and delivery. He makes no example through execution or punishment of a household. Gubin reports again on the allocation assigned in the storehouse but not yet received by the village. Gaesomun reverses the order of priorities, directing that this shipment be delivered before other duties. Other transport is delayed accordingly. Before that year's sowing, Gubin actually carries the delayed allocation as well and hands over the household shares in the courtyard before the gyeongdang. Miran checks the grain and tools she receives, and confirms her household's one remaining transport turn after sowing. The households that carried first hear that their turns were moved forward, not erased ([the protection provided](../settings/030-society/160-yeon-protection.md#yeon-protection), [the official delivering it](../settings/040-agents/043-gubin.md#gubin)).

When other nobles protest that their own troops were lost too, yet only the Malgal bereaved receive preferential treatment, Gaesomun maintains the standard of equal sacrifice. Sokro sees him apply strictness to powerful people on his own side as well. Having received the supplies, Miran does not entrust him with her daughter's future, but at the next discussion she treats the center's promise as a real option. Belonging grows through repeated acts of this kind; one great gift does not complete it.

That year, instead of shouting the same signals louder at young people whose speech and lives differ, Mujin breaks them into short parts and checks their meaning. The historian notes that this learning and trade were not gifts Gaesomun first created, but rested on a foundation laid by earlier kings' eastward expansion. With iron tools, salt, and grain coming in and hides and forest products going out, a center that keeps its promises and residents who answer calls to service continue to test one another ([the basis of trade and education](../settings/030-society/140-eastern-incorporation.md#eastern-incorporation)).

In a wider view establishing the bounds of that relationship, the historian distinguishes responses to small raids from beyond the border—demands for return, trade restrictions, and limited pursuit—from the earlier defeat of hostile forces such as Dolji Gye, who led several settlements and their troops, to regain control of the frontier. Even if pursuing poor borderlands with a large army was costly, the duty to protect harmed residents remained. This history is grounds for distinguishing whom Goguryeo bound through protection and service, not for merging the communities now cooperating with old hostile groups or all Malgal. Do not settle the dates and combatants, which the sources report differently, into one king and year ([different scales of response](../settings/030-society/130-border-retaliation.md#border-retaliation), [Dolji Gye in earlier accounts](../settings/040-agents/096-dolji-gye.md#dolji-gye)).

A report from Chojibu, a mobile pastoral community in the northwest, says the spring movement of livestock overlaps with military service. The young people taught at the winter base's gyeongdang do not all become cavalry on the same day. Gaesomun has equipped households provide rotating contingents to keep the duty covered, without halting the whole community's movement. The families of those who leave first must drive livestock alongside other households, so the burden does not disappear. Place this report beside sowing, hunting, and fishing in the eastern village to show the different seasonal costs within Malgal service. Do not open a separate scene for a new chief ([Chojibu's movements and service](../settings/030-society/120-pastoral-malgal.md#pastoral-malgal), [different eastern livelihoods](../settings/030-society/110-different-malgal-communities.md#different-malgal-communities)).

Sokro recognizes that knowing local routes and understanding orders from the center are different skills, and mixes units for joint service. He explains the purpose and rendezvous to subordinates who know only their own language, and reports his own local judgment to the central officers ([conditions of trust](../settings/030-society/170-trusted-light-cavalry.md#trusted-light-cavalry)).

When the light cavalry returns to the planned rendezvous rather than abandoning its mission to pursue loot, Gaesomun assigns it an interdiction mission farther away. Real authority and reporting responsibility increase together. Recognition is not earned by abandoning a way of life or a language. Sokro finds reason to defend Goguryeo in an army that protects his family and uses his judgment. This accumulated trust makes later dispersed operations possible, but the community swears no oath binding it to the Yeon family forever.
