Tang’s thousand Turkic cavalry lure the relief force; Li Shiji’s forward camp looks small, so Go Yeonsu commits more. Zhangsun Wuji and Niu Jinda’s combined 11,000 have been moving along the six-kilometer route behind hills since about one double-hour before frontal engagement. Movement and re-forming a column stretched through a narrow section take about one and a half double-hours. Once frontal fighting begins, Li holds until the detour force is ready. Moving between sides, the audience first sees the difference between Goguryeo’s immediate advantage and the time Tang is waiting for ([route and timing](../settings/050-systems/200-jupilsan-defeat.md#jupilsan-defeat)).

Goguryeo heavy troops seek the emperor’s camp through an opening in the front. Malgal mobile troops including Sokro moved earlier along the hill edge but their approach is delayed, so the two strikes do not combine. The emperor and nearby Tang respond; when the flank-and-rear attack arrives, Goguryeo must protect both front and way back. Xue Rengui’s charge in light outerwear is actual combat within the confusion; one person’s prowess does not explain the whole defeat.

Orders and routes overlap and the relief army divides. Once bridges and retreat are cut, Go Yeonsu and Go Hyejin cannot join outside forces even by reforming camp. Command surrenders to preserve those remaining. Sokro escapes outside the surrendering group but cannot bring all kin and subordinates. Nallo looks for a way out and is trapped with the formation. Defeat narrows into different choices for survival, not a single explanation at the end.

Retain the work’s outcome among 60,000 engaged: 20,000 dead, 36,800 surrendered, 3,200 escaped. No contemporary commander calculates it exactly on the spot. The historian distinguishes records’ claims from work-specific losses and prevents the impression that the rear 90,000 become a new Ansi army intact. The expectation that surrender guarantees life breaks differently for different people in the next event.
