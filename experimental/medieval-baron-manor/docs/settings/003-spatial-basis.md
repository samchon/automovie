**Status:** production invention, user-confirmed graph.

A shallow U-shaped two-storey main house wraps a small central garden that opens to the south. The west, rear and east parts are one building where floors, walls, roof and circulation meet, and no storey uses a different envelope. Every room belongs to one storey and is reached through actual doors, galleries, the corridor and the central stair.

The approach path within the grounds runs from the southern garden side to the central entrance of the main house. No route requires passing through an outbuilding or crossing the garden pond to enter. No ceremonial space of a great lord's hall and no defensive works are added.

**Status:** derived, a production area computed from dimensions the author adopted.

The design budget for the overall outline is 15.2m east-west and 11.5m north-south. The unbuilt part of the central garden is taken as 6.5m east-west and 6.0m north-south. Measured on the horizontal projection of the outline and the garden boundary, the gross area per storey is 15.2×11.5−6.5×6.0=135.8㎡, and the two storeys total 271.6㎡. This includes the outer walls, inner walls and the roofed galleries, and excludes the garden, external steps and eave projections.

Subtracting the 5.2㎡ budget for the stair floor opening on the upper storey gives a production area budget of 266.4㎡. This is a model scale for expressing the target of about 265㎡, not a measured effective residential area. This delivery requires no re-measurement of effective area per room and no physical fitness report.

The dimensions are not measurements from the images; they are a production decision to accommodate narrow galleries, space for furniture and the household functions of two storeys together. If the fixed graph cannot hold within this budget, the area is not enlarged covertly; it is adjusted at the earliest design owner.

**Status:** production invention, user-confirmed graph.

The ground floor uses the entrance, the single central turning stair and one continuous gallery facing the garden as the skeleton of movement. The entrance connects directly to the gallery and to the space under the stair. The family hall, the kitchen and pantry, the study and ledger room, and the storage and washing room each have a direct door to the entrance or the same gallery. No relationship in which another required room can be reached only by passing through one room is allowed.

The west wing is assigned primarily to the family hall, the rear service side to the kitchen and pantry and to storage and washing, and the east to the ledger room. This is a directional budget for household uses; the actual room boundaries and door positions are owned by the space design. The gallery runs continuously along the U, and no locked room or broken floor is inserted along it.

**Status:** production invention, the form is user-confirmed and the riser composition is a production decision.

The stair is one set that starts at the ground-floor entrance, turns 90 degrees at one central half landing and reaches the landing of the upper straight corridor. It uses a model composition that divides the 2.88m storey height into 16 equal 0.18m risers. The treads, half landing, upper landing and railings express the turning connection between the two storeys.

The 5.2㎡ floor opening on the upper storey is part of [the area budget](#area-budget). If storage is placed under the stair, it is owned as an item of the same main house. No internal second stair, straight through-stair or external stair to the private floor is built.

**Status:** production invention, user-confirmed graph.

On the upper floor, one short east-west straight corridor starting at the central landing directly connects the master bedroom, the two small bedrooms, the shared washroom and privy, and [upper shared storage](002-household.md#upper-storage). No chain of bedrooms reached by passing through a room door and no U-shaped corridor are made. Even where the master bedroom and one small bedroom extend deeper into the wings, their entrance doors must open directly at the end or side of the same straight corridor.

The corridor is placed within the rear range without exceeding the overall width of the house. The actual door positions and room division are set by the space design. The door and screening of the shared washroom and privy are a private-floor composition distinct from the ground-floor work spaces.

## Surface responsibility of the first structural stage {#surface-responsibility}

@evidence principles/core/settings.md#fact-status Surface responsibilities are an adopted production rule derived from the user instruction, not measurements of a completed model.
@evidence principles/core/settings.md#observable-identity Shared boundary faces, ceilings, roof undersides and opening surrounds are owned as actual geometry.
@evidence principles/core/settings.md#source-support The surface rule governs this production's authorship without asserting an external building standard or proof of structural safety.
-->

**Status:** production invention, an ownership standard applying the user instruction.

The first structural design submits, decomposed by owner, the floor, ceiling and every wall face of each space, and every exposed outer wall, corner, roof, underside, opening surround and doorway on the exterior. For a shared wall, one boundary provides the complete faces on both sides; identity is never imitated by an exterior wall and an interior wall that happen to overlap.

The space owner is responsible for the boundary, thickness and direction of faces and for the actual voids of openings. The model owner is responsible for the whole geometry of posts, beams, window frames, door leaves and roof members attached to those faces, and the material owner for the finishes attached to stable surfaces. The instance owner is responsible for member placement and for duplication and contact. Even the top of a ceiling invisible under the roof or a wall behind furniture states its ownership according to its actual exposure, and no ownerless surface is handled as an observation exclusion.
