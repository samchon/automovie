# Repeated principles of the temple models

This contract applies scale and UV0 per H2 to all 49 independent model prototypes of the temple. The joint boundary and the neutral review board are covered by a separate model population obligation. The shape and dimensions of each prototype are owned by `docs/models`, and material binding and placement by the later branches.

## Scale datum and representation ceiling {#temple-reference-scale}

Authority: the reviewed shared model design of the temple and the user instruction of 2026-09-27. The datum below applies to every prototype H2 and the source that implements it. If the occupancy or UV0 of a prototype departs from this datum, the review of that prototype and its material binding fails.


When the user's 2026-09-25 instruction to make objects widened the existence scope, 35-objects and 30-interiors of settings had no identities or per-room places for 16 objects. The two parent documents were repaired first, and the concrete dimensions and joints of each object belong to its model H2.

Every temple model follows the right-handed Y-up [coordinate convention](../settings/00-delivery.md#coordinates) with lengths in m and sets no separate front or up axes. The shared scale datum is the adult walking envelope of width 0.6m, depth 0.4m and height 1.9m of the [use profile](../settings/10-building.md#use-profile). The dimensions of each model are derived from the relevant settings range ([objects](../settings/35-objects.md), [envelope](../settings/20-envelope.md), [interiors](../settings/30-interiors.md)) and the clear dimensions of the reviewed spaces (door clear width and height, colonnade column budget, roof underside height), and primitive default sizes are not used as dimensions. The comparison rule checks the model's occupancy box against both that settings range and the walking envelope. If a piece of furniture outside the range or larger than the walking envelope blocks a room passage, that model H2 fails. The [representation ceiling](../settings/50-production.md#fidelity) requires solid members to read at review distance, and the [surface language](../settings/20-envelope.md#material-language) puts the ownership of material values with materials. Under the [production split](../settings/00-delivery.md#build-scope), models own the prototypes and instances own placement and copies.

The representation ceiling is deterministic blocking geometry. The visual claims it allows are the silhouette at review distance (eye height 1.6m, about 2~25m to the subject), member separation (plinth, shaft and capital; the stiles, rails and panels of door frames and leaves; rim, body and neck), real voids (door openings, basin interiors, shelf cells, the gap under the chest lid) and real thickness. Carved ornament, fluting, tool marks, geometric wood grain and tile-by-tile irregularity, geometric weathering, the fine form of rope and weave, and the exact proportions of a specific ancient style are not claimed. Curved faces are polyhedra with the segment counts each H2 sets, and smooth normals are used only where that H2 requires them. The colour, roughness, grain and pattern of materials are set by materials. A model emits both stable surface IDs and the following UV0 physical coordinates.

When discrete members repeat inside one prototype, the H2 sets their count, spacing, the position or angular phase of the first member and which face of the reference member they attach to. A circular repeat records the central angle of the first member and the direction of progression, and the projection of a member attached to a polygonal face is measured from that face's outward normal, not from the vertex circumcircle. A linear repeat records the first centre or first edge and the axis of progression. This rule does not set in place of instances how many times a prototype is placed in the building. The local origin of each H2 is the placement datum of that prototype, and instances sets the real position, direction and quantity.

Every emitted part has one finite UV0 pair per position. From the local origin, 1 UV unit is 1m. The default planar projection is chosen by the principal normal axis of each triangle. Normal +X uses (U,V)=(−Z,Y), −X (Z,Y), +Y (X,−Z), −Y (X,Z), +Z (X,Y) and −Z (−X,Y). In each case U×V points along the outward normal, so patterns on the two sides are not mirrored. At hard edges where the face changes and between different part or surface IDs, vertices are copied to split the seam. The projection axis does not change within one triangle. Cut end faces are projected again with the new face normal, and repeated members on the same face keep the local origin of each prototype, so placement does not choose a new UV origin. materials decides repeat frequency, colour, roughness and texture images in these metre coordinates. Binding a texture to a part without UV0 must fail source and viewer validation, and it must not silently fall back to a solid colour.

The side of a Y-axis solid of revolution, instead of a planar projection, unwraps the angle θ=atan2(−Z,X) over [0,2π], starting from the +X ray and turning toward −Z, and uses U=the real radius of each section ring×θ. V is the generatrix length starting from the local bottom ring that accumulates `√((ΔY)²+(Δradius)²)` over each straight segment of the section, and it equals Y for a cylinder. The vertices of the θ=0 seam are copied. The top and bottom faces of discs use the default ±Y projection. The large-circle angle 0 of a YZ-plane torus turns from +Z toward +Y, of an XY-plane torus from +X toward +Y, and of an XZ-plane torus from +X toward −Z. The arc length of the large-circle centreline is U and the arc length of the small tube section is V, and the small tube angle 0 points along the outward radial direction of the large circle. The seams at the two closing angles 0 are copied. The θ=0 of an X-axis cylinder turns from +Z toward +Y, with the length along the +X axis as V. A Z-axis connecting pin takes the +Z axis as reference with θ=0 turning from +X toward +Y, and unwraps U as the circumference arc length and V as the length along +Z. A pin facing −Z unwraps U from the same +X seam turning toward −Y, with V as the length along −Z. An oblique branch cylinder sets θ=0 by projecting +X orthogonally onto the axis unit vector at the branch start; if +X is parallel to the axis it projects +Y, and if that is parallel too, +Z, in the same order. The length direction of the branch is V, and the seam at the start is translated to the end. Every open tube (including the Bézier tubes and the half-ellipse tube of the bucket) takes the centreline arc length from the first anchor point to the last anchor point set by its H2 as U and the tube section arc length as V. The local axis least parallel to the start tangent is chosen in X, Y, Z order and projected orthogonally onto the tangent to set the tube angle 0, and that normal is then translated along each span to join up the seam. The first end ring is perpendicular to the first segment chord, the last end ring to the last segment chord, and the middle rings join with the exact mitre of the neighbouring chords. Squared timbers whose lengthwise grain reads take the member long axis named by their H2 as U and the in-plane axis orthogonal to it as V. Irregular faces follow the default planar rule above. Normals, UVs and part ranges are emitted together by the concrete exported class of modelSources, and the class does not choose a projection method that is not here.

The following table names every surface that differs from the default planar projection. The length-axis U starts at the small-coordinate end of the local long axis each member occupies and increases along the positive axis written in the table. Square legs and columns start at the bottom end, rafters at the eave end, and truss struts at the low foot. For length-axis exceptions such as the timber long-axis projection where the table does not write V separately, the V direction is set to n×u from the unit outward normal n of each face and the unit U direction u. Where the table names V directly, as for sloped tiles and neighbouring roofs, that entry takes precedence. The V of the inner faces of jars and bowls starts at the lowest ring of that inner section, and the inner seam does not run on from the outer face. Seams split at the member ends and circumference seams of the table. A member in the negative length direction is flipped to the prototype's positive length axis before placement, and instances does not rewrite UVs.
