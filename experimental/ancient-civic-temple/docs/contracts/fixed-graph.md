# Fixed spatial graph

This target records the user-confirmed graph for the reusable civic temple. It does not authorize a different building type or a hidden circulation system.

## One-storey courtyard temple graph {#one-storey-courtyard-temple-graph}

The deliverable is one rectangular, single-storey civic temple of approximately 430 m² gross footprint, with one central open courtyard and one continuous covered colonnaded loop around it. The entrance opens directly into the courtyard. Every enclosed room has a named door to the loop; no room is reached through another room, a disconnected corridor, a concealed chamber, a mezzanine, or a stair. The rear axis contains the small sanctuary, the west side contains the communal votive room, the east side contains the aligned administration, records, and votive-storage rooms, and the rear-right edge contains a small service yard. The courtyard contains one low circular stone basin and one narrow upward water stream. If a proposed detail requires changing this graph, authorship stops and the coordinator must decide.

Authority: the user's 2026-09-16 brief. The approximately 430 m² value is a target range, not a survey result. Success is a compiled containment and opening graph in which every required room belongs to the ground storey and has a physically continuous route from the entrance. Failure is an orphaned space, a door without a host void, a route that crosses a room, a second circulation loop, an extra storey, or a missing required room.

Review question: can the compiled topology be traced from the entrance to every required room and back along the one named colonnaded loop without inventing an opening or connector?

Sources: user brief, 2026-09-16; [buildingSMART IFC 4.3 space-boundary relationships](https://standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4_3/HTML/lexical/IfcRelSpaceBoundary.htm)
