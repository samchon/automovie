Images 01/03/04 provide the grounds. Earth-red curved tiles primarily slope inward toward the courtyard, collecting rainwater at colonnade eaves. As in image 01, the west wing and south/north colonnades use mono-pitch roofs high at the perimeter, hiding their high ends behind outer parapets along the front, west and rear offering-room portions. Parapets read as horizontal plaster tops capped with stone coping. The east work wing has image 01's central-ridge gable; the rear sanctuary gable rises above coping as the building's highest mass. The small front porch gable sits between two parapets. The central courtyard remains fully open, while surrounding roofs shelter the colonnade from rain.

Author-admitted ranges relative to Y=0: support tops of courtyard-side colonnade eaves/east external-wall eaves 3.2~3.8m; outer parapet coping tops 4.5~5.2m; highest sanctuary ridge 6.8~7.8m; mono-pitch slopes 10~16 degrees; gable slopes 18~28 degrees; exposed eave projections 0.3~0.6m. Low mono-pitch slopes are an author choice fitting the approximately 7m-deep west wing beneath coping height, judged against image 01's gentle tiled slope. They are neither historical roof surveys nor waterproofing calculations; later models determine tile overlap.

Roof design settles sections, drainage and joints within these ranges. Beams/rafter ends and tile overlap/thickness show beneath eaves. A tile pattern on a flat slab cannot replace a gable or mono-pitch slope. Chimneys/railings are not fixed requirements and are not arbitrarily added; parapets extend outer walls rather than serve as railings.
