    Validate the read-only world a building is analysed against.

    Two rules carry the weight. The first is physical: an instant whose sun is at or below the reference horizon may not declare a direct beam, because a source under the ground plane illuminates nothing and a beam declared there is how a "daylight" study quietly becomes fiction. The second is about ownership: every context id is checked against the ids the building already owns, so a neighbour's mass can never be addressed as, or mistaken for, a part of the work.

    Instant ordering is validated rather than repaired. Sorting silently would make two differently-authored contexts produce the same artifact and hide an authoring mistake that a reader needs to see.

    Under the typed context-container premise, violations are returned with paths rooted at $input; resident instant and occluder slots are visited by index and absent records are refused at their slots. Other nested container shapes remain a caller premise.

    Directions (north, ground up, each sun) must be finite and longer than 1e-12 but need not be unit length; units must be "meter", instant times strictly increasing, illuminances finite and at or above zero, and an outdoor relative humidity null or within [0, 1]. Occluder planes follow validateSolidPlanes. The horizon rule is skipped while the sun or the ground up has an unusable measured length. A nonfinite measured norm is a range violation; horizon arithmetic is skipped for that invalid direction.
