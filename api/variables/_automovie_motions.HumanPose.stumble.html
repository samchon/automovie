    A stumble from a shove that the body steps out of: a push of 0.5 m/s from ahead, the speed whose extrapolated centre of mass a 9 degree backward lean gives a small woman's pendulum (omega l sin 9, 0.50 m/s; 0.54 m/s on the average man's), the largest backward lean young adults recover from by stepping (Pitts et al. 2024, Appl Ergon 117:104236, unloaded: 9 degrees backward, 20 forward). A shove at walking speed, 1.2 m/s, is a fall for every body: four recovery steps catch at most 0.95 m/s pushed backward on the small woman and 1.17 m/s on the average man. Those limits are people's: released from a backward lean, young adults recover by stepping from at most 16.4 +- 3.1 degrees (Telonio, Cyr, Moglo and Smeesters 2005, ISB XX abstract 430), and more steps do not raise the lean recovered from (Cyr, Moglo and Smeesters 2005, ISB XX abstract 401: 30.4, 33.2 and 32.2 degrees forward with one step, two and no limit); the same pendulum reads 16.4 degrees as 0.90 m/s on the small woman and 0.97 m/s on the average man, and 1.2 m/s as a lean of 22.0 and 20.3 degrees, past the measured threshold.
