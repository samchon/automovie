    Installable action laws for everyday gestures. Use the keys to extend an authoring chain, and register the same laws when planning its performance.

    const Chain = HumanChain.extend(HUMAN_EVERYDAY_LAWS);
    const composition = Chain.subject("guest").stand().curtsy().node;
    new HumanPerformance({
      seed,
      performers,
      composition,
      laws: Object.values(HUMAN_EVERYDAY_LAWS),
    });

    Type Declaration

    • coverFace: IAutoMovieMotionHumanActionLaw<
          Pick<IAutoMovieMotionHumanHandToFace.IParams, "manner" | "seconds">,
      >

      Cover the face with both bare hands. The open palms settle on the cheeks with the fingers upward toward the eyes. The head bows into the cover as both hands arrive at atFace. The seconds parameter sets the additional hold after taskEnd before applying the action's manner and time scale.

      Wróbel, Zickfeld and Ciesielski (2025) describes face hiding as part of crying behavior. The bilateral pose, 15 degrees of head flexion and the default 1.2 s additional hold are authored choices.

    • curtsy: IAutoMovieMotionHumanActionLaw<IAutoMovieMotionHumanCurtsy>

      A small ceremonial curtsy. The Royal Household describes a small curtsy as a traditional optional greeting (https://www.royal.uk/encyclopedia/greeting-the-king-and-queen). Canadian Heritage describes placing the right foot behind the left heel and bending the knees slightly (https://www.canada.ca/en/canadian-heritage/services/meeting-royal-family.html). Melinda Coy's 2019 Practical Etiquette guide for Dickens Fair performers teaches a rear step and a flat-footed dip (https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5bae69697fdcb819deb44456/t/68e2f5d69da9b523c4d22f95/1759704534424/Practical%2BEtiquette.pdf). The performer transfers onto one leg, sets the other foot across and behind it, dips with both feet grounded, rises, then uncrosses and restores the entry stance. The depth, phase durations and step margins are provisional staging choices.

      This floor variant uses flat feet in the dip. The heel and toes stay on the floor in each plant. The law refuses a body or stance whose solved feet fail the plant, clearance or reach checks at the key poses and the intervening witnesses. The arms, hands and head are available for layers.

      Register with HumanChain.extend({ curtsy }) and HumanPerformance's laws. Enter from standing and cut into another action only after recovery.

    • headTilt: IAutoMovieMotionHumanActionLaw<IAutoMovieMotionHumanHeadTilt>

      Tilt toward one shoulder, hold, then return over the entry posture or a carrier that yields its head. Hadar, Steiner and Clifford Rose observed single-direction movements during conversational listening. The 20 degree cap and the default 0.9 second phrase divided into thirds are provisional. The 0.6 second minimum leaves 0.2 seconds for the carrier's fade at each end.

      See

      https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00986881

    • swipeTouchscreen: IAutoMovieMotionHumanActionLaw<IAutoMovieMotionHumanSwipeTouchscreen>

      One fingertip reaches a named touchscreen, swipes in its local X direction and withdraws. strokeStart is touch-in, strokeEnd (also relax) is touch-out. The hand returns to its entry pose at full duration. The stroke takes 285.1 ms by default, the mean touch-in to touch-out duration from Jeong and Liu (2017, doi:10.1080/00140139.2017.1313457). Jeong reports the measured mean in Table 2.6 of his dissertation. The shared transport rule times the reach and withdrawal. The contact carries no weight. The screen belongs to a stationary fixture and the body keeps its entry stance. Stage a free hand in front of the glass and within reach of the entire stroke. The next action can cut in once the hand has withdrawn.

    • tipHat: IAutoMovieMotionHumanActionLaw<IAutoMovieMotionHumanTipHat>

      Take a stiff hat by its declared brim grip, lift it briefly and set it back on the head (Emily Post, Etiquette, 1922, “Lifting the Hat,” https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/14314). The lift is provisionally one third of the head radius. strokeStart closes the grip, stroke marks the lift's apex and strokeEnd reseats the hat. The action uses a still base. Stage the hat on the performer's crown with its wear socket facing inward and keep it worn until the action starts.

    • wipeTears: IAutoMovieMotionHumanActionLaw<
          Omit<IAutoMovieMotionHumanHandToFace.IParams, "task" | "other">,
      >

      Brush tears from one cheek. The chosen hand rises to its own cheek, wipes up and down along the cheek and returns to its entry pose. atCheek marks arrival and taskEnd marks the end of the wipe. The side, repetition count, timing and manner are open. A held tissue can supply the contact point.

      Wróbel, Zickfeld and Ciesielski (2025) treats face touching as a visible component of crying. The wipe's path, palm shape and default timing are authored choices.
