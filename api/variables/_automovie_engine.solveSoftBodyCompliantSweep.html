    solveSoftBodyCompliantSweep: (

    Run one sweep of compliant distance constraints over a lattice, in place: every edge of the lattice, its row and column neighbours, its diagonals and its second neighbours, projected one after another with its Lagrange multiplier, the extended position-based dynamics of Macklin, Müller and Chentanez 2016 ("XPBD: position-based simulation of compliant constrained dynamics", MIG 2016, pp. 49-54, equations 18 and 17).

    For an edge between particles i and k with rest length l, the constraint is C = |x_i - x_k| - l, its gradient the unit direction n from k to i, and the time-scaled compliance alpha~ = alpha / dt^2 with alpha the family's compliance in metres per newton. The multiplier's increment is dlambda = (-C - alpha~ lambda) / (w_i + w_k + alpha~), and the particles move by w_i dlambda n and -w_k dlambda n, w the inverse masses. Unlike the relaxation weight of the plain position-based solver, compliance represents a physical constitutive parameter with explicit step scaling (the paper's section 4). Finite Gauss-Seidel iteration leaves residual error: four and sixteen sweeps need not produce identical stretch or bend. Zero compliance requests an inextensible edge but does not certify convergence. On a cord, a one-wide lattice, the second neighbours are its bending constraint in distance form: the distance between the ends of two consecutive segments, which is regular at a straight rest where the angle between the segments is not differentiable.

    Edges are visited in a fixed order (row-major particles, each with the neighbours that follow it in SOFT_BODY_NEIGHBOURS), so a solve is deterministic. lambda holds one multiplier per particle and neighbour slot and is reset by the caller at the start of each step.
