    decomposeJointRotation: (

    ) => IAutoMovieResolvedJointAngles = ...

    Recover a joint's flexion, abduction and twist, in degrees, from its bone-local rotation: the inverse of jointToQuaternion for the same axes and frame. IK solvers compute quaternions and lower them through this into the angles a pose carries.

    Changing basis by M = [flexion | abduction | twist] turns a proximal twist's qTwist · qAbduction · qFlexion into Rz·Ry·Rx and a distal twist's qAbduction · qFlexion · qTwist into Ry·Rx·Rz. The explicit clinical order qFlexion · qAbduction · qTwist uses Rx·Ry·Rz. The inverse reads each matrix from dot products of the axes with the rotated axes. At the gimbal (middle angle ±90°), only one combination of the outer angles is determined. The inverse pins flexion for a legacy proximal twist, abduction for a legacy distal twist, and twist for the explicit clinical order to zero, preserving the observable combination. The pin holds within a projected cosine of the square root of the unit roundoff, about 1.5e-8, where the two outer angles are otherwise rounding noise and the pinned angles rebuild the rotation within 1e-7 radian. A left-handed basis, such as the default (flexion × abduction = −twist), is extracted against −twist and the twist sign restored, so the round trip holds for any orthonormal basis.

    frame lifts the rest-relative angles to clinical ones, clinical = sign · r + neutral. A domain with a non-null constraint also considers the rotation's second Euler chart, (f + 180, 180 − a, t + 180) for proximal twist and flexion-abduction-twist, and (180 − f, a + 180, t + 180) for a distal one, and the full-turn alias of each angle nearest the constraint's range. The candidate with the least jointRomOvershoot wins, the principal chart on a tie. Every candidate is the same rotation, so a pose no chart can hold keeps its overshoot instead of being clamped.

    Throws on a non-finite quaternion component or an invalid axis basis (see normalizeJointAxes). A non-unit quaternion is read as its rotation matrix, which is only a rotation for a unit quaternion.
