    Variable HUMAN_ATTRIBUTE_EFFECTSConst

    HUMAN_ATTRIBUTE_EFFECTS: IAutoMovieMotionHumanAttributeEffects = ...

    What each group attribute moves, as one table every reader takes its numbers from, so changing a row reaches every law and pass that reads it.

    An effect is a value read between its value at the attribute's 0 and at its 1 (HumanAttribute.effect, linear). Attributes shape how and when, never what: no row decides that a member misfires, routs or falls.

    • discipline.axes: the proficiency axes a member's discipline replaces its drawn proficiency on (timing, reaction, anticipation), so a drilled member keeps time and answers a word more tightly. Direction from the tapping literature (trained timers vary less; Repp and Su 2013, Psychon Bull Rev 20:403); the identity mapping is provisional.
    • discipline.lock: the share of members whose steps lock to a beat track (#3461): 0.62 at 0, the share of side-by-side civilian pairs that lock step frequency (Nessler and Gilliland 2009, Hum Mov Sci 28:772), and 1 for drilled troops at 1.
    • discipline.correction: the phase-correction gain of the linear phase-correction model (Vorberg and Wing 1996): stable between 0 and 2, and held fixed by a synchronising person (Repp and Keller 2008). A search for measured gains in paced stepping (Repp 2005; Repp and Keller 2004, 2008) found no table read; 0.3 to 0.7 is provisional.
    • discipline.dress: the factor on a member's dress bound about its slot (HumanEnsemble.station, read by the walk family's station keeping); provisional.
    • fatigue.pace: the factor on a steered member's desired speed (HumanCrowd.steer) and on the speed of a walk-family gait that draws its own tempo (walk.locomote; a cadenced march keeps its count), from fresh to the weary manner preset's pace (AutoMovieMotionManner.PRESETS.weary, itself provisional), so one table owns weariness.
    • morale.latency: the factor on a response latency's mean (hesitation, HumanResponse.latency); provisional.
    • morale.speed: the factor on a fleeing member's desired speed, from the nervous to the normal desired speed of Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek 2000 (Nature 407:487: about 1.5 against 1 m/s), so low morale runs faster.
    • morale.herding: the herding share p of the same model, the weight of the neighbours' mean direction; 0 for a steady crowd, and the provisional 0.4 at broken morale, inside the model's own range of 0 to 1.
    • morale.fluctuation: the factor on a fleeing member's individual fluctuation, the spread of its drawn desired speed. Nervousness n raises the fluctuation strength as it raises the desired speed, both by the same mixing, eta = (1 - n) eta_0 + n eta_max beside v_0 = (1 - n) v_0(0) + n v_max (Helbing and Johansson, Pedestrian, Crowd and Evacuation Dynamics, Encyclopedia of Complexity and Systems Science 2009, arXiv 1309.1609; Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek 2000, Phys Rev Lett 84:1240, on panicking pedestrians' large fluctuations), with low morale as high nervousness. The review leaves eta_max / eta_0 to calibration; 2 at broken morale is provisional.
