    Lay out the quads of a band that changes a sweep's column count, in the band's own coordinates: s round the band from 0 to 1, t from the start ring at 0 to the end ring at 1.

    The band has rows even rows. Its middle row, row floor(rows / 2) from the start, reduces the finer count F to the coarser K by the standard 2-to-1 quad reduction of subdivision modelling: a block takes four fine columns into two coarse columns through six quads, with three interior vertices half-way across the row, the outer two of valence three and the middle one regular; every other column passes straight through as one quad. With b = (F - K) / 2 blocks and K - 2b straight columns, the fine ring stays regular and the band's extraordinary vertices are its 2b interior poles of valence three and the coarse ring vertices at the blocks' corners, which gain the poles' diagonals: valence five beside a straight column, six between two blocks. A band with both rings regular would need interior valence-five poles to balance the valence-three ones, since the band's interior 4 - valence and its rings' 3 - band degree sum to zero; this strip trades that for one row. The rows before the middle row have the start count and the rows after it the end count, so the band can be as tall as its neighbours' rows require.

    Blocks and straight columns alternate as evenly as rounding allows: of the b + K - 2b items round the row, block i takes the item holding (i + 1/2) n / b, rounded toward the row's middle, so a band reversed in s places its blocks at mirrored items, except where an odd number of blocks meets an even number of items. Each ring's points are evenly spaced, k / F and j / K, so both weld onto evenly divided neighbours; a block's interior vertices lie half-way between its second, third and fourth fine points and the midpoint of its first coarse column, its middle coarse point and the midpoint of its second coarse column. A 2-to-1 band has no straight columns and so no shear; any other ratio shears its straight columns, the price of even rings. Equal counts give a plain grid.
