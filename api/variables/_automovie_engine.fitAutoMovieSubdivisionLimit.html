    Solve control positions of a quad cage whose Catmull-Clark limit surface passes through given targets, one per vertex.

    The limit point of a cage vertex is a fixed linear combination of the vertex and its one-ring. An interior vertex of valence n goes to (n^2 P + 4 sum E + sum D) / (n (n + 5)), its edge neighbours E and the opposite corners D of its quads (Halstead, Kass and DeRose 1993, SIGGRAPH, "Efficient, fair interpolation using Catmull-Clark surfaces"); a boundary vertex, whose boundary is an infinitely sharp crease, goes to (A + 4 P + B) / 6 along its two boundary neighbours, the cubic B-spline limit subdivideAutoMovieSurface gives boundaries. Interpolation is the linear system L P = T. It is solved by Jacobi iteration P <- P + (T - L P) from P = T, which converges when every eigenvalue of L lies between 0 and 2; on a regular grid they lie between 1/9 and 1, and each stencil is a convex combination dominated by its own vertex. It stops when the largest residual falls below 1e-10 of the targets' extent or after 2,000 sweeps, and returns the residual it reached, so a caller can refuse a fit that did not converge.

    Pinned vertices keep their target as their control position and are not solved, which is how a cage meets a fixed junction ring exactly: their limit points then depend on the cage around them.
