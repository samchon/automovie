    The helix's course, along the rim's crest from the front limb over the top and down the back to the lobule. Each inner centre is the crest read by FamilyConicScan.ts helix at 70, 90, 110, 130, 160, 200, 240 and 270 degrees round the ear's middle, sunk 1.8 mm toward the head; the two ends lie inside the ear's plate, in front under the front limb and in the lobule.

    Over the top, the crests at 70, 90 and 110 degrees are carried out from the ear's middle ([9, 1576] in the side view) by 1.3, 1.0 and 1.2 mm and 0.4 to 0.5 mm farther toward the head. Read along rays from that middle (a scratch probe of the leaf with the tube merged, the farthest vertex of each face within 0.6 mm of the ray), the leaf's rim reached 0.6 to 1.4 mm past the tube there, and from above its rim stood behind the tube as a lip of thin facets, the slivers on ear.high-oblique; carried out, the tube reaches past the leaf within 0.3 mm all over the top and the lip is gone.

    On the plate (plate) the front limb and the top stood 1.3 to 5.7 mm outside the plate's face (a scratch probe, the signed distance from the course to the plate every millimetre), so the tube hung free over the top with a gap under it and its front end was a capped tube in the air. The two top centres are moved 1.2 mm toward the plate's nearest point, so the tube crosses the plate along the top; the front limb is sunk 2 mm and carried on down the helix's crus into the head, its first centre [16, 1578, 72] under the crus's skin, so its front end is buried in the head. Its lower limb from 1,570 to 1,555 mm stands 1 mm farther out, clear of the antihelix (below).
