    The drill cadences and paces by regulation, steps a minute and metres.

    • british1792: ordinary time 75, quick time 108 and quickest (wheeling) time 120 on a 30-inch (0.762 m) pace: Dundas's Rules and Regulations (1792) as reproduced by the Hamilton Papers' annotations (founders.archives.gov, Hamilton 01-24-02-0270 and -0384) and the Napoleonic infantry march rates compiled by Rod's Wargaming; provisional until the regulation's text is read.
    • french1791: ordinary 76 and accelerated 100 on two French feet (0.65 m): the Règlement of 1 August 1791 as reported in the Vicomte de Noailles's letter to Hamilton (1800); provisional until its text is read.
    • us2021: quick time 120 and double time 180 on 30 inches: US Army TC 3-21.5, Drill and Ceremonies, the march's own HumanGait.MARCH and HumanGait.DOUBLE.

    HumanGait.march reads a row by its regulation and its time by name; the pace is every marcher's, as a regulation prescribes one step to all.
