    Function physicalShapeNearest

    • physicalShapeNearest(
          pose: IAutoMovieRigidTransform,
          shape: IAutoMoviePhysicalShape,
          a: IAutoMovieVector3,
          b: IAutoMovieVector3,
          taper?: readonly [number, number],
      ): IAutoMoviePhysicalShapeNearest.IResult

      Where a world segment comes nearest an admitted sphere, Y capsule, oriented box or convex hull placed by a parent pose: the signed distance, negative when the segment enters the shape, the point of the shape's surface there, the outward normal at that point and the point of the segment. A segment whose two ends coincide is a point, so this is also the point query.

      The shape's frame is the parent pose composed with the shape's own transform, as in physicalShapeBounds. A sphere is its centre and a capsule its axis segment, each widened by the radius, so both reduce to the closest points between two segments (closestPointsBetweenSegments, Eberly 2023); the normal runs from the axis point to the segment point. A hull's nearest or deepest segment parameter is exact from physicalHullSegment. A box is convex, so its signed distance is convex along the segment, and 44 golden-section steps narrow the segment's unit parameter below 1e-9 before the least of both ends and the two final probes is taken. The point and the normal are read at that parameter from the box's exact signed distance or from physicalHullNearest.

      When the segment passes through a sphere's centre or a capsule's axis, no direction is nearest; the normal is then the shape's local X axis, a direction perpendicular to a capsule's axis.

      A segment widened by a radius that runs linearly from one end to the other (a tapered capsule, the round cone of a limb that narrows toward its far joint) is read by the same search: its signed distance is the least over the segment's points of the point's signed distance less the radius there. The point's distance to a convex shape is convex along the segment (the distance to a convex set is a convex function and the segment's points are affine in the parameter), and the radius is affine in it, so the difference is convex and the golden-section search finds its minimum for every shape kind, a sphere and a capsule included.

      Parameters

      • pose: IAutoMovieRigidTransform

        The shape's parent pose, world metres.

      • shape: IAutoMoviePhysicalShape

        The admitted shape.

      • a: IAutoMovieVector3

        One end of the segment, world metres.

      • b: IAutoMovieVector3

        The other end, world metres.

      • Optionaltaper: readonly [number, number]

        The radii the segment is widened by at a and at b, metres, not negative; absent for the bare segment.

      Returns IAutoMoviePhysicalShapeNearest.IResult

      The signed distance in metres (less the widening, for a tapered segment), the surface point, the outward unit normal and the segment point, all in the world frame.
