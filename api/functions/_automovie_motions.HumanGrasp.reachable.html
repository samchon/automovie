      The reachable variant of a symmetric hand goal: among the goals a family of turns produces (about a grasped part's own axis, where any azimuth wraps it alike, or about the palm's normal, where the palm's contact is unchanged), the one the arm can actually take from a posture, by the error left after the arm's fit, in millimetres of the hand: the wrist's position miss plus the hand's rotation miss carried as an arc over the hand's length, the characteristic length that makes a pose error dimensionally homogeneous (Angeles, Fundamentals of Robotic Mechanical Systems). The elbow's turn from its hint about the shoulder-to-wrist line, the one freedom a placed wrist leaves the elbow (Tolani, Goswami and Badler 2000), is reported as departure and costs nothing here. The authored turn wins ties by a hundredth of a millimetre per degree of turn. Grasp planners rank a part's symmetric grasps by reachability the same way (Miller et al. 2003, Automatic grasp planning using shape primitives), since an authored grip frame alone may ask the wrist for more deviation than it has.

      A grip is held through a stroke, not at one instant: a lifted bottle is still in the same grip at the top of the lift, a clasped hand at both ends of the pump. stroke names those further key poses for a candidate, and a candidate's error is the worst over the grasp and its keys, so the chosen grip is the one the arm keeps over the whole stroke. Each key is fitted with the grasp's own arm solution as its reference, unless it declares reference: false, the way the law will show it; the returned reference is that solution, for the law to pass on every goal of the stroke (HumanPosture.IGoal.reference).

        The further key poses the same grip must reach, from its grasp goal and from the hand the arm shows for that goal: what is held is rigid in the shown hand, so a key that places the held thing is built from the shown one. None when absent.

      • turns: readonly number[] = TURNS

        The turns tried, degrees; -90 to 90 in steps of 15 when absent.

      • Optionalrequired: IReachableRequired

        What a grip must hold before its cost counts: a measure of its per-key errors, judgements and the bounds the keys hold their judgements to (the grasp's, then each stroke key's; NaN where none) and the most it may be. A grip within the bound is chosen before any beyond it; with none within, the one nearest the bound is returned for the caller to refuse. Absent, the least cost alone decides.

      • Optionaladmits: (wrist: IWrist) => boolean

        Whether the caller can take a candidate's grasp goal at all: a candidate it refuses is tried only when it refuses every one. Every candidate when absent.

      Returns IReach

      The best turn and tilt, their goal, the hand the arm achieves for it, and the arm's solution there.
