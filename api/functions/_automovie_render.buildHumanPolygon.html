      Draw a rigid polygon blockout from the generated skeleton, in metres.

      Every module is procedural and comes from the skeleton alone: joint positions, tips and skin girth radii, never the Human's skin surfaces, so the polygon figure keeps working however the realistic skin is built. The engine's resolveAutoMovieHumanoidRingModules owns the rings and their points, which the physical profile also takes its colliders from. Modules use eight-sided sections at their selected levels. Joint spans set torso, palm and foot breadth; girth radii set thickness; the neck runs from the neck joint to the head joint and the head closes on the head's tip. Each rest-space module is moved once by its bone's world placement. Adjacent rigid modules can overlap or separate when posed; this is not skin binding or a watertight skin.

      The blockout stays deliberately simple: a fix corrects a wrong value (a radius, a length, a frame) within the same primitives and section count, never adds geometry, smoothing, anatomy or realism, because its job is a cheap, deterministic silhouette for motion checks and diffusion hints.

      The head attachment uses the head joint's rest axes: +X forward, +Y up and +Z left. Its bounds describe the emitted polygon, before the posed transform. The caller may attach a face to head.object in that same local frame.

      Owned surface, actual bone/mesh registries, pose/palette ports, generating sections, head attachment and cleanup. Local probe points use registered rest point minus the corresponding rest joint; actor-root placement belongs to the parent.
