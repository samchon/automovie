    Function limitSubdivisionRing

    • limitSubdivisionRing(
          ring: IAutoMovieSubdivisionRing,
          vertex: number,
      ): IAutoMovieLimitSubdivisionRing.IResult

      The limit position of a ring's vertex and one limit normal per sector of faces between its infinitely sharp edges.

      Every edge must be smooth (0) or infinitely sharp (10); stepSubdivisionRing spends any semi-sharp remainder first. A vertex with no sharp edge is smooth: its limit is (n^2 C + 4 sum E + sum D) / (n (n + 5)) and its tangents are the cosine and sine masks with A = 1 + cos(2 pi / n) + cos(pi / n) sqrt(2 (9 + cos(2 pi / n))) on the edges and cos(2 pi i / n) + cos(2 pi (i + 1) / n) on the diagonals (Halstead, Kass and DeRose 1993), giving one normal. A dart, with one sharp edge, has another subdivision matrix, so its limit and tangents come from that matrix's eigenvectors through subdivisionRingEigenbasis. An interior vertex of valence two has no tangent plane and is refused, as is any vanishing or non-finite normal.

      With two sharp edges the vertex is on a crease, whose limit is the B-spline point (A + 4 C + B) / 6 of the crease neighbours. Each side's normal crosses the crease tangent with a cross tangent into that side. For a side of two faces this is the regular B-spline cross derivative, exact. A side of three faces refines on its own as a boundary ring whose dominant tangent eigenvalues, about 0.580 across and 0.5 along the crease, stand clear of the next, 0.410, so its exact tangents come from subdivisionRingEigenbasis. Other sides take OpenSubdiv's masks, Biermann, Levin and Zorin's (2000) for four or more faces and the vertex-to-mean-of-crease-neighbours mask for one face. Those are approximations with no exact replacement: under the standard crease rules a side of four faces has a tie for its second tangent eigenvalue and a wider side has two dominant eigenvalues above the crease's 0.5, while a side of one face has a degenerate parametrisation, so the limit surface has no tangent plane containing the crease there.

      With three or more sharp edges the vertex is a corner and stays put; each sector's normal crosses the tangents of its two bounding crease curves, which leave the corner along their edges, oriented by the sector's own winding. A corner sector whose two crease curves leave in one line has no tangent plane the curves determine, and takes the winding-weighted normal of its ring quads instead.

      Parameters

      • ring: IAutoMovieSubdivisionRing

        A one-ring whose sharpness is only 0 or 10.

      • vertex: number

        Vertex index, for the diagnostic.

      Returns IAutoMovieLimitSubdivisionRing.IResult

      The limit position, the unit normal of each sector, and each ring face's sector.
