    Function projectileContinue

    • projectileContinue(
          projectile: IAutoMovieProjectile,
          hit: IAutoMovieHit,
          outcome: IAutoMovieProjectileContinue.Outcome,
      ): IAutoMovieProjectileContinue.Segment

      What a projectile does after a contact, by the outcome the caller decided:

      • stick: it stops at the contact, its point carried depth on along its velocity into the struck body, and rests there;
      • bounce: it leaves on a new flight from the contact, its velocity's normal part reversed and scaled by the restitution and its tangential part reduced by Coulomb friction's impulse, min(mu (1 + e) |v_n|, |v_t|) (the rigid-body impact model of Brach, Mechanical Impact Dynamics, 1991, ch. 4); when roll is given and the rebound would rise less than the body's radius above the surface under gravity pressing toward the surface, it rolls instead;
      • roll: it rolls along the surface with its velocity's tangential part, slowed at the rolling-resistance coefficient times the gravity pressing it on the surface, a = c max(0, -g_n), to rest at v^2 / (2 a);
      • pass: it continues through on the same flight, losing a share of its speed.

      Every coefficient is the caller's: the engine names no projectile. Restitution and the lost-speed share are finite values in [0, 1]; friction, rolling resistance and embedding depth remain finite and nonnegative. Gravity's pull along a tilted surface is not modelled in a roll, so a roll on a slope keeps the deceleration of a level one (stated, not hidden). An explicit roll with outward normal gravity is rejected: an unbound surface cannot hold that body against gravity. Neutral normal gravity permits a zero-resistance roll; it does not turn a bounce into one.

      Parameters

      • projectile: IAutoMovieProjectile

        The flight that made contact.

      • hit: IAutoMovieHit

        Its contact, with the surface's normal.

      • outcome: IAutoMovieProjectileContinue.Outcome

        What happens.

      Returns IAutoMovieProjectileContinue.Segment

      The segment that follows, timed from the original flight's launch.

      Throws

      When the hit has no normal, a coefficient is negative or not finite, restitution or speed loss exceeds one, or an explicit roll would need a surface to pull against outward gravity.
