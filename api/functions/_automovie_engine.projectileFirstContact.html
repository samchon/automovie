    Function projectileFirstContact

    • projectileFirstContact(
          projectile: IAutoMovieProjectile,
          colliders: readonly IAutoMovieProjectileFirstContact.ICollider[],
          span: IAutoMovieProjectileFirstContact.ISpan,
          options: IAutoMovieProjectileFirstContact.IOptions,
      ): IAutoMovieHit | null

      A projectile's first contact with any of several colliders over a span of its flight: when, where on the struck surface, the surface's outward normal there, and which collider.

      The projectile is a sphere about its flight's point, or a capsule from that point to a tail fixed in its body (an arrow from its point back to its nock), turned by its attitude. The sweep is the engine's one contact resolver (resolveAutoMovieContactEvents) with the projectile as its single weapon and each collider as a body measured by its own nearest-point reading, so the scan, root refinement and the tie rule are the resolver's. Its step is chosen from the flight so that the projectile moves less than thickness in one step: thickness over the largest speed any point of the projectile reaches in the span, which is the larger end speed for a drag-free flight (the squared speed is convex in time), including an explicit zero drag factor; positive drag uses the flight's finite global bound (projectileDragFlight.fastest), plus a spinning tail's turning speed. A collider thinner than thickness can be crossed within one step: the stated blind spot, as the resolver states its own. The point and the normal are the struck collider's nearest-point reading against the projectile at the contact second. The scan reads only signed separation and can defer the surface witness when the collider supplies an equivalent scalar reader.

      Initial touch or penetration is reported by default. After resolving that episode, departing names its collider. A fixed plane can then classify departure for a sphere or fixed-attitude tail from the normal motion of this flight, deferring only that pair until actual positive clearance or proving no fresh entry in the span. Later reentry and other colliders keep the original scan. A continuously bounded finite convex shape can certify the same departure for a drag-free ball. Rotating tails and opaque colliders retain initial reporting unless their physical model supplies a certificate.

      Parameters

      • projectile: IAutoMovieProjectile

        The flight.

      • colliders: readonly IAutoMovieProjectileFirstContact.ICollider[]

        What it may meet; projectileFirstContact.plane and projectileFirstContact.shape build the common ones.

      • span: IAutoMovieProjectileFirstContact.ISpan

        Seconds since launch the search covers.

      • options: IAutoMovieProjectileFirstContact.IOptions

        The projectile's radius and tail, thinnest collider and optional already-resolved initial collider.

      Returns IAutoMovieHit | null

      The first contact, or null when the flight meets nothing in the span.

      Throws

      When the span is not finite and forward, thickness is not finite and positive, radius is not finite and non-negative, a tail lacks an attitude, or a departure certificate is invalid.
