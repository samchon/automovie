      Adaptive Simpson integration of a shooting seed's vector integrand.

      A panel compares one Simpson rule with two half-panel rules. Their difference divided by 15 estimates the refined rule's error, and the same Richardson correction forms its value (Gander and Gautschi, Adaptive Quadrature--Revisited, BIT 40 (2000), 84--101, https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/wxg/selected_works/section_07/160.pdf). The caller measures each panel's componentwise absolute estimates in its output's unit. Those measured errors are summed, and the panel with the largest error is split until the global estimate meets the budget. A panel component whose error added to that component's magnitude (the coarse integral of its absolute value) leaves it unchanged in floating point is resolved and counts no error, Gander and Gautschi's termination test: no refinement can move the computed sum there. So a budget below the arithmetic's resolution ends once every panel is resolved, instead of splitting every panel toward machine width.
