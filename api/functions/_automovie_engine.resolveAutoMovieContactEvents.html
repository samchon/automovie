    Function resolveAutoMovieContactEvents

    • resolveAutoMovieContactEvents(
          input: IAutoMovieResolveContactEvents.IInput,
      ): IAutoMovieResolveContactEvents.IEvent[]

      The contacts of several moving weapons with several bodies that may each carry one of those weapons, resolved together and in time order: who meets what, and when.

      Every weapon is a sphere whose centre moves on its free path (the path it takes until it meets something), or a capsule, a segment swept by that radius, when it also gives the axis's other end: a shin, a forearm, any limb segment that strikes along its length, so another body crossing the segment between its ends is met, not passed between sparse points; a tapered capsule's radius runs linearly from one end to the other, as a limb narrows from one joint to the next. Every body is a signed distance at a second, from a point and, for capsule weapons, from a segment (the least signed distance over the segment's points). Both may depend on which weapons have already stopped, because a stopped weapon changes the pose of the body that carries it. The method is the event loop of impulse-based simulation (Mirtich 1996, Impulse-based Dynamic Simulation of Rigid Body Systems, ch. 3) with continuous collision detection for each candidate (Redon, Kheddar and Coquillart 2002): find every moving weapon's first time of impact against the bodies it does not belong to, stop only the earliest, and re-evaluate every other weapon against the poses that stop produces, until no weapon meets anything. Each first impact is found by a fixed-step scan of the signed gap and a bracketed root search of the first crossing to a nanosecond (firstAutoMovieContactImpact), so the result is deterministic; a contact shorter than one step (a weapon and a body passing through each other within it) is the scan's stated blind spot, so the step must stay below the time either needs to cross the other's thickness. A weapon that states the span it is live in (from, to) is scanned only on the scan's own steps that reach into that span, since outside it the weapon meets nothing; the steps are the same as a scan of the whole span, so the result is too, and a performance of many strikes scans each strike over its own window instead of over the whole performance. Since only the earliest impact is stopped, each round reads the moving weapons' scans together, one grid step at a time and in the order the weapons become live: the step at which a first scan finds a contact ends the round, so no weapon is read past the earliest impact, and one that becomes live after it cannot be earlier. Ties in time are broken by the weapons' ids, and a weapon that touches the moment another stops is stopped at that same second, so the result does not depend on the order the weapons are listed in.

      A weapon may produce both endpoints together through sample; this replaces its separate endpoint calls for that exact query. The declared presence of tail still fixes its sphere or capsule kind. Samples are recomputed at every query and after each committed stop, including when two events share the same second.

      Initial penetration or touch is a contact. A body may explicitly certify that this initial episode was resolved and is departing: either no new entry occurs in this search, or a finite positive-gap witness precedes any new entry. Only that pair is deferred until its witness; the scan clock, other bodies and later entry remain unchanged. Unknown departure retains initial reporting. The body's physical model owns this information because signed distance alone cannot establish it.

      A contact is mutual. A weapon and the body that carries it are two measures of one limb (a capsule and that body's signed distance), and they need not agree to the millimetre, so of two limbs swung into each other one weapon touches the other body before the other weapon touches back. When the part a weapon meets carries a weapon of its own that is still moving (the body says so through carried), that weapon stops at the same second against the striker's body: neither limb is driven on into the one that has already stopped on it.

      Weapons may be the capsules of one rigid part (group): an arm's upper arm, forearm and fist. The part stops where any of its capsules first meets a body, so once one stops, the others are scanned no more and give no event: what they touch afterwards is the stopped part resting on the body it met, not a second strike.

      This resolves simultaneous strikes consistently: two fists crossing each other's arms, two shins meeting, a block that arrives first. It is a kinematic resolution of contact times, not a force simulation.

      Parameters

      • input: IAutoMovieResolveContactEvents.IInput

        The weapons, the bodies and the span.

      Returns IAutoMovieResolveContactEvents.IEvent[]

      The stops, in time order.

      Throws

      When the span or the step is not positive, the span or step is not finite or the scan grid's step count over the span is not a safe integer (a RangeError from the scan), a weapon id repeats, a weapon's live span is not finite or ends before it begins, a tail radius is given without a tail or is not a finite non-negative length, a sample changes its declared sphere/capsule kind, a capsule weapon meets a body that measures no segment, a departure witness is invalid, or a body names a carried weapon that is not its own.
