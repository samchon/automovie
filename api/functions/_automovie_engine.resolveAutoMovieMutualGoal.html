    Function resolveAutoMovieMutualGoal

    • resolveAutoMovieMutualGoal(
          input: IAutoMovieResolveMutualGoal.IInput,
      ): IAutoMovieResolveMutualGoal.IGoal

      The point where two people's hands meet when both reach for it: a handshake's clasp, a handover's exchange, a high five.

      Neither person's goal is the other's hand, which moves; both aim at one shared point both can reach comfortably, the way joint-action studies find people converge on it. Each person has a comfortable reach, the shoulder-to-hand distance of the family's working elbow flexion, and the point is where both arms are at it at once: in the vertical plane through the two acting shoulders, the lower of the two points that lie one comfortable reach from each shoulder, so the hands meet below the line between the shoulders. For two equal people that is the midpoint between them, where human-human handovers take place (Hansen et al. 2017, Human-human handover tasks and how distance and object mass matter, Perceptual and Motor Skills 124(1), reviewed by Kopnarski et al. 2023, Front. Psychol. 14:1147296), sqrt(reach^2 - h^2) below the shoulders at the horizontal half gap h. Between people of unequal stature the same rule moves the point up and toward the taller one, who reaches down more steeply and less far forward while the shorter one reaches forward: the exchange height rises with stature (Sutiphotinun et al. 2020, reviewed by Kopnarski 2023). The rule for unequal partners is provisional, as no study of the clasp point of partners of unequal stature or arm length was located; it replaces a horizontal division with a mean height that left the taller partner's arm straight at the end of its reach.

      Shoulders farther apart than the two comfortable reaches leave no such point, so both arms extend by one common factor of their comfortable reaches and the hands meet on the line between the shoulders; shoulders nearer than the difference of the reaches shorten both the same way. An action that has its own preferred contact height (height, as an object is handed over at chest height) passes it: the point then lies at that height where both arms are the same fraction of their comfortable reaches (by a bracketed crossing along the horizontal line between the shoulders, on which one distance grows as the other shrinks), and the height is moved to the nearest one inside the band both straight arms reach there.

      A meeting is refused, not stretched, when it lies beyond a straight arm: the error gives both limits and the widest horizontal shoulder gap the pair can close, so the author moves the people instead of receiving an arm torn off its shoulder.

      Parameters

      • input: IAutoMovieResolveMutualGoal.IInput

        Both sides' acting shoulders and reaches.

      Returns IAutoMovieResolveMutualGoal.IGoal

      The meeting point and each side's shoulder distance to it.

      Throws

      When a reach is not positive or exceeds its limit, the shoulders coincide, or the point lies beyond a limit.
