    Function fitHumanPhysicalStandingPose

    • fitHumanPhysicalStandingPose(
          input: IAutoMovieFitHumanPhysicalStandingPose.IInput,
      ): IAutoMovieFitHumanPhysicalStandingPose.IResult

      Admit a supported Human pose to native joint coordinates once before a simulation. Three actual points on each foot preserve all translational and rotational degrees of freedom as the legs settle into native axes. The ground plane, collider definitions, the pelvis's orientation and its horizontal place are retained.

      The feet are planted at this handoff. The motion foot model stands a foot on its sockets, which lie on the bounds of the foot's colliders at rest, but the drawn heel hull does not reach that plane: in the quiet Stand its lowest point is 33.8 mm above the floor (m-average; 37.9 mm m-tall) while the forefoot hull touches it. A body handed over that way stands on its forefoot alone and falls. So each foot is turned in the direction of the ankle's flexion axis about its forefoot's bearing point, which keeps its place on the floor, until the heel and forefoot hulls touch the floor together: about 16 degrees of dorsiflexion, which leaves the ankle about 38 mm lower and 15 mm further back (m-average; 44 mm lower m-tall, 32 mm f-small). A standing leg is within a few millimetres of full extension and cannot reach that ankle from the authored pelvis, so the body above the feet is set straight down by the drop the most demanding leg needs to stand its knee a shank's length from its planted ankle, about 41 mm (m-average; 46 mm m-tall, 34 mm f-small). That leg turns only its shank, by about 2 degrees, keeping its thigh; in a symmetric stance both legs do. A leg the drop brings nearer its ankle than that, as the unloaded leg of a weight shift, also bends its knee forward in the plane of its own bend, turning its thigh, until it reaches. The joint solver then gives each shank's turn to the knee's native axes, which turns a thigh mostly about its own length once the shift is wide: after an 80 mm shift by up to 0.8 degrees, its knee moving 0.3 degrees about the hip. The pelvis keeps its orientation and horizontal place, and the trunk and head keep their authored shape. Keeping the bearing point, not the ankle, keeps the mass center where the authored stance holds it over the forefoot: turned about the ankle instead, the planted contacts move 15 mm forward under it and m-tall steps out of its stance within a quarter second.

      The arms are opened at this handoff too. The displayed arm hangs with its forearm resting on the waist, which the motion audit admits as the skin's give, 2 mm inside the spine's hull (m-average; 10 mm m-large). A rigid hull has no give, and a pair the profile does not exclude would be pushed apart on the first substep, so each arm keeps its own shape and turns outward about its shoulder's abduction axis by the least angle that leaves it the fit's tolerance clear of every collider outside it, 0.5 degrees (m-average; 2.1 degrees m-large, under 0.01 degrees f-small and m-tall; HumanPhysicalArmRoom).

      The physical pose therefore differs from the authored one by that turn at the feet, those leg turns, that drop of the body and that opening of the arms; the difference is this handoff's correction of the rigid colliders' mismatch with the drawn feet and the soft body, not motion.
