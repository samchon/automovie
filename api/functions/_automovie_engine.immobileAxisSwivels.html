      The swivels at which a joint with one immobile axis can take the rotation asked of it, in closed form.

      A joint whose range leaves one clinical axis immobile (an ankle's axial twist) has two degrees of freedom, so of a requested three-degree rotation the chain's swivel must supply the third: the joint takes the rotation exactly only where the rotation demanded of it reads that axis at its neutral. For a demanded rotation that is a fixed rotation turned about a fixed axis by the swivel, the reading is a sinusoid's root (swivelJointReadings): the middle angle asin(s) is neutral where s - sin n vanishes, and an outer angle atan2(y, x) is neutral, in the principal chart or the alternate one a half turn on, where y cos n - x sin n vanishes. There are at most two such swivels (swivelArcs.roots), Tolani, Goswami and Badler's analytic treatment of a joint limit as a function of the swivel (2000, Graphical Models 62).

      The roots are candidates, not certificates: the joint's two free axes may still lie outside their intervals there, and a reading inside the decomposition's gimbal band is pinned instead, so the caller scores each and keeps a candidate only when its own measure finds the rotation met.
