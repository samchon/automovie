    • resolveAutoMovieBalanceOutcome(

      What an impulse does to a standing body's balance (#3429): it absorbs it within its base, catches it with capture steps, or falls.

      The impulse J changes the centre of mass's velocity to v + J / m and its angular momentum about the centre by (p - c) × J. The body is a linear inverted pendulum of length l with eigenfrequency ω0 = sqrt(g / l), whose extrapolated centre ξ = x + v / ω0 must lie within the base of support for the body to stay (Hof, Gazendam and Sinke 2005, J Biomech 38:1-8): inside the support polygon, the body absorbs the push.

      Outside it, the body steps, the capture point ξ diverging from the centre of pressure as the pendulum does, d(t) = d0 exp(ω0 t) (the capture point of Pratt, Carff, Drakunov and Goswami 2006, as the stepping literature states it): until the first foot lands, first after the impulse, the centre of pressure is held at the base's edge along the lead, so d0 is the capture point's distance beyond that edge. A foot lands at most reach (a leg length, as stagger reaches) beyond the last centre of pressure and catches the capture point within its half length, margin; one that falls short moves the centre of pressure to its front edge, reach + margin on, and leaves the rest to diverge over the next swing, step: d_{k+1} = (d_k - reach - margin) exp(ω0 step). The recurrence shrinks to a catch exactly when the first landing's distance is below its fixed point d* = (reach + margin) a / (a - 1), a = exp(ω0 step), the infinite-step capture distance in the framework of Koolen, de Boer, Rebula, Goswami and Pratt 2012 ("Capturability-based analysis and control of legged locomotion, Part 1", Int J Robot Res 31(9)); at or past it no number of steps catches the body and it falls. Both papers are located, not yet read at the source; the recurrence is derived here from the linear inverted pendulum's divergence. The steps are laid along the lead from the base's edge.

      The bounds are the impulse magnitudes along the push's horizontal direction at which the outcome changes: m ω0 b to leave the base, with b the distance from the current extrapolated centre to the base's edge along the push, and m ω0 (e + d* exp(-ω0 first) - lead) to leave the capture region, with e the centre's distance to the base's edge along the push and lead the current extrapolated centre's distance from the centre. *
