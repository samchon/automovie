      A wheel on a fixed pivot: the formation turned rigidly about its pivot flank, so the line stays straight as it wheels. In the drill books one flank stands fast while the rest moves round it; "the outward wheeling man always lengthens his step to 33 inches" from the ordinary 30, and every other man shortens his step "in proportion as he is nearer to the standing flank on which the wheel is made" (Rules and Regulations for the Formations and Movements of the Militia of Lower-Canada, c. 1814, after Dundas 1792).

      The wheeling frame is a course standing on the pivot, the from formation's blocks moved so each slot stands where it stood at the word, its heading turning and sampled every HumanFormation.SAMPLE seconds: a frame that only turns carries every slot round the pivot exactly between samples. Its angular speed is the slowest member's travel speed lengthened by LENGTHENED, over the outward radius, so the outward man takes the lengthened step and nobody a longer one; it gathers and sheds that speed at HumanFormation.ACCELERATION over the same radius. Each member keeps its own slot of the wheeling formation as a station, so at a called cadence its steps shorten with its radius (walkStation.cadenced) and the pivot turns on its own ground. Its way on paper is the arc its slot draws, a point every ARC degrees.
