    Class HumanPerformance

    A performance of people and props: their chained actions placed on the film clock, every cut checked against the actions' interrupt tables, and each person's displayed pose a pure function of film seconds.

    Construction resolves the composition with the engine's schedule resolver. Each intent's timing comes from its law's plan, made from the state the performer is in when the intent starts: the previous action's displayed pose and velocity at its cut or end, its posture and its planted feet. A cut (an intent's until) is legal only inside a window of the cut action's interrupt table for the next action; otherwise construction throws with the legal windows. A proximity cut is found by scanning the 24 Hz grid for the first frame within the distance and closing on the crossing by a bracketed root search to HumanPerformanceSchedule.CUT, about 4e-8 s, deterministic on a fixed grid whose last sample is the action's end; a distance never met leaves the action uncut. Given rules, the placed schedule is checked with the engine's legality check and a violation refuses the performance. An input equal to one the process resolved recently reads that resolution again (HumanPerformanceState.of): the resolution is a pure function of its input. Authored airborne turn counts use staged rigid rotation by default, keeping the law's centre path, heading and articulation. Explicit physical mode solves their angular momentum instead; a supplied momentum always uses physical integration regardless of this authored-turn policy.

    Sampling a person at a second takes the active action's posture, resolves it to a pose, adds the decaying inertialization offset of the transition that entered it, re-solves every planted or gripping limb onto its goal so no contact slides, and clamps to the range of motion (HumanSegment). A person before their first action stands at their starting place. Additive layers (gaze, breathing) apply over every action without cutting it. While the schedule is resolved, a person's poses may read people whose actions are not placed yet, so nothing is cached until every action is placed; then caches and transition offsets start afresh.

    prop

    • prop(subject: string, seconds: number): IAutoMovieRigidTransform

      A prop's world frame at a film second: its rest frame until a hand grips it, then carried by the gripping hand with the offset it had when the grip closed, and left where the last grip released it.

      A grip is any action's contact window of kind grip on a hand whose support is this prop. The hand's grip socket rides the hand bone, so the prop is a child of the hand while held: the rigid coupling of the engine's IAutoMovieAttachment, a bone plus an offset, with the offset captured at the grip's start instead of authored. When grips overlap (a handover), the one that closed last carries the prop. A hold an action leaves (IHumanActionPlan.holds) is a grip lasting until a later action takes that hand over. A released prop stays where the last grip to end released it, unless an action launched it: then it flies from the launch and rests where it struck (HumanPropFlight) until a later grip takes it.

      Parameters

      • subject: string

        The prop.

      • seconds: number

        Film seconds.

      Returns IAutoMovieRigidTransform

      Its frame.
