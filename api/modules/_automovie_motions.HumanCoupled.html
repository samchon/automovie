    Namespace HumanCoupled

    The two-body compositions of #3440: carries with their lift and set-down, a supported walk, and throws and takedowns, each a mutual record over shipped laws, with no law per hold.

    • carry: the bearer performs bearing with the borne person named, the borne person borne on the bearer, so the pair is one coupled meeting (#3437): a fireman's carry (overShoulder), a piggyback, a cradle, a supported walk (supported), a drag, each with the lift into it and the set-down out of it as the borne body's support sequence.
    • throw: the thrower takes a collar-and-sleeve grip and enters under the thrown body, its fulcrum lowered to the height of the thrown body's front (clinch with under, tsukuri), and the thrown person answers the pull through it (perturb, #3439) with the author's outcome, turning over the throw's fulcrum on the thrower when thrown. The pull itself is the performance's force input (HumanPerformance.IInput.forces, sized by HumanForceExchange.breaking or pull), since a force starts no action.

    The fulcrum of each throw is the thrower's region the thrown body turns over in the throw's common description: seoi-nage over the upper back and shoulder, o-goshi over the hip at the lower back, o-soto-gari backward over the reaping thigh, the single-leg takedown over the shoulder driven into the hip.

    • carry also gives the dance's waist lift and overhead lift (waistLift, overheadLift: the lifted body coupled to the lifter, the overhead one held an arm's length above the shoulders).
    • supportWalk: one person walked between two helpers, each with bearing on its side of a supported walk and the helped person borne on both, one coupled meeting of three roles.
    • helpUp: helping someone up from the floor, both performing support (#3435), the helped hand gripping the helper's palm until the feet bear alone.
    • grappling: both performers follow one authored grappling scene, including its lead side, result and clock.

    Arm wrestling is HumanSocial.armWrestle with the author's winner; the loser's trunk turn the winner's torque gives is owed to #3439. A tug of war is #3451's.
