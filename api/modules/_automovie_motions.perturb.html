    Namespace perturb

    One perturbation law whose response follows from the forces on its performer (#3430): the body absorbs an impulse within its base, catches it with capture steps, or falls, as the engine's balance outcome decides from the impulse, the body's mass and its support (resolveAutoMovieBalanceOutcome), or as the author fixes the outcome.

    The impulse is the sum of the impulses on the performer (IHumanActionContext.forces) from one pendulum time constant 1 / ω0 before the action's start, the span within which the body has not yet moved by more than its own response, to the start. Its outcome:

    • absorb: the hips travel along the push and come back over the feet as a critically damped pendulum at the body's own frequency, x(t) = v t exp(-ω0 t) with v the centre's speed after the impulse, its peak v / (e ω0) at t = 1 / ω0; the feet stay planted.
    • step: stagger's capture step at the centre's speed after the impulse, from the side the impulse comes from (Hof, Gazendam and Sinke 2005; the step's landing from Maki and McIlroy 1997, as stagger cites).
    • fall: knockdown from the side the impulse comes from.

    An author's outcome the impulse cannot produce is refused with the impulse range that would produce it, never clamped; with no impulse on the performer the outcome is an absorb of nothing, a still stance.

    First pass: the step outcome lays one capture step, stagger's; the engine's outcome may ask for more, which this law refuses naming the fall instead until the multi-step plan lands (A folds stagger into this law's step). The absorb shows no trunk motion beyond the hips' travel; the ankle and hip strategies (Horak and Nashner 1986) are not yet split.
