    Namespace WorkoutEquipment

    The exercises of the workout law that are done on a box or a bench the author names (on): the step-up, the incline push-up (the hands on the bench), the decline push-up (the feet on it) and the hip thrust (the upper back on its edge).

    Each solves its angles from the support's own height: a step-up's knee bends as far as lifts the foot onto the box with the other leg straight, and a push-up's pitch puts the shoulders the arm's reach above the hands with the toes where they bear. A hip thrust's back turns about the chest's joint over the bench's edge: at the lockout the trunk and the thighs are one line over upright shanks, pitched as the edge's height asks, and at the bottom the legs are solved in the sagittal plane onto the same place of the feet. The hip thrust is the one Contreras et al. describe (Journal of Applied Biomechanics 32:254-260, 2016, Methods and Figure 1): the back on a bench about 16 inches high, the feet flat, full hip extension at the top; RISEN, SUNK and DROP are not from it. The part that bears on the prop is pinned on the prop's place; the other is kept by the angles.

    With no support named, the kind is done on an imagined one a quarter of the stature high, so the row still plans alone.

    First pass: a step-up stays on the box's edge, the trailing foot hanging beside it, and does not step down the far side; the feet of an incline push-up and the palms' turn of a decline one are the keys', not solved onto the floor; a hip thrust's feet are on their place at the bottom and at the lockout and follow the blend of the angles between, the back does not roll on the edge, and nothing lies across the hips; every angle not solved is provisional (NSCA's Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning, 4th ed., for the exercises' positions).
