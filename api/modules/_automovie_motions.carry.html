    Namespace carry

    Walking to a floor point while carrying a load or pushing a wheeled one: a bag in one hand, a box in both hands before the body, a child on the hip, or a cart or stroller pushed by its handle.

    The walk is the walk law's own (walk.plan), the base every unclaimed channel keeps, so footfalls, pelvis and the free arm's swing are the walk's; the load claims the arms that hold it, which stop swinging (arm swing is suppressed on the loaded side), and leans the trunk by its mass, a share of the body's mass in [0, 0.25] (the issue's band):

    • oneHand: the loaded arm hangs straight beside the body with the hand closed on the handle, and the trunk leans laterally away from the load (Zhang, Ye and Wang 2010, Comput Methods Biomech Biomed Engin 13:339).
    • twoHands: both palms press the sides of a box as wide as 0.8 of the shoulders, held at waist height a third of the arm before the root or beyond the abdomen by the forearm's radius, and the trunk leans back over it (Chaffin et al. 2006's front load).
    • hip: the hand of the hip side lies palm up under the child's seat before that hip, and the trunk leans away to the other side.
    • push: both hands grip a handle at the elbow's height a forearm before the shoulders, the upper arms hanging, a shoulder width apart, and the trunk leans forward (Hoozemans et al. 1998 on handle height in pushing).

    A held load's lean is the trunk's balance over the hips (tilt): the trunk leans until its own weight's moment about the hips matches the load's, the head, arms and trunk weighing 0.678 of the body with their centre 0.626 of the way from the hips to the shoulders (Winter 2009, Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement, Table 4.1). It is an upper bound: the legs also tilt toward the free side (Zhang, Ye and Wang 2010). A pushed handle's forward lean is PUSH degrees per unit of the cart's mass share, with no located study of the slope (a push's force is the cart's rolling resistance, which the law does not know). The lean is shared between the chest and the spine and grows in over the first RAMP seconds. The load itself is not a prop yet, so no grip window is declared. Events: the walk's. An overlay may take the head and, under a one-hand or hip load, the free arm and hand (the plan's yields), so a wave or a phone rides a carry as it rides a walk.

    Not yet: shoulder straps, backpacks, overhead and head loads, a level tray, the child in the arms, on the shoulders or in a carrier, pulling a trolley or a suitcase, the cart as a moving support (A2), changing hands (shiftLoad) and the frozen hand offset of a held prop.
