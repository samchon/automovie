    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanPutDown

    Setting a held prop down at a place and letting go.

    The goal is the prop's resting frame, not the hand: the author names where the prop's origin goes (and optionally its heading), and the hand is placed wherever holds the prop there, by the rigid hand-to-prop offset the grip has kept since it closed (the prop's displayed frame and the hand's at the start, so whatever the grasp was, the same grasp lays the prop down). With on, the prop is put on a bone of the performer's own body instead, at its frame on that bone as the body stands at the start, and is worn there from placed (IAutoMovieMotionHumanActionPlan.wears). With onto, it is set on another prop's socket, its own socket laid on it, and rides that socket from placed as the other prop's articulations pose it (IAutoMovieMotionHumanActionPlan.mounts): an arrow nocked on a bow's string, drawn back with it. A prop is put down as it rested: its resting rotation is the one it was declared with, turned about the vertical to the asked heading, so a bottle carried at a tilt still lands upright. The arm keeps the configuration it carried the prop in (IAutoMovieHumanLimbGoal.reference). Inverse kinematics solves the arm; the rest of the body holds the posture it was entered in.

    Phases: a transport of the hand to the placing frame, timed by the reach-to-grasp movement time over the travel (HumanPrehension), its last 0.05 m straight down so the prop lands rather than slides (provisional setback); the release, the fingers opening flat over 0.2 s (provisional) as the grip ends, which leaves the prop where it was placed; and the hand's return to where the arm hangs, rising the same 0.05 m first, over another movement time.

    Events: placed transfers the prop from the grip to its resting frame. release marks the end of finger opening. A placing frame beyond the arm's reach is refused, as is a placement without a destination.
