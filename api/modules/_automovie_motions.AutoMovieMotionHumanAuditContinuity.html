    Namespace AutoMovieMotionHumanAuditContinuity

    Whether every joint centre of a displayed motion travels a continuous, humanly fast path: no jump, no out-and-back spike and no impossible speed.

    A body is continuous in time, so the distance a joint centre covers shrinks with the interval it is read over, while a branch flip of an inverse-kinematics fit, a swapped reference or a mis-phased blend moves it by the same distance however short the interval. Three readings follow, all on the displayed bones over the whole motion:

    • jump: the displacement that is still there when the interval around it is 1 / (rate * 4^LEVELS) long (resolve). At 24 Hz that is 41 microseconds, in which a joint at 100 m/s covers 4 mm. A frame interval is resolved when some joint's displacement over it departs from the mean of its two neighbours' by more than the limit.
    • reversal: a joint that goes out and straight back within two intervals of 1 / REVERSAL seconds, read as the shorter of the two displacements times the cosine of their opposition. A fit that flips for one frame and returns shows here even where each displacement alone is a plausible speed. The window is a time, not the caller's grid: on a coarser grid a stroke that oscillates by design (a rub, a wave) would go out and back within two intervals, so a turn found on the coarse grid is read again on intervals of that length across its two.
    • speed: the fastest any joint centre travels over a grid interval, metres per second, the bound a caller sets from the action (a hand raised to the face does not reach a punch's speed).
