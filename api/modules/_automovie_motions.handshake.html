    Namespace handshake

    Shaking hands with a partner: both bring the same hand's palm to one shared point, clasp, pump together and let go.

    The goal is the meeting, not the arm: each palm socket is brought to the point handshakeOffer.point places between the two offering elbows, where both forearms reach it with the upper arms as they offer (the taller of two unequal partners extends the elbow and reaches down, lowering the upper arm toward hanging before the arm straightens, the shorter flexes the elbow and raises the forearm, and neither raises the upper arm), with the palm facing the partner's palm across the line between the two people and the fingers pointing at the partner along the line that halves the angle between the two forearms, so two right hands meet web to web with their thumbs up; the two hands then turn in their palms' planes together, the palms' contact unchanged, to the pair of turns whose open hands keep every phalanx off the other's forearm and which both arms reach best at the clasp and at the pump's top and bottom alike (HumanClasp.choose), so each hand's fingers close round the other's hand rather than lie along its wrist. Inverse kinematics solves each arm (HumanHand.place, the Final IK and Unreal Control Rig practice of an effector socket on a world target), and through the clasp and the pump the arm keeps the configuration it clasped in (HumanPosture.IGoal.reference); the feet stay planted as the stand law places them. Both partners read the point from one meeting record (handshakeMeeting), computed once from the two shoulders, so the palms meet without either reading the other's moving hand. A seated pair whose arms alone fall short leans its trunks forward over the seat, the hand's target first (HumanSeatedReach, Chan and Dubey 1995), the lean coming in over the reach and going over the return. The comfortable reach is the shoulder-to-palm distance of an arm offering a hand: the upper arm 30 degrees forward of hanging and the elbow flexed 45 degrees, which slopes the forearm 15 degrees down to the hand, both provisional, as no measurement of the offering arm was located. The slope is what lets two hands clasp with both elbows low: each hand's fingers clear the partner's forearm only when they point about 65 degrees off it (measured on the sweep's bodies between two turns of 30 and two of 37.5 degrees), the wrist deviates 30 degrees less the 5.5 the pump takes, and two level forearms leave the rest to a raised elbow; two forearms each sloping 15 degrees down supply it. gap is the chest gap at which both such arms meet, the distance partners stand at to shake hands. The limit is the straight arm less the pump's amplitude, so the pump stays within reach above and below the meeting point.

    Phases, the handshake's reach, grasp, shake and release (Jindai et al., reviewed by Prasad et al. 2022, Human-Robot Handshaking: A Review, Int. J. Soc. Robot.): the reach is a minimum-jerk transport whose time is the reach-to-grasp movement time over the comfortable reach (HumanPrehension), the longer of the two partners' so both palms arrive together; the hand opens during the reach to the offered hand of HumanClasp.open and its digits close from there on the partner's hand by the clasp (HumanGrip); the shake moves the shared point up and down at 2.43 Hz for two cycles, the greeting handshake of Tagne et al. 2016 (0.90 s of main shaking at 2.43 Hz, as tabulated by Prasad et al.), with a vertical amplitude of 25 mm about the point that is provisional (no study located reports the amplitude; the robot reference of Wang et al. used 10 cm peak to peak) and a sin^2 envelope so the pump starts and stops at rest; then the fingers open and the hand returns to where it hung over another reach time.

    Events: clasp and gripClosed when the palms meet and the fingers close, shakeEnd when the pump ends, release as the hand opens. The hand grips the partner's palm region from clasp to release. A handshake cut into a stand before clasp is a declined reach: the hand returns under the transition's inertialization, continuous in velocity, with no offset left once the blend ends.

    Both partners must stand, or sit leaning, within reach when the handshake starts; the mutual goal refuses a gap neither arm can close. The partner's shoulder is read at the start, so the partner's earlier actions must be placed first, which HumanChain.pair arranges.
