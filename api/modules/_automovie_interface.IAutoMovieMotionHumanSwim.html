    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanSwim

    Swimming on a declared water plane (#3254, M25): treading water and the front crawl. There is no fluid: the root floats at the plane, a height the author declares, and the limbs cycle by joint angles with their goals released, as a swing arm does (AutoMovieMotionHumanArm.swing).

    • Treading water: upright with the mouth 5 cm above the plane (provisional), the hands sculling out and in at waist depth and the legs kicking alternately (a flutter standing in for the eggbeater, whose foot speed sets the height, Sanders 1999), one cycle a second (provisional).
    • Front crawl: prone with the hips a chest's half depth under the plane, or as much higher as the breath needs, gliding ahead at 1 m/s (provisional). The body rolls 45 degrees (provisional) about its long axis toward each arm as it recovers, a whole-body rotation about the hips (IAutoMovieMotionHumanPosture.rotation, #3423) so the pelvis keeps the stroke's own joints. The shoulder turns one way round: its abduction runs from 180 degrees overhead to 0 at the hip and back, in front of the trunk for the pull (45 degrees of flexion at its middle, the elbow bent 40) and behind it for the recovery (20 degrees of extension, the elbow bent 90 and the forearm turned 45 degrees toward the head, so the hand hangs under the elbow clear of the plane; all provisional). Each arm pulls from overhead to the hip over 60 % of the cycle and recovers over the water, elbow high, in the rest (provisional shares). The arms are apart by the propulsive share less the index of coordination, which runs from a novice's catch-up to an elite's near-opposition with the sequencing skill (Chollet et al. 2000 as #3254 reads them; PROPULSIVE, COORDINATION). Six kicks per cycle (provisional) and a breath to the right once a cycle, the head turned 70 degrees (provisional) on the rolled trunk as the right arm recovers, which brings the mouth 2 cm (provisional) over the plane: there is no bow wave whose trough a real swimmer breathes in, so the body floats as high as its own mouth needs. One cycle lasts 1.4 s (provisional).

    Events: entryRight{i}, entryLeft{i} and breath{i} per cycle for the crawl, cycle{i} for treading.

    The breaststroke, the backstroke and floating are keyed strokes (SwimStroke), planned by their own table on the same plane.

    Not drafted: the eggbeater proper, entering and leaving the water.
