    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanRise

    Standing up from a seat, in the four phases of sit-to-stand (Schenkman, Berger, Riley, Mann and Hodge 1990, Phys Ther 70:638): flexion momentum, where the trunk flexes forward while the seat still bears the weight; momentum transfer at seat-off, where the hips leave the seat and the body moves forward over the feet; extension of the hips and knees; and stabilisation.

    The goal is the seat socket's path from where it rests to where it stands over the planted feet; the root follows from it and the trunk's pitch, and the legs are solved onto the unmoved feet. The trunk flexes on a minimum-jerk profile over the first half of the rise to a peak of at least 35 + 50 (1 - r) degrees, so it is still flexing fast when the seat is left (peak trunk flexion closely follows seat-off, Schenkman et al.). Seat-off comes at the first frame from 40 % of the rise (the flexion then 94 % of its peak; provisional) to 45 %, at which the extrapolated centre of mass of the engine's whole-body model (Hof, Gazendam and Sinke 2005) enters the feet's base of support: the forward momentum of the flexing trunk then carries the body over the feet. When no frame does, the peak is raised in 5 degree steps up to 70 degrees, then the feet are drawn further back (below), and a rise whose feet stand too far ahead even then is refused. From seat-off the socket travels forward and up to standing at 85 %, the trunk returns upright by the end, and the hands leave the thighs, or the armrests they pushed on, over the next 30 %. The time is 2 (1 + 0.6 (1 - r)) seconds for a seat at r of the popliteal height, the seat height being where the socket rests (provisional, as the sit's).

    Before it, a body sitting deep edges forward on the seat until its hip joints stand at most one thigh length behind the ankles, so the knees come near over the feet, as people do before rising from a deep seat (a provisional rule, kept 0.6 thigh lengths inside the front edge when the entered sit names its seat); the edging takes 0.4 + 1.5 d seconds for d metres (provisional).

    Then each foot standing ahead of its knee is drawn back, one at a time over DRAW seconds with a LIFT clearance, until the ankle stands behind the knee with the shank 12 degrees forward of vertical, inside the 10 to 15 degrees of a prepared sit-to-stand (moving the feet behind the knees reduces the trunk lean the rise needs; Shepherd and Koh 1996, Scand J Rehabil Med 28:79; values provisional). A body that cannot leave the seat over feet drawn that far draws them further, 2 degrees of shank at a time, up to the ankle's 20 degrees of dorsiflexion (the rig's range of motion). A pelvis that stands more than 1 cm aside of the feet's middle, as after a shift along the seat, has both feet set across by that distance in the same draws, the foot on that side first: the body rises between its feet, not over the edge of their base.

    The rise reads only the seated posture it is entered from, so it follows a sit, a shift or any posture with the socket on a seat. Events seatOff and standing; the seat window ends at seat-off.

    A seat above 1.2 of the popliteal height with a foot off the floor, as a bar stool or a saddle is sat on, is got off instead (HumanMountOff), ahead of the seat or to the side to names, with the same events.
