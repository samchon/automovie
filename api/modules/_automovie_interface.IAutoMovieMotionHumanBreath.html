    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanBreath

    Breathing as an additive layer: the chest, the upper chest and the lumbar spine extend and the shoulder girdles rise a little with each inhalation, on every action, at a seeded resting rate that exertion raises and that recovers afterwards, with an occasional seeded sigh.

    Breathing is written as joint angles rather than an effector goal, because no body point is brought anywhere: it is a rhythmic change of the trunk's own shape. Every planted or gripping limb goal is left untouched, and the arms and legs are solved by inverse kinematics after the trunk, so contacts and held hand goals stay where they are.

    The law, with its sources:

    • Resting rate 12 to 20 breaths a minute, the adult normal range (Cretikos et al. 2008, Med J Aust 188:657); each performer's rate is a seeded draw from a normal of mean 15 and SD 2 truncated to that band. Children breathe faster; the centiles of Fleming et al. 2011 (Lancet 377:1011) apply below 18 years, and a caller passes such a rate as rate.
    • Inspiration takes 0.42 of each cycle (Ti/Ttot of quiet breathing, Tobin et al. 1983, Chest 84:202); inhalation and exhalation each follow a minimum-jerk profile, so the layer is continuous in value and velocity.
    • Sighs: humans sigh about twelve times an hour (Ramirez 2014, Prog Brain Res 209:91), so each cycle is a sigh with probability period / 300 s, drawn from the cycle's index; a sigh's amplitude is 2.5 times a tidal breath (provisional: a sigh is described as about two to three tidal volumes, the joint scale of that volume is unmeasured).
    • Exertion: each declared bout raises the rate toward 43 a minute and the amplitude toward 5 times tidal at level 1 (peak breathing frequency and tidal volume of exercise to exhaustion, Loe, Steinshamn and Wisloff 2014, PLoS ONE 9:e113884), rising from its start over a minute of the exercise (Whipp and Ward 1990, Med Sci Sports Exerc 22:62) and recovering as (1 - u)^3 over its recovery time (the polynomial shapes are no measurement; ventilation rises and recovers roughly exponentially, and a polynomial keeps the computation exact in ECMAScript). Overlapping bouts do not add: the hardest one drives the breathing at each instant, so no number of bouts passes the level-1 rate and amplitude. The phase is the closed-form integral of the rate, so a rate change never jumps the phase.
    • Angles at one tidal breath (provisional; no located study gives the trunk joint angles of quiet breathing): chest extension 1.5 degrees, upper chest 1 degree, lumbar spine 0.5 degrees and each shoulder girdle raised 1 degree.

    The phase runs on film seconds from a seeded offset, so breathing continues smoothly across action boundaries.
