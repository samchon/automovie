    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanConversation

    Where a person in a conversation looks: at the partner's face for seeded glances, away between them, more while listening than while speaking.

    Kendon (1967, Acta Psychol 26:22) and Argyle and Cook (1976, Gaze and Mutual Gaze) found that listeners look at the speaker about 75 % of the time and speakers at the listener about 40 %, in glances of a few seconds. Each participant's gaze alternates glances at the partner's face and looks away; glance and away durations are drawn from exponential distributions whose means give those shares: a listener 3 s on and 1 s away, a speaker 2 s on and 3 s away (glance means provisional, chosen inside the few seconds reported, so that on / (on + away) is the cited share). A look away goes to a point 25 degrees to a seeded side of the participant's heading and 10 degrees below the eyes; conversing people face each other, so that is beside and below the partner. A turn boundary starts a new glance at the partner, as speakers look up at the end of their turn (Kendon 1967).

    The result is a gaze layer's requests, so the conversation's gaze never cuts the bodies' actions. In a group, each span can name a different addressee so the gaze follows the authored exchange.
