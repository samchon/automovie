    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanBallSport

    Team ball skills. The original dribble, dig, set and throw-in describe implied-ball motion (#3247), and the throw-in can release a held ball (#3377). The court releases publish the real spherical ball through the common prop flight owner (#3698). An independently placed stageCatch can take custody at a realized interception, including after a bounce. The high-hand contest and the volleyball stroke variants have their own support and hand timing.

    • Dribble: the knees give a twentieth of the stature (provisional), the palm faces down at the hand's side ahead of the hip, pushes down a quarter of the arm and rises again count times at 2 bounces a second (provisional).
    • Dig: in a crouch, both arms reach from the displayed shoulders, nearly straight, to a platform pointing 40 degrees below the horizontal, the palms together; the platform lifts 10 degrees at the contact.
    • Set: in a crouch, both hands stand above the forehead, palms up and forward; at the contact the legs extend and the hands rise overhead.
    • Throw-in: both feet stay on the ground (the Laws of the Game), both hands are drawn behind the head while the trunk arches back about the hips, and they come over the head and forward as the trunk flexes, along a line rising at 30 degrees, the distance-best release angle (Linthorne and Everett 2006), to where it meets the arms' reach about the flexed shoulders.

    The existing implied phrases use a ready position, stroke and return. The court and volleyball variants define support and arm timings for their respective skills.
