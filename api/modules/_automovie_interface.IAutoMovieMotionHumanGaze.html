    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanGaze

    Gaze as a goal: the eyes' lines brought onto a target, with the eyes, the head and neck and the trunk recruited in that order as the target lies farther from where the body faces.

    Gaze shifts recruit the eyes first and the head only beyond a threshold: Freedman (2008, Exp Brain Res 190:369) reviews head-unrestrained gaze shifts in which the head contributes little below about 15 to 20 degrees and an increasing share beyond; Hollands, Ziavra and Bronstein (2004, Exp Brain Res 154:261) find that large turns recruit the eyes, head, trunk and feet in that order. The split here keeps the final eye-in-head eccentricity at most EYES (15 degrees) on each axis, gives the head and neck the remainder up to HEAD (provisional comfortable cervical share: 60 degrees of yaw, 40 down and 30 up, inside DEFAULT_HUMANOID_ROM), and, when the trunk may join, gives the chest and lumbar spine the yaw beyond that up to TRUNK (40 degrees, provisional, inside their combined twist range), 60 % to the chest and 40 % to the spine. What none of these can hold stays on the eyes, clamped to their range, so a gaze too far behind the body is visibly short; turning the feet is the look action's step-turn.

    The head and neck angles come from the engine's gazeChainJoints, which splits one aim over the neck and the head inside their ranges; the eyes are then solved exactly: each eye's direction to the target is read in its head's frame by forward kinematics of the trunk and head, and turned into the eye's twist (yaw) and flexion (pitch), which compose as twist then flexion in the default joint basis (jointToQuaternion), so the eye's forward meets the target exactly within the eye's range.

    Timing: the eyes move on the saccadic main sequence, duration 2.2 A + 21 milliseconds for an amplitude of A degrees (Bahill, Clark and Stark 1975, Math Biosci 24:191; Carpenter 1988), and the head and trunk on a minimum-jerk profile of 0.15 + 0.004 A seconds (provisional: head movements are slower than saccades and lengthen with amplitude, Zangemeister and Stark 1981; the coefficients are unmeasured). Because the eyes are re-solved against the moving head on every frame, they hold the target while the head catches up, as the vestibulo-ocular reflex does, and a moving target is pursued.
