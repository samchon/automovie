    Namespace AutoMovieHumanSiteViewPart

    A body part lofted from view knots at named sites: the composition every body part shares between its own laws and the view-to-section operator (AutoMovieViewLoft), so the trunk, the limbs and the digits differ only in their axis and their knots.

    Knots. Each site stands at a station of the part's axis and gives the four edges of the box there as signed chart coordinates relative to the axis (the extremes along +u, +v, -u, -v), and what owns the fullness: a taut-tape girth or a sourced conic shoulder. An outline uses the natural cubic spline of its knots (the operator's), so each published measure is a knot and the outline is C2 between. Explicit jets/ranges select the shared C2 quintic owner instead of the default natural cubic.

    Fullness by ownership. A tape's site gets its common conic shoulder solved on that site's box by the Illinois rule on the taut-tape girth (AutoMovieViewSection.solve; a tape outside the box's reach keeps the reach's end with the operator's diagnostic); an untaped site keeps the caller's sourced shoulder. Both become knots of one shoulder outline (kind: "shoulder"), so one owner kind runs along the whole part; the caller's sourced quarter departures, if any, ride beside it. A pole's knot repeats its neighbouring site's shoulder, since no owner speaks there and the pole's own station carries no section.

    Stations. Every site is a layer, so every measure stands on a section of its own; each run between two consecutive sites is cut into the fewest equal intervals no wider than the caller's gap. The caller keeps sites apart: two sites a millimetre apart leave an interval a fraction of its neighbours', which the C2 station rule prints as a ring.

    Diagnostics, never a throw: sites not strictly rising, a box that does not hold its axis (an edge on the wrong side), a tape no section fits; the operator's own follow.
