    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanDance

    A solo dance step locked to a declared beat (#3253, M23): the bounce, the side-to-side sway, the step-touch and the box step.

    The beat comes from the author's tempo, never from audio: beat k falls at k 60 / bpm seconds, 120 bpm when absent (people resonate near 120 bpm, van Noorden and Moelants 1999; the spontaneous locomotor tempo is near 2 Hz, MacDougall and Moore 2005). Each beat is an event beat{k}. Locked to a cue track (lock, #3378), the beats are the track's instants instead: the first on the track's first beat at or after the start, the rest at the mean interval of the track's beats, so footfalls land on a score's beats whatever its tempo (a tempo that drifts within the step is followed at its mean, a first pass).

    • Bounce: the knees give on every beat, the hips lowest on the beat by 1 % of the stature (provisional; vertical bounce follows the rhythm, Burger et al. 2013), the feet planted.

    • Sway: the hips travel side to side over two beats, at each side on a beat, by half the feet's spacing, the feet planted. They leave the middle for the first side through the first beat and return to it after the last, and ride the arc that keeps both legs at their standing length.

    • Step-touch: on odd beats one foot steps a shoulder width to its side, on even beats the other closes beside it; the direction turns every two beats. Each foot travels over the half beat before its footfall, so the footfall lands on the beat; the hips follow the feet's mean and sink until each leg reaches its foot, the travelling one too.

    • Box step: six footfalls that trace a square and return, one a beat: the first foot forward, the other to its own side level with it, the first closed beside it; then the other foot back, the first to its own side level with it, the other closed. The square's side is a shoulder width (provisional); the feet and the hips move as in the step-touch.

    The optional waltz box adds a three-beat change in height and a turn around a world-space pivot. A pair can share that pivot and a cue track, with one dancer beginning backward. This action owns foot contacts.
