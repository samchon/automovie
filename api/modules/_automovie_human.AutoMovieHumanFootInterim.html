    Namespace AutoMovieHumanFootInterim

    A foot and its toes from the person's foot dimensions and the reference set's foot outline (AutoMovieHumanReferenceOutlines.FOOT: lateral and medial edges about the heel-to-toe axis and the dorsum's and sole's heights, per foot length; AutoMovieHumanFootPlaces: the foot's yaw, the ankle's place along it, the toes' along maxima and across loop means; estimated from the private reference set, no published source gives a foot's outline).

    The foot's axis turns out by its yaw from the heel, which stands behind the ankle centre by the ankle's place. Its sites are the heel half-way to the ankle's place, the ankle's place, the instep and the ball (instepLength, ballOfFootLength), plus all distinct outline-table rows. The axis stands at half the lowest dorsum among the sites, and each site's box is the table's edges about the axis there. The forefoot closes half-way from the ball to the shortest toe's tip, where the toes have taken over.

    Each toe runs from its retained cascade/root-u proxy to a generated distal apex. That apex combines its retained foremost along reach with its first accepted loop's vertex-mean across position: these are different observations, not a measured three-dimensional tip or MTP/PIP/DIP landmarks. Its breadth is a packing proxy: the mean gap to its neighbours' across means, or the one gap at either end, since the toes fill the forefoot side by side. The actual leg's sagittal cut replaces the retained ankle-cut envelope in the dorsal source. Each toe root and hidden start read the final parent's complete native field at their own across place. The resulting local vertical chord owns proximal centre and depth, with its available physical derivatives and original frame retained explicitly. Distal depth belongs to the individual toe: the retained FootPrior D/B ratio times its across-gap breadth in the original per-L pattern, before the common transverse scale. That ratio's extraction site is unverified, and its transfer to this foot-axis chart is a modelling proxy. The FOOT dorsum envelope includes all toes and cannot supply an individual depth. The moving origin height and signed plantar edge share one angle clock, knot partition and jets; their sum is the authored nonnegative absolute plantar curve. This local transfer does not assert identical parent and child contact arcs or world C2. The midpoint and hidden inner start are generating stations, not measured phalangeal joint centres; the latter remains owed.
