    Namespace AutoMovieHumanDigitStations

    Where along a digit's centre line its sections stand.

    The reference's bone-normal sections of f1's digits stand every 1.9 mm (39 to 52 per finger, 51 on the thumb); a digit with fewer sections than its reference has lost curves the reference holds, so the rule here keeps the reference's density, derived from the digit's own bones and not copied from any person:

    • one section every STEP mm of the line from the first section past the web to where the tip rounds, the reference's spacing;
    • one every JOINT_STEP mm within JOINT_WINDOW mm either side of each interphalangeal joint, so the turn of the centre line and the joint's relief window are resolved by more than one section;
    • one every quarter of a relief's half width within each window of the profile's narrow reliefs (the palmar creases, the proximal nail fold, the plate's apex window), so each relief is read as the bump it is;
    • the tip's three rings, close together, so the cap's sections keep the rounded end tangent.

    Counts follow from the digit's length: an adult finger of 55 to 95 mm from its first section gives about 33 to 52 sections.
