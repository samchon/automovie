    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanFlinch

    A flinch: the protective startle away from a sudden threat or a blow, the head and trunk pulled away from it, the shoulders raised and the hands brought up before the face, then a return to what the body held.

    The startle pattern (Landis and Hunt 1939, The Startle Pattern; Brown et al. 1991, Brain 114:1891) starts with the eye blink at 30 to 50 ms, the head at about 60 ms and the arms near 100 ms, peaks within about 0.2 s and fades over a few tenths of a second, so the flinch reaches its peak at 0.2 s and returns by 0.6 s. Its size grows with intensity: the body above the hips leans lightly away from the threat (a quarter of the reach's lean limit) and the hips shift away by 1.8 % of the stature, so the head moves by the trunk's height above the hips times the lean's sine plus the shift; the neck ducks lightly, the hands close into fists and rise to cover the face (provisional sizes, no located kinematic amplitude). The lean pivots at the hips and the feet stay planted. Every part leaves what the entry held and returns to it, so a flinch out of quiet standing ends in quiet standing. The threat is a target (the incoming weapon's region, a prop, a point), read at the start.

    The action is rows over the posture it was entered in: one phrase with no preparation, whose attack is the rise and whose retraction the return, and a sway of the root and the trunk, a head, two hand and two arm primitives that all rise to the stroke instant and fall to the end on the clock's envelope. A guard or a defence may cancel it from the peak on, a stagger or a knockdown at any time.
