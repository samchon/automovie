    Namespace AutoMovieHumanChestWall

    The chest wall's box at a girth: the thorax's breadth and depth without the breasts, at the underbust (ISO 8559-1 5.3.8, the inframammary fold), which has a girth and no published breadth or depth. The breast's separate source placement reads its own local wall; this underbust box is not its amplitude.

    Source: Heinz et al. 2003 (J Stat Educ 11(2), 260 women and 247 men, docs/sources/heinz-2003-body-dimensions.md) take the rib cage's own measures at the nipple line by caliper: chest depth (che_de) from the mid-sternum to the spine and chest diameter (che_di) across the rib cage, with the chest girth (che_gi) at the nipple line in men and just above the breast tissue in women, so in both sexes the three describe the thorax without the breasts. Derivation: the depth and the diameter, each on that girth by ordinary least squares by sex (LINES; test_human_survey_chest_wall), evaluated at the underbust girth, which wraps the thorax under the breasts. This height/tissue transfer is a model premise. The compressed calipers supply an aspect ratio, not a skin box: their ellipse is scaled uniformly to the skin tape by the engine's section length owner. No new regression or tissue thickness is fitted.

    The caliper presses through the soft tissue to the bone: 84 of the 260 women's and 109 of the 247 men's chest girths lie above the box of their own depth and diameter. Enlarging only to the rectangular perimeter would force a rectangular skin section. The ellipse convention instead keeps the thorax's depth over diameter (0.681, SD 0.069, in women; 0.696, 0.066, in men).

    Premise: the thorax keeps its depth-on-girth and breadth-on-girth law over the few centimetres between the nipple line and the inframammary fold.
