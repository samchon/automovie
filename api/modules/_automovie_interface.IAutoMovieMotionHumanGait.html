    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanGait

    Named ways of going somewhere on foot; the composition gives the destination. Register the ones a film uses.

    A style whose speed is one of the walk's or the run's paces is a row of that action with its pace, stride and arm carriage fixed: stroll, hurry, trudge, swagger, jog, sprint. Their scales are provisional until each style's study is read (the manner presets' issue lists them).

    A style whose speed no pace has runs the walk's stepping plan in a gait of its own, as the run does, and counts as a walk wherever actions are told apart by kind:

    • march keeps the drill's cadence, 120 steps a minute, and its step, 30 inches (0.76 m) for a soldier whose hip stands 0.93 m high, scaled by the body's hip height (US Army TC 3-21.5, quick time; the scaling and the reference hip height are provisional). The cadence governs: the way is covered in the whole number of steps nearest it in drill steps, so over a way that is not a whole number of them the step gives and the cadence stays.
    • doubleTime keeps 180 steps a minute on the same step, which is a jog's speed, so it is run in the jog's gait with the count kept.
    • prowl is the sneak on deeply bent knees; sad and proud carry a mood in speed, arm swing and trunk; limp spares one leg, standing on it briefly; drunk is slow, unsteady in time and wanders off its line. All provisional, each constant saying what was read and what was not.
    • sneak goes slower than the slow walk on shorter steps, with the pelvis lowered and the arms held close (provisional values; no study of stealthy gait has been read).
