    Namespace stand

    Quiet standing: both feet planted where they are (or under the hips when entered from the air), the pelvis settling over them, the column upright on the plumb line and inclined slightly forward at the ankles (standUpright), arms hanging relaxed, and the body swaying slightly.

    The feet keep their entry plants so a stop never slides them; a foot that was not planted lands at its rest place beside the other. The root moves from its entry position to the midpoint of the feet over the first 0.5 s on a minimum-jerk profile and sits 5 mm below full extension so the knees are not locked. Each foot turns out 7° from the heading, the mean adult foot progression angle (Perry and Burnfield, Gait Analysis, 2nd ed.). Arms hang relaxed (relaxed): 12° of elbow flexion, the hand in its position of rest (HumanHand.shape, relaxed), and the least shoulder abduction that hangs the hand beside the thigh, its digits clear of the body. Postural sway moves the root on two seeded smooth signals below 1 Hz, side to side and front to back in the body's frame, with an RMS of 3 mm for an elite and 4.8 mm for a novice: quiet-stance centre-of-pressure sway of healthy adults is a few millimetres RMS (Prieto et al. 1996), and experts sway less (Perrin et al. 2002, provisional factor 1.6); a given sway replaces that RMS, and 0 stands still. The window of the noise keeps the start and the end of the stand exact. A law acting with one side that stands on this law's body passes that side (plan's side): the side-to-side draw is signed in that side's frame, so a left action's sway is the mirror of a right one's for every seed, its statistics unchanged.
