    Namespace pushPull

    A heavy load that is not carried moved by its two handles (#3351): a crate, a wardrobe or a car pushed ahead walking forward, pulled toward the body walking backward, or a sack dragged behind walking forward; or pushed and pulled at and not moved at all, the scene where force is shown and nothing moves.

    The hold is the sled push's (pushSled): the hands reach the handles where the load stands, close on them and are carried with the chest, so the gripped load goes where the hands go; the push's grasp search, its sink onto bent knees and its refusal of a handle out of reach are its own. What the haul sets is the way and the trunk's lean:

    • push: the walk goes forward to to, the trunk leaning into the load.
    • pull: the walk goes backward to to, the body keeping the heading it enters with, so it faces the load it draws, the trunk leaning back.
    • drag: the walk goes forward to to with the handles behind the body, the trunk leaning forward.

    The lean is the measured one for the declared load at the handles' height (pushPullLean, Lett and McGill 2006), so a heavier load leans the trunk further. The walk is the push's slow one, its steps shorter than a free walk's. A load that does not move (moves: false) is pressed for seconds standing in place, the hands bearing on its handles without gripping, so it stays where it stands.

    First pass, not yet: walking a tall load corner by corner (the load's own pivot on the floor is not modelled), the breakaway and per-step events, the step-to-step pauses a seed draws under heavy loads, and the load's friction slowing the walk.
