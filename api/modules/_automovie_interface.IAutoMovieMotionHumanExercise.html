    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanExercise

    A standing bodyweight exercise counted in repetitions (#3245, M24): the squat, the forward lunge, high knees and the jumping jack (ACSM's Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription, 11th ed.).

    Every repetition has four spans: eccentricStart (lowering, or opening), bottom (the pause at the bottom), concentricStart (rising, or closing) and top (the pause on top), numbered from 1, so the counts are exact and the tempo is the declared eccentric, pause and concentric seconds (2, 0, 1 when absent, the common 2-0-1 tempo; provisional).

    • Squat: the hips sink and the trunk pitches over the planted feet (AutoMovieMotionHumanTrunk.crouch), the hip shift solved for balance. The depth names the thigh's angle from the horizontal at the bottom: quarter 60, half 45, parallel 0 degrees; the hips' drop follows from the thigh and shank lengths with the shank leaning forward up to 35 degrees (provisional, the ankle's working dorsiflexion), and the trunk pitches 15, 25 or 40 degrees (provisional).
    • Lunge: the hips come down until the lead thigh is level over an upright shank and travel forward until the trailing thigh hangs under its hip, that leg's shank level behind it on a foot stood on its toes with the ankle at its dorsiflexion range; the lead foot steps out to stand a thigh ahead of the hips there, and steps back.
    • High knees: one knee rises per repetition, alternating, the ankle under a thigh raised to the horizontal ahead of the hip, the hips carried over the standing foot.
    • Jumping jack: the feet jump apart to 1.5 half shoulder widths each side of the midline (provisional) with the arms raised overhead, and back; a hop of 4 cm (provisional) lifts the body in each jump, the feet free while they travel.

    Events: the numbered repetition instants. A cut at a repetition's top into standing or another exercise is legal.

    Not drafted: floor exercises (push-up, plank, sit-up, glute bridge, mountain climber, burpee), loaded lifts, machines and skipping rope; they need hand and back supports on the floor.
