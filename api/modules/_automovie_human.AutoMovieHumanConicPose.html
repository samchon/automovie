    Namespace AutoMovieHumanConicPose

    A conic family carried to a pose: what a change of its course's joint angles asks of the family besides moving its centres.

    A jointed course (AutoMovieViewAxis.jointed) rounds each interior centre by a turn of arclength 2 half, which leaves both neighbouring segments at the set-back d from the centre; between two turns the course is the segment's own line, a straight run. The straight runs are bone and the turn is the joint, so a pose must keep each run on its bone:

    1. Set-back. d = half g(angle) with g a function of the turn's angle alone (share, read from the engine's own preparation). At another angle the same half gives another set-back, and every station past the turn slides along its bone. A posed turn therefore takes half' = half g(angle) / g(angle'), which keeps d, and the turn's arclength changes by 2 (half' - half).
    2. Stations. Every station of the family (knots, tips, relief windows, roll knots) is carried by a piecewise-linear map from the reference course to the posed one (map). A run's skin rides one segment, the bone that ends at the run's far centre, so every station of a run keeps its distance from that centre; the first run keeps its distances from the first centre. A turn's span is mapped linearly onto what lies between its neighbours' runs: its own changed arclength and, where a run's near centre stands on another segment than its bone (a built corner fixed in the parent), whatever length the line between the two centres gained or lost. That length is absorbed at the joint the turn stands for, not along the bone.
    3. Twist. The chart is carried from up through each turn by that turn's own rotation, so a joint's rotation about its bone's axis moves no centre and leaves the chart where it was, while everything on the segments past it turns. The posed family takes a roll (AutoMovieHumanConicLoft.IRoll) that brings the chart on each run to where that run's segment carries the reference chart; along a run the roll goes linearly from the angle of the run before it, at its proximal joint, to its own at its distal joint, as a twist chain spreads a forearm's pronation along its length, and it holds its value across a turn.

    At the reference pose all three are exact no-ops: the halves, the stations and the arrays that hold them are the family's own.
