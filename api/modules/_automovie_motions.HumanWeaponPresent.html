    Namespace HumanWeaponPresent

    Presenting any held launcher with the weapon as the effector, and the shot's press and recoil: a pistol, a long gun, a crossbow, a bow, a couched lance (#3491's generalised present).

    The weapon is a prop the hand holds (HumanObject.prop, taken by its grip); the law reads its working point tip and its sighting edge (a firearm's muzzle and sight when absent) as the hand holds them, so the weapon's line is what is aimed. The line runs from the anchor to the target: the eye (the head's joint, a handgun's), the cheek (CHEEK of the way from the head's joint down to the firing shoulder, a long gun's or a crossbow's), the face (the mouth's region, where a bow's drawing hand anchors under the jaw or at the mouth's corner, provisional) or the arm (the firing shoulder's joint, a lance couched under the armpit). Absent, the anchor is the eye for a handgun and the cheek for a long gun, today's rule. With no weapon named the hand itself points, and with no target a place straight ahead at eye height stands in.

    present brings the tip onto the line and holds it, or lowers the weapon to a ready position; fire presses the trigger (driving the weapon's trigger articulation to its full travel by the break and back by the end, IHumanActionPlan.drives), turns the muzzle up about the hand and returns it onto the line. HumanWeaponFirearm keeps both under its firearm names. Both may be performed over a carrier, a stand, a ride or a stance on a moving floor: then they claim the trunk's turn and the holding arms and hands, and their plan, made in the world at their start, is carried by the support's motion (HumanSupportCarry, #3491).

    A handgun is held in both hands unless one is asked, the support hand wrapped beside the shooting hand; a long gun (a prop that declares a foreEnd) takes the support hand on its fore-end and is refused in one hand. A target more than TURN degrees off the heading is refused: the performer turns first.

    First pass: the target is read once at the action's start, the holster is a prop where the scene stands it and does not ride the body, and no reload is planned. The recoil's size and times are provisional: no recoil-recovery study was read (#3348 lists it as research to do).
