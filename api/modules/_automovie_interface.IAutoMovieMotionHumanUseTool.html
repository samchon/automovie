    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanUseTool

    Working with a one-handed tool held in the hand, in cycles over a work point while standing: hammering, sawing, stirring or turning a screwdriver.

    The tool is the prop the hand holds (prop, taken up before, as pickUp does), rigid in the hand from the instant its grip closed, so its working point is its own face contact at a fixed offset in the hand's frame (AutoMovieMotionHumanHeld), and the goal is that point on the work, the hand following from it. The tool's line runs from its grip to its face (createTool's contacts). The work fixes that line (a hammer's level at the strike, a spoon's upright, a screwdriver's along the heading, a saw's sloping down to the cut), and the hand turns the least that lays the held tool on it: a hammer's face and a saw's teeth turned down onto the work, a screwdriver and a spoon with the palm inward, as a saw and a screwdriver run on with the forearm. The saw's slope is the one of SLOPES the arm holds with the least strain (AutoMovieMotionHumanBend.strain). A hammer turns about the wrist up to RAISE degrees and strikes back down onto the work point once a cycle; a saw pushes and pulls its teeth along its line over the work point by a stroke of half the forearm; a spoon circles inside the vessel it stirs (HumanToolWork); a screwdriver turns about its own axis into the work, the forearm's pronation and supination of 60 degrees each way (inside the functional forearm rotation of Giachino, Benoit and Heaney 2015, J Wrist Surg 4). The hand reaches the working pose in the aimed movement time (McIntosh et al. 2018), works cycles cycles of PERIODS seconds (provisional), and returns, its fingers kept closed on the tool as the grip left them. Experts vary amplitude less (Bril et al. 2010, J Exp Psychol Hum Percept Perform 36:825); the seeded variation is not drawn yet. Events: ready, per cycle contact<n> (the strike, the stroke's middle, the circle's nearest point, the turn's end), and recover.

    The plan carries the tool's working point in the hand (IPlan.tool), so a capture reads it from the displayed hand; the tool stays held after.

    Not yet: two-handed tools (shovel, broom, mop), kneeling or seated work, fatigue (Cote et al. 2002, 2005).
