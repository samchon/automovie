    Namespace AutoMovieLoftFrame

    Section planes of a chain: where each harmonic section lies in the world.

    A section's harmonic coefficients are planar coordinates (x, z) in mm. A frame places them in the world:

    P = origin + x * u + z * v

    with orthonormal in-plane axes u and v. A chain's stations increase along its length; the side of the plane they advance toward is a property of the chain's layer sequence, not of the frame.

    The horizontal default

    A layer without a frame lies in the horizontal plane at world height equal to its station: origin (0, s, 0), u = +X, v = +Z. Every recovered trunk, limb and digit chain uses this default, which reproduces the world height slicing of the saved loft.

    Oriented layers

    A chain whose sections must turn, such as a foot leaving a horizontal ankle section for sections across its length, or an ear leaving the head surface, gives each principal layer its own frame. Between two layers:

    • the origin follows a cubic Hermite curve whose end tangents are the Catmull-Rom differences of the neighbouring layer origins, so the origin path is C1 across layers;
    • the orientation follows a cubic Hermite curve on the rotation group, whose end angular velocities are given per layer (the chain uses the same three-point differences of its neighbouring layer rotations on both sides of a layer), so the in-plane axes stay orthonormal and turn with an angular velocity that is continuous across layers.

    The angular velocity matters wherever the section plane turns. A point at distance x from the turning axis moves out of the plane at x times the angular velocity; with the spherical linear interpolation (slerp) used before, that speed jumped at every layer, and on skin lying across the plane (the underside of the chin, the nape, the upper lip) each jump was a crease of up to four degrees along the layer. Without angular velocities the interpolation is slerp.
