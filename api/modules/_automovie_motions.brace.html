    Namespace brace

    Bracing for an expected impact, push or a falling body: the stance widens and staggers along the line of the coming force, the body lowers and leans into it, and the hands come up in front, all set before the force arrives.

    People preparing for a predictable perturbation adjust their posture in advance, anticipatory postural adjustments (Massion 1992, Prog Neurobiol 38:35): they enlarge the base of support along the expected force, lower the centre of mass and lean against the force, keeping the feet in place once set (Horak and Nashner 1986, J Neurophysiol 55:1369; Maki and McIlroy 1997, Phys Ther 77:488, on changing the base of support). The goals: the foot on the side away from the force steps back along the force's line by STAGGER leg lengths and out until the feet stand a shoulder width apart (a staggered, wider base); the pelvis drops until the knees flex KNEE degrees over the middle of the new base. A requested hip or lumbar correction uses the actual bearing sole or soles. The trunk inclines 10 degrees toward the force; and both palms come up ahead of the chest's front surface (its sternum region) along the force, facing it for an impact or a push, turned up and lower to receive a catchBody. The arms rise from the entry's and hold the palms there as one trajectory planned clear of the body (AutoMovieMotionHumanArmPlanned, through gestureHands.planned), so no forearm passes through the trunk on the way. Step size, drop and lean have no measured source (Massion describes the adjustments, not these magnitudes). The stepping foot swings over 0.4 s, a walking swing (Perry and Burnfield; Pachi and Ji 2005), with a 3 cm lift, and the set is reached at set, 0.5 s after the start, the end of the 400 to 500 ms in which early postural adjustments precede a predictable impact (Krishnan, Latash and Aruin 2012), and held for the rest of the action.

    The force comes from toward (a person, prop or point the force comes from) or from straight ahead when absent.
