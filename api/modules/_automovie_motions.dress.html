    Namespace dress

    Putting on a hat or a pair of glasses held in one hand, with both hands, while standing.

    The item is the wearable prop the hand holds since a pick-up (prop, by its hold): that hand carries it, rigid in the hand (AutoMovieMotionHumanHeld), so that its own wear socket comes onto the head's region it is worn on (HumanWorn: a hat's on the crown, a pair of glasses' on the nose's bridge), spectacles facing the way the body faces and a hat, round about its crown, at whichever turn the arm, fitted within its ranges, brings the wear socket nearest the crown at (the shared chooser, HumanFreedomChoice, the turn a wrapped freedom); from the stroke it is worn on the head (IHumanActionPlan.wears) and the hand lets go. The other hand steadies it at a place on the performer's own head, read on the posture so it follows the head: a hat's brim at the side of the head's girth (HAT, provisional), the palm on the head's own surface; a pair of glasses' temple just before the ear, the index finger pointing back along it. Both hands rise to a place a quarter of the forearm before the item's final place in front of the face (the item held up), then move it onto the head in a stroke, hold it a moment and return. The reach takes the free-space movement time (AutoMovieMotionHumanTiming.transport) of the farther hand's way from where it starts to where it holds the item up, in free space before the face, the stroke a beat (AutoMovieMotionHumanTiming.PAUSES). The shoulder's elevation stays within the functional ranges of daily tasks (flexion 121, abduction 128 degrees, Namdari et al. 2012, J Shoulder Elbow Surg 21:1177), which the capture checks. Events: the phrase's ready (item held up), strokeStart, stroke (item on), relax (hands off).

    Not yet: the jacket through rigid sleeve sockets, shoes and socks while seated, zips and buttons (garment sockets as props), and a watch on the other wrist.
