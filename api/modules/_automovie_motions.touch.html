    Namespace touch

    One or both hands touch a partner's or the performer's own region with a tap, pat, stroke, rub, press, squeeze, hold or scratch (Jung et al. 2017). Social touches (#3284) and self-directed touches (#3285) are rows of this one law, each stating effector, region, kind and timing.

    Free-space transport brings the hand to the region; the kind's stroke repeats count times, holds, then returns in the reach's time. Manner scales tempo, lift and travel. The entry body holds except for the rise and lean the arm needs. A grounded performer can take one step toward an external site beyond its planted reach (touchStance). Seated and carried touches are bounded by the reach from their existing base. Self regions use the entry limb, except that the other arm is presented across the waist, palm down, on the same envelope (touchPresented). A leg's region follows the legs as a lean leaves them. Thus a touched limb stays independent of the touching hand or has an explicit presentation.

    Events: the phrase instants (ready on the region, strokeStart to strokeEnd the repetitions, relax leaving). A touch may be cut before it arrives or from relax on, into standing, a walk, a gesture or another touch, the declined handshake's rule.
