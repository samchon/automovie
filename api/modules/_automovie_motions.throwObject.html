    Namespace throwObject

    Throwing a held object to a target: overarm, underarm, or a gentle toss.

    The goal is a launch: the object, held at the hand's grip, leaves the hand at the release with the velocity whose drag-free ballistic path takes the point that flies, the held prop's own origin (or the grip of an empty hand, throwObjectLever), through the target. The release angle is the style's (throwObjectForm.ANGLE, provisional; underarm precision throws regulate speed and angle by distance, Dupuy, Mottet and Ripoll 2000, J Sports Sci 18:375), and the speed follows from the range equation v^2 = g d^2 / (2 cos^2 a (d tan a - h)) for the horizontal distance d and the rise h to the target; a target the angle cannot reach is refused. The chamber and the finish are places around the standing shoulder in arm lengths (throwObjectForm.PLACES, provisional); the release is read from the shoulder of the trunk's release pose, at the arm's reach for the elbow's flexion where a study gives it (throwObjectRelease). The hand is drawn back to the chamber on minimum-jerk progress (the windup), then driven on a cubic Hermite curve from rest at the chamber to the launch velocity, taking twice the path's length over the launch speed (the time of constant acceleration from rest), carries that velocity unchanged for throwObjectForm.CARRY seconds on each side of the release point, so the launch does not depend on the instant the hand opens within it, and goes on to rest at the finish over the same time; it holds, then returns to where it hung. The trunk turns toward the throwing side in the windup, back square to the line by the release and through to the other side by the finish, so the trunk leads the arm (the proximal-to-distal sequence, Putnam 1993, J Biomech 26 Suppl 1:125), and inclines forward from the cocking to the style's throwObjectForm.TILT at release (Fleisig et al. 1999), rising again in the recovery. Events: windup at the start, cocked (the end of cocking, Fleisig et al. 1995's maximum external rotation for an overarm throw), release, followThrough, recover.

    The plan carries its launch (of), so a catcher can intercept the path. A thrown prop rides the hand by a grip window to the release and is launched there (IHumanActionPlan.launches), so it flies the same path; angle replaces the style's release angle for a row with its own. An implement held in the hand lengthens the lever the launch leaves from, and a turns attitude spins the prop at the lever's rate rounded to whole half turns to the target (throwObjectLever). Not yet: the stride, the two-hand chest pass and overhead throws, the thrown prop riding the implement before the release (it leaves from the implement's point, set on its way, until a prop can hold a prop), the release-timing scatter of the endpoint-error model (Hore et al. 1995), and feints.
