    Namespace gestureStroke

    The stroke of each gesture: where each hand surface goes, turned which way and in which shape, as a function of the stroke's progress, and which joints a gesture moves directly.

    Hand gestures are effector goals. Each names a hand surface, a world point and a hand rotation; the gesture law transports the hand there in the preparation phase and back in the retraction, and the arm is solved by inverse kinematics (HumanHand). Points are built in the performer's own frame (x left, y up, z forward from the root) from the body's measure, or on a target or on the body's own face, so every gesture fits any stature and proportion. A place of the air is written in the names of AutoMovieMotionHumanTargetGrid.Place (a height, a lateral and a distance, with a nudge in arm lengths where the stroke moves about it), a hand's turn in the axes of HumanHand.Direction, and a joint angle as a level of AutoMovieMotionHumanRange:

    • wave: the open palm faces forward beside the head, at eye height, outside the shoulder and near; the hand swings side to side 0.06 arm lengths, tilting as the forearm turns about the elbow, about 2.5 cycles a second (provisional).
    • raiseHand: the open palm forward, raised from the shoulder to overhead by the extent; cheer raises both fists and pumps them 0.08 arm lengths.
    • stopPalm: the open palm faces forward at shoulder height, inside the shoulder and far, pushed 0.05 arm lengths further in the stroke.
    • point: the index fingertip on the line from the eye on the pointing side to the target, as people point (Henriques and Crawford 2002, J Neurophysiol 87:1677, found the fingertip placed on the line from the dominant eye; the side's eye is the provisional choice), at the distance where the arm reaches with 0.95 of its length, the fingers along the line and the palm down.
    • palmsUp: both open palms up, forearms forward at the waist, turned a quarter of the way out, set 0.15 arm lengths out and forward and lifted 0.03 in the stroke, the offering and not-knowing gesture; shrug adds it under raised shoulders.
    • beckon: the palm up at chest height, 0.05 arm lengths lower and nearer, while the fingers curl and open, about 1.7 cycles a second (provisional).
    • clap: both palms face each other ahead of the chest, 0.1 arm lengths below and 0.17 ahead of its place, part 0.09 and meet at the midpoint on each beat, four beats a second (free applause clusters near 4 Hz, faster than the near-2 Hz synchronised rhythm, Neda et al. 2000, Nature 403:849).
    • thumbsDown: the fist ahead of the chest, the palm outward and the knuckles ahead and 45 degrees down, so a straight thumb, which leans toward the knuckles, points down; the elbow is asked out at the shoulder's height, which leaves the forearm a quarter turn instead of a half (provisional place, tip and elbow).
    • rubHands: the palms together, sliding 0.03 arm lengths opposite ways along the fingers.
    • palmsTogether: the palms pressed together ahead of the chest, the fingers up and forward, held: the clap's hand pose without its parting (a prayer, a wai or gassho greeting).
    • touchFace: the index fingertip, hand flat, on a face region with the palm toward the face and the fingers up, held; pushGlasses slides it 12 mm up the nose bridge; scratchHead rubs it 15 mm on the head's side four times a second (provisional).

    Gestures of the head, shoulders and trunk are joint angles, stated by gestureJoints; pointing and self-touch read the posed trunk in gestureTouch; the strokes ride the signals of gestureSignal. Amplitudes and distances that express the gesture's size scale with the Effort extent; contact points and the pointing line do not. Each hand goal widens the elbow's hanging hint by its own share of the upper arm (out: 0.3 for a wave, 0.2 for a raised hand, a cheer and open palms, 0.1 for palms together, none for the rest; provisional, read from no measured source). Every value above without a source is provisional.
