    Namespace look

    Standing and turning the eyes, head and trunk to a target, either at once or, when cued by something seen or heard, after a seeded reaction time: the orienting response of a bystander.

    The feet stay planted where they are (the stand law places the body), and the gaze goal is the eyes' lines on the target, recruited eyes, head and trunk in that order (HumanGaze); the target is tracked while the action lasts. Use the gaze layer to look while doing something else; this action is for a gaze that is the whole performance or that must turn the trunk.

    An uncued look at a target outside the 120 degree field the performer attends ahead (60 degrees either side of the heading) is refused: nothing there could have drawn the look, so the author declares the cue. A cue delays the shift by a simple reaction time drawn from an ex-Gaussian, the distribution simple reaction times follow (Hohle 1965, J Exp Psychol 69:382): a visual cue seen with mu, sigma and tau of 260, 35 and 70 ms for a novice to 220, 25 and 40 ms for an elite on the reaction axis, and an acoustic signal 40 ms faster in mu, since simple auditory reaction is faster than visual by a few tens of milliseconds (Welford 1980, Reaction Times). The values are provisional: they place the mean inside the 200 to 300 ms of simple visual reaction in adults, and no study located measures orienting onset by skill.

    Events: gazeOn, when the eyes reach the target, and settled, when the head and trunk end their turn.
