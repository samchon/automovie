    Namespace AutoMovieHumanArmBones

    The arm's bony landmarks and joint centres, placed from the glenohumeral centre and the public lengths at their published definitions before any skin exists, in the left frame (calibration millimetres).

    ANSUR II measures acromion-radiale (upperArmLength) and radiale-stylion (lowerArmLength) with the arm hanging, each with a beam caliper parallel to its segment's long axis. De Leva 1996 Table 2 places the joint centres from those landmarks along the segment axis: the shoulder centre 33.7 (F) / 34.5 (M) mm distal of the acromion and the elbow centre 13.9 / 14.3 mm proximal of radiale, both read off acromion-radiale with the arm adducted, whose reconstructed lengths are 322.7 and 330.5 mm (Table 4 joint-centre lengths plus Table 2 landmark offsets); the wrist centre 1.5 mm distal of stylion. As shares of the adducted acromion-radiale both sexes agree, 0.1044 and 0.0432 (33.7 / 322.7 = 0.10443, 34.5 / 330.5 = 0.10439; 13.9 / 322.7 = 0.04307, 14.3 / 330.5 = 0.04327). These retained ratios are approximations: Table 2, Fig.1, Table 4 and the Upper arm text contain differing mean reconstructions; the Upper arm text states AR 324.1 / 331.8 mm. They are not one exact individual regression (docs/work/law-definition-audit.md, Arm measurement closure).

    The active GH has its own population/frame-transfer owner. In the straight neutral measuring convention H=AR-d-e, where d is that actual downward acromion-to-GH projection and e=.0432 AR. The old .8524 AR expression applies only when d=.1044 AR. A bone's length is then carried rigidly to bind. Radiale belongs to the radius and turns with the forearm: it stands 0.0432 AR past the elbow centre along the forearm, and stylion radiale-stylion past radiale along it.
