    Namespace lieDown

    Lying down on the floor from standing into a position: supine, prone, or on the left or right side. Entered on the knees or crouched over the hands, as a crawl ends, the body goes from the posture it displays through the route's postures after the kneel; any other base is refused (floorEntry).

    The movement passes through the floor postures people use to get down (VanSant 1988, Phys Ther 68:185, codes the components of the reverse rise): to lie on the back the body sits down onto the floor and rolls back; to lie face down it kneels, goes onto hands and knees and lowers itself forward; to lie on a side it lies on its back and rolls onto that side. Each posture is a set of joint angles and a whole-body tilt (POSES, provisional), passed through in joint space on minimum-jerk progress. At every second the body rests on the floor: the root's height puts the lowest point of the body's collision shapes on it (knockdown.rest). Entered standing, the feet keep their place while the body goes down to the route's first posture. The mean of their bearing soles gives the root path and each foot is fitted to its own stationary socket. The pelvis then stays over the first landing, or its entry place when already on the floor. Rolling from supine turns about the longitudinal axis with the dependent heel fixed horizontally. Each phase takes PHASE seconds (provisional), scaled by the manner's tempo; the position is then held hold seconds. Events: getDown at the start, the arrival at each posture on the route (seatContact, kneeContact, handsDown, supine, prone, lateral) and settled.

    Not yet: lying on a bed or a sofa (A2 support frames, A8 surface), the recovery position, sleep with seeded position changes (Skarpsno et al. 2017; De Koninck et al. 1992), rolling over and rising from a bed.
