    Namespace roll

    A forward or backward roll on the floor: the body crouches into a tuck over its feet, rolls once round over its back as a ball rolls, without flight, and rises from the tuck on its feet where the roll has carried it.

    The tuck is a set of joint angles (TUCK, provisional: the trunk and the neck flexed, the hips and the knees folded, the arms round the shins). Crouching, the joints go from the entry's into the tuck while the ankles stay where they stood. Rolling, the tucked body turns once about its lateral axis (forward or back), its root placed each second so its lowest joint centre lies on the floor at the ankles' standing height, and carried along by rolling without slipping: the root advances by the turn times its height above that lowest point, integrated at plan time. Rising, the crouch is played back from where the roll ends.

    Contact windows: the feet plant through the crouch and the rise, the back lies on the floor over the roll's middle and the seat at its end (a forward roll) or start (a backward one).

    First pass: the hands do not plant on the floor at the start (the arms hold the shins), the head is not kept off the floor by the hands in a backward roll, and the floor's contact is read on joint centres, not the skin.
