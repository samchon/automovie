    Namespace HumanMusketDrill

    The musket's drill (#3488), the one musket lexicon: the 1764 Manual Exercise's carry positions, its Prime and Load in its own order, and its firings, each a row of a shipped law on the musket's, the cartridge's and the pouch's affordances. One soldier's rows; the manual's three ranks are the crowds campaign's to arrange.

    In the manual's order: handleCartridge (a pick up of a cartridge from the pouch's slot), biteCartridge (a touch of the cartridge's end to the mouth), prime (a touch of the cartridge to the pan), shutPan (an operation of the frizzen hinge), castAbout (presenting at a high ready, the muzzle up before the body), charge (a put down of the cartridge into the muzzle), drawRammer (an operation of the ramrod slider, then a pick up of the rod), ramDown (an operation inserting the rod into the muzzle, withdrawn), returnRammer (a put down into the pipe), seize (the right hand on the small of the stock, the left still under the butt), recover (the left hand from the butt to above the lock, the firelock upright before the body, its butt at the left breast, the manual's Make Ready beginning "SPRING the firelock briskly to the recover"), makeReady over it (an operation of the cock hinge to full cock), then presentMusket and fireMusket. takeFirelock takes the musket by its butt in the left hand, shoulderArms carries it shouldered against the left shoulder, its butt on the hip, and orderArms sets its butt down by the foot.

    The manual gives the order and the count of motions, not their durations: each row's timing is its law's, provisional, and the composed Prime and Load is to fall inside a trained soldier's rate, which a primary source is still to give (secondary sources give about three rounds a minute). Biting and pouring are the hand brought to the place; the fingers' fine work waits for #3420.
