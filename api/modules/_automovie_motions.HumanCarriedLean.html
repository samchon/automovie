    Namespace HumanCarriedLean

    The carrier's lean against the load of a carried person (#3349): a load on the back brings the trunk forward, a load before the chest or pulled from ahead brings it back, so the carrier's and the load's common centre of mass stays over the feet.

    The lean is an additive layer, as breath and affect are: the whole body pitches about the midpoint of its planted feet by the angle, the inverted pendulum whose moment balance the angle solves (HumanLoad.lean), on top of whatever the carrier's own action holds, so a walk keeps its joints and counter-rotation and its feet stay planted. A walk keeps its trunk for itself, so the lean cannot be part of bearing, which is an overlay; the composition schedules the layer over the carry: layers: { carrier: [{ layer: HumanCarriedLean.layer("piggyback"), from: { of: "carry" }, to: { of: "carry", event: "end" }, fade: HumanCarried.TAKE }] }, with carry the anchor of the carrier's action.

    The direction is the mechanics' and the load-carriage literature's: the trunk's forward lean grows with a load carried on the back (Knapik, Harman and Reynolds 1996, "Load carriage using packs", Applied Ergonomics 27, a review). The sizes are provisional: no study of carrying a whole person was read for them. A piggyback leans 20 degrees forward, a load over the shoulder 10; a cradle and the drags lean back 10. The dance lifts have no table angle: their lean is the coupled load's moment balance alone (HumanCarriedCouplingLoad.load), and none without it.

    With the carried person named, bearing performed as its own action tips the carrier by the coupled pair's own balance through tipped (HumanCarriedCouplingLoad.lean), forward and sideways; performed over a walk, which keeps its trunk, the composition schedules that tip as a layer (coupled); the table's layer is left for a carry with no person.

    First pass: the layer's lean is a constant of the carry, coming in over the window's fade; it does not follow the carrier's stride.
