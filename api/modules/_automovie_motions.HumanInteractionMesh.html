    Namespace HumanInteractionMesh

    Adapt the spatial relations of a multi-body key pose to bodies of other proportions by an interaction mesh (Ho, Komura and Tai 2010, "Spatial relationship preserving character motion adaptation", ACM TOG 29(4), article 33): a volumetric mesh over every joint and contact point of all bodies at the key pose records how they lie among each other, and the adapted pose is the one that deforms that mesh least, by its Laplacian coordinates, while each body keeps its own bone lengths and every contact its pairing, two points coinciding or held at a fixed offset.

    • Mesh. The Delaunay tetrahedralization of the points (tetrahedralizeAutoMovieDelaunay, Bowyer-Watson on Qhull's joggled input). Its edges are the points' neighbourhoods.

    • Laplacian coordinates. Each point's offset from the inverse-distance weighted mean of its neighbours, excluding bone edges so deformation does not oppose the bone lengths being changed (Ho et al. 2010, equation 3 and section 5.1; HumanInteractionMeshLaplacian).

    • Solve. A connected set of fixed offsets determines the whole shape up to translation. Without a supplied start, target-length bones in their reference directions give a candidate alongside those offsets. The continuous side test bounds its advance, and every residual and orientation is checked. Other cases minimize the deformation of the Laplacian coordinates with a progressively tightened equality penalty (Nocedal and Wright 2006, §17.1). Each sparse Gauss–Newton model holds the Cartesian terms exactly and linearizes the length of a bone along its current direction. The line search uses the actual energy.

    • Sides. An element's determinant must stay strictly positive. Within the geometric accuracy of collapse, the smooth IPC barrier adds repulsion from the element's reference altitude scale. The projected Gauss–Newton component of the Hessian gives the local quadratic model, and the determinant's cubic in the step fraction limits the line before an element can turn inside out (Li et al. 2020 §§4.2–4.4, technical supplement §§4 and 6).

    Ho et al.'s full text (author upload: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220183715_Spatial_Relationship_Preserving_Character_Motion_Adaptation) uses collision constraints on bounding volumes during iterative morphing. This key-pose adaptation instead keeps every recorded tetrahedron's orientation as a hard domain, with the physical contact accuracy of the whole-body fit (HumanBodyFitMerit.TOLERANCE), stricter than #3438's 10 mm bone and contact acceptance.
