    Namespace AutoMovieMotionHumanAuditBalance

    Whether a body standing on both feet alone keeps its balance: the margin of stability of Hof, Gazendam and Sinke (2005) at every instant where both feet are declared planted on the ground and nothing else bears the body.

    The extrapolated centre of mass is the engine's whole-body centre plus its velocity over the eigenfrequency of an inverted pendulum 1.34 hip heights long (Hof et al.'s length). The velocity is a central difference: over the grid's two neighbouring instants when a whole motion is measured, so the reading resolves no pose the audit's other checks have not, and over 2 * STEP when one instant is asked for alone. The margin is its distance inside the base of support, the convex hull of the outlines of both feet where they bear on the floor (HumanFoot.base, Winter's definition). A person whose extrapolated centre leaves that hull must step or fall, so a motion that keeps both feet planted while it lies outside shows a body no one could hold. While a part other than a foot bears on the ground (a bear window, a knee or a shin, or the plant of a palm laid on the floor), the base is the planted feet's outlines and those parts' contact points (AutoMovieMotionHumanAuditStatics.point), and the instant is read when every open window bears on the ground through the hold (#3425). An instant where a hand grips, a seat or a back bears, or a foot is not planted is not read: the feet are not the only support there. Nor is an instant within the pendulum's own time constant (the square root of its length over gravity, about a third of a second) of a foot leaving the ground or of another support taking the body (the seat a person lowers onto): that body is taking the step Hof et al.'s condition demands, and in steady walking the extrapolated centre does lie ahead of the leading foot through the double support (Hof 2008, Hum Mov Sci 27:112). An instant is read only inside an action whose balance is its content (HumanPhysics.judged: a staged physical outcome, or physics its caller asked for); a posed action on planted feet computes no balance and is not judged by it.
