    Namespace knockdown

    A knockdown: the legs give way and the body goes down away from the blow, sitting onto the floor and falling back to lie supine.

    The collapse is a buckle, not a dive: the knees and hips fold while the pelvis goes back and down behind the feet (as in the stepping-response failures of Maki and McIlroy 1997, where a push too large for one step ends in a fall), the seat meets the floor at 0.45 s, about the free-fall time of the pelvis from its height slowed by the buckling legs, and the trunk rolls back until the back rests on the floor by 1.1 s, the arms trailing. The feet stay where they stood until the seat lands; then the seat stays where it landed and the heels slide out as the legs straighten.

    Each pose rests on the floor without penetrating it: the root's height is solved so the lowest point of the body's own collision shapes touches the floor. The seat and the lie are fitted to the body the same way: the knee at the seat is the one that puts the feet on the floor the seat sits on, and the lying arm's extension and elbow are the ones that lay the arm on the floor beside the body, so any stature sits and lies on the floor exactly. The body goes down away from the blow (the from target), turned so its back falls toward that direction.

    The trunk's recline and the hip's fold at the seat are provisional readings; detailed falls derived from the Euphoria behaviours belong to tier 2 of #3201.
