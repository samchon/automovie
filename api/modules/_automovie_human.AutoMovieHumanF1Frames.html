    Namespace AutoMovieHumanF1Frames

    f1's segment-frame tree in its reference pose: every segment's parent, its frame built from f1's own joint centres and landmarks (AutoMovieHumanF1Joints, AutoMovieHumanF1Landmarks) by the rule written beside it, and the chain of reference rotations that returns it.

    The chain is stored (AutoMovieHumanF1Placing.CHAIN, written from these rules by test/src/reference-conics/FamilyConicRebase.ts --tables) and is the one definition every part is placed by: joints and world read it. The rules here are its written definition: segments applies them to the landmarks, which at the reference pose are themselves read through the stored chain, and a unit test holds that the rules return that chain within rounding, so a rule and the stored chain cannot part unnoticed.

    The tree: pelvis (root) > thorax > head > ear and nose; thorax > breast; thorax > humerus > forearm > hand > each finger's proximal, middle and distal phalanges, and hand > the thumb's metacarpal, proximal and distal phalanges; pelvis > femur > tibia > foot > each toe's phalanges. Pelvis, thorax and head are distinct frames although one trunk family carries all three, so the ear, the nose and the breast have their anatomical parents; they are rigid to each other for now.

    Handedness: every frame is right-handed with X forward, Y by ISB and Z = X x Y, which points along +Z for the stored side, the side whose datums the joint table holds. Under ISB's convention that side plays the right limb (the reference rig's _r joints), while the repository's views label it left; the figure is mirror-symmetric, so this is a naming difference, not geometry. The twin is the reflection in z = 0, AutoMovieHumanSegmentFrame.mirror, whose joint rotations are D R D with D = diag(1, 1, -1).

    Origins: every articulated segment's origin is its proximal joint centre, the joint the chain pivots it about. Where ISB puts its own origin elsewhere (the forearm at US, the tibia at IM, a phalanx midway between its base and head), that is a constant translation inside the segment that changes no axis and no joint angle. Rigid parts keep the origins named in their rules.

    Joints and rigid parts: the articulated joints are the shoulder, the elbow, the wrist, the fingers' and the thumb's, the hip, the knee, the ankle and the toes' (AutoMovieHumanF1Placement.ARTICULATED), and the neutral pose sets those to the identity. The ear, the nose and the breast are rigid parts (AutoMovieHumanF1Placement.RIGID): their rotation says how the part stands on its parent and is a constant of the body, so the neutral pose keeps it.

    Two readings of the reference pose that look unanatomical are the definitions' own, and no frame is tuned to mend them:

    • The elbow reads 27.0 degrees of flexion, while the lines through the joint centres (GH to the elbow centre, the elbow centre to the wrist centre) stand 36.6 degrees apart. ISB's long axes are not those lines: the humerus's runs from the epicondyles' midpoint to GH and the forearm's from the ulnar styloid to that midpoint, and US stands on the wrist's ulnar side, off the wrist centre, so the two ISB axes stand 27.4 degrees apart (FamilyConicFrames.ts).
    • The wrist reads -19.8 degrees of axial rotation. The forearm's Z comes from the plane of the two styloids and the epicondyles' midpoint, the hand's from the line across the knuckles (the fifth metacarpal head to the second), a declared substitute for the third metacarpal's unpalpable sagittal plane; the two substitutes are different lines of the wrist and the hand, and their angle about the long axis is the reading.
