    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanRun

    Running to a floor point, at a jog, a run or a sprint: the walk's footprint plan in a running gait, with a flight phase between stances.

    A run differs from a walk by its duty factor, the share of the stride a foot is on the ground, below one half so both feet leave the floor once a step (Alexander 1984, "The gaits of bipedal and quadrupedal animals"; Novacheck 1998, "The biomechanics of running", Gait & Posture 7): 0.4 at a jog, 0.35 at a run and 0.25 at a sprint, so the swing lasts 2 (1 - duty) step periods. The speed comes from the Froude number, v = sqrt(Fr g l), at 0.6, 1.3 and 5 (about 2.4, 3.5 and 6.8 m/s at a 0.93 m hip). The step frequency is the measured quantity: nearly constant over endurance speeds and rising toward a sprint (Cavagna, Franzetti, Heglund and Willems 1988, J Physiol 399:81), about 172 steps a minute at a recreational runner's preferred 2.9 m/s (Heiderscheit et al. 2011, Med Sci Sports Exerc 43:296). Scaled by the pendulum time sqrt(l / g) (Alexander 1984), it is 0.8 + 0.08 Fr (a provisional fit: 170 steps a minute at 2.9 m/s on a 0.93 m hip), so the longest step is l sqrt(Fr) / (0.8 + 0.08 Fr), about 0.9, 1.3 and 1.9 hip heights, inside the step lengths measured across running speeds (Novacheck 1998). The foot lands on its ball with the heel held FOREFOOT degrees up, the forefoot strike of sprinters (Novacheck 1998) and of runners who strike without an impact transient (Lieberman et al. 2010, Nature 463:531); the angle is provisional. The pelvis rides the spring-mass stance (runStance): highest at contact and toe-off, lowest at mid-stance where the stance knee is most flexed (Blickhan 1989), and a foot in the air asks nothing of it. The swing foot rises 0.15 to 0.35 hip heights as the knee folds in recovery, the elbows hold near 90° and swing with the shoulders, and the trunk inclines forward of a walk's and more as the pace rises (Thorstensson et al. 1984): 5, 8 and 12 degrees for a jog, a run and a sprint (provisional).
