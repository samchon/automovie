    Namespace highJump

    A high jump in the Fosbury flop (#3377): a one-foot take-off, a half turn that puts the back to the bar, a backward lay-out over it with the back arched, the legs lifted after the hips pass, and a landing on the back. The author may name the final support foot of an incoming approach (World Athletics TR27). The foot must already be planted.

    The take-off is the jump's push-off (GymnasticsTakeoff), aimed so the centre of mass peaks at the bar axis plus the authored reserve. It comes down at the landing place at the height it has lying on its back there. The flight is ballistic in the centre of mass (GymnasticsFlight), and its rotation runs through the whole flight from the take-off, as the angular momentum a flop builds during the take-off (Dapena 1980, "Mechanics of rotation in the Fosbury-flop", Med Sci Sports Exerc 12:45-53: most of the somersault's angular momentum is made on the ground) is kept in the air: the half turn about the vertical eased over the whole rise, so the back is to the bar at the apex, and the backward somersault about the body's left axis eased in over the first half of the rise and then at a constant rate, level at the apex and kept to the touchdown. The two overlap from the take-off on; turning them one after the other, each in a fraction of the rise, spun the body upright to inverted in a frame or two. The arch is keyed: the joints go from the take-off's to arched at the apex and to piked, the legs lifted, after it. The body comes down when the lowest of its shapes reaches the landing surface, and lies on its back there (GymnasticsMat).

    The bar is the scene's high jump, a prop whose bar affordance is the crossbar (createHighJump's). Its axis is read where the prop stands at the action's start. The requested apex must exceed the take-off centre and fit within the vertical take-off speed bound RISE. The horizontal take-off speed is not stated: it follows from the landing place the author gives, which world-class finalists reach leaving the floor at 4.14 ± 0.37 m/s (the same table of Nicholson et al. 2022).

    First pass: no curved run-up and no lean into the take-off; the arms do not lead; the angular momentum appears at the take-off instead of being built over the push; the apex falls where the landing place puts it, the crossbar's plane can be reached before or after that apex. The barCrossing event records the centre's passage within the physical span, when present. The push-off's own flight, which aims the take-off, leaves 20 to 24 mm from its take-off pose, so the flight starts from that pose's centre with the vertical speed for the requested apex, and the body does not step at the take-off.

    Events: takeoff, apex, optional barCrossing, touchdown, settled, relax.
