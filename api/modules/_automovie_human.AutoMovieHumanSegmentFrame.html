    Namespace AutoMovieHumanSegmentFrame

    The segment-frame law: a body segment's frame built from its landmarks by a written definition, and the chain that hangs the segments on their parents by one rotation per joint.

    A frame is an origin and three unit axes X, Y, Z, all in the figure's frame (millimetres), right-handed: Z = X x Y. The ISB recommendations write every segment so (Wu et al. 2002, J Biomech 35:543-548; Wu et al. 2005, J Biomech 38:981-992): X anterior, Y along the segment toward its proximal end (cranial for the trunk), Z to the right. The figure keeps one side, the stored side (+Z), and its twin is the reflection in z = 0 (mirror); ISB's right-side definitions read on the stored side.

    A joint's zero is the child's axes parallel to its parent's, the zero of ISB's joint coordinate systems (the anatomical position). With the axes as the columns of a matrix A, a joint's rotation is R = A_parent^T A_child, the child's axes written in its parent's frame, and a pose is one rotation per joint: world composes A_child = A_parent R down the tree, so the reference rotations return the reference frames and identity rotations the neutral body. A segment's origin is the joint centre it pivots about; its place in its parent's frame, A_parent^T (o_child - o_parent), is a constant of the body.
