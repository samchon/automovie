    Namespace crouch

    A crouch or a squat held in place: the hips sink over the planted feet to a knee flexion the depth names, the trunk inclines over the feet, the hands come to rest on the thighs above the knees, the body holds, then stands again.

    The depth is a knee flexion, not a height, so one depth fits every body: partial is the parallel squat of about 100 degrees (Escamilla 2001, Med Sci Sports Exerc 33:127) and full the deep squat at the knee's range end of 150 degrees, inside the 141.6 degrees measured in kneeling (Acker et al. 2011, J Arthroplasty 26:319) and below the 157 +- 6 degrees of a heels-up squat (Hemmerich et al. 2006, J Orthop Res 24:770), which the range table does not reach. The shank tilts forward by the crouching ankle's dorsiflexion (ANKLE, within the range table's), plus the heel lift when the heels are up (the foot pivots about its toes' joint, which stays on the floor), and the thigh takes the rest of the knee flexion, so the hip joint's place over the ankle follows from the leg's two lengths. On the way down the shank and the thigh tilt together, each by the descent's share of its own tilt, so the hips follow the leg's arc and the ankle never passes its range: the heel stays down until the ankle reaches the range and then rises by the rest, the heel-off of a range-limited ankle. A depth whose thigh angle and the hip's flexion range leave no room for the trunk is refused with the deepest knee flexion the heels allow: a full squat with flat heels leaves the thigh past the hip's range at the crouching ankle, so it asks for heels: "up".

    The trunk inclines to the squat's measured key (INCLINE) by the same share as the legs. When the caller opts into balance, the lumbar joint turns the trunk by the nearest angle that brings the centre of mass over the measured soles. One rigid rotation describes the changed mass points. See Hof, Gazendam and Sinke (2005) for the static support criterion. The inclination is shared by the lumbopelvic rhythm: the pelvis tilts about the hips as far as the folded thighs leave the hip room and the spine flexes for the rest, the rounded back of a deep squat (HumanTrunk.incline); the hands rest on the thighs just above the knees, as people support the trunk on the thighs (Kingma, Faber and van Dieen 2016, J Biomech 49:881). The descent takes 1.0 s for a partial squat (half a self-paced repetition, Hannan and King 2022) and that times the square root of the full squat's greater knee travel, about 1.22 s, for a full one (DESCENT) at the reference hip (REFERENCE_HIP) height, scaled by the square root of the hip height (dynamic similarity, Alexander 1984) and by the manner's tempo, the hold hold seconds and the rise as long as the descent. Events: descend at the start, lowest, ascend.
