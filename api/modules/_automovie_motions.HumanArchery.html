    Namespace HumanArchery

    Archery under its own names. Shared grasp and placement laws take the same inert prop from its slot to a moving socket. The release rows can hand it off to a scene-authored animation carrier on a film cue (#3713–3714).

    • takeArrow: a pick up of the arrow by its shaft, from where the composition says it lies (a quiver's slot site).
    • nockArrow: a put down of the arrow's nock onto the bow's nock socket, its shaft on the bow's rest.
    • presentBow: presenting the bow in one hand with the line of aim from the face (HumanWeaponPresent, anchor face), the bow's rest as the working point the line from nock passes through, independently of the rest's surface normal, the bow arm extended to its own reach along the line (extended), as the shot cycle's set-up raises it toward the target.
    • drawBow: an operation of the bow's string slider by the drawing hand, performed over presentBow (HumanChain.over) from the set-up, so the string is drawn while the bow rises and the bow arm extends (the shot cycle's set-up and pre-draw, KSL Shot Cycle); the hand takes the string where the raised bow carries it (operateDrive.IPart.place), and the string's drive moves the nock and, by follows, bends the limbs (#3476).
    • loose: a release of the drawing hand, or a staged hand-off at open and visible string recovery when a continuation is supplied.
    • spanCrossbow: an operation of the crossbow's string (a straight pull with the foot in the stirrup) or its crank, to full travel.
    • presentCrossbow: presenting the crossbow with the cheek anchor, fixed rail mark and separate support-hand contact.
    • shootCrossbow: a staged trigger and string cue with a bolt hand-off at strokeStart when a continuation is supplied.

    The phases are Nishizono et al.'s as Simsek and Ertan cite them (bow hold, drawing, full draw, aiming, release, follow-through); their durations are not yet read at a source and are the shipped laws' own, provisional. A draw tradition (the Mediterranean draw, the thumb draw of a horse bow) is the composition's choice of hand and anchor, not a law.
