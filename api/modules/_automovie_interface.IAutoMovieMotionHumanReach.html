    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanReach

    Whether a limb can put its end on a point, and what happens when it cannot.

    An arm's end is its wrist and its root the shoulder joint; a leg's end is its ankle and its root the hip joint. The end reaches every point no farther from the root than the limb's two segments laid straight (the upper arm and the forearm, the thigh and the shank). A goal beyond that leaves the inverse kinematics with a straight limb that stops short, so a law decides beforehand by one of three policies:

    • refuse: the action is not planned, and the error states the limb, the goal and the shortfall in millimetres; the choice for a contact that must be met (a handshake, a grasp, a strike on a person).
    • lean: the trunk tips forward about the hips by the smallest pitch that brings the shoulder within reach, up to LEAN_LIMIT; the choice for a reach a person would bend into (a table, an offered object). It refuses when that limit is not enough, and for a leg, which has no lean.
    • clamp: the goal moves along its line from the root onto the sphere of the limb's reach; the choice for a goal with no contact (a point in the air, a gesture, a drill without a target).
