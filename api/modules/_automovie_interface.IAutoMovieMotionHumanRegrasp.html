    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanRegrasp

    A held object moved within or with the hand that holds it, without being put down: turned about one of its own axes (a key in a lock, a cap), shifted to another of its grips (a pen turned into a writing grip, a phone from a carry grip to a texting grip), or rolled across the fingers (a coin). Rows name each kind (HumanRegrasp).

    twist: the wrist does the turn and the grip holds. The hand, and the object riding it with the offset stored at closure, rotate together about the object's axis line, an affordance whose translation lies on the axis and whose +X is its direction, so the object turns about its own axis only; the angle is counter-clockwise looking down the axis.

    regrip: the object moves in the still hand from the grip it is held by to the grip a named affordance takes (HumanGrasp.hand, the grip a pick-up of that affordance would close), as the grip window's in-hand motion (IAutoMovieMotionHumanContactWindow.inHand): its origin on a straight line in the hand and its rotation along the shortest arc, both on one minimum-jerk profile.

    roll: the object rolls without slipping from the forefinger's pad to the little finger's across the relaxed hand, turning about the palm's normal by the distance over its radius (the affordance axis's radius).

    This is the first pass the in-hand manipulation issue states for when the literature gives no deterministic finger-gaiting rule that fits (#3447): the digits keep the hand's shape while the object moves, so no digit lifts and replaces; a schedule of digit contacts waits on that rule. A turn beyond the forearm's range would need such regrips between turns.

    Every kind follows a minimum-jerk profile (Flash and Hogan 1985). Its duration is 0.6 s for a quarter turn, a grip change or a roll across the hand (provisional: a search for measured key-turning and in-hand manipulation times found none), a twist's in proportion to its angle. The hand must hold the object at entry, or the act is refused. Event done at the end; the hand keeps the object after.
