    Namespace defend

    A defence against one placed strike: a glove, forearm or shin brought to where the incoming weapon will be, or the head moved off its line.

    When the defence starts comes from the defence outcome law (DefenceOutcome): the defender reacts to the strike's launch after a seeded reaction time, earlier by the anticipation lead the strike's preparation allows (its telegraph), and the author may force the outcome (in time, late or fail), which the law realises with plausible timing. Where it goes comes from what the defender sees at that start: the attacker's moving limb (the hand or foot whose velocity points most toward the struck region) and the line from it to the region on the defender's own body. Nothing about contact is decided here: the strike stops on the first surface it meets, so an in-time cover is struck on the glove, a late one is struck through, and a slip that moved the head off the line in time is missed (the strike's tracking cannot follow a move that starts within one correction latency of its contact).

    Kinds (IBA coaching manual pp. 57 to 67; kickboxing research of #3201, defence table):

    • cover: the glove on the attacked side over the attacked head region, palm toward the incoming line, elbow down (both gloves for a central region).
    • parry: the glove opposite the punching hand meets the fist on the line a forearm's length ahead of the region and turns it aside.
    • elbowBlock: the elbow on the attacked side dropped over the attacked body region, the glove kept at the jaw.
    • highBlock: the forearm on the attacked side braced beside the head, the glove above the temple.
    • slip: the head moved off the line by its own radius, the fist's and a margin, through the knees and a trunk lean, outside the punching arm.
    • roll: the head dropped under the line by two head radii through bent knees and a forward lean, then up.
    • sway: the upper body leaned back over the rear foot until the head clears the line.
    • check: the leg on the attacked side lifted, knee up and out, shin turned into the kick, the foot dorsiflexed (the toes-up variant).

    The defence holds until just after the strike's contact and returns to the guard over one hand recovery. The geometry itself is built by defendGeometry on the first posture after the read.
