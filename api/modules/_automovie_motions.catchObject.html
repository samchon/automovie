    Namespace catchObject

    Catching an object thrown by another action: the hands meet it where its path enters the reach, give with it, and bring it to the chest.

    The goal is an interception. The throw's plan carries its launch (throwObject.of), so the path is known in closed form, drag-free. The catch point is where the path first comes within REACH arm lengths ahead of the catcher's root along its heading (provisional), and the contact instant the path's time there; a path that never comes so near, or a point the arms cannot reach from the shoulders, is refused. With two hands the palms take the object from both sides, each palm on its surface across the catcher's left-right axis; with one the palm takes it from behind, facing the way it comes, or with a worn glove (glove) the hand turned as that palm would be and moved so the glove's pocket meets it. The fingers take the turn about the palm's normal (ROLLS, upward first) the arm holds at the contact with the least strain (AutoMovieMotionHumanBend.strain), so the wrist's range decides it. The ball's size is the ball's own (ball, HumanBall). The hands travel there on minimum-jerk progress over the aimed movement time to an object of its size (McIntosh et al. 2018, AutoMovieMotionHumanTiming.aimed), arriving at contact; the anticipatory prepare instant is 100 ms before impact (Lacquaniti and Maioli 1989, J Neurosci 9:134). After contact the hands give GIVE along the object's velocity over GIVE_TIME (the issue's 0.05 to 0.15 m, provisional), which is secured, then carry it to before the sternum and hold it. Events: reach, prepare, contact, secured, held.

    Not yet: hand closure timed by optical expansion (Savelsbergh et al. 1991), a running catch (McLeod and Dienes 1996), a fumble, the catch against the chest, and a held prop handed from the throw.
