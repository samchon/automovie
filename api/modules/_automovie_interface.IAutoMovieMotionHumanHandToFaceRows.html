    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanHandToFaceRows

    A hand-to-face task of daily living while standing: drinking, sipping, eating with a spoon, biting held food, a phone at the ear, reading a held sheet, brushing the teeth, combing the hair, washing the face and wiping the mouth (M14, #3234).

    Every task takes the drinking task's phases (Alt Murphy et al. 2006, 2011): reach and forward transport to the face, the task at the face, back transport and return. Each is a row of the touch generator (AutoMovieMotionHumanTouch) on a region of the performer's own head: the effector is the tip of the implied tool (a cup's rim, a spoon's bowl, a brush's head, a phone's face) stated in the hand's axes as shares of the hand's length, or a bare palm, so the task is performed with no prop and the hand stands where the tool would put it. A grooming task repeats its stroke at the face (rub kinds); eating brings the head forward to meet the spoon (van der Kamp and Steenbergen 1999), drinking tips the head back only once the vessel is at the lips, in the drinking phase that follows the transport (Alt Murphy et al. 2011), reading flexes the head by 35 degrees, inside the 33 to 45 degrees of Lee et al. 2015, and holds the sheet 36.2 cm from the eyes (Bababekova et al. 2011, the mean for text), on the line pitched down by the head's flexion and 15 degrees of the eyes (provisional). Tool lengths, the head's shares and the sip's extension are provisional.

    Events: the phrase instants, with atMouth (or atEar, atFace, atSheet) at ready and taskEnd at strokeEnd. The back transport may be cut into a put-down or standing from relax.

    With prop, the task is performed with the prop the hand already holds (a glass a pickUp took): the prop's own point, a vessel's lip or a phone's screen, replaces the implied tool (HumanHeld), the fingers keep their grip, and the hand holds the prop through the task and after it. A prop that is not in the hand is refused. With the grasp the prop was taken up by for its use, the hand holds it at the face as that grasp presents it there (HumanHeldUse), so the task asks the wrist only what the grip was chosen for; with other as well, the other hand takes the prop's other handle where the first carries it, as a camera is held in both hands.
