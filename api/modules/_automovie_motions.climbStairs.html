    Namespace climbStairs

    Climbing a flight of stairs up or down from standing before its first nosing, step over step (one foot per tread) or step by step (both feet on each tread), ending with both feet on the last tread.

    The flight lies ahead along the heading: its first nosing start metres ahead of the toes, count treads of riser and tread metres, rising for up and falling below the floor for down. The goals are footprints: each foot lands with the middle of its sole on the middle of its tread, at its standing lateral place; a sole too long to keep EDGE from both edges there hangs its heel over the nosing ascending and its toes over the next one descending, as a long foot stands on a short tread, and on the landing stands EDGE past the last nosing. A swinging foot takes the shortest path over the flight that keeps every point of its sole above the surface under it and CLEARANCE above each edge it passes (AutoMovieMotionHumanClearance): ascending it lifts beside the riser and follows the line of the nosings, descending it moves forward and lowers once its heel is past the nosing. The pelvis moves between the stances on minimum-jerk progress over the middle of the two feet, half a step behind them over the floor (climbStairsPelvis). Its height at a stance is the highest from which both legs reach their feet, so the lower foot's leg is straight and the upper one's bent; it changes while a foot swings and holds in double support, and is lowered wherever either leg, planted or swinging, could not reach its foot.

    The trunk inclines forward by ASCENT_LEAN going up and by a walk's inclination going down, shared between the pelvis and the spine by the lumbopelvic rhythm and tipped about the hips, since the trunk flexes forward more in stair ascent than in level walking (McFadyen and Winter 1988, J Biomech 21:733).

    The cadence is 90 steps a minute (Mian, Narici, Minetti and Baltzopoulos 2007, Gait Posture 26:463), scaled by the square root of the hip height (Alexander 1984) and by the manner's tempo; each swing takes 0.8 of a step and the double support the rest (provisional). Risers from 100 to 180 mm and treads from 280 mm are the 2010 ADA ranges (504.2), the defaults 170 and 280 mm; a riser above 0.35 of the hip height or a tread shorter than 0.6 of the foot is refused with the body's feasible range (provisional limits). Events: toeOff<n> and treadContact<n> per step, numbered from 1. A foot on a tread bears on the prop on names, or on the fixed floor frame when no prop is named; a foot on the floor before the flight bears on the ground. The treads repeat the settled stance's flat feet, while the feet leave the floor from where the entry put them.

    Not yet: handrail use (no bar affordance exists, A8), landing turns, ramps and slopes, which belong to the walk on a sloped support.
