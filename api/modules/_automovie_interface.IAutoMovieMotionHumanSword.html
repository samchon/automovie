    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanSword

    Blade work with the blade as the effector: the cut on the eight lines, and what every blade action shares. HumanSwordThrust holds the thrust, HumanSwordGuard the guards and HumanSwordParry the parry.

    The blade is a prop the hand already holds (HumanObject.prop of a sword, then pickUp by its grip); the law reads its point and edge from the prop's point and edge affordances as the hand holds them and aims those. With no blade named the hand itself is aimed, and with no target a place at the blade's reach ahead stands in, so every action also plays as a solo drill.

    Cuts are named by the side they come from (Meyer's cutting diagram, 1570: descending, rising, horizontal and vertical lines; #3202, decision 8). HumanBlade names rows of these in a tradition's words.

    A cut or a thrust that a parry is to meet is declared parried: it stops short of its target at its decisive instant, where the parry's blade meets it. The outcome is the author's, never the geometry's.

    First pass: the target is read once, at the start; a body does not stop the blade; binds, winds and redirection are not planned; every pose constant is provisional, from the treatises' figures.
