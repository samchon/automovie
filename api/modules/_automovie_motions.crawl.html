    Namespace crawl

    Crawling on hands and knees along the heading, from standing: the body gets down onto hands and knees, then crawls a number of cycles in the diagonal pattern, each hand moving with the opposite knee like a quadruped's trot (Patrick, Noah and Yang 2009, J Neurophysiol 101:603).

    The goals are floor points. The knees rest a shank's radius over the floor under the hip joints with the thighs upright, the shins lying back to feet tucked on their toes (kneelKeys.tucked); the palms rest flat with each wrist under its shoulder and the arm at ARM of its length, so the trunk's pitch is the one that puts the posed shoulders at that height. The pelvis travels at a constant speed of one stride a cycle, as high as each thigh reaches its knee from it; each limb is planted on a floor point for DUTY of the cycle, centred on its own share, and swings to the next one a stride ahead on an arc, a hand LIFT high and a knee KNEE_LIFT, rising on the swing's time and travelling on its minimum-jerk progress so it leaves the floor before it moves, the left hand and the right knee half a cycle apart from the right hand and the left knee. Each hip joint rides a thigh over its own knee, so a swinging knee is lifted by the pelvis turning about the spine's axis (the hip hike of a quadruped's swing), which the spine and the chest turn back so the shoulders stay level over the hands. The crawl is placed so each tucked foot's toes' joint is where the standing foot rolls onto its toes, and the limbs land side by side, the first steps reaching each limb's own footprint. Getting down from standing, the supports take the body in turn (crawlDown): the feet roll onto their toes where they stood, the body sits onto its knees in the kneel's time to lower and sit back (kneel.PHASES), leans to its hands and comes forward over its knees. Entered on the knees or crouched (a crawl ends kneeling, a bear crawl crouched, and declares it), it blends from the displayed entry over DOWN seconds instead, and any other base is refused (floorEntry). The cycle, the stride, the duty and the lift are provisional, scaled by the square root of the hip height and the manner's tempo. Events: down (on hands and knees), and per limb and cycle <limb>Liftoff<n> and <limb>Touchdown<n>, the limb one of leftHand, rightHand, leftKnee, rightKnee.

    The bear crawl is the same gait on the hands and the feet: the knees are held HOVER off the floor, so the hips ride that much higher, and each foot, on its ball behind its knee, is planted while its knee is. The inchworm is its own walk (crawlInchworm).

    Each knee on the floor bears through a window of its lower leg at the knee's region while its foot does.

    Not yet: the bear crawl's own timing (MacLellan et al. 2012 measured hands-and-feet crawling; the knees' cycle is kept), the belly crawl, turning and rising from the crawl.
