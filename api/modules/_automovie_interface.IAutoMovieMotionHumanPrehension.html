    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanPrehension

    Reach to grasp as two coupled components, the model of prehension since Jeannerod (1981, 1984): the transport carries the hand to the object and the grasp shapes the hand, opening it to a peak aperture during the transport and closing it on the object as the transport ends. Every number here belongs to the reaching and grasping family; none is borrowed from striking or locomotion.

    • Movement time. McIntosh, Mon-Williams and Tresilian (2018, J. Exp. Psychol. Hum. Percept. Perform. 44:1022, table 1) found reach-to-grasp time poorly fitted by Fitts' law and well fitted by a two-factor model, MT = a + b lg A + c W for the grasped width, with the group fit a = -306.07 ms, b = 297.27 ms per decade of amplitude A in millimetres and c = 0.65 ms per millimetre of width W (r^2 = 0.97). Amplitudes below the study's shortest (170 mm) take its value, since the fit is not valid there; skill varies the duration through the cross-task timing variability (HumanSkill.jitter), no study of reaching speed by proficiency having been located.
    • Peak aperture. It scales with the object's width with a slope of about 0.8 (Smeets and Brenner 1999, Motor Control 3:237, as cited by Utz et al. 2015); the intercept, 25 mm, is provisional (the full regression was not reachable): it keeps the peak 5 mm above the widest graspable object, about 100 mm (McIntosh et al. 2018's hypothetical maximum grip).
    • Peak timing. Aperture peaks at 60 to 70 % of the transport (Jeannerod 1984, J. Mot. Behav. 16:235); a seeded draw places it inside that band.

    The transport path superposes two minimum-jerk units (Flash and Hogan 1985; Flash and Henis 1991): the first carries the wrist from where it is to an approach point set back from the grasp along the object's approach axis, the second, overlapping its end, carries it the last stretch along that axis onto the grasp, so the hand arrives moving along the approach and never through the object, as the deceleration phase of a reach to grasp is the longer one (McIntosh 2018, time after peak speed). Both units end at rest, so the wrist stops exactly on the grasp with zero velocity and acceleration. The hand turns to its grasp rotation on a minimum-jerk slerp finished by the second unit's start, so it is oriented before the final approach.
