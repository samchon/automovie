    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanFoot

    A foot on the floor as a rigid rocker: where its ankle and its sole points are when it pivots about its heel or its ball by a pitch, at a heading.

    Gait analysis describes stance as three rockers (Perry and Burnfield, Gait Analysis, 2nd ed.): the heel rocker, where the foot rolls down about the heel after initial contact; the ankle rocker, foot flat while the tibia advances; and the forefoot rocker, where the heel rises and the foot turns about the metatarsal heads until toe-off. Each is a rotation of the rigid foot about a floor point, so the contact point stays fixed while the ankle moves, which is what keeps a planted foot from sliding.

    The foot's geometry is read from the body's own sockets: the heel and ball points relative to the ankle joint at rest, and mtp, the toes' joint, the metatarsophalangeal axis the forefoot rocker turns about with the toes flat (Perry and Burnfield), above the ball's floor point. A socket riding the toes bone (the toe tip, when the profile jointed the toes) bends at the ball by the toes' flexion: through the forefoot rocker the toes extend at the metatarsophalangeal joints and stay flat on the floor while the heel rises (Perry and Burnfield), so a walk extends them by the rocker's angle. pitch is in degrees about the foot's lateral axis, positive lifting the toes; toes is the toes' flexion from the foot in degrees, positive lifting them; heading is the foot's yaw about +Y in degrees (0 faces +Z).
