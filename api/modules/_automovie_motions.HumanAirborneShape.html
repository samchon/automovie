    Namespace HumanAirborneShape

    The body shapes held in flight (#3428): tuck, pike, layout, straddle and the twisting arm sets, each a layer laid over a jump's flight window, so the flight pass (HumanPerformanceFlight) reads the composed shape and turns the body by it: a tuck turns faster than a layout by the ratio of their moments about the somersault axis.

    Each shape holds clinical joint angles for the channels it claims, set over what the flight holds there; a goal the flight holds on a limb the shape claims is moved to where the shape's joints put that limb's end joint, since a held tuck is a joint shape and not a foot placed somewhere, and a layer's weight moves a goal both postures hold but keeps one only the base holds until the full weight drops it at once, which would snap the limb mid-air. The positions are the gymnastic and diving position names (tuck: hips and knees bent; pike: hips bent, knees straight; layout: straight; straddle: legs apart), with angles that are provisional: no rulebook or kinematic study was read for them.

    Schedule a shape over the flight as a windowed layer, from the jump's takeoff to its touchdown.
