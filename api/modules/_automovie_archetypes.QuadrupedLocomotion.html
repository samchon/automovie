    Namespace QuadrupedLocomotion

    Explicit authored clocks for locomotion absent from the measured walk tables.

    A clock is supplied in metres per second and seconds. No missing biological period, stance, spread or speed band is inferred. Spread is authored zero, making these deterministic construction clocks, not zero-variance samples. The species' measured walk remains unchanged; faster gaits borrow its named construction pose ranges, not measured faster-gait joint kinematics.

    Dagg (1974), doi:10.1111/j.1469-7998.1974.tb03144.x, distinguishes the camel's lateral walk from its running pace. Here a pace is constructed with simultaneous ipsilateral contacts and opposite sides half a stride apart. Dagg (1967), Gaits and Their Development in the Infraorder Pecora, pp.65–66, observed sika trot and rotary gallop. The trot uses coincident diagonals; the rotary gallop uses authored quarter-stride intervals, with opposite leading fore/hind sides. These fractions are constructions, not measured full-phase statistics. Their durations always come from the caller.

    Each faster gait's first authored speed is its automatic change boundary, not a claimed biological transition. Absolute scaling preserves all clocks. Admission makes a private numerical snapshot for a ride; no species registry, model identity, motion law or foreign function is modified.
