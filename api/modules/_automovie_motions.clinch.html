    Namespace clinch

    Taking a clinch grip on a partner standing within reach and holding it: the two-handed neck tie of Muay Thai (plum), or judo's collar and sleeve grip (collarSleeve, kumikata).

    The goals are the partner's regions, read on the partner's displayed body at every second, so the grip follows the partner. In the plum both palms lie on the back of the partner's neck (the nape), one each side of it (SPREAD of the half shoulder width, provisional); in the collar and sleeve grip the right hand closes on the partner's left collar (neckLeft) and the left hand on the partner's right sleeve at the elbow (elbowRight), mirrored for a left-handed grip. Each hand reaches its region in the aimed movement time to a 5 cm grip (McIntosh et al. 2018), holds hold seconds and lets go. The feet stay planted. Events: the phrase's ready (grip, both hands closed), strokeStart, stroke, strokeEnd, relax (release).

    With under the clinch is a throw's entry (tsukuri): the body lowers and moves across the floor through the reach until its fulcrum region stands at the partner's region that lies on it, the contact the throw turns over (#3439), and at the height of the partner's centre of mass, so the fulcrum is under the body it turns; the throw itself is the partner's perturb under the pull.

    Not yet: pummelling, underhooks and overhooks, and the turn-in's own footwork (Koshida et al. 2016).
