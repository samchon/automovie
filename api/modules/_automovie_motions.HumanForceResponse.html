    Namespace HumanForceResponse

    A partner's response to a pull through grips (#3439), the outcomes of perturb a sustained force decides: resist, yield and thrown.

    The pull is the horizontal sum of the sustained forces on the performer open at the action's start (IHumanActionContext.forces), lasting until the first of them ends; when none is open at the start, the first to open within the action is answered from its onset, the body standing until then, as a pull through grips begins only once they close (the grip, kumikata, comes before kuzushi, tsukuri and kake: Kano, Kodokan Judo, Kodansha 1986). Its mechanics are HumanForceExchange's:

    • resist: the body leans away from the pull about the ankles until its centre has moved back by the moment balance's shift, over the body's own time constant 1 / ω on a minimum-jerk profile, and holds for the pull. A pull whose shift passes the base behind is refused with the force range that can be resisted.
    • yield: the body tips along the pull about the ankles as its centre travels by the linear inverted pendulum under the pull, until its extrapolated centre leaves the base (kuzushi, the plan's end). A pull that ends first is refused with the force that breaks the base within it.
    • thrown: the yield to kuzushi, then the body, frozen in that pose, turns about the thrower's fulcrum (fulcrum, a region of by) by the swing's law from the momentum the pull left, leaves it at release where its centre comes level with the fulcrum, and flies, its centre on the parabola from the release and its orientation from the conserved angular momentum (#3426, integrateAirborneRotation), until its lowest joint reaches the floor (land). A throw with no fulcrum, or whose swing stops before the vertical, is refused. The composition cuts into the fall or the breakfall at land.

    The centre moves as standing balance moves it with the feet side by side (Winter 1995): along the facing by the whole body's tip about the ankles, across it by the pelvis carried sideways over the planted legs.

    First pass: the grip sites are not held to the puller's hands by a root solve while the body tips, and the body is rigid through the swing and the flight.
