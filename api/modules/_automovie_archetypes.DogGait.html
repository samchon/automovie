    Namespace DogGait

    Provisional canine timing with measured walk data and faster point prototypes.

    Light et al. 2010, AJVR 71:997, doi:10.2460/ajvr.71.9.997, measured 56 healthy Labradors walking at 0.6–0.9 m/s: stride 1.11 ± 0.13 s and left-fore stance 0.62 s and hind stance 0.56 s. Both rows express one pooled interval; they do not assert speed-stratified measurements or a fitted speed response. The Labrador timing is provisional for these Husky prototypes. No reference height was read for those Labradors, so absolute speeds/times are preserved.

    Griffin, Main and Farley 2004, JEB 207:3545, doi:10.1242/jeb.01177, report fore contact 15% of a cycle after ipsilateral hind contact in their dogs. Rebased to left fore zero, that gives phases LF=0, RH=.35, RF=.5, LH=.85. This additional cohort is not a claim of paired Husky gait measurements. The shared cycle retains the LF stride mean; the paper's separately averaged hind stride mean and duty percentages are not asserted to be identical.

    The existing horse walk's fold, clearance, body oscillation and top-joint follow remain explicitly borrowed construction kinematics, not measured canine motion. The neck remains unanimated because this table supplies no measured canine nod; zero declares no animation, not measured zero motion. Choi et al. 2021, Sensors 21:6366, Table 1 (doi:10.3390/s21196366), observed one American Akita by high-speed video: trot at 2.69 m/s has mean fore support/swing 0.23/0.34 s and hind support 0.21 s; gallop at 4.34 m/s has fore support/swing 0.14/0.31 s and hind support 0.16 s. Their sums 0.57/0.45 s are representative common fore cycles, not directly reported stride means or measured stride SDs. Hind support+swing separately sums 0.55/0.48 s. Faster rows therefore declare zero authored variation, not a zero measured standard deviation. A singleton speed is not expanded to an unobserved band. The experiment is provisional for all three Husky morphometric cohorts.

    Trot uses synchronized diagonals, also observed in six retrievers by Catavitello et al. 2015, PLOS ONE 10:e0133936. The gallop's complete phase construction is Choi's desired transverse robot reference, not measured canine phases: swap its right-lead sides and rebase the new left fore to 0. The inclusive authored switching speeds 2.69/4.34 are the faster tables' first available points, not measured preferred transitions. Canter remains unavailable. Borrowed horse fold/clearance/bob remain explicit construction proxies for every gait.
