    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanThrowObjectLever

    The throw's lever: the arm from the shoulder to the hand, lengthened by an implement the hand holds (a sling, an atlatl), turning as one rigid lever at the release (#3490).

    At the release the arm and the implement move as one rigid body: a velocity shared by every point along the lever from the shoulder to the working point, plus a turn about the shoulder, v(p) = u + w x (p - s). The implement keeps the grip the hand holds it by, so its working point lies where the release's hand rotation puts it (no wrist turn beyond its range is asked), and the launch is aimed from there. The turn is the least one that carries the launch velocity's part square to the line from the shoulder to the working point, w = (r x v) / |r|^2, and the shared velocity its part along that line, so the working point leaves with the launch velocity while the hand, nearer the shoulder, moves only as fast as the lever there: the implement's gain. Through the carry the hand turns at w. With no implement the working point is the grip, and the hand carries the launch velocity unturned, the throw as it was.

    A thrown prop keeps the lever's angular velocity in flight, so a knife's spin (ITurns) is the lever's rate, rounded by the act to the nearest whole number of half turns over the flight to the target, at least one, so the stand-off decides how the point arrives (spin).
