    Namespace pickUp

    Reaching for a prop's grip, grasping it and lifting it clear.

    The goal is the grip, not the arm: the hand's grip socket is brought onto the prop's grip affordance along its approach axis (HumanGrasp), taken as the archetype declares it: untilted, and from the side about the grip's axis the arm reaches with the least wrist error among the sides its declared symmetry allows (any side of a round grip, either of a half-symmetric one, the declared one otherwise; pickUpSearch.TURNS); a use's grasp is searched over its own turns and tilts. Then the fingers open to the peak aperture on the way and close on the prop's colliders until contact (HumanGrip), and inverse kinematics solves the arm. Where the arm reaches the grip from the posture the action is entered in, the rest of the body holds that posture, so a pick up continues a stand or the deepest point of a bend with its feet planted, and only the reaching arm and its fingers move. Where it does not, a standing body is lowered toward the grip over the reach, as the bend law lowers it (pickUpPosture): a slight lean at a counter, a stoop or a squat for a low one, the feet planted; a seated one leans from its seat; and where the arm still misses, the trunk turns toward the grip first and a standing body then takes one adjustment step (pickUpAdjust). The lift is the whole body's: the trunk or the stance carries what the arm cannot, and where neither does, the step is taken for the grip and the lifted place together (pickUpStance). A body that moved for the grip or the lift straightens after it over as long as the reach, the hand riding with the shoulder, and the event upright closes the action.

    Phases:

    1. Reach: the reach-to-grasp transport (HumanPrehension): the two-factor movement time of McIntosh et al. (2018) for the wrist's travel and the grasped width, with the performer's timing variability; the hand comes in the last stretch along the approach axis from a setback equal to the peak aperture, so the open hand clears the object, and the aperture peaks inside Jeannerod's 60 to 70 % band.
    2. Load: the hand holds for 0.15 s with the fingers closed, the preload and load phases that precede lift-off in a precision lift (Johansson and Westling 1984, Exp. Brain Res. 56:550); the duration is provisional, as the phase durations themselves were not reached.
    3. Lift: the wrist rises lift metres (0.1 m when absent, provisional: about a hand's breadth clear of the support, or 0 when the other hand already holds it) on a minimum-jerk profile timed by the same movement-time law. Where the arm alone cannot bring the wrist to its lifted place, the trunk inclines toward it, or a lowered body straightens part of the way, over the lift (pickUpLift.raise); a lift the planned hand still falls short of by more than the arm solver's place tolerance is refused on lift (pickUpLift.judged).

    The hand grips the prop from gripClosed to the end, which attaches the prop to the hand (HumanPerformance.prop), so the prop rides the lift, and the plan declares a hold from gripClosed, so the hand keeps the prop with its closed fingers through later actions (carried with the trunk while a walk or a turn drives the body) until a put down or a hand over takes it. A second hand steadies a prop the other already carries; it takes the grip without a lift, and an independently requested lift is refused on lift.

    Events: gripClosed, liftOff, lifted, and upright after a body lowered for the grip or inclined for the lift straightens. A pick up cut into a stand before gripClosed abandons the reach; after lifted it continues into a stand, a walk, a put down or a hand over. A grip no lowering brings within the arm, or one beyond the arm of a body that is not standing, is refused with the step named.
