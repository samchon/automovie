    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanWorkoutLoad

    The load a workout is done with: a prop the performer already holds (a barbell, a dumbbell, a kettlebell taken by pickUp), carried through the exercise's keys instead of being refused.

    The hand that holds keeps its grip: the plan declares the grip and leaves the hold as it found it, so the prop rides that hand. A prop held in one hand asks no more. A prop with a second handle (a bar) is laid level: the holding hand stays where the key's arm puts it and is turned so the line to the other handle runs to the performer's other side with the knuckles continuing the forearm, and the other hand is laid on that handle by the arm's solve, as a long gun's support hand is.

    A lift whose kind says so (WorkoutKind.midfoot) carries the bar's middle over the middle of the feet, so the bar of a deadlift or a press travels in a vertical line over it. The rule is provisional: it is the coaching rule of the Starting Strength method, whose own pages refused this session's reading, and the one measurement read is narrower. Hancock, Wyatt and Kilgore, "Variation in Barbell Position Relative to Shoulder and Foot Anatomical Landmarks Alters Movement Efficiency", International Journal of Exercise Science 5(3):183-195, 2012 (https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijes/vol5/iss3/1), abstract: six weightlifters' deadlifts started with the bar over the navicular (the mid-foot) moved it 37.5 +/- 13.7 mm horizontally backward, against 66.7 +/- 12.9 mm when started over the metatarsophalangeal joint; a vertical path is the limit that start approaches, and no source was read for the press. The holding hand is swung about its shoulder at the arm's own reach, forward or back and to either side, by what the key's arms leave the bar's middle off that line, so the bar hangs centred between the hands and each handle is as far out of its own shoulder; the mid-foot is the middle between the heel and the toe of each foot the entry stands on.

    First pass: the load weighs nothing in the motion (no lean, no slower tempo, no bar path round the knees); a bar is kept level across the body whatever the key, so a one-sided or turned carry is not written; the holding hand's turn about the bar is the straight wrist's, whatever the lift (no hook or mixed grip).
