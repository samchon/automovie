    Namespace HumanVerdicts

    The verdict report of a performance: every physical bound that holds for any human motion whatever it means to do, read on the film a composition gives, with each finding placed on the performer and the action it happens in.

    A composition that plans is not yet a composition that performs well: a hand may pass through a hip, a foot may skate. A production checks its own scene with this report before it looks at a single frame, and a scene is accepted only when the report is clean, a cited signature value holds (HumanSignature) and the stills show no flaw.

    const report = HumanVerdicts.of(performance);
    if (!report.clean) throw new Error(HumanVerdicts.describe(report));

    The bounds, the ones the vocabulary's own sweep holds every action to:

    • timing: every declared contact window and event is well formed.
    • tangle: no part of a body passes more than 10 mm into another part of it (the skin's give where a hand rests on the body).
    • floor, slip, lift: nothing sinks under the floor, and a planted foot neither slides nor leaves it, each within 5 mm.
    • jump, reversal, speed: every joint centre travels a continuous path with no out-and-back spike and no impossible speed.
    • balance: while both feet alone bear the body, its extrapolated centre of mass stays over them.
    • skate, loose, afloat: a foot the motion shows on the floor does not skate, and a declared plant is neither loose nor afloat.
    • statics: while a held body's bearing contacts all declare their load, the load-weighted contact centre lies under the centre of mass within 2 % of stature (AutoMovieMotionHumanAuditStatics).
    • range: every joint stays within its range of motion, within ROM_TOLERANCE (0.01 degrees).
    • unloaded: a foot no plant on the ground holds dorsiflexes no further than an ankle bearing no weight does (ANKLE_DORSIFLEXION.unloaded), within ROM_TOLERANCE.
    • turn: no bone spins more than 5 turns a second between frames.
    • partner: two people do not pass more than 10 mm into each other.

    A performer who swims or climbs a ladder is not carried by the floor, so the floor, plant and balance bounds are left unread for that performer.
