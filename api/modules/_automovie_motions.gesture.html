    Namespace gesture

    A communicative gesture while standing: the hand, head or trunk moves through Kendon's phases (Kendon 1980; McNeill 1992, Hand and Mind): preparation from rest to the stroke's start, the stroke that carries the meaning, an optional post-stroke hold, and retraction to rest.

    The feet stay planted and the body stands as the stand law places it; performed over a carrier (a walk, a seat, a ride) the body is the carrier's, instant by instant, and the gesture takes only the parts it moves. Each moving hand is an effector goal (gestureStroke), solved by inverse kinematics with a natural elbow hint widened by the gesture and the Effort; the moving arms are planned over the whole gesture as one trajectory (gestureHands.planned): from what the entry left to the stroke's goals in the preparation, on them through the stroke, and back to the relaxed hanging arm's in the retraction, while the fingers take the gesture's shape and leave it on the same share. Head, shoulder and trunk gestures add their joint angles under an envelope that rises in the preparation and falls in the retraction; a bow pitches the whole trunk about the hips by its depth (15, 30 or 45 degrees are the conventional light, ordinary and deep bows) while the hips move back to keep the body balanced over the feet (HumanBalance.shift).

    Transport time is the free-space time of the distance moved (AutoMovieMotionHumanTiming.transport), and at least the time each moving arm's arc takes within the audit's travel bound (gestureHands.reaching), 0.3 s for head and shoulder gestures (no source found), and for a bow its depth at the measured rates of standing bows, down in 1.21 s and up in 1.38 s for 60 degrees, held 0.95 s at the deepest (Kawabata et al. 2009, ISBS 27). The Effort's tempo scales every phase, its extent the gesture's size and its elbow term the arm's width (HumanEffort).

    The plan names the hands it moves (hands), so a gesture with a hand that holds a prop is refused, naming the free hand.

    Events: prepare, strokeStart, strokeEnd, retract. A gesture may be cut into another during its retraction, making a gesture unit, and into standing or moving before its stroke or after it; its stroke is never cut, since a cut stroke loses the gesture's meaning.
