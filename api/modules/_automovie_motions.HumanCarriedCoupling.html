    Namespace HumanCarriedCoupling

    The coupling of a named carry (#3437): the meeting kind whose borne role rests on the bearer by several contacts with load shares, whose borne mass and centre enter the bearer's balance, and the per-instant solve that puts the borne body on the bearer's displayed regions.

    • Key pose. The carry's posture of the borne body (its heading, tip and leg angles, HumanCarried.CARRIES) placed with its resting region on the bearer's region, and turned about that point so its held regions come nearest the places the row's holds put the bearer's hands; the borne legs and a lone bearer's stance (trunk inclination and knee lowering) are the carry's own and upright changed least so every hold lies within its arm, a hold beyond it refused (HumanCarriedCouplingFit.fit); it is a pure function of the two bodies and the carry, so the record is the same whichever role plans first and wherever the pair stands.

    • Floor. A drag (HumanCarriedDrag) also rests the dragged body's lowest region on the floor (role ground), which takes its share of the weight, so a dragger carries only what its contacts bear.

    • Contacts. The resting region on the bearer's region, unless a lift holds it at the arms' reach above (IRow.rise), where it touches nothing; each bearer hand's palm under the borne region it holds (HOLDS), both bearing; and each clinging hand on the bearer's region it clings to, a grip.

    • Load shares. The bearing contacts' shares of the borne weight are the least-norm shares whose weighted centre lies under the borne centre of mass, the vertical reactions of a body at rest on point supports (static equilibrium of forces and of the moments about the vertical's two horizontal axes), none of them a pull.

    • Borne body per instant. The borne key pose rides the bone its resting region lies on (the anchor), a parent constraint at the key offset, as a held object keeps its offset in the hand: the resting region stays on its region however the bearer's trunk turns and bends. The bearer's hands reach the held regions in the same anchor frame (HumanCarriedCouplingPose.holds). It is per instant, with no state, so it cannot lag a turning bearer.

    • Capacity. The borne mass is refused above the mass whose moment about the bearer's base exceeds the bearer's own at a right angle, m d <= M h, the bound beyond which no lean balances the pair (HumanLoad.lean). No study of person-carrying capacity was found (a search on casualty-carry capacity relative to the rescuer's mass returned none), so no tighter per-carry bound is set.

    • Two helpers. A carry with another side (PAIRED: a supported walk) takes a second helper as the role second, standing where its side's region meets the borne body's on that side; the borne body rests on both, each helper bearing its contacts' share.

    First pass: the key pose is the carry's table posture with its legs and the bearer's stance fitted, not a whole-body fit of both bodies (HumanBodyFit); the borne centre is read with the arms at their clinical zero. The bearer tips by the pair's balance forward and sideways (HumanCarriedCouplingLoad.lean); the capacity reads the moment along the facing alone (HumanLoad.lean).
