    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanJump

    A jump to a floor point: a push-off from a planted foot, a ballistic flight and a two-footed landing that absorbs and rises to standing.

    The push-off starts from the entry exactly, so a walk cut while a stance foot is planted continues without a jump in position or velocity: the root follows a quintic Hermite path from the entry root and velocity to the take-off root and velocity over PUSH seconds. The take-off foot is the planted one (the front one in the entered body's own frame when both are, the rear being about to leave), held on the floor while it comes down about its heel if it was still in its heel rocker and then rises about the toes' joint from its entry pitch to 40°, its toes extending, the stance rockers in order and the forefoot rocker of a push-off, the floor contact it declares being the heel while it pivots there and the toes' joint after; the other leg, the reaching leg, leaves the floor at the cut and withdraws under the body, its ankle drawn under the hip and raised to 0.3 hip heights, the free-leg tuck of a one-foot take-off, its foot turning from its entry rotation. The arms swing forward and up from their entry angles with the push (shoulder flexion to 60°), the arm action that adds to take-off velocity (Ashby and Heegaard 2002, "Role of arm motion in the standing long jump", J. Biomech. 35).

    Whatever the push-off moves on a path of its own (the take-off foot's pitch, the reaching foot, the turn to the jump's heading, the shoulders) also continues the rate it was entered with: the path starts at rest, and the entry rate times the engine's carriedRate term (unit slope and no acceleration at the cut, zero value, velocity and acceleration at its end) is added to it over the push. A rate is carried only as long as its room affords, the term peaking at carriedRate.PEAK of its duration times the rate: the reaching foot's velocity and spin, a rigid motion of the foot, for no longer than brings its heel or ball to the floor, and a take-off foot whose heel was coming down about its ball for no longer than lays it flat. The root's own push-off path starts at the entry's position, velocity and acceleration, so a walk's stance, whose root is accelerating downward at several metres per second squared, is not cut to a path at rest acceleration: the planted knee is solved from the root and would show that step as a change of its flexion rate within one frame. The reaching ankle carries its entry acceleration the same way (the engine's carriedAcceleration, over the carry of its velocity), so the reaching knee is not left to absorb the root's.

    Flight is ballistic in the centre of mass, the one point gravity alone moves while airborne: from the apex height apex above take-off (0.06 hip heights plus a tenth of the jump's length when absent, provisional), the vertical take-off speed is sqrt(2 g apex), the flight time solves the centre's landing height, and the horizontal speed covers its distance in that time, the closed form of a drag-free projectile (v = d / T - g T / 2, as the engine's ballistic solvers). At each airborne instant the root is placed so the body's centre of mass, read by the engine's mass model from the posture there, lies on that parabola, so moving the limbs in flight shifts the trunk the way it would. The legs travel in the body's frame from their take-off placements to their landing footprints, lifted at mid-flight. Both feet land flat on footprints at the destination and at its height (a box's top, a step; the flight's default apex then also clears that rise, and an apex that does not is refused), at the body's rest separation, turned out by the stance angle; the root lands 0.03 hip heights low with the flight's velocity, absorbs to 0.12 hip heights low on a quintic path that brings that velocity to rest over ABSORB seconds, then rises to the upright stand (standUpright) over RISE seconds. Planted legs are kept reachable by resolveHumanPosture.lower. The push-off is drawn by jumpPush, the landing's places by jumpLanding and the paths by jumpPath.
