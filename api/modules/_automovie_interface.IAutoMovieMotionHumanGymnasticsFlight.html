    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanGymnasticsFlight

    The airborne kernel of the gymnastic and jumping laws (#3377): a body in flight is a set of joint angles, a heading and a tip, placed where its centre of mass lies on the drag-free parabola its take-off speed gives.

    A flight posture is written by its joints alone, with no limb goal, so placing it by its centre moves only its root and the centre read from it is the one the film shows; a posture a law wrote with limb goals is first frozen into the joints its own resolution gives. A rigid turn carries either kind, its world goals going round with its root.

    The physics is the drag-free projectile's closed form, as the jump's flight uses it: the centre rises and falls under gravity alone, so a take-off's vertical speed fixes the flight time to any height, and a body that keeps its shape turns at the constant mean rate of its kept angular momentum, so a turn's angle is that rate times the time. A turn about the centre of mass leaves that point on its parabola.
