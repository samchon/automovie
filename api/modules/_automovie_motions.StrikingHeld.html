    Namespace StrikingHeld

    A thing held in the hand as the effector of an action: a blade whose point and edge are aimed, a firearm whose muzzle is aimed.

    A held prop is rigid in the hand from the instant the grip closed, so its working point (a sword's point, a muzzle), its axis (the way the blade or the barrel runs) and its edge (the way a blade cuts, a barrel's top) are constants of the hand's frame for as long as it is held. This reads them once at the action's entry, from where the prop is displayed and where the hand is displayed, so the law aims the same thing the performance shows whichever way the hand took the prop. With no prop named the hand itself is the effector: the grip's centre, its line the hand's own from the wrist to the knuckles, its edge the little finger's side, as a knife-hand cuts.

    The frame is the performance's own grip frame: the grip socket's point with the hand bone's rotation. A goal built here puts the working point, not the wrist, on its place: the goal's contact is the working point, so where the wrist cannot turn the hand as asked the point stays and the turn gives way.
