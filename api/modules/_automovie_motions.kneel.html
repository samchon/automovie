    Namespace kneel

    Kneeling from standing: on one knee (half kneel), on both knees upright, or sitting back on the heels, held, then rising the way it went down.

    The goals are floor points, solved by kneelKeys. The kneeling leg steps back onto its tucked toes while the hips travel along the heading from where they stand to where the step key puts them as the foot travels, so they trail the foot and arrive after it lands; with the balance option (IAutoMovieMotionHumanBalance) they run instead a course that keeps the extrapolated centre of mass between the feet (the extrapolated-centre course; Hof, Gazendam and Sinke 2005), going to the middle between the two feet. Through the step the hips ride as high as the less extended leg reaches its foot, the reach going from the standing leg's to REACH of its length, a reverse lunge, and the pelvis tips forward by what keeps the leg behind inside the hip's extension range (kneelKeys.lean). Then the hips lower until the knee touches the floor straight under its hip joint with the thigh upright, the shin lying back along the floor to the foot. For both knees the front leg then swings back and its knee lands beside the first, and for sitting back the hips turn about the knees toward the heels until the knees flex kneelKeys.SIT degrees. Pollard, Porter and Redfern (2011, J Appl Biomech 27:233) give the four kneeling postures. The hands rest on the thighs as the body settles.

    Each phase moves on minimum-jerk progress; its length is provisional, scaled by the square root of the hip height (Alexander 1984) and by the manner's tempo. The root is lowered wherever a planted leg could not reach its foot. Each foot plants while it keeps its floor point, and each knee on the floor bears there (a bear window on its lower leg, at the knee) from the key it comes down in until it leaves, both on the floor the feet stand on. Events: stepBack at the start, kneeContact, secondKnee for both knees, seatOnHeels for sitting back, settled, and when it rises kneeOff and stood.
