    Namespace dive

    A fall onto the floor on purpose from a stance: a goalkeeper's dive to the side, or a sliding tackle forward on the hip.

    Every second the body is a set of joint angles blended from the entry's toward the kind's (SHAPES, provisional), turned about one axis of its own (the dive about its forward axis, so it lands on its side; the slide about its lateral axis, leaning back), and placed with its root on a path:

    • dive: a crouch with the feet planted and the knees bending under the lowered root (AutoMovieMotionHumanTrunk.preparePlanted), then a flight whose hips move REACH statures to the side on a parabola APEX statures above the line from take-off to landing, the body turning a quarter about its forward axis on the way while its joints extend from the crouch's solved angles into the shape, then lying on its side where it landed. The flight is the one airborne part, on that one axis.
    • slide: the body leans back and sinks onto its hip while its hips slide REACH statures forward and slowing to rest, the lead leg reaching ahead and the trailing one bent under, never off the floor.

    Landed, the root is placed so the lowest joint centre lies at the ankle's standing height above the floor, as on the feet.

    Contact windows: the feet plant through the crouch; landed, the seat lies on the floor (the hip) and, in a dive, the lower hand plants beside it.

    First pass: the take-off is not driven by the legs' push, the landing does not absorb the fall through the arm, and a slide from a run starts from the run's posture at rest, not its speed.
