    Namespace walk

    Walking to a floor point in whole steps, ending with the feet side by side.

    Speed comes from dynamic similarity (Alexander 1984): people of different leg length walk alike at equal Froude number v^2 / (g l), so each pace is a Froude number on the hip height l: slow 0.12, normal 0.25 and fast 0.32, which give 1.05, 1.51 and 1.71 m/s at the reference hip (walkRoot.REFERENCE_HIP, 0.93 m). Step length grows with the square root of speed (Grieve and Gear 1966): 1.2 l sqrt(sqrt(Fr)), 0.8 hip heights at the normal pace (about 0.4 stature, Perry and Burnfield); the step count is rounded up so every step is at most that long, at the pace's own cadence (WalkPace).

    Each foot follows the three rockers of stance with its contact point fixed on the floor, and swings between footprints on a minimum-jerk path lifted clear of the floor (walkSteps): 60 % stance and 40 % swing of each stride, so each double support lasts a tenth of a stride (Perry and Burnfield). The pelvis advances fastest near double support and slowest near midstance. Its height follows the two contact heights and accounts for the raised leg's share of the 4–5 cm whole-body excursion. It sways 2 cm toward the stance foot and lowers wherever a planted leg cannot reach its foot, so no plant is ever stretched off its point (walkReach). A swing joins the stance its foot left, as the leg reached it at toe-off, to the stance it lands in, as the leg reaches it at heel strike (walkFoot), and the pelvis reads a swinging foot only through those two stances. The pelvis turns 4° and tilts 4° with the stride, the chest counter-rotates, and the arms swing opposite the legs about 15° with the elbows bent 15 to 30° (Perry and Burnfield, normal gait kinematics).

    The explicit footprints and cut entry pose give the engine's IK their stance targets. The audit reads the same windows through contactMask. Proficiency only varies step timing.
