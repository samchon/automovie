    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanAffect

    Affect displays as an additive layer: laughing, sighing, yawning, coughing, sneezing and shivering, each a short pattern of trunk, shoulder, head and jaw angles over any action, starting and ending at zero.

    These are joint-angle patterns because no body point is brought anywhere: the display is the trunk's and head's own convulsion or stretch, the way an additive animation layer carries breathing or a laugh over locomotion. Planted and gripping limb goals are untouched, and the arms are solved after the trunk, so contacts and held objects stay where they are. Each display is the sum of its pulses under an envelope that rises and falls on minimum-jerk ramps, so it is zero with zero velocity at its start and end.

    • laugh: a train of laugh notes about 210 ms apart (Provine 1996, American Scientist 84:38), each a 3 degree trunk flexion and 3 degree shoulder lift pulse, over a 5 degree head extension; a laugh of seconds repeats notes while it lasts, fading over its last third.
    • sigh: a deep inhalation over 40 % of the sigh raising the shoulders 6 degrees and extending the chest 4, then an exhalation that lets the shoulders fall 2 degrees below rest and the head drop 4.
    • yawn: the jaw opens 25 degrees and the head extends 15 with the chest extending 5, peaking at 40 % of the yawn (a yawn lasts about six seconds, Provine 1986, Ethology 72:109; profile provisional).
    • cough: three sharp trunk flexions of 10 degrees with the head 8, 0.5 s apart, each rising in 0.12 s and recovering over 0.38 s (provisional).
    • sneeze: an inhalation that extends the head 10 degrees over 70 % of the sneeze, then a sudden flexion of the head 25 and the chest 10 in 0.1 s, recovering by the end (provisional).
    • shiver: the shoulders hunch 8 degrees while they tremble 1.5 degrees at 4 Hz, a visible shivering burst rate below the 12 Hz Nyquist limit of a 24 Hz film (shivering muscle tremor itself is faster, near 10 Hz, and would alias; provisional).

    Amplitudes and timings are provisional except where cited; an intensity scales every amplitude.
