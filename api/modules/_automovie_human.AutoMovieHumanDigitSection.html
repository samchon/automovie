    Namespace AutoMovieHumanDigitSection

    A digit's section square to its bone: a palmar half and a dorsal half that meet at the radial and ulnar side points (the digit's midlateral lines, where palmar and dorsal skin meet), assembled as one periodic cubic B-spline section by AutoMovieHumanSeamKnot.assemble.

    The section lies in its own frame: origin on the bone, b across the digit toward the thumb's side, n toward the palm. Each side point is a guide owning its point (+-w, 0), its tangent along n and its curvature kappa_s, the tightest turn of the section (docs/techniques/ surface-composition.md, Sectors and guides). Each half is its own sector:

    palmar, theta in [0, pi]:  (w cos theta,  h_p s_p(theta))
dorsal, theta in [0, pi]:  (-w cos theta, -h_d s_d(theta))
s(theta) = sin theta + a sin theta cos^2 theta + c sin theta cos^4 theta

    s(pi/2) = 1, so each half reaches its depth h at its apex, with a level tangent there. At a side point x'' = -w and y' = h s'(0) = h (1 + a + c), so its curvature is w / (h s'(0))^2: both halves meet the guide's speed lambda = sqrt(w / kappa_s) and curvature exactly by taking s'(0) = lambda / h. At the apex the curvature is h (1 - 2a) / w^2, the ellipse's h / w^2 times rho = 1 - 2a, so a sets the apex's flatness and c the side's turn independently. Both halves join their guides with tangential acceleration zero, as the guides publish, so the assembled ring is the sectors' own curves at their knots and C2 everywhere.
