    Namespace approach

    Walking up to a person, a prop or a point and stopping at an interpersonal distance, facing it.

    The goal is the stop, not the steps: the front of the performer's chest (the sternum region) must end gap short of the target, horizontally, along the line from where the performer stands to the target (HumanProxemics). The law turns that goal into the floor point the feet's midpoint must reach, gap plus the chest's lead over the feet back from the target, and lets the walk law place whole steps to it, so the stop is exact without sliding (the walk's footprints are inverse-kinematics goals) and the body faces the target because it walked straight at it.

    The target is read once, at the start: a person's region (the sternum for Hall's chest-to-chest gaps, upperBack to stop behind someone), a prop's point or a free point. For handing the gap comes from both people's own measures, so a short and a tall partner meet at a gap both can reach; a prop or point target uses the performer's measure alone. A target already nearer than the requested gap is refused with both distances, since an approach never backs away.

    A side stops on a named side of a person target instead, in that person's own frame (their sternum's outward normal is their front), at the same gap, and the walk's final heading turns the body to face them on the last steps. The person approached on a side is walked round like one named in avoid, so a stop behind them is not reached through them. People named in avoid are walked round: where the straight leg would pass one nearer than the walker's lateral clearance (0.5 m, CLEARANCE; walkers keep an elliptical personal space wider ahead than to the side while circumventing a person, Gerin-Lajoie, Richards and McFadyen 2005, Motor Control 9:242) plus half the walker's chest breadth, a waypoint abeam that person takes the walk round on the side that deviates least. Walkers moving past each other are not avoided here; reciprocal avoidance belongs to the engine. An Effort sets the walk's step length, arm swing and elbow width (HumanEffort); bound flow's elbows held in cannot be shown, since the walk only widens them.

    With track, a person who moves is followed: their placed root is read ahead over HORIZON seconds for where and when they come to rest, the stop is taken against the target carried and turned with their root to that place, and the walk waits in its first posture so that it arrives LAG after they stop, which keeps the follower behind a leader on the leader's own line. Where their way bends by more than BEND the walk goes by the places they passed, and with track: "pace" it matches their speed instead of waiting (walkStation; Rio, Rhea and Warren 2014).

    Events are the walk's (each foot's toe-offs and heel strikes, lastFootfall, arrive), and depart where the walk waited.
