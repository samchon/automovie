    Namespace IAutoMovieMotionHumanArmClearance

    The room a forearm needs beside its own trunk and beside the other arm.

    The other arm's room is the far side of the chest's median plane: an elbow crosses it only as far as its wrist's goal does.

    A hand goal fixes the wrist and leaves the elbow free on a circle. An elbow place whose point is outside the trunk can still run the forearm through it, so the forearm is judged along its whole length: its axis, sampled from the elbow toward the wrist, keeps the forearm's radius from the colliders of the trunk, the neck and the head at the elbow, tapering to the wrist's own clearance at the wrist, where the palm lies on the surface the hand was sent to. The limb fit, which places the legs before the arms, judges the thighs too (obstacles); a caller that poses only the trunk judges the trunk alone (trunk).

    An arm that is not fitted to a goal but carried in joint space between two arms has no elbow place to choose and no goal under its wrist; it is read whole (room), against another body's colliders too when one stands in its way, and its elbow let out or folded until the forearm asks no more room than the carry's two ends hold (cleared).
