    Interface IAutoMovieWaterFeature

    The binding that makes an independent fluid domain a building's water feature.

    This record, not the fluid domain, is the building-owned half. The domain stays a free-standing computational unit; the feature says that one logical space of a building is the basin the domain fills and which boundaries form its rim. A production world with no building places the same domain by not writing a feature.

    Nothing here restates geometry. The basin's extent is the architecture's logical space and the lattice's extent is the domain's grid, and the engine checks that the binding resolves rather than trusting a name.

    The production builder hands validateWaterFeatures each building's features together with every fluid domain of the shot, after refusing a feature whose environment names no staged building. The validator refuses a blank or repeated id, an unknown kind or mode, a blank material, an environment other than the building being validated, a space or rim boundary that does not resolve there, a rim boundary that does not bound the space, a domain that is absent or itself invalid, and lattice cell centres at bed height that fall outside a basin stating a volume (the four corner cells decide a convex basin, every cell any other). A basin that states no volume is not measured.

    boundaries

    boundaries: string[]

    Ids of the environment's boundaries forming the rim that retains the water, such as the coping of a basin or the parapet of a roof pool. Each must resolve, appear once, and bound space. May be empty for a feature retained by its own bed alone.

    mode

    mode: AutoMovieWaterFeatureMode

    How the feature is evaluated over shot time by lowerWaterFeature.

    • static: always the authored step-0 state, for a mirror pool that must read identically in every frame of a cut.
    • flowing: the simulated state, plus a flowing hint the viewer stores for a ripple shader to scroll along the solved velocity field; no shader in this repository reads it yet.
    • simulated: the simulated state with no additional surface animation.
