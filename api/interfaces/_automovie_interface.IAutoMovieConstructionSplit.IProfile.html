    Interface IProfile

    A curved seam: the shared contour both bands start from in place of the straight chord, such as a crotch's midsagittal line from the front fork under the perineum to the back fork.

    The seam plane holds the chord and the host end section's outward normal. In it the seam is the clamped cubic spline, on chord-length parameters between its knots, through the chord's first end (host phase a), the authored knots and its second end (phase b). It is parameterized by its own course, not by the chord, so its knots need not advance along the chord: a seam whose web stands well out of the end beside a fork, as between the toes on top of the foot, runs out along the host first and then turns across. At each end its tangent is the line where the host's tangent plane meets the seam plane, oriented out of the host, with a derivative as long as the adjacent knot distance. So the seam leaves each fork along the host's own surface. Each band's chord columns stand on the curve at equal arclength shares. Their start ribbon is the seam plane's unit normal toward the band's own arc, of length L with second derivative zero. The two bands therefore leave the seam in opposite directions with a common tangent plane, G1 across it.

    Within corner of clock on each side of a fork, the cross ribbons turn by the quintic smoothstep through the inward diagonal at the fork. On the arc they turn from the host's own ribbon toward the ruling, inside the host's tangent plane. On the seam they turn from the ruling toward the seam's direction away from the fork. The corner column then leaves the fork between both boundaries instead of along either one. The bands are G1 to the host there, not G2. The forks are G1 when the host's normal at each fork lies in the seam plane, as on the midline of a mirror-symmetric host. Otherwise the remaining angle stays measurable and is not hidden.

    Engine admission: at least one knot; every knot finite, along not negative, and apart from the knot before it (the first from the seam's first end, [0, 0]); the realization refuses a knot whose along exceeds the chord's length, which needs the host's geometry; corner finite, positive and below pi / 2; reach, when given, finite in (0, 1].

    Optionalfair

    fair?: number | [number, number]

    The radius of the disc about each of this seam's two forks over which the drawn skin is faired, as a share of the seam's chord (the distance between its two forks): the vertices whose path along the mesh from the fork is shorter than the radius are moved so that the bi-Laplacian of their positions vanishes, with the two rings outside the disc held, so the fill meets the skin round it G1 (the fork patch of the branching-surface literature, Gabrielides, Ginnis and Kaklis 2007, in the discrete form of Botsch and Kobbelt 2004). At a fork the host's end and two bands meet in a corner no one surface owns, and each band turns sharply beside it over about its corner window, which grows with the seam. The fill belongs to no surface: its vertices keep the chart coordinates they were drawn at. In (0, 1], one share for both forks or one for each, [a, b] in the seam's order; absent, the forks are left as drawn. The two sides of an end may want their own: between toes the web stands deep on the sole and shallow on top.

    Optionalheading

    heading?: [number, number, number]

    A world direction the seam plane holds in place of the host end's outward normal, unit or not, not along the chord; depth is then measured along its part square to the chord, oriented out of the host. A seam between two digits that leave a limb's end aslant to its course lies in the plane between the digits, which holds their direction and not the course's. Absent, the end normal.

    Optionalreach

    reach?: number

    In a split into more than two (outer), the share of its length each band's cross ribbon keeps at a fork, rising to the whole by the quintic smoothstep over corner of clock on each side: the first derivative by the share, the second by its square, so a column only slows across its start and the band's tangent plane and curvature there, G2 to the host along an arc, are kept. Inside a fork the band's corner is convex, and columns leaving square to the ring and to the seam converge within about the turn's window; shorter ribbons leave the rest of each column to its rail, which runs round the child in order. In (0, 1]; absent, 1. The first seam with a profile gives the split's, as it gives corner.

The first seam with a profile gives the split's, as it gives corner.
