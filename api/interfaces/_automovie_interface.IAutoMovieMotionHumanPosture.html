    Interface IAutoMovieMotionHumanPosture

    One instant of a performer as an action law writes it: where the body stands and faces, the clinical angles of the joints it sets directly, and the goals of the limbs it places by inverse kinematics.

    Every point is in world metres of the canonical frame (+Y up). root is the rig's origin, the floor point under the pelvis at rest, so lowering it by 0.1 lowers the whole body 0.1; yaw turns the body about +Y in degrees (0 faces +Z, 90 faces +X), then pitch tips it forward and roll toward its right, both in degrees about the turned axes. joints holds clinical degrees [flexion, abduction, twist] read through HUMANOID_REST_FRAME; a bone a limb goal solves takes the goal's result instead. A limb goal places its end joint (the wrist or the ankle) on point, bends its middle joint toward bend and, when rotation is given, turns the hand or foot to that world rotation from the rest. A planted limb (planted: true) is re-solved onto its goal after transitions, so a foot on the floor or a hand on a grip never slides.

    A resolver-issued preparation may privately associate its exact translated raw object with the source's actual cold solution. This adds no public resolved state: original goals remain authoring demands, and copies or changed solver context receive ordinary resolution.

    Optionalrotation

    rotation?: IAutoMovieHumanPostureRotation

    A rigid turn of the whole posture about a world point, applied after the heading: the root's position and rotation and every world limb goal (its point, its bend and its rotation) go round about by turn, while a goal attached to a bone (on) moves with its bone. It carries the orientations the heading's Euler angles cannot blend or read back, such as a somersault through the vertical or a twist about the long axis of a pitched body, so yaw, pitch and roll keep meaning the facing a law reads on the floor plan. Absent for no turn. A planted world goal cannot go round with the body and is refused with it (turnHumanPosture).
