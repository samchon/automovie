    Optionalat

    at?: "mouth" | "ahead" | { region: AutoMovieHumanoidRegion }

    Where the tool is used, which is where the grasp must also hold: ahead presents it ahead of the shoulder, as a blade or a barrel is aimed; mouth brings its working point to the performer's own mouth, as a vessel's lip is drunk from; a region brings it to that named region of the performer's own body, as a razor's edge is laid on the jaw or a comb on the scalp. ahead when absent. At the mouth or a region the level part of the line runs from the hand back into the body (a cigarette's or a spoon's forearm line points at the lips), and square stays upright.

    line

    line: "across" | "along" | "forearm" | "square" | "oblique" | "worn"

    How the tool's line lies to the forearm when it is presented: forearm continues it, as a pistol's bore and a thrusting point do; oblique rises 45 degrees from it, a sabre grip across the palm; square stands upright on it, a hammer grip; across runs level across the body toward the other hand, as a camera or a bar held in both hands lies, whatever the place; along lays the tool along the surface of its place with the working point's own face on it, as a razor lies on the jaw or a comb on the scalp, its way along the surface the hand's own (a place on the body only); worn puts the thing on that place as it is worn (HumanWorn.frame), the whole frame of its wear socket on the region, as a hat goes on the crown or spectacles on the nose (a region only).

    Optionalround

    round?: boolean

    For a thing worn round about the region's normal, as a hat about the crown, that its turn about it is free: the grasp holds at whichever turn suits it; spectacles are not, and stay facing the body's way. False when absent.

    tip: string

    The affordance id of the tool's working point: a blade's point, a muzzle, a hammer's face. The tool's line runs from the handle to it.
