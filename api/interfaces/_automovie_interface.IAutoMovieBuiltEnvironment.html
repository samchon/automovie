    Interface IAutoMovieBuiltEnvironment

    A complete code-authored building work containing one or more building units.

    The record separates the visible element hierarchy from the logical space hierarchy, so a continuous hall may contain named rooms, floors, an attic, and a double-height void without inventing walls, while a stair, lift, ramp, or skybridge explicitly connects those spaces.

    This record owns building geometry: facade ladders, rails, external stairs, balconies, roofs and helipads belong here; surrounding terrain, parks, sky and natural water belong to the production world. Sun, season, orientation, reference ground and neighbouring occluders are separate analysis context. Open model/element data does not enforce that semantic ownership by inspecting depicted content. An indoor water feature composes a separate engine fluid domain with a building space, and open element kinds reuse the same transform/model primitives across styles.

    Ordinary TypeScript produces the record; a building class may use loops, parameters, and reusable parts. Lowering turns it into scene models, set pieces, and locomotion spaces, naming the scene space of logical space s as ${id}/${s} and the set piece of element e as ${id}/${e}.

    buildings

    buildings: IAutoMovieBuildingUnit[]

    Independently owned and independently placed building units in this work; at least one.

    Ownership is total: every element and every logical space descends from exactly one unit's roots, so nothing in the work is unattributed. A skybridge is a work-owned relation between two units rather than a third unit, so its connector may cross units even though its roots may not.

    Optionalpopulations

    populations?: IAutoMovieBuiltPopulation[]

    Compact repeated populations this work stages, each in one logical space.

    A repeated part such as thousands of roof slates is a population rather than thousands of elements. Declaring it here is what lets a space answer for it: a population placed only in the production world is invisible to this record, so a room asked what it holds would answer with a plausibly small box that leaves out its dominant geometry.

    Omitted is equivalent to an empty list.
