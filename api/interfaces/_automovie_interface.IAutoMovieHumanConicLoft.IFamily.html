    phase?: number

    Largest step between neighbouring phases, radians, beside the finest relief net's columns: the mesh's density round the part held apart from its nets'. f1's trunk took its phases from its finest net, the eyes' at 105 columns (0.016 rad); with that net coarsened to 73 columns for its own sake the whole face's mesh went from about 400 phases round to 270 and read in level bands and blocks.

    Optionalpitch

    pitch?: number

    Largest distance between two neighbouring phases at the part's widest section, mm; absent, PHASE_MM. A small part whose skin turns within a few millimetres takes a finer pitch: at 4 mm the ear's thin rim was sampled by three phases and its edge drew a row of teeth.

    Optionalroll

    roll?: IRoll

    The chart's roll about the course; absent, none. A part whose sections turn in their planes along the course (a sheet that curls along its length, as the ear's plate does from the lobule up to the helix's top) keeps its long axis on u only if the chart turns with it: read in a chart carried by the course alone, its extremes trade places as it turns, and a field that follows one extreme jumps to the next.

    Optionalturn

    turn?: number

    Largest turn of a ring's tangent between two neighbouring phases, radians; absent, the phases are uniform. With it the phases are spaced by how fast the rings turn as well as by pitch: at every phase the density is the most any ring asks there, its length over pitch or its turn over this, so a sharp edge that only some sections have (a sole's) takes its phases close together where it stands, and the rest of the part keeps its spacing. f1's leg on uniform phases set by its thigh's girth turned by up to 0.83 rad between two of them round the forefoot's outer edge, which showed as level stripes.
