    Hold the limit perimeter of a surface network's girth rings, the rings that declare perimeter, by solving a radial factor along each sweep.

    A girth ring runs once round one sweep at one u. Along a row of valence-four vertices whose loop neighbours lie opposite, beside rows of such vertices, the Catmull-Clark limit is the tensor-product cubic B-spline, so the limit along the row is the periodic uniform cubic B-spline whose control points are (A + 4 P + B) / 6 for each vertex P and its two neighbours A and B across the row; its perimeter is integrated by five-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature on every span. Each sweep's factor is the sampled-overshoot-policy spline of interpolateAutoMovieSpline through 1 at the sweep's other rings at one u, which meet neighbouring parts and stay, and through a value at each girth ring, holding its end values beyond the outermost knots; IAutoMovieSweepRig.girth folds it into the scale. The girth values solve the system "every measured perimeter equals its declaration" jointly by Broyden's method (Broyden 1965, Mathematics of Computation 19(92):577-593), one rest fit per step: the Jacobian starts diagonal, each perimeter in proportion to its own factor, which is exact where the fit interpolates the row and no neighbouring sample reads another factor, and each step's rank-one update learns how neighbouring rings couple through the spline, cell averages or a least-squares fit, so rings close together converge together where updating each alone would oscillate or crawl; a step that would make a factor non-positive is halved. It stops when every perimeter has relative error at most 1e-9 and refuses after 24 fits. Non-finite measurements/steps and zero representable progress are refused before a factor update can enter nonterminating positivity backtracking. The initial diagonal uses each measured perimeter directly, without reconstructing it from a potentially cancellation-dominated residual.
