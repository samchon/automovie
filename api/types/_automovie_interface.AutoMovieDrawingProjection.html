    What a view does to the design before it draws it.

    A plan, a reflected ceiling plan, a section and an elevation are one orthographic projection with two decisions: whether there is a cut plane, and whether the page basis is mirrored. Naming them lets a view state the drafting convention it honours rather than leaving the reader to infer it from a direction.

    • plan looks down. Material wholly above the cut is removed except within the view's overhead band, what the plane passes through is drawn cut, and what lies below is drawn projected.
    • reflected-ceiling-plan conventionally looks up and cuts the same way; negating page right supplies the mirror. Matching floor/ceiling page positions additionally requires compatible origins, opposite vertical directions and matching up hints; the literal alone does not align independently authored views.
    • section cuts on a plane across the view direction and keeps what is beyond it.
    • elevation has no cut. Nothing is removed and nothing is drawn cut; the origin fixes the page origin and the plane the view's depth is measured from.

    The literal states intent only: the view's direction and origin place the plane, and nothing checks that a plan looks down or that a section plane is vertical.
