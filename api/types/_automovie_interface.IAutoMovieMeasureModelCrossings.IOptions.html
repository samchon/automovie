    Type Alias IOptions

    Report every pair of a model's mesh parts whose surfaces cross, measured where the parts actually sit in the model's frame.

    Each mesh part is first carried into the model frame by its own transform and, when attached to a bone the rest pose resolves, by that bone's rest placement, the placement autoMovieModelPartMatrices owns; a part with neither keeps its coordinates exactly. Bicubic recipes use the canonical engine's retained finite mesh, including declared partial output; primitives and unrecognized geometry remain outside this mesh crossing query. A pair appears once, in model part order, only when at least one triangle on either side is crossed, with each side's crossed triangle count and their combined coplanar count. Touching is not crossing: the underlying measureAutoMovieMeshCrossings test is strict, so shells meeting along a seam do not report.

    An empty result does not mean a healthy model, nor a full one a broken one. A layered character rests with shells inside each other on purpose: the eyeball inside the lid, lashes rooted in skin, the tongue against the teeth. What carries information is the change from a rest pose, so a caller measures its own rest pose once and compares. The report is geometry, not acceptance: crossed triangles hidden inside a closed shell and visible once it opens look the same here, and that judgement stays with rendered review.

    withinParts also measures each part against itself, adding before that part's pairs one entry whose part and other are both that part, counting its triangles that another triangle of the same part crosses. That is for a continuous skin partitioned into segments, where a fold swallowing its own crease is exactly the penetration the partition exists to find; the same strict rules apply, so a triangle against itself and neighbours sharing an edge or corner without folding are touching. Throws, naming the part, on a mesh part whose index buffer is malformed, and passes on the crossing measure's own refusals.
