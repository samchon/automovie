    Closed vocabulary of anatomical surface regions on a humanoid body: the places another body or an object touches, strikes, grips or presents to.

    Names follow surface anatomy (Gray's Anatomy surface landmarks; the region divisions of anatomical charts): head and face, neck, the anterior and posterior trunk with the hypochondriac regions over the liver (right) and spleen (left) and the lumbar regions over the kidneys, and the limbs by segment and aspect. Paired regions carry their side, the body's own left or right. Contact points the body itself presents (the palm, the tip and the pad of every digit, the sole, the heel, the seat) are regions too, so a key, a string, another body or an object can be aimed at them. Each region is anchored to one humanoid bone and resolved to a point and an outward normal on that bone's collision shapes by the engine, so the same name serves a generated Human, an imported character or a parametric figure. The list grows additively; no name is ever repurposed.
