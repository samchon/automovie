    Type Alias IProps

    Caller-owned data for one sweep of compliant distance constraints over a lattice, in place: every edge of the lattice, its row and column neighbours, its diagonals and its second neighbours, projected one after another with its Lagrange multiplier, the extended position-based dynamics of Macklin, Müller and Chentanez 2016 ("XPBD: position-based simulation of compliant constrained dynamics", MIG 2016, pp. 49-54, equations 18 and 17).

    For an edge between particles i and k with rest length l, the constraint is C = |x_i - x_k| - l, its gradient the unit direction n from k to i, and the time-scaled compliance alpha~ = alpha / dt^2 with alpha the family's compliance in metres per newton. The multiplier's increment is dlambda = (-C - alpha~ lambda) / (w_i + w_k + alpha~), and the particles move by w_i dlambda n and -w_k dlambda n, w the inverse masses. Compliance is a material parameter rather than a per-sweep relaxation weight; finite sweep counts and timestep choices can still change the numerical result. Zero compliance requests a hard distance constraint without guaranteeing zero residual after one sweep. On a cord, a one-wide lattice, the second neighbours are its bending constraint in distance form: the distance between the ends of two consecutive segments, which is regular at a straight rest where the angle between the segments is not differentiable.

    Edges are visited in a fixed order (row-major particles, each with the neighbours that follow it in SOFT_BODY_NEIGHBOURS), so a solve is deterministic. lambda holds one multiplier per particle and neighbour slot and is reset by the caller at the start of each step.

    Only predicted and lambda are mutated, with separate storage required for the working and preparation arrays. Each undirected edge updates only its lower-index endpoint's multiplier slot. Negative rest slots, zero effective denominators and collapsed current edges are skipped. This helper validates no buffers or numerical inputs and retains none; simulateSoftBodyCore supplies domain-admitted preparation and chooses the number of sweeps.
