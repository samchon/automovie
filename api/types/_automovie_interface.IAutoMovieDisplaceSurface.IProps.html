    A subdivided surface and one normal-displacement height per vertex, the relief that is finer than a cage can carry, such as an engraved groove or a tendon's ridge.

    Each vertex moves by its height, metres, outward when positive, along its limit normal, or the normalized mean of its distinct normals where a crease or boundary gives it several; its limit position and its refined control position move alike. Each normal then turns by the change the displacement makes to the mesh's area-weighted normal there: n + D / |D| - M / |M|, normalized, where M sums the area vectors of the quads that use the normal before the displacement and D after it. This is a discrete form of Blinn's (1978) perturbed normal: it adds the tilt the height's slope makes, keeps the exact limit normal wherever every quad around a normal is undisplaced, and agrees with the displaced shape to the mesh's resolution, so relief narrower than about two faces of the subdivided mesh aliases and belongs at a finer level. The orientation check is local to each normal group's aggregate area; it does not certify every face orientation or global absence of self-intersections. The heights usually come from a field evaluated at each vertex's locateAutoMovieSurfaceVertices location, which depends on no pose, so a posed surface takes the same heights.
