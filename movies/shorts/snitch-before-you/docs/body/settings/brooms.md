# Riding brooms

## Flight support and its limits {#flight}

Each rider has one simple brown wooden broom, 2.4 metres long, with a straight narrow shaft and a compact dark bristle bundle at the rear. The shaft has a 0.65-metre clear rear riding section between the normal seat and the bristles, enough for a second rider to straddle behind the first. Its smooth wood admits a hand sliding along it; no strap, saddle, harness or crossbar is present. The two brooms have the same capacity and appearance. Seated support acts through pelvis and thighs around the shaft; hands guide pitch and direction. Pulling the nose up while securely seated makes a gradual climbing turn and reduces downward speed. Flight produces rushing air and low bristle vibration, with no visible engine or exhaust. These are invented magic-flight rules for this production, not canonical mechanics or an installed physics feature.

A seated rider can steer with either hand while retaining pelvis and thigh support. A broom can briefly support two riders, including one loading the rear shaft before he sits, but the extra load slows pitch recovery. A dropped rider receives ordinary downward acceleration, with no automatic levitation or hidden tether. A riderless broom holds its last direction and slows gradually; it does not follow a falling rider or return on command in this scene. Holding a flying shaft from below supplies a tiring handhold and does not restore seated support; the broom continues its existing route under that load.

A transfer requires parallel shafts and nearly matched velocity. Carina's empty rear shaft may approach Harry from his left at lower-chest height, below his existing right-hand grip. His left hand closes on her shaft before his right releases the old one and closes beside the left. With both hands on the new shaft he can bend his elbows, bring his lower chest over the wood, and swing his near knee across it to raise his pelvis into a rear straddle. He must not perform a stationary one-arm pull-up. Carina remains seated ahead of his contact zone, her knees and right steering hand clear of his hands and legs. Her broom yields down under the transferred load and recovers in a broad climbing arc. The old broom needs an unobstructed route above and to Harry's right. Screenplay owns the actual approach, release, mount and recovery; these constraints do not prescribe shot cuts or reveal order.
