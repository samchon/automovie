# Two practice goal ends

## Hoops, uprights and clearance {#structure}

There are six dull pale-metal ring goals, three at each end of the [pitch](arena.md#airspace). Each ring has a six-metre inside diameter and a centre forty-five metres above the grass. A separate narrow upright supports each ring. Within either end the uprights are at y=-12, 0 and +12, leaving twelve metres between pole centre lines; no net, cable, platform or crossbar spans the gaps. These dimensions and equal heights are invented usable geometry, not official Quidditch measurements.

The eastern set lies at x=0 and the western set at x=-160. The open route between the eastern southern and middle uprights is centered near y=-6, below their rings. Riders can pass through that gap without scoring or entering a hoop. Chasers aim the Quaffle through rings, while Keepers defend their fronts; a Seeker passing below is not a shot. The rings/uprights stay fixed throughout the scene, supply a coarse depth/position reference and do not collapse, open or emit sound. Simple rings and poles are sufficient; stands or decorative architecture are not required.
