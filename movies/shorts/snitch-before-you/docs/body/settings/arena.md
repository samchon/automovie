# Practice pitch and eastern runoff

## Route, population zones and sensory baseline {#airspace}

The scene uses an open daylight Quidditch practice pitch over a flat green oval lawn, with fourteen student riders in two complete teams. [Players](players.md#population) owns their roles; [goals](goals.md#structure) owns both goal structures. These dimensions and training geography are invented for this scene, not official pitch specifications. East is positive x and north positive y, in metres. The eastern goal line is x=0; the western line is x=-160. The principal lawn is 220 metres long and 100 metres wide, centered at x=-80, y=0. Short grass continues into a clear eastern runoff to x=+320. The ground remains below the whole pursuit; it is not an unseen landing cushion.

Each goal line has uprights at y=-12, 0 and +12. The Seekers' eastward route can use the southern gap around y=-6, below the hoops, then continue beyond the eastern goals. Background ball play occupies the central/northern field, mostly x=-140 to -20 and y=+8 to +38. Keepers can patrol their own goal fronts without occupying the southern through-gap. The used height range is seventeen to forty-five metres. No stands, buildings, nets, cables, crossbars, trees or additional aerial obstacles border the transfer route.

The eastern runoff separates a Seeker mishap from players still defending or contesting balls inside the field. A rider beyond x=+100 is over a hundred metres from the eastern Keeper's normal goal-front station, whereas another Seeker can remain a few metres away. Turning, accelerating and crossing that distance takes time; no participant can teleport or summon a remote rider to safety. A player who sees a rescue already supported can follow at a safe distance rather than collide with its rear shaft. Screenplay owns actual positions, recognition, approach and the decision to make room.

The sky is pale blue with broad even daylight from high southwest. There is no rain, lighting change or strong crosswind. The lawn, both goal ends and differently colored riders provide moving depth and direction references. Flight makes rushing air and low bristle vibration; the nearby Snitch adds a thin wing buzz, a received Quaffle a soft leather impact and a bat strike a brief wooden/metal knock. No spectator murmur, disembodied call or off-screen commentator is present. Projected short speech is audible between nearby Seekers; reduced flight noise permits their arm's-length recovery exchange.
