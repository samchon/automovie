## Film-experience causes shared by old and new

**The immediate upstream cause is an incompletely resolved sequence of visible actions, not a shortage of descriptive adjectives.** Settings repeatedly delegate actual seeing, routes, support transitions and counterpart reactions to the screenplay. The screenplay returns several of those duties as "banks", "accelerates", "folds", "keeps closing" or "leaves room" without fully fixing the change the audience must see. The adapter then becomes an unacknowledged choreographer, choosing curves, clocks, support points and gaze transitions. Some of those inventions are good engineering; none proves that the missing dramatic action was authored.

**Body action and camera were then optimized for endpoint alignment rather than embodied performance.** Upright torsos, bent seated thighs and dangling lower legs are repeatedly reused. The long hanging section mostly changes a free arm and a small body angle, so fatigue/danger reads weakly. The rigid coarse torso/robe envelope further masks local spinal action. These are problems with pose design, silhouette and staging even in a polygon hint; the brief does not require photorealism, elaborate cloth or detailed fingers. More mesh detail by itself would not supply a convincing line of action or weight transfer.

**Tracking and pacing flatten the distinction between ordinary travel, pursuit, danger and recovery.** Old10–17.5s keeps very similar paired framing and near-horizontal shafts; old26–35s keeps a largely vertical hanging shape. Both films retain similar-scale side views over long intervals with largely featureless sky/lawn. New curved paths and larger relative lead are real improvements, but the camera still cancels much absolute motion. New46.5–50.5s has clearer mount stages while the shaft remains visually calm. Fixed nominal intervals contain too few strongly differentiated changes to earn their duration. This is not a request for random shaking, exaggerated unsafe speed, new acrobatics or cutting away from the required continuous hand transfer.

**Action geography and information are not consistently delivered to an unbriefed viewer.** The old40–41.667s approach puts Harry behind Carina's torso/legs; old47.5–48.333s hides much of his folded upper body again. New separates the offer and rear mount better, but background inserts remain isolated and the reason for the Keeper's attention change is weak. Gold is identifiable, but reach→failed support→seen danger→foregone catch→matched offer needs stronger visible links. An informed reviewer can reconstruct those links from source while an ordinary viewer cannot be expected to do so.

**My earlier acceptance used the wrong breadth of conclusion.** It correctly recorded silent media, coarse representation, sampled observation limits and a recoverable event order, but treated those as sufficient for an acceptable motion-hint experience. I underweighted static pursuit, prolonged low-change hanging, hidden approach/mount action and the missing cinematic/causal bridges. Numerical contact and encoded-profile success cannot establish screen quality. Earlier "ready" language must not be carried forward as approval of energetic action or sufficiently detailed screenplay. Historical reports remain intact; this report explicitly narrows/revises that experience judgment. This does not require inventing unseen continuous artifacts or denying the readable transfer sequence.
