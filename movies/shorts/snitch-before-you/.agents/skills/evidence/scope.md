# Scope and graph ownership

## Choose the participating work

Ordinary coding and standalone assets can retain the default engineering configuration. Add actual asset or delivery duties where their respondents can establish them; a blank project needs no narrative mode, stages, or gate.

Choose the route from the actual commission under [writing workflow](../screenwriting/workflow.md#route-and-parents): `long`, direct `short`, or `short-with-settings`. Select only that route's real parents and populations; omitted short-form layers need no empty files. Both short routes retain screenplay construction, expression and independent review. A disabled pass composes its complete draft; it does not waive the route's duties.

Complete work is the default scope. Select a first-part pilot only when the user commissions that slice, preserve work-wide settings and the exact part's inherited lineage, and identify which whole-work obligations it cannot establish. Restore complete scope and complete populations before claiming the whole work complete. Route an expansion or replacement of closed material through [upstream repair](../screenwriting/repair.md).

## Bind claims to their owners

Use [contract adoption](adoption.md) to decide the meaning and narrowest honest respondent of a new duty. Production-specific and language-specific questions belong in their corresponding story-contract families. Unit principles, whole-population obligations, discovery, upstream repair, expression revision, and independent review remain distinct responsibilities even when related questions share a checklist.

Add ordinary native claims through the production configuration, selecting the actual respondents, references and review requirements. Read the installed package's composition API for supported fields. A scene answers its own behavior; the designated population respondent answers a whole-work duty.

Choose source, narrative and output respondents according to the achievement they can establish. Native claims select their actual files, references and review requirements; include the relevant population when checking inherited work or a pilot. Independent review judges whether those respondents and relationships establish the intended result.

## Preserve actual relationships

On the long route, treatments consume settings; scripts and screenplay cover the treatments they realize. Compact short screenplay cites its actual settings dependencies directly instead of inventing those intermediate populations. Event-to-scene coverage can be many-to-many when their partitions differ. Long-route final screenplay preserves its actual script file and section lineage; rewriting working editions preserve the corresponding frozen construction identities. Keep source citations, selections, and respondent addresses consistent with those relationships.

For commissioned execution, use [execution traceability](execution.md) for settings-to-asset, screenplay-to-performance/shot and shot-to-edit relationships. Preserve their actual many-to-many form, respondent responsibilities and current observation scope. A source citation or native target coverage alone cannot prove invocation, edit inclusion or visible effect.

Asset and delivery claims use builders or scoped observation and handoff reports that can establish the actual output. A coarse or stick-figure hint may require accurate motion without photorealism; a screenplay-only handoff cannot establish playback. [Delivery review](../review/delivery.md) owns inspecting the commissioned output and recording what remains unobserved.
