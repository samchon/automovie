# Invitation note

## Form, ownership and readable use {#note}

One uncreased white paper rectangle, roughly 10 by 15 centimetres, carries dark handwritten English: "Saturday. Three o'clock. Courtyard." It is an invitation aid, not an order or a binding agreement. Carina initially holds its lower edge in her right hand with the writing facing herself. It can be hidden behind her right hip, offered face up, released, and picked up by a short edge. It has no magical movement or self-correction. On release, gravity makes it flutter downward to the dry paving between the pair; it stays there in the quiet air until lifted. The tiny forehead brush, rather than a paper impact, is the gag's soft contact sound. The paper rustles when gripped or moved and makes a faint dry tap on landing.

A handover changes ownership only when the receiving fingers close on the paper before the giving fingers open. A withdrawn offer leaves the paper with its giver. A dropped note has no remaining grip and must be lifted before another offer. The recipient's free palm can receive the note without touching its giver. Writing is visible only from the inscribed side.
