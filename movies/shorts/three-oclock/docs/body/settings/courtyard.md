# Quiet school side courtyard

## Space, support and sensory conditions {#courtyard}

This invented courtyard is a shallow waiting recess beside a school wing, off the main departure route rather than a crossing between classrooms. Its level paved rectangle is eight metres east to west and four metres north to south. Flush pale-gray limestone slabs form a dry matte surface, with narrow level joints that provide continuous footing and let a paper note lie flat. The north boundary is an eight-metre-long, 2.4-metre-high masonry wall with dull beige plaster and a thickness of 0.25 metres. A matching four-metre west return contains an unroofed passage opening, 1.2 metres wide and open to the sky, centred 1.4 metres north of the paving's south edge. This is a gap in the return rather than a door or arch. It connects to a side passage; the school's busy departure path is beyond another wing and does not cross this recess. The south edge is open to light and unused level ground rather than another through-path. The visible set uses plain wall, paving and the [single bench](bench.md#bench); unused school rooms and the distant main route are outside this place's used area.

The after-lessons weekday-afternoon variant has an unoccupied bench and no incidental pedestrians in this off-route recess. This is a quiet public waiting corner, not an empty school or a locked private room. People may approach or leave through the west gap; a person by the bench facing north has their back toward the approach from the paving to the south. The clear depth south of the bench's front edge is 3.6 metres. Its west-half position leaves a route from the gap to the space south of it. A face-to-face separation of about 0.85 metres allows an arm's-length paper exchange, and half a metre of retreat can clear one person's downward reach. For a supported low reach, feet can remain planted roughly shoulder-width while knees and hips flex and the torso inclines forward. Inward head paths at this separation can meet at forehead level before either hand reaches a note between the feet. A low-speed brush permits a hips-back, knees-straightening recovery without a fall or head recoil. Actual placement, contact and recovery curves still require future motion verification; these are constraints, not a claim that a Human rig has passed them.

Soft late-afternoon daylight enters from the open south side and leaves the north plaster readable behind dark figures and white paper. The air in the recess is still. A small group of garden birds beyond the north wall supplies occasional quiet chirps softened by the wall; it remains outside the courtyard and outside the used view. The calls are an unseen environmental source rather than an individually participating bird in the recess; species and exact count affect no exchange or recognition. Footsteps can make short dry paving taps; cloth and paper make close rustles, and a light forehead brush can make a small contact tick. These sources are available environmental and material facts.
