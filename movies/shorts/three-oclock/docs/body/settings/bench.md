# Stone rehearsal bench

## Fixed form, placement and use {#bench}

One plain backless bench is a matte medium-gray limestone block, 1.6 metres long east to west, 0.4 metres deep north to south and 0.45 metres high. Its flat seat is the top of the solid block; it has no separate legs, loose cushion, metal hardware or moving part. Its edges are slightly eased so the coarse silhouette remains simple. It rests immovably on the level paving with its back long edge against the [courtyard's north wall](courtyard.md#courtyard). Its centre is 2.2 metres east of the west boundary, so its left and right ends lie 1.4 and 3.0 metres from that boundary. The block's south edge leaves the courtyard's defined 3.6-metre clear depth.

The after-lessons baseline leaves the seat unoccupied and the area immediately south clear. The bench can serve as a fixed addressee for someone rehearsing, without becoming a hiding place or a moving obstacle. It is world furniture with no personal custody. Its stone does not sound or move by itself; any later sitting, touch or impact would need an actual performed contact. This short's required note exchange can occur in the clear paving south of it without contact with the block.
