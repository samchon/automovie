# The rivalry

## Familiar opponents {#rivalry}

Carina and Harry are fifteen-year-old Hogwarts schoolmates who meet voluntarily for private practice. Neither has authority over the other. Both expect nonlethal wand shots, close evasions and brief arm contact; a grip is a tactical attempt that ends when its holder loses advantage or the other asks for release. Neither agrees to injuring joints, pinning a person after surrender or using danger to demand affection. Their practice has no audience, prize or formal referee. This invented free-practice session is not a school lesson or supervised assessment.

Carina wants Harry's undivided attention and covers personal interest with competition. Harry recognizes her skill but initially reads her approach as the next tactic. Their familiarity makes a repeated gesture recognizable without an account of earlier matches. Each can choose to stop the duel, and concern for the other's immediate safety takes precedence over winning. Protective contact may alter their mutual attention; it does not establish a couple, a promise or a confession. Acknowledged concern can coexist with a return to competitive posture.
