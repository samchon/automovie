# The damaged stone support

## Support, cap and residual debris {#support}

One abandoned grey stone support stands on the [court's](training-court.md#court-and-support) mapped base. It is a fixed architectural remnant, not a participant or an active training device. The square base is one metre across and 0.12 metres high. A plain upright rises from it: x=1.4 to 2.0, y=2.3 to 2.8, with its top at z=3.0 metres. The upright remains standing throughout this scene. Nobody begins the practice knowing that its projecting cap is about to detach, and neither rival has inspected its joints.

The one rectangular cap is 0.8 metres wide east to west, one metre deep north to south and 0.25 metres thick. Initially it occupies x=1.3 to 2.1, y=1.3 to 2.3, z=2.70 to 2.95. It projects south from the upright's front face rather than resting over solid masonry. A shallow stone bearing ledge at its rear lower edge supports it at y=2.3, z=2.70. A small mortar fillet around that rear lower contact is exposed on the south-facing joint; it is already cracked. A remaining mortar web across the upper rear contact holds the cap against the upright. The cracked lower fillet, rear bearing and upper retaining web are distinct parts of this one assembly, not three separate obstacles. The joints are dull light grey against the darker stone and can be read as broad simple strips at polygon scale.

Removing the weakened lower fillet allows the cap's front to tip down while the rear bearing and upper web briefly retain it. South of the upright there is free air for that rotation: the cap does not have to pass through the upright. Failure of the last retaining web permits the cap to leave the shallow bearing and fall south to the floor. The conservative main-mass danger region is x=1.2 to 2.2, y=0.8 to 2.4. A whole person and carried wand entirely west of x=1.0 have clearance from that region. The bearing is not a permanent hinge, and a shield cannot suspend the cap. Exact fracture, rotation and clearance performance remain to be verified in future geometry and motion; these are authored constraints, not a tested physical simulation.

The cap remains one principal block when it lands. The assembly also owns a finite residual group: two angular chips no larger than four centimetres from the first joint strike, one short stream of sand-sized mortar grit from the loosening contact, and three angular stone fragments no larger than eight centimetres from the floor impact. All share the support's grey stone or light-grey mortar material. Chips and grit settle beside the base; the three impact fragments can scatter west toward the pair after their main-mass escape. They are the only objects the [shield](wand-magic.md#wand-magic) must stop. There is no continuing shower, secondary collapse or newly exposed hazard.

A single brief grey dust plume may rise from the cap's floor impact within about a metre of the fallen block, remaining below the standing pair's waists and thinning promptly. It cannot conceal whether they cleared the main mass or obscure the later hand release. The screenplay owns the actual strike, successive joint states, rotation, release, impact, debris arrivals and sound onsets. The cap's initial suspension is silent; grit, grinding, the heavy floor impact and fragment ticks become audible only with those events.
