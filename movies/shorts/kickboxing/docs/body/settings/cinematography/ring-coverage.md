# Ring coverage

## Lens kit {#lens}

### One moving camera {#one-camera}

One camera inside the ring with a normal-to-short-telephoto vertical field of view, so faces and gloves read at medium size without wide-angle distortion. It never cuts: the whole film is one take, as the [fight coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#law) records.

## Framing grammar {#framing}

### Sizes {#sizes}

Wide while the fighters reset, holding both full figures, the referee and canvas around them; medium two-shot while an exchange is live; a push-in on the man hit by a clean landing. Each size holds long enough to be read before the next change.

### Side-on {#side-on}

The camera watches both fighters side-on from across their line, on the side away from the referee, so he never blocks an exchange.

## Camera movement {#movement}

### Operator {#operator}

The camera behaves like a ringside operator: it moves only when the action gives it a reason, orbits around the fighters rather than crossing between them and follows a beat behind.

### The finish {#finish}

From the last exchange before the finish, the camera widens into a side-on view that holds the kick, the fall, the neutral corner, the count and A's celebration to the end without a cut.

## Lighting style {#lighting}

### Neutral {#neutral}

Neutral overhead light with soft frontal fill, as the [ring](../sets/ring.md#light) describes; the light never changes during the film.

## Grade {#grade}

### Clean {#clean}

A clean, slightly cool grade over the dark surround; skin tones and glove colours stay true so the fighters remain distinct.
