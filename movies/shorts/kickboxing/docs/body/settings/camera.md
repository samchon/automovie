# Camera

## Look {#look}

The bout plays as one continuous take inside the ring, on the original Human polygon figures, without a cut. The camera behaves like a ringside operator. It watches both fighters side-on from across their line, on the side away from the referee, so he never blocks an exchange. It moves only when the action gives it a reason, and it follows a beat behind, as a person carrying a camera would. [Ring craft](../../research/ring-craft.md#camera) records the broadcast and fight-film sources.

## Framing {#framing}

While the fighters reset, the view is wide enough to hold both full figures, the referee and canvas around them. When an exchange starts, it closes to the two fighters. On a clean landing it pushes in on the man who was hit. It settles on each framing long enough to be read before the next change.

## Anchors {#anchors}

At 143 seconds the camera begins to widen into the side-on view of the finish. It holds that view without a cut through the Brazilian kick, the fall, the neutral corner, the count and A's celebration to the end of the film.
