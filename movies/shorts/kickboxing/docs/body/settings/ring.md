# Ring

## Space and boundaries {#space}

The fictional indoor ring has a six-metre square usable fighting area within a 6.4-metre pale matte canvas. Four ropes enclose it, with padded posts outside the usable mat. A dark apron separates the canvas from the surrounding darkness. Two diagonally opposite light-padded corners are neutral; the other corners are red and cyan. Fighters and referee have clear routes inside the ropes.

A begins screen-left and B screen-right, facing one another near center. The referee begins behind their line, clear of their first exchange. A far neutral corner remains reachable by a supported diagonal retreat after the knockdown. [Scenery source](../../../src/assets/ring/createInspectionRing.ts) owns the executable geometry; authored dimensions do not establish certified competition equipment.

## Light and sound {#light}

Broad neutral overhead light and soft frontal fill separate the gloves, limbs and contact shadows. The surrounding audience stays outside the image. Close breaths, glove impacts, foot friction and the referee's voice carry a restrained indoor reverberation. Canvas landings sound duller than glove-on-glove contact. A clear bell marks the start and official end; there is no music.

## Rules {#rules}

The contest adopts relevant [GLORY v2.2 principles](../../research/technique.md#rules): legal targets, obedience to the referee, separation to the farthest neutral corner after a knockdown and no attack on a downed opponent. At 80 kg the fictional fighters lie within that document's middleweight range; the film claims no accredited event or separate 80 kg division. The [referee](referee.md) owns the count and result. The screenplay chooses the exchanges and their outcome.

## Zones and landmarks {#zones}

The centre of the canvas is the ground both fighters want; taking it back is a beat of its own. Around it lies open ring, where they circle and trade. Near the ropes, a body length and two short steps from them, a fighter starts to feel trapped and circles out toward the side with more room. The red and cyan corners belong to the fighters' teams. The two light-padded neutral corners are where the referee sends a fighter after a knockdown, always the one farthest from the fallen man.
