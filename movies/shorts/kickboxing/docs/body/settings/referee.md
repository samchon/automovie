# Referee

## Identity and voice {#identity}

The referee is an adult man, 178 cm tall and 76 kg, with a regular athletic build. A white long-sleeve shirt, black trousers, black shoes and bare hands distinguish him from both fighters. His focused face and independent head turns show which exchange he is watching. He speaks clear, firm English in a middle register, using brief commands and an even count.

The original Human polygon figure has his own body, root, hands and visible face. His mouth opens during speech and closes between phrases; this production does not promise phoneme-level lip sync. His open-hand start signal and counting fingers remain readable from the chosen camera.

## Position and authority {#authority}

He takes short supported steps outside the fighters' immediate paths, turning to keep their hands, head movement and support in view. He can direct the standing fighter toward the farthest neutral corner while watching the fallen fighter. Counting begins when the standing fighter is inside that corner facing ring center; leaving it pauses the count.

He assesses readiness at eight. An uninterrupted count to ten ends this contest by knockout, after which he signals the result and attends to the downed fighter. The [adopted rules](../../research/technique.md#rules) establish his authority, including an earlier stoppage for immediate assistance when necessary. His words, gestures and actual timing belong to the screenplay; he gives no medical diagnosis.

## Role behaviors {#behaviors}

These behaviors follow the officiating mechanics in [ring craft](../../research/ring-craft.md#referee).

### Positioning {#positioning}

He is never idle. He walks with the circling fighters and keeps a side view across their line, as far from them as the ring allows. He stays out of their paths, never stands behind a fighter or between a fighter and the ropes, and never crosses between the fighters.

### Closing in {#closing}

He shortens his distance when the fighters work on the ropes, when they enter the clinch for a knee, and when a stoppage may be near. He signals and steps in at an angle, never from behind.

### Stance and signals {#signals}

He keeps an officiating stance: knees soft and both hands ready in front of the waist. He uses brief commands: "Fight!" at the start, "Down!" at a knockdown, "Neutral corner!" with a pointing arm, then the count with his fingers, and a two-handed sweep to end the contest.

### Knockdown {#knockdown}

He sends the standing fighter to the farthest neutral corner and keeps him in view while counting over the fallen fighter. He stops the count if the standing fighter leaves the corner.
