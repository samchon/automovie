# Championship exchanges

The owner judged the earlier bout a sequence of single strikes, without the combinations, feints and layered defense of elite kickboxers, and required a screenplay that reads as a fight between champions. The owner also fixed the finish as a Brazilian (question-mark) kick instead of a plain high roundhouse. This page records the sources behind the adopted exchange patterns and how the [screenplay](../body/screenplay/kickboxing.md) uses them. The sources are coaching articles, fight reports and the rulebook. None of them supplies measured joint angles or phase timings, so the motion still derives those from the actual rig and its tests.

## Rules that bound the choreography {#rules}

[GLORY Official Rules v2.2](https://glory.pinkyellow.network/assets/rules/glory-rules-2026-v2.pdf), effective 16 March 2026, section 5.07.A, which the [technique research](technique.md#rules) already adopts for the count:

- A.4 bans linear or thrusting kicks to the legs. Leg attacks must arc, so every low kick here is a round kick and the teeps go to the stomach.
- A.7 bans elbows, and A.10 bans throws, sweeps and pushes meant to off-balance or down an opponent. The film uses none of them.
- A.11 permits a clinch only to throw an immediate single knee; the athlete must then disengage. Each knee here, in 45–60 and 105–120, comes from a brief two-glove hold behind the head and releases at once.
- A.12 permits catching a leg only for a single immediate strike with at most one step. The film blocks or checks kicks instead of catching them, so no rule-bound catch timing has to be staged.
- C.1 counts continuously raising the front leg without a technique as stalling. Every check here answers a real or feinted kick.

## Combinations end in kicks {#combinations}

Dutch kickboxing, as taught by Ernesto Hoost, Peter Aerts and Ramon Dekkers, throws two-, three- or four-strike punch combinations and finishes them with a kick. Hoost's signature was a left hook to the liver followed almost immediately by a right low kick. Against Andy Hug he combined a right cross, a left uppercut and a right low kick. A left hook puts the opponent's weight on the lead leg, which makes the following right low kick hard to check. Thom Harinck described Aerts hiding high kicks behind punches or pushes.

- [Dynamic Striking: combining punches and kicks with Ernesto Hoost](https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/combining-our-punches-kicks-with-ernesto-hoost)
- [Bloody Elbow: Dutch Muay Thai moves to remember](https://bloodyelbow.com/2018/05/10/dutch-muay-thai-moves-to-remember-part-1-ernesto-hoost-k1-peter-aerts-ramon-dekkers-rob-kaman-glory)
- [Evolve MMA: Dutch kickboxing's influence on modern Muay Thai](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/the-influence-of-dutch-kickboxing-on-modern-muay-thai-fighters/)
- [GLORY: The Art of Low Kicks](https://glorykickboxing.com/news/the-art-of-low-kicks)

Adopted:
- A's jab, cross, hook and right low kick (15–30).
- A's jab, cross, body hook and low kick (45–60).
- B's jab, cross, liver hook and right low kick (90–105, 105–120 and 135–150).
- B's jab, cross, hook and right high kick (75–90).
- Combinations that end in body kicks (60–75 and 120–135).

## Defense keeps attacking {#defense}

Checking raises the bent lead leg so the shin meets the incoming kick. Coaches stress countering at once from the check, before the kicker recovers; a late counter gives the kicker time for another attack. Elite counter-strikers such as Giorgio Petrosyan work at the edge of punching range, slip or step back into counters, and draw predictable returns by attacking the guard.

- [Evolve MMA: how to check kicks](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/how-to-check-kicks-in-muay-thai)
- [Diesel Gym: how to defend low kicks](https://dieselgym.co.uk/how-to-defend-low-kicks-diesel-technique-of-the-week/)
- [MMA Sucka: Giorgio Petrosyan, diagnosing the doctor](https://mmasucka.com/opinion/diagnosing-doctor-giorgio-petrosyan/)

Adopted:
- Every checked low kick except A's last one in 135–150 is answered from the replanted leg: by A in 0–15, 30–45, 75–90, 90–105, 105–120 and 135–150, and by B in 30–45.
- Combinations are met with a parry, slip, roll and block in turn rather than a single cover.
- The slip outside a cross is followed by a left hook to the liver (15–30).
- The step-back sway (0–15) and the pivoting check hook (45–60).

## Feints change the guard {#feints}

A drag or hip-turn feint of the rear low kick makes the opponent lift his leg to check. The real kick or a straight punch lands while he stands on one leg or as he replants. A pump jab moves the opponent's hands before the real attack.

- [Dynamic Striking: attacks off the jab with Jake Mainini](https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/attacks-off-the-jab-with-jake-mainini)
- [Evolve MMA: advanced striking set-ups and combinations](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/20-advanced-mma-striking-set-ups-and-combinations-you-should-add-to-your-arsenal/)

Adopted:
- Pump-jab feints into body attacks (0–15, 15–30, 30–45 and 105–120).
- B's low-kick feint into the real low kick (30–45) and into the cross (120–135).
- A's pump jab and low-kick feint into the straight right (75–90).
- A's body-kick feint into the cross over the lowered arm (120–135).

These scripted feints have consequences. The ambient feints of the rhythm layer between exchanges remain separate.

## Setting up the head kick {#head-kick}

Superbon stopped Petrosyan in 2021 after body kicks. He said he anticipated Petrosyan's return punch, changed to a hook and followed with the head kick. Patterned body kicks teach an opponent to lower the near arm over the ribs.

- [Sportskeeda: how Superbon set up the head kick knockout of Petrosyan](https://www.sportskeeda.com/mma/news-one-championship-how-superbon-singha-mawynn-set-head-kick-ko-giorgio-petrosyan)

Adopted:
- A lands the right body kick in 60–75, then repeats it later in that group and in 120–135 and 135–150, where B blocks.
- A's body-kick feint in 120–135 shows that B drops his left arm whenever A's right knee rises, and A's cross punishes the opening.

## The Brazilian kick {#brazilian-kick}

The Brazilian or question-mark kick is associated with Ademir da Costa and with Glaube Feitosa's K-1 knockouts. It starts in the chamber of a body or low kick. The support heel turns toward the target, the hips roll over and the knee rises above the target line, so the lower leg comes down over the opponent's guard. It works when the opponent reads a body kick and lowers his arm.

Target and striking surface differ by variant:
- Feitosa's 2005 K-1 USA knockouts landed on the side of the head from above, "behind Williams' right ear" in one fight, with the leg falling "in a sort of slingshot fashion" ([Sherdog: Feitosa captures K-1 USA crown](https://www.sherdog.com/news/articles/1/Feitosa-Captures-K1-USA-Crown-2766)).
- Technique guides aim the head version at the chin or side of the head, striking with the instep or the ball of the foot; the shin is the surface of the low-kick variant ([Lowkick MMA: question mark kick](https://www.lowkickmma.com/question-mark-kick/), [Evolve MMA: how to throw a question mark kick](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/how-to-throw-a-question-mark-kick-in-muay-thai/)).
- One coaching guide lists the head, neck or collarbone as targets of the descending kick ([Fiji Muay Thai](https://fijimuaythai.com/?p=2048)).

Further background: [Lowkick MMA: technique of the day, the Brazilian kick](https://lowkickmma.com/technique-of-the-day-the-brazilian-kick) and [Wikipedia: Glaube Feitosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaube_Feitosa).

Adopted: at 150.000, A's right Brazilian kick starts in the same chamber as his body kicks. The knee climbs above B's shoulder line and turns over, and the lower leg descends over B's lowered left arm. A's instep and distal shin strike behind B's left ear, at the junction of jaw and neck — not the crown, and not a lateral shin swing to the jaw. The descending blow drives B's head toward his right shoulder, so he still falls onto his right side. The [kick guard decision](kick-guard.md) applies unchanged: A's kicking-side right glove reaches forward at the head line and his left glove guards through chamber, delivery, recoil and replant.

## Output rate {#output}

Public GLORY statistics are event- or fighter-specific rather than league averages. A heavyweight round in [GLORY's CroCop–Bonjasky numbers](https://glorykickboxing.com/news/by-the-numbers-crocop-vs-bonjasky) shows 24 and 31 strikes thrown. A [FightMetrics-based summary](https://fightforecast.substack.com/p/giga-chikadze-has-better-stats-in) credits Giga Chikadze with 7.46 strikes landed per minute. The composed bout derives 68 attacks for A and 70 for B in 150 seconds, including their scripted feints. That is about 27 per fighter-minute, deliberately denser than a typical full round for a two-and-a-half-minute film. Recovery steps, circling and resets still separate the combinations within every fifteen-second group.
