# Guard during kicks

The owner requires the kicking-side arm to extend forward while the opposite hand protects the head, through preparation, delivery and recovery. This applies to low, body and high round kicks on either side. It replaces this film's downward/backward arm-swing intent. The production adopts this specific defensive variant; a style-dependent coaching choice is not a universal physical law.

[Ruerngsa, Charuad and Cartmell, *Muay Thai: The Art of Fighting*](https://www.singto.co.uk/Techniques/artoffightingebook.pdf), printed/PDF pp.98–100, is the direct coaching reference. Page99 warns that pulling the kicking-side arm behind the body exposes counters and delays recovery, and recommends keeping the fists in front of the face. The surrounding pages cover support rotation and possible counters. The book supplies no calibrated arm angles or measured phase timings. The retained [page99](../../references/private/kick-guard/manual-099.png) and adjacent pages were opened at 1400 px; they are coaching text and an isolated photograph, not a recorded complete cycle.

Different demonstrations use different arm actions. Aaron Swenson's [original FightCamp lesson](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/training/how-to-do-a-roundhouse-kick-in-kickboxing/), “Upper Body,” explicitly teaches an arm-swing variant. The original publisher's [Trevor Wittman lesson](https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/how-this-one-drill-can-help-you-deliver-devastating-round-kicks-according-to-coach-trevor-wittman), “Applying The Kick,” describes leading with an extended striking hand while coordinating shoulder and hip rotation. Its YouTube embed returned player error153; the normal watch page now reports members-only access. No video was acquired, watched or bypassed, and its prose does not establish an observed phase sequence.

Gavagan and Sayers' [2017 measured comparison](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182645) supports treating the round kick as coordinated pelvis, hip, knee and COM motion. Its fixed head-height target and expert sample do not establish a single competitive guard. The existing locally inspected phase figure remains a coordination reference, with the limits already recorded in the index.

The implementation should derive forward reach from the actual arm and glove geometry, keep the glove at the protective head line and blend with the kick's common preparation/recovery phase. The opposite glove stays beside the head. This geometry and the bilateral application are production inferences implementing the owner's chosen performance. No world-space endpoint adjustment, reference-pixel angle or balance-only hand drop is justified by these sources.

Verification must inspect both sides at preparation, chamber, contact, recoil and replant, including overhead/side views with actual rig guides. Fitted goals must preserve head clearance and arm reach; the actual physical frames must retain the protective shape without self penetration. Source tests, static goal measurements and screenshots establish their respective scopes. Continuous normal-speed playback and actual receiver interaction remain required before accepting the film.
