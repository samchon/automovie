# Referee mechanics

The owner judged an earlier cut's referee idle. This study records how a professional referee positions himself, closes in, signals and runs a knockdown, the laws his behavior follows and the measurable targets a composed round must meet.

## Positioning {#positioning}

The [Association of Boxing Commissions Referee Manual of Professional Boxing](https://www.abcboxing.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/box-ref-manual-july-2022.pdf) (rev. July 2022) sets out the positioning rules adopted here:

- Stay as far from the boxers as possible, use the perimeter of the ring and move gracefully, without bouncing.
- Keep the pace of circling boxers while holding a side view of both.
- Keep a window between the boxers and never get caught behind them or the action.
- Shorten the distance when they fight on or near the ropes, in infighting and clinches, and when a stoppage may be near.
- Break at an angle, never from behind, and never walk between the boxers after a break.

Law: the referee's desired point lies across the fighters' line from their centre, on the side he already holds unless it runs out of room, in which case he goes around behind the action rather than between the fighters. He stands as far out as the ring allows, but no farther than one and a half times his close distance, so he can keep pace with circling fighters (film choice reconciling "as far as possible" with "keep the pace"), keeping his own shoulder half-width inside the ropes. On the ropes and in a clinch he closes until one step and his arm's reach bring him to the nearer fighter, so he can break them. He walks, never runs: a comfortable adult walking speed is 1.39 m/s and the maximum walking speed 2.53 m/s ([Bohannon 1997](https://doi.org/10.1093/ageing/26.1.15), Age and Ageing 26(1):15–19, men in their twenties); fighters circling at a fighting cadence turn their line faster than a comfortable walk can follow from his working distance, so he walks briskly, at most the maximum walking speed. Film choice: he aims at where the fighters were half a second earlier, so he follows rather than anticipates.

## Stance and signals {#signals}

The manual's stance keeps the knees soft and the hands ready. Commands are brief: "Fight!" with a palm that rises and sweeps down to start, "Down!" with a pointing hand at a knockdown, "Neutral corner!" with a pointing arm, the count with fingers, and both hands sweeping across the body to end the contest.

## Knockdown {#knockdown}

At a knockdown he sends the standing boxer to the farthest neutral corner, keeps him in view while counting and stops the count if he leaves. The count follows the [rules](../kickboxing/rules.md#knockdown). Law: the farthest neutral corner is the neutral corner farthest from the fallen fighter; the referee counts from a point beside the fallen fighter that keeps that corner in his view. He stands a step clear of the lying body, never over it, on the far side of the body from the standing fighter, so that facing the fallen man keeps the corner inside the central 120° of his binocular field ([Spector, Clinical Methods, ch. 116: visual fields](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK220/)), and his hands, signalling over the body, stay in the open; he walks around the body, never across it.

## Count and decision {#count}

The referee announces each second of the count with a downward motion of his arm, timed at one-second intervals ([Florida Admin. Code 61K1-3.033](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61K1-3-033)); in Muay Thai he counts one to ten after a one-second wait and shows a hand signal for each second ([WMC Muay Thai rules §16, knock-down](https://wmc.muaythai.sport/about-muaythai/muaythai-rules/16-knock-down)). At a knockout he may raise the winner's hand ([WAC 36-12-140](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/WACArchive/2024/htm/WAC%20%2036%20-%2012%20%20CHAPTER/WAC%20%2036%20-%2012%20-140.htm)). The film's referee raises his counting arm beside his head early in each second and brings it down pointing at the fallen man, keeps his feet a step clear of the lying body, and at the winner signal raises his hand toward A. The figure's hands carry no articulated fingers, so the finger count itself is not shown.

## Measurable targets {#targets}

- A side view of the fighters' line for at least 90% of the bout: the angle between his direction from their centre and their line is at least 60°.
- He never stands between the fighters.
- He keeps moving: at least 1 m of walking in every fifteen-second phase of the bout.
- From the knockdown he is never closer than one of his steps to the lying body, and from the count on he faces the fallen fighter with the standing fighter within 60° of his facing.
