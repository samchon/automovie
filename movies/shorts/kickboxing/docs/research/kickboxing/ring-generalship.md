# Ring generalship

The owner judged an earlier cut static: the fighters only moved forward and back. This study records how champions use the ring between and during exchanges, the laws the footwork follows and the measurable targets a composed round must meet. None of the sources supplies exact speeds; each value below states its derivation.

## Coaching sources {#sources}

Coaching sources agree on four behaviors:

- Do not chase or back straight up. Move laterally, take angles, and keep the opponent in front ([Evolve MMA: strategic footwork, angles and lateral movement in Muay Thai](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/strategic-footwork-using-angles-and-lateral-movement-to-control-the-ring-in-muay-thai/), [Evolve MMA: ring generalship for boxing](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/how-to-improve-ring-generalship-for-boxing/)).
- To cut off the ring, mirror the opponent's lateral escape rather than following his path, and take away the directions he can move until he reaches the ropes or a corner ([Dynamic Striking: how to cut off the ring with Teddy Atlas](https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/how-to-cut-off-the-ring-with-teddy-atlas), [Fight Primer: cutting off the ring](https://www.fightprimer.com/finding-the-art-cutting-the-ring-99-yo)).
- Circle away from the opponent's power hand. For two orthodox fighters, that means toward one's own left, away from the opponent's rear hand.
- Pivot off the line instead of retreating straight back. A fighter on the ropes circles out toward the side with more room.

The [IBA Coaches Manual](https://www.iba.sport/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/AIBA-Coach-Regulations-Manual_WEB.pdf), printed pages 36–39, adds the stepping rule: the foot on the side of travel moves first and the other follows to restore the stance's separation, so the feet never cross.

## Movements {#movements}

| Movement | Law |
| --- | --- |
| Circle | The mover steps toward his lead side, so the pair rotates about the opponent. |
| Press | The presser walks in. The receiver gives ground on a 45° diagonal toward his lead side while the presser cuts across at half that lateral speed, mirroring the escape. |
| Escape | Inside the rope zone, the receiver circles off toward the side with more room. |
| Pivot | The mover swings a quarter circle around the opponent on his lead side, turning on the lead foot. |
| Centre | The pair walks back toward the middle of the ring. |
| Range | Each strike's entry brings the attacker to that strike's own range; after the last strike he steps back out to outside range. |

Both stances hold their ground while a strike is in flight; footwork resumes at the strike's replant.

## Exit {#exit}

Coaches teach stepping off the line after a combination, toward the side it finished on: a lead-hand finish pivots or steps to the lead side, a rear-hand finish slides out to the rear side, the guard staying up and a safety jab covering the way back to the outside ([Easton BJJ/Kickboxing: focus of the week](https://eastonbjj.com/news/focus-of-the-week-12516-2/), [Easton: focus of the week 6/20/16](https://eastonbjj.com/move-of-the-week/focus-of-the-week-62016/), [Fighters Only, issue 133](https://fightersonly.com/article/ext/38168/Issue+133/1)). Straight back keeps a fighter on his opponent's line and reads as backing up. The film's exit lands at outside range one step off the line toward the last strike's side: the arc whose chord is one step on the circle of the exit range.

## Stance {#stance}

Kickboxing and Muay Thai coaching describe the stance in words, not angles ([ONE Championship: mastering the Muay Thai stance](https://www.onefc.com/lifestyle/muay-thai-stance/), [LowKickMMA: Muay Thai stance](https://www.lowkickmma.com/muay-thai-stance), [Evolve MMA: common footwork mistakes in Muay Thai](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/commons-footwork-mistakes-in-muay-thai/)). The film translates them into ranges:

| Quantity | Coaching | Film range | Film value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Knee flexion | "knees slightly bent", never straight or locked, so either leg lifts at once to check or kick | 25–45°, above the straight-legged IBA boxing drawing and up to a deep crouch | 32° |
| Stance length | "a little more than shoulder-width" (ONE) to "at or slightly smaller than shoulder-width" (LowKickMMA) | 0.9–1.3 shoulder widths | 1.2 |
| Weight | "evenly distributed over both legs" (ONE), shifting with the technique | even between the feet | even |
| Guard rhythm | light feet, a pulse through the knees rather than bouncing on the toes (ONE); the lead glove paws to measure distance | knee pulse a third of the stance drop, lead glove a fifth of the reach, at the stepping cadence | as stated |

The picture measure of stance knee flexion is accepted inside the film range.

## Step and margin {#step}

- A step is at most half the fighter's stance length, so the following foot can restore the stance without crossing (IBA). The stance length is measured on the fighter's own body.
- Film choice: one step every 0.35 s between exchanges, about three per second. No source in this archive measures fighters' step cadence; the owner judged two per second, a walking cadence (about 100–120 steps per minute; [Tudor-Locke et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2017-097628)), as slow, so the film takes the light cadence of running. Ouergui's low-intensity phases of 2.3 ± 0.8 s ([exchanges](exchanges.md#activity)) bound how long a footwork phase lasts, not its cadence.
- An entry is at most one stance length: the lead foot steps or lunges and the rear foot drags behind to restore the stance (IBA stepping jab).
- Outside range, where both fighters stand off and feint between exchanges, is one step beyond the [range](techniques.md#reach) of the longest kick, the teep: from there no strike lands without its entry.
- A stance origin stays inside the ropes by the fighter's shoulder half-width plus his glove, measured on his body. The rope zone is that margin plus two steps, the distance in which a retreating fighter has two steps left.

## Measurable targets {#targets}

- At least 30% of the fighters' footwork path is lateral to their line.
- The line between the fighters turns through at least one full revolution over the bout.
- The pair works on the ropes for some of the bout but no more than a quarter of it.
- Both fighters always stay inside the ropes.

Measured on the displayed bodies across the whole film (window.__afReport().measures):

- Mean stance-origin distance at outside range at least the teep's range; during exchanges between the shortest punch range and outside range.
- Close range (under clinch range) outside pressing, the clinch and the finish: at most 10% of those instants.
- Stance knee flexion inside 25–45°.
- Foot travel at least 20 m per fighter-minute, a third of a 0.35 s cadence of half-stance steps, with at least 30% of it lateral.
- Idle share (no hand or foot faster than 0.1 m/s) at most 10%.
- Median peak fist speed of punches, at the glove's knuckles, inside 7.3–12 m/s ([punch timing](techniques.md#punch-timing)); median peak midfoot speed of round kicks inside 10.94–15.54 m/s, the Muay Thai maximum linear foot velocity of 13.24 ± 2.30 m/s at the metatarsal markers ([kick timing](techniques.md#kick-timing)).
