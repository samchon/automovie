# Ring generalship

The owner judged an earlier cut static: the fighters only moved forward and back. This study records how champions use the ring between and during exchanges, the laws the footwork follows and the measurable targets a composed round must meet. None of the sources supplies exact speeds; each value below states its derivation.

## Coaching sources {#sources}

Coaching sources agree on four behaviors:

- Do not chase or back straight up. Move laterally, take angles, and keep the opponent in front ([Evolve MMA: strategic footwork, angles and lateral movement in Muay Thai](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/strategic-footwork-using-angles-and-lateral-movement-to-control-the-ring-in-muay-thai/), [Evolve MMA: ring generalship for boxing](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/how-to-improve-ring-generalship-for-boxing/)).
- To cut off the ring, mirror the opponent's lateral escape rather than following his path, and take away the directions he can move until he reaches the ropes or a corner ([Dynamic Striking: how to cut off the ring with Teddy Atlas](https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/how-to-cut-off-the-ring-with-teddy-atlas), [Fight Primer: cutting off the ring](https://www.fightprimer.com/finding-the-art-cutting-the-ring-99-yo)).
- Circle away from the opponent's power hand. For two orthodox fighters, that means toward one's own left, away from the opponent's rear hand.
- Pivot off the line instead of retreating straight back. A fighter on the ropes circles out toward the side with more room.

The [IBA Coaches Manual](https://www.iba.sport/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/AIBA-Coach-Regulations-Manual_WEB.pdf), printed pages 36–39, adds the stepping rule: the foot on the side of travel moves first and the other follows to restore the stance's separation, so the feet never cross.

## Movements {#movements}

| Movement | Law |
| --- | --- |
| Circle | The mover steps toward his lead side, so the pair rotates about the opponent. |
| Press | The presser walks in. The receiver gives ground on a 45° diagonal toward his lead side while the presser cuts across at half that lateral speed, mirroring the escape. |
| Escape | Inside the rope zone, the receiver circles off toward the side with more room. |
| Pivot | The mover swings a quarter circle around the opponent on his lead side, turning on the lead foot. |
| Centre | The pair walks back toward the middle of the ring. |
| Range | Before an exchange, the attacker steps in to the range his combination needs; after it, both step out to kicking distance. |

Both stances hold their ground while a strike is in flight; footwork resumes at the strike's replant.

## Step and margin {#step}

- A step is at most half the fighter's stance length, so the following foot can restore the stance without crossing (IBA). The stance length is measured on the fighter's own body.
- Film choice: two steps per second between exchanges, a walking cadence. No source in this archive measures fighters' step cadence; Ouergui's low-intensity phases of 2.3 ± 0.8 s ([exchanges](exchanges.md#activity)) bound how long a footwork phase lasts, not its cadence.
- A stance origin stays inside the ropes by the fighter's shoulder half-width plus his glove, measured on his body. The rope zone is that margin plus two steps, the distance in which a retreating fighter has two steps left.

## Measurable targets {#targets}

- At least 30% of the fighters' footwork path is lateral to their line.
- The line between the fighters turns through at least one full revolution over the bout.
- The pair works on the ropes for some of the bout but no more than a quarter of it.
- Both fighters always stay inside the ropes.
