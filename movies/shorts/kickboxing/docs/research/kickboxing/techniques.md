# Kickboxing technique guide for authoring and rigging

Current synthesis, 2026-11-03. Read this alongside the [source and rights index](../index.md). Source-observed relations below come from the actual locally opened diagrams, photographs and selected timestamped frames. **Inference** means a mechanical interpretation, not a measured source result. **Film choice** means a staging decision. No reference clip or produced film was continuously watched in this study. The retained MP4 was sampled into explicitly identified frames; audio was not reviewed.

This is a movement-authoring guide, not a substitute for supervised physical training. IBA provides a real coaching textbook for the boxing subset. Evolve and GLORY supply genuine Muay Thai/professional kickboxing coaching, with variant and rules limits. The Jeff Chan material is a cooperative MMA striking instructional, not a kickboxing bout. Transfer the demonstrated hip/leg/support coordination; do not transfer takedown tactics, unrestricted catches, elbow use, or a relaxed teaching guard into this commissioned kickboxing exchange. PLOS includes a measured Muay Thai subgroup; Karate/Taekwondo results do not define this film's stance.

## Laws {#laws}

The laws below are what the behaviors read. Each value keeps its source, unit and conditions; a value without a measurement says so and names the analogue it borrows. Every shape is stated in the performer's own body frame (+X forward, +Y up, +Z own left), never in ring coordinates, and every target is a region of the receiver's actual body, never a point in space.

### Punch timing {#punch-timing}

- A single maximal jab takes 0.405 s from movement onset to contact, the shortest delivery of six punch types measured in fifteen amateur boxers ([Stanley, Thomson, Smith and Lamb 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/24748668.2018.1525651), International Journal of Performance Analysis in Sport 18(5):835–854, abstract). The film uses it as the delivery of a combination's first punch.
- Inside a combination, a punch's projection starts at the previous punch's impact. Four alternating-hand punches took 1.06 ± 0.46 s (left lead) and 1.04 ± 0.42 s (right lead) from the first contact to the fourth; four same-hand punches took 1.35 ± 0.27 s ([Piorkowski, Lees and Barton 2011](https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/3757/1/piorkowski-et-al-2011-punch-kinematics.pdf), Sports Biomechanics 10(1):1–11, Results; ten competition-standard boxers, pooled punch types). The contact-to-contact interval is therefore 1.05 / 3 = 0.35 s between alternating hands and 1.35 / 3 = 0.45 s between two punches of the same hand.
- An alternating hand completes one projection and one recovery in two intervals, so a punch's recovery to the guard takes the remaining interval, 0.35 s.
- Hooks reach higher contact speed but take longer than straights in single maximal delivery (Piorkowski 2011); the combination intervals above are pooled over straights and hooks, so the film keeps one interval for both inside combinations.
- Fist speed: the single maximal cross reached 8.22 ± 1.08 m/s at contact (Piorkowski 2011, Discussion, wrist marker), against 8.9 m/s for one professional and 8.16 m/s for amateurs in earlier studies; normalised straight-punch peak speeds spread from about 7.5 to 15 m/s (its Figure 5). Measured crosses to lead hooks span 7.3–12 m/s (Frontiers in Sports and Active Living 2024). The film times each punch's extension so the fist, the glove's end point, peaks at the cross's 8.22 m/s along its own path from the guard, and the picture measure reads the speed at that end point, the glove's knuckles.

### Kick timing {#kick-timing}

Eight expert Muay Thai practitioners kicked a target at mastoid height ([Gavagan and Sayers 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182645), PLOS ONE 12(8):e0182645, Results). Execution ran from the support leg's toe-down to the kicking heel's touchdown: 1.02 ± 0.15 s in total, preparation 0.074 ± 0.018 s, recoil 0.65 ± 0.17 s, with chamber and extension taking 14% and 11% of the execution.

- Launch to chamber key: preparation plus chamber, 0.074 + 0.14 × 1.02 = 0.22 s.
- Launch to impact: chamber key plus extension, 0.22 + 0.11 × 1.02 = 0.33 s.
- Impact to replant: the recoil, 0.65 s.
- Foot speed, from the same study's Table 2 (markers on the 1st and 5th metatarsals): the Muay Thai maximum linear foot velocity during the extension is 13.24 ± 2.30 m/s and the velocity at impact 7.22 ± 1.47 m/s, so the foot peaks before contact and still travels at 0.55 of its peak when it lands. A national taekwondo study measured slower roundhouse kicks, 7.36 ± 1.02 and 8.16 ± 0.70 m/s peak foot velocity over five athletes per group ([Detjareny et al., ISBS 2012](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5389), Table 2), and its authors note these sit below earlier reports; elite taekwondo roundhouse kicks reached 14.7 m/s at the toe marker ([Kim, Kim and Im 2011](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-10-31.xml-Fulltext)). The film's picture measure follows the Muay Thai study, whose phases it already uses.

The study measured head-height kicks. The film applies the same phases to the low, body and switch kicks, an approximation stated here because no source in this archive times them separately. The teep is a forward-extended kick with no measured timing; it borrows the round kick's launch-to-impact and recoil as its closest analogue. The clinch knee rises through the chamber only, so it lands at the chamber key and recoils over the same 0.65 s.

### Standing knees {#standing-knees}

A figure that holds its weight on its feet never locks both knees. In walking the knee meets the floor about 5° flexed and stays flexed through the stance phase ([Perry and Burnfield 2010](https://www.routledge.com/Gait-Analysis-Normal-and-Pathological-Function/Perry-Burnfield/p/book/9781556427664), Gait Analysis, chapter 5), so an upright standing or walking figure keeps at least one supporting knee at 5° or more. The knee takes the weight at about 15° in the loading response (the same chapter); the film sinks an upright figure's root until one supporting knee holds that 15°, because near a straight leg a millimetre of hip height is several degrees of knee and a target at 5° itself would straighten under any fitting error. In the fighting stance both knees stay inside the translated 25–45° range ([stance](ring-generalship.md#stance)). Only flight is exempt: the kicking or checking leg, a hop and a fall. The picture measures read, on every standing frame of the displayed bodies, the least knee flexion and the share of frames with both knees under 5°, which must be zero.

### Reach and range {#reach}

A strike reaches only as far as the performer's actual limb plus the step he takes. Range at contact is therefore measured on the two bodies: the distance from the attacker's stance origin to the receiver's target region along the line of attack equals the striking limb's reach from its root joint (shoulder or hip) in the attacker's own frame plus the receiver region's offset from his stance origin. A combination is a range cycle: the fighters stand off at outside range, one ring step beyond the teep's range, from which no strike lands without its entry; every strike carries its own entry from its launch (a step-in or drag that lands with a punch, a slide or switch done by a kick's chamber key) to that strike's own contact range, as the stepping jab carries its own step (IBA), and after the last strike the attacker steps back out to outside range in one push-off beat. Clinch range, the knee's range, belongs only to pressing, the clinch and the finish. No sources supply a fixed range in metres, and none is adopted. A kick's reach runs from the hip to its surface: to the lower shin for a shin kick. The Brazilian kick's instep comes down from the turnover's raised knee, so its range is that geometry's horizontal run: the thigh climbing at the turnover's elevation, then the shank from the knee to the ankle's front, where the instep and distal shin meet on the region behind the ear ([Brazilian kick](brazilian-kick.md#law)).

### Contact surfaces and targets {#surfaces}

| Technique | Attacker surface | Receiver region | Line, in the attacker's frame | Aim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jab, cross | Glove knuckles | Face, or the guard that covers it | Straight forward | Tracks the head until launch |
| Hook | Glove knuckles | Side of the jaw | Arc inward across the body | Tracks until launch |
| Uppercut | Glove knuckles | Chin from below | Rising | Tracks until launch |
| Body cross, body hook | Glove knuckles | Ribs and liver | Straight or arc at waist height | Tracks until launch |
| Low kick | Lower shin | Outside of the lead thigh, or the raised checking shin | Arc, falling slightly | Commits at the chamber key |
| Body kick, switch kick | Lower shin | Ribs below the near elbow, or the arm that blocks | Arc at waist height | Commits at the chamber key |
| High kick | Lower shin | Side of the head, or the raised forearm | Arc at head height | Commits at the chamber key |
| Teep | Sole | Stomach | Straight forward | Tracks until launch |
| Clinch knee | Knee | Stomach | Rising | Tracks until launch |
| Brazilian kick | Instep and distal shin | Behind the ear at the jaw–neck junction | Descending over the guard | Commits at the chamber key |

GLORY bans linear kicks to the legs, so every leg attack arcs ([rules](rules.md#legal-actions)). A kick commits to what the receiver shows at its chamber key, which is how a disguise works: a later evasion relates to the swept line, not to a homing foot.

### Defenses {#defenses}

| Answer | Limb | Meets | Body-frame shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parry | Rear glove | Straight punches, the teep | The glove turns the incoming line aside across the face and returns to the cheek |
| Catch or cover | Glove and forearm on the attacked side | Straight punches, hooks, uppercuts | The glove closes the attacked corridor beside the head, elbow down |
| Slip | Whole body | Straight punches | Head moves outside the punch by knees, hips and trunk, eyes on the opponent |
| Roll | Whole body | Hooks | Head passes under the arc on a curved path through bent knees and hips |
| Sway | Whole body | Straight punches, the end of a combination | Upper body leans back over the rear foot |
| Elbow block | Elbow and forearm on the attacked side | Body punches, body kicks | The elbow drops over the ribs; the other glove stays on the cheek |
| High block | Forearm and glove on the attacked side | High kicks | Forearm braced against the side of the head |
| Check | Lead shin, raised | Low kicks | The bent lead leg lifts with toes pulled up and turns the shin into the kick |

A defense begins when the receiver reads the attack's launch and completes by its contact, so a defense never answers an attack it cannot see coming: a feint's read happens at its shown fraction, a disguised kick's read at its chamber key.

### Guard during kicks {#guard-during-kicks}

During every round kick, the kicking-side glove extends forward at the attacker's own protective head line and the opposite glove guards the cheek, through chamber, delivery, recoil and replant, on either side. The [kick guard decision](kick-guard.md) records the coaching source and the owner's choice.

## 1. Stance, guard and recoverable support

**Open images:** [IBA p.34, front-oblique and overhead stance](../../../references/private/iba/page-34.png), [Jeff Chan 08.000s, paired full-body side-oblique stance](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-08.000s.png), [40s, opposite front-oblique view](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-40s.png). IBA figures show staggered feet, separated tracks and gloves protecting the face; Chan's live demonstration shows flexed supporting knees and a released heel. These are different orientations and moments, not calibrated dimensions. The coach is the black-shirted participant with green gloves; the partner wears blue. Identity follows the original publisher's attribution rather than facial recognition.

**Start and trigger:** Enter a stance that can advance, retreat, rotate or unweight a leg. In the film, orthodox means own left side leads; camera-left is not anatomical left. Hold the chin modestly down with eyes toward the opponent, elbows near the ribs and gloves positioned to cover the jaw/temple. The exact hand height changes with range and incoming weapon. A double high cover is a response, not a permanently clenched silhouette.

**Support and action:** Keep both legs visibly available for flexion; the pelvis belongs above a plausible support region. A heel can lift while the forefoot remains planted. A visibly touching sole does not reveal pressure, exact weight percentage or COM. Treat readiness to lift a leg as a choreography requirement, not a claim that a photograph measured equal weight.

**Response and recovery:** The opponent must react to the actual delivery and distance. Retract the striking hand along a defensible return while the other glove remains useful; recover the feet and knees into an available next action. Do not let an endpoint solver straighten both knees, lift the pelvis or slide the support foot merely to hit a glove target. Do not reset every combination through a rigid neutral idle.

**Rig mapping:** Guard targets are actor-local glove/forearm relations to jaw/ribs plus collision clearance; elbows remain independent bend-plane constraints. Stance is root height, pelvis orientation, hip flexion/abduction, knee flexion and foot placement together. No universal knee degree, heel height or stance width is inferred from perspective pixels.

## 2. Footwork, distance and the stepped glove touch

**Open image:** [IBA p.36, overhead forward-step sequence](../../../references/private/iba/page-36.png). It shows the direction-side foot advancing and the other foot following to restore separation. A footprint diagram explains order; it does not measure ground force or the film's timing.

**Start/trigger:** Move because the next glove contact, punch or exit requires a new distance. Choose which foot remains available to support the body before the other swings. For a forward orthodox step, advance the lead foot, receive the landing, then bring the rear foot with it without crossing or collapsing the base. Reverse the order appropriately for retreat; lateral direction changes require their own support transfer.

**Hands/hips/head:** Hands remain available while the pelvis travels between contacts. Root travel cannot begin as an unsupported whole-body glide. During a planted interval, hold the chosen support patch in world space; release the heel if rotation needs it. A swing foot clears the floor, lands, and then becomes an available support. This is an authored kinematic support sequence, not a force simulation.

**Film choice:** The opening left-glove touch is a courteous stepped reach, not a strike. Advance, land, reach with a relaxed arm while the other hand stays available, withdraw and restore the fighting distance. The authored opening lead step and following rear-foot landing must survive fitting independently. Both fighters need separate feet and approach distances; a single symmetric hand meeting point cannot stand in for the whole action.

**Exit:** After a strike/defense, recover the striking limb and take a small supported distance step if the story calls for it. Let the opponent track that exit. Never move a foot through the floor or drag a supposedly planted patch to keep a desirable screen composition.

## 3. Jab, cross and body-level combinations

**Open diagrams:** [jab p.42](../../../references/private/iba/page-42.png), [cross p.43](../../../references/private/iba/page-43.png), [body jab p.44](../../../references/private/iba/page-44.png), [body cross p.45](../../../references/private/iba/page-45.png). The paired views show the striking line with the non-striking glove retained. Cross diagrams couple the rear side and trunk; body diagrams show lower-body participation. These are coaching illustrations, not motion-capture angle tables.

**Jab:** Begin in available range, move the lead glove toward its actual target with shoulder participation, keep the free glove protecting, then recover. A stepping jab includes the support/landing sequence; arm extension alone cannot invent reach. The receiver's catch, cover or evasion must be timed to the glove's world path.

**Cross:** Carry rear-side hip/trunk rotation into the punch while allowing the rear heel to release. Keep the knee following its anatomical leg plane and foot orientation rather than twisting a planted whole sole arbitrarily. The free glove guards. Recover the punching arm and pelvis into the next supported phase; a cross-to-low-kick sequence can continue rotation/load preparation instead of freezing in stance.

**Body shot:** Lower the delivery level through knee/hip flexion and actual pelvis/root descent, with controlled trunk inclination and an opponent-facing head. A forward spine fold with unchanged pelvis does not reproduce the illustrated lowered support. Do not solve the hand-to-body target by raising the pelvis and straightening knees. The opposite elbow/glove still protects available targets.

**Inference and implementation:** The exact share of root drop, hip bend and trunk inclination depends on the target and proportions. Author their joint intent, preserve a feasible complete pose/root at the selected peak, and report actual final hand/receiver separation after FK. Numerical pose bounds are anatomical feasibility limits, not prescriptions from the manual.

## 4. Cover, catch and parry

**Open diagrams:** [double cover p.57](../../../references/private/iba/page-57.png), [catch p.58](../../../references/private/iba/page-58.png), [parry p.62](../../../references/private/iba/page-62.png). These give distinct hand/forearm intercepting shapes on a supported body. Do not merge them into one generic raised-arm animation.

| Technique | Trigger and supported action | Opponent response and recovery | Rig requirement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High cover | Incoming head attack; bring the named glove/forearm into the attack corridor with elbows controlled and chin protected. Knees remain available. | Attack meets the actual forearm/glove region or is explicitly staged as a near miss; restore vision/guard and distance. | Target an actor-local protective surface, not a detached world hand point; retain head clearance and elbow bend direction. |
| Catch | Incoming straight punch; a compact receiving glove intercepts in front of its protected region. | Attacker retracts, receiver returns the glove or counters after a supported transition. | Name receiving hand, receiving patch and attack witness; preserve the other hand's guard. |
| Parry | Incoming line can be redirected; make a small lateral hand/forearm interception while maintaining the other protection. | Punch path continues or retracts from the actual meeting, then the parrying hand recovers. | Author tangential direction and time, not just one peak hand coincidence; avoid shoulder/elbow hyperextension. |

**Limit:** IBA is the boxing subset. Kickboxing leaves body/leg threats and kicks after punches. The chosen film uses compact protection and supported recovery; it must not import a boxing-only narrow stance or assume a hand parry answers a low kick.

**Body versus high-kick arm shielding:** Read the blue-shirted defender in the existing Chan [08.000s guard](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-08.000s.png) → [09.500s preparation](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-09.500s.png) → [10s body-level interception](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-10s.png) → [10.500s continued cover](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-10.500s.png) → [11.000s recovery/transition](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-11.000s.png). Gloves remain available near the face as the near arm/elbow faces the body-height incoming shin; exact contact is obscured. This is sampled cooperative MMA instruction, not a measured defense-only cycle. [Evolve, sections3–4](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/5-basic-muay-thai-blocks-to-know-for-your-defense-game/) supplies the body-shield text cross-check and warns that lowering both hands exposes the head.

For a head-height attack, [Skarbowsky’s original publisher](https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/blocking-high-kicks-with-jean-charles-skarbowsky), paragraphs after its video, describes an incoming-side arm braced near the head with optional opposite-arm reinforcement. The [locally opened original poster](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/skarbowsky-high-kick-block-poster.jpg) shows that clustered protective shape against an elevated leg. It is an edited promotional still; onset/recovery video was unavailable. Author the shield toward the actual attack height, preserve supported knees/feet and other available head protection, then return to guard as the leg clears. Use actor-local arm surfaces, controlled shoulder/elbow shape and final whole-limb clearance; these are staging constraints, not force or injury-prevention proof. The retained cover/catch/parry/duck sources still lack a complete photographic timing cycle.

## 5. Slip and weave: head motion belongs to a supported body

**Open diagrams:** [lead-side duck p.64](../../../references/private/iba/page-64.png), [rotational defense p.67](../../../references/private/iba/page-67.png). Actual drawings show lowered/bent lower-body involvement and a curved evasion direction. They do not provide the film's required head offset or a universally safe depth against knees/kicks.

**Named outside slip:** [England Boxing Part1, printed/PDFp.85](../../../references/private/england-boxing/outside-slip-page-85.png) explicitly names this technique; open its original [left](../../../references/private/england-boxing/outside-slip-left-photo.jpg) and [right](../../../references/private/england-boxing/outside-slip-right-photo.jpg) paired photographs separately. They show off-line head/shoulder placement with defensive hands and staggered feet. The text describes a compact displacement with slight knee bend/body turn and return to guard, and distinguishes inside-slip exposure. These are different response views, not consecutive measured phases. Keep this small lateral evasion distinct from IBA’s named duck and the film’s deeper weave. Choose the side from the actual punch/free-hand threat, preserve support, and verify final head clearance; no image-derived joint degree or weight percentage is adopted.

**Start/trigger:** Identify the incoming glove path and the side to which the head clears it. A slip is a small supported displacement from that line. A weave continues under/around a hook through a curved path. Keep eyes toward the opponent and guard available; do not put the head on an arbitrary sine-wave detached from the attack.

**Support/action:** Bend knees and hips, lower the pelvis when the chosen weave needs depth, and transfer laterally without feet skating. The head's world-height change depends on root, pelvis, spine and neck together. Pelvic tilt is rotation; it is not itself pelvis translation. The raised/lowered hip crests must agree with own-side support and trunk lean. Knee bend can increase while the pelvis rises if the solver moves root upward: this is exactly why knee angle alone cannot establish a successful duck.

**Recovery:** Clear the punch, rise through support, then counter/exit without an abrupt head snap or crossed feet. Kickboxing staging should avoid an unnecessarily deep boxing duck into a possible knee/kick; this is a deliberate film choice, not a ban on all weaves.

**Constraints:** Use simultaneous actual head path/drop, root-height freedom, foot-patch targets and preferred leg/pelvis shape. Keep gravity-world drop separate from actor-local lateral direction. Evaluate the final head world point and pelvis world height, not the authored preference alone.

## 7. Body/high round kick: chamber, turn, impact, recoil and landing

**Open sequence:** [PLOS Fig.3, PDF p.6](../../../references/licensed/plos/figure-3-phase-sequence.png), [measured Fig.5, PDF p.9](../../../references/licensed/plos/figure-5-measured-curves.png). Attribution: Gavagan CJ and Sayers MGL, 2017, PLOS ONE12(8):e0182645, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182645), CC BY4.0. Fig.3 is a common illustrative sequence, not an identified Muay Thai athlete. PanelD is still airborne. Fig.5 distinguishes Muay Thai dashed curves from the other disciplines; do not take a Taekwondo curve as the kickboxing model.

**Additional actual full-body views:** [Chan 09.500s chamber](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-09.500s.png), [10s body delivery](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-10s.png), [10.500s withdrawal](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-10.500s.png), [11.000s replant/transition](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-11.000s.png). These are one switch/body-kick demonstration viewed rear-obliquely. [72s opposite oblique body delivery](../../../references/private/dynamic-striking/chan-72.000s.png) shows the opposite-side view, a raised thigh, bent support knee and supporting foot visible. It is a separate repetition, not a synchronized second camera of the first kick. Partner forearms receive the kick; head-height impact is not shown.

**Phase constraints:**

1. **Preparation/chamber:** Establish support before lifting the other foot. Hip flexion/abduction and knee flexion place the thigh and compact the distal leg; avoid an isolated knee hinge with a motionless pelvis.
2. **Turn and delivery:** Couple pelvis/hip rotation, support-foot yaw and permitted heel release. The support knee follows a plausible leg plane. Under the owner's [current guard choice](kick-guard.md), extend the kicking-side hand forward at the protective head line and retain the opposite glove at the head. Keep that protection during recoil and replant. A bent knee can remain at delivery; extension is not an instruction to lock it.
3. **Impact:** Author the receiving forearm/rib/guard region and the attacking shin surface separately. Recompute both witnesses after all FK/root solves. The target residual, mesh clearance and visible reaction are different evidence.
4. **Recoil:** Clear the receiver and recover the knee/hip path rather than teleporting the foot backward through the target. Preserve balance and hand recovery through the transition.
5. **Landing/stance:** Replant in an available stagger, settle support and reconnect the next step or punch. Fig.3 alone does not show landing; Chan's later selected frames provide that missing phase for this body-kick demonstration.

**High-kick limit:** Increasing target height requires a feasible combination of hip motion, pelvis/trunk orientation and support, not a universal copied knee angle or ankle height. The retained sources do not give a complete original high-kick-to-check multi-angle cycle. That phase remains authored inference requiring actual film observation; body-kick frames must not be mislabeled high kicks.

## 8. Measured quantities and what images cannot establish

PLOS studied eight experts per discipline, three selected maximal kicks toward a fixed elevated target. Its measured group curves describe that task/population, not low-kick sparring or compulsory poses. [Riansoi et al. 2019](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JSST/article/view/215734) studied ten male Muay Thai beginners with motion capture/force plates. Open [Table2, PDF p.6/printed76](../../../references/private/support-study/pdf-page-6.png) and [joint-moment Table3, PDF p.7/printed77](../../../references/private/support-study/pdf-page-7.png): force and joint moment are distinct measured quantities. No numerical source mean is adopted as a film joint preset here.

Pixels can show sole contact, heel release and gross segment orientation. They do not measure plantar pressure, force, exact COM, depth-accurate joint angles or collision penetration. A software “load” scalar is not a force-plate result. Keep measured-source statements, transferable coordination and staging choices separate.

## 9. Closed Human rig and final evidence mapping

The reusable Human API should expose anatomy and constraints, not kickboxing names. In this project's anatomical frame +X is forward, +Y up, +Z own left; world gravity is separate. Clinical angle signs and own-side joint frames must be defined once and tested generically. Positive ankle dorsiflexion means toes up; plantarflexion is the opposite. The correct selected variant still depends on technique, source and phase.

| Authoring requirement | Generic controls/constraints | Actual evidence to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stance/body drop | Root translation, pelvis orientation, hip DOFs and knee flexion preferences | Final pelvis/head world heights and both foot-patch residuals; preferred degrees alone are insufficient. |
| Knee direction | Anatomical hip frame, knee hinge/pole with side-aware orientation; flexion-dependent tibial freedom | Actual knee/ankle path and no implausible twist/hyperextension. |
| Planted support | Named whole-foot or forefoot patch with position and orientation targets; explicit release/landing intervals | Final noncollinear patch witnesses and floor clearance. A whole-foot lock is incompatible with an authored heel-release pivot. |
| Punch/cover/check contact | Local attacker and receiver surface witnesses, independent target path | Residual recomputed after both actors' final FK/root solves, plus whole receiving/attacking limb clearance. |
| Slip/weave | Actor-local lateral head motion, world-vertical head/root intent, simultaneous support and leg/pelvis preferences | Final head displacement relative to incoming glove path and actual pelvis descent. |
| Recovery | Continuous pose/root and explicit foot/hand phases through the next event | No endpoint-only success, hidden support slide, discontinuous guard reset or uninspected inter-event path. |

The observed first15 failure batch is archived in the ignored engineering/reference notes: almost straight final stance knees, insufficient actual head drop with a rising pelvis during weaves, body-level intent overwritten by fitting, support-patch errors, incomplete receiving calf/foot clearance and stale/incomplete contact witnesses. These were source and saved-world observations at the named operator barrier, not a new watched render. The guide supplies constraints to the motion owner; only the validation operator's next complete actual-world and continuous-view round can establish the revised film's result.
