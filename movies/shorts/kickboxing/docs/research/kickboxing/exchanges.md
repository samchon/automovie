# Championship exchanges

The owner judged an earlier bout a sequence of single strikes, without the combinations, feints and layered defense of elite kickboxers, and required a fight between champions finished by a Brazilian (question-mark) kick. This study records how such exchanges are built, which answers meet which attacks and what each pairing can produce, and the measurable targets a composed round must meet. The sources are a time-motion study, a competition analysis, coaching articles, fight reports and the rulebook; none supplies joint angles, which the [technique laws](techniques.md#laws) and the actual bodies own.

## Activity structure {#activity}

Ouergui et al. timed 45 elite bouts from two WAKO world championships ([Time-motion analysis of elite male kickboxing competition](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Time-Motion%20Analysis%20of%20Elite%20Male%20Kickboxing%20Competition.pdf), J Strength Cond Res 28(12), 2014):

- High-intensity actions last 2.2 ± 1.2 s.
- Low-intensity footwork phases last 2.3 ± 0.8 s.
- Consecutive high-intensity actions are 3.4 ± 1.2 s apart.
- Fighters perform about 25 high-intensity actions per two-minute round.
- Fighting and non-fighting time stand at about 1:1.

Steps, slides, bounces, turns and feints count as low-intensity activity, so movement between attacks fills about half of a fight.

Romanenko analysed 161 bouts from the 2022 WAKO World Championship and the 2023 European Championship ([Comparative analysis of competitive activity in ring disciplines of kickboxing](https://journals.uran.ua/martial_arts/article/download/341569/330356/795080), Martial Arts 4(38), 2025, CC BY 4.0). In K1, series are mostly built from punches, and kicks prepare or finish them. Short two-strike combinations dominate, with mixed punch–kick and kick–punch sequences typical. The extracted text reports single-to-series strike ratios of 0.50:1 and 0.48:1, so about two thirds of strikes are thrown in series; the Ukrainian body text did not extract legibly, so neither ratio is attributed to a particular discipline.

## Combinations end in kicks {#combinations}

Dutch kickboxing, as taught by Ernesto Hoost, Peter Aerts and Ramon Dekkers, throws two-, three- or four-strike punch combinations and finishes them with a kick. Hoost's signature was a left hook to the liver followed almost immediately by a right low kick; against Andy Hug he combined a right cross, a left uppercut and a right low kick. A left hook puts the opponent's weight on the lead leg, which makes the following right low kick hard to check. Thom Harinck described Aerts hiding high kicks behind punches or pushes.

- [Bloody Elbow: Dutch Muay Thai moves to remember](https://bloodyelbow.com/2018/05/10/dutch-muay-thai-moves-to-remember-part-1-ernesto-hoost-k1-peter-aerts-ramon-dekkers-rob-kaman-glory)
- [Evolve MMA: Dutch kickboxing's influence on modern Muay Thai](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/the-influence-of-dutch-kickboxing-on-modern-muay-thai-fighters/)
- [GLORY: The Art of Low Kicks](https://glorykickboxing.com/news/the-art-of-low-kicks)
- [Muay Thai Guy: how to KO and break legs with leg kicks](https://www.muay-thai-guy.com/blog/how-to-ko-and-break-legs-with-leg-kicks)
- [Evolve MMA: 5 effective low-kick combinations in Muay Thai](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/5-effective-low-kicks-combinations-in-muay-thai/)

Coaches time the low kick to the moment the receiver's weight sits on the target leg: as he steps, or after a setup loads the lead leg, such as a body shot that brings the elbows down or a hook that pushes the weight sideways. A faked low kick followed by a cross punishes a receiver who checks early.

Law: a combination is two to five strikes of one fighter whose launches follow each other at the [combination intervals](techniques.md#punch-timing); a kick in it stands first or last. A hook to the body or head before a rear low kick loads the receiver's lead leg, so a low kick that follows a hook in the same combination lands unless the receiver is already standing on one leg to check.

## Defense keeps attacking {#defense}

Checking raises the bent lead leg so the shin meets the incoming kick. The check must start early: a shin still rising when the kick arrives lets it land. Coaches stress countering at once from the check, firing off its rebound as the foot lands, before the kicker recovers; a late counter gives the kicker time for another attack. Elite counter-strikers such as Giorgio Petrosyan work at the edge of punching range, slip or step back into counters, and draw predictable returns by attacking the guard.

- [Evolve MMA: how to check kicks](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/how-to-check-kicks-in-muay-thai)
- [Diesel Gym: how to defend low kicks](https://dieselgym.co.uk/how-to-defend-low-kicks-diesel-technique-of-the-week/)
- [MMA Sucka: Giorgio Petrosyan, diagnosing the doctor](https://mmasucka.com/opinion/diagnosing-doctor-giorgio-petrosyan/)

Law: a defender meets each strike of a combination with the answer the [defense table](techniques.md#defenses) assigns to its line, changing answers through the combination rather than holding one cover. A counter launches from the answer's end: from the replanted foot after a check, from the outside position after a slip, from the rising body after a roll.

## Feints and reads {#feints}

A drag or hip-turn feint of the rear low kick makes the opponent lift his leg to check; the real kick or a straight punch lands while he stands on one leg or as he replants. A pump jab moves the opponent's hands before the real attack.

- [Dynamic Striking: attacks off the jab with Jake Mainini](https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/attacks-off-the-jab-with-jake-mainini)
- [Evolve MMA: advanced striking set-ups and combinations](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/20-advanced-mma-striking-set-ups-and-combinations-you-should-add-to-your-arsenal/)

Law: a feint shows the first part of a real technique and returns along the same path, so its timing is the shown technique's own: a pump jab shows half of the jab's delivery, a low-kick or body-kick feint shows the kick up to its chamber key. The receiver reads the shown technique and starts that technique's answer; the answer holds until the feint has returned. The attack that follows strikes the region the answer opened. A shoulder feint shows the first 0.3 of the cross's delivery, the rear shoulder's dip before the fist leaves, and draws the rear-glove parry; a level feint shows the first 0.3 of the body cross's delivery, the knees bending to change level, and draws the elbow block over the ribs. A stance switch exchanges the lead and rear feet in place on their own tracks and turns the body into the opposite stance; the film's switch returns to orthodox within the movement, because every technique shape is orthodox.

## Habits and patterns {#habits}

Superbon stopped Petrosyan in 2021 after body kicks; he said he anticipated Petrosyan's return punch, changed to a hook and followed with the head kick ([Sportskeeda: how Superbon set up the head kick knockout of Petrosyan](https://www.sportskeeda.com/mma/news-one-championship-how-superbon-singha-mawynn-set-head-kick-ko-giorgio-petrosyan)). Patterned body kicks teach an opponent to lower the near arm over the ribs.

Law: a habit is a trigger-to-response rule. Once a receiver has met a repeated attack with the same answer, he gives that answer to the attack's visible trigger, here a rising right knee, before he can tell what follows. The settings of each fighter record his habits, their triggers, speed and reliability.

## The Brazilian kick {#brazilian-kick}

The Brazilian or question-mark kick is associated with Ademir da Costa and with Glaube Feitosa's K-1 knockouts. It starts in the chamber of a body or low kick; the support heel turns toward the target, the hips roll over and the knee rises above the target line, so the lower leg comes down over the opponent's guard. It works when the opponent reads a body kick and lowers his arm. Its surfaces, targets and the measurement on the actual bodies are in the [Brazilian kick study](brazilian-kick.md).

## Outcome table {#outcomes}

The outcome of an attack follows from the attack and the receiver's answer, decided before geometry: the bodies then realize it and an audit checks that they did. A pairing absent from the table is not a legal composition.

| Attack | Receiver's answer | Outcome | Contact the bodies must make |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jab, cross | None, or an answer to another attack | Landed | Glove on face |
| Jab, cross | Parry | Parried | Glove on the parrying glove |
| Jab, cross | Catch or cover | Blocked | Glove on glove or forearm |
| Jab, cross | Slip, sway | Evaded | None; the glove passes the head |
| Hook, uppercut | None | Landed | Glove on jaw or chin |
| Hook, uppercut | Cover | Blocked | Glove on glove or forearm |
| Hook | Roll | Evaded | None; the glove passes over the head |
| Body cross, body hook | None | Landed | Glove on ribs or liver |
| Body cross, body hook | Elbow block | Blocked | Glove on elbow or forearm |
| Low kick | None | Landed | Shin on outside of thigh |
| Low kick | Check | Checked | Shin on shin |
| Body kick, switch kick | None | Landed | Shin on ribs |
| Body kick, switch kick | Elbow block | Blocked | Shin on forearm and elbow |
| High kick | High block | Blocked | Shin on forearm |
| Teep | None | Landed | Sole on stomach |
| Teep | Parry | Parried | Sole on forearm |
| Clinch knee | None | Landed | Knee on stomach |
| Brazilian kick | Elbow block read from the body-kick chamber | Landed | Instep and distal shin on the jaw–neck junction behind the ear, coming down over the lowered arm |
| Any feint | The answer its shown technique draws | Read | None; the answer opens its region |
| Any feint | None | Ignored | None |

A landed blow moves the receiver's struck region away from it; a blocked, checked or parried one stops at the defending limb; an evaded one meets nothing. Whether a landed blow staggers or drops the receiver is the screenplay's decision; only the finishing Brazilian kick drops him in this film, under the [rules](rules.md#knockdown).

## Output rate {#output}

Public GLORY statistics are event- or fighter-specific rather than league averages. A heavyweight round in [GLORY's CroCop–Bonjasky numbers](https://glorykickboxing.com/news/by-the-numbers-crocop-vs-bonjasky) shows 24 and 31 strikes thrown. A [FightMetrics-based summary](https://fightforecast.substack.com/p/giga-chikadze-has-better-stats-in) credits Giga Chikadze with 7.46 strikes landed per minute.

## Measurable targets {#targets}

A composed round meets these targets, measured on its resolved schedule:

- Exchanges start at intervals whose mean lies within one standard deviation of 3.4 s (Ouergui, consecutive high-intensity actions).
- At least 75% of strikes are thrown inside combinations of two or more, and at least 85% of exchanges contain a combination. This is denser than the roughly two thirds Romanenko observed, because the film compresses a championship round into 150 seconds of action; the exceptions are single counters, the range-resetting teep and the clinch knee.
- Every kick prepares or finishes a combination, except checks, teeps and the clinch knee.
- Each fighter throws between 20 and 40 strikes per minute of action, bracketing the observed rounds and the film's compression.
- Initiative changes hands: each fighter opens at least a third of the exchanges.
