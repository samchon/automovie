# Rules of the contest

Primary sources were inspected on 2026-11-02–03 and 2026-11-07. This study records the rules that bound what the fighters and the referee may do, the knockdown procedure and the limits of what the film claims medically. This is a fictional contest, not an accredited GLORY event.

## Weight and contest {#contest}

[GLORY's 16 March 2026 v2.2 rules](https://glory.pinkyellow.network/assets/rules/glory-rules-2026-v2.pdf), section 2.01, include 80 kg in middleweight. The film claims no separate 80 kg division.

## Legal and illegal actions {#legal-actions}

GLORY v2.2 section 5.07.A, effective 16 March 2026:

- A.4 bans linear or thrusting kicks to the legs. Leg attacks must arc, so every low kick is a round kick and teeps go to the stomach.
- A.7 bans elbows, and A.10 bans throws, sweeps and pushes meant to off-balance or down an opponent.
- A.11 permits a clinch only to throw an immediate single knee; the athlete must then disengage.
- A.12 permits catching a leg only for a single immediate strike with at most one step. The film blocks or checks kicks instead of catching them.
- C.1 counts continuously raising the front leg without a technique as stalling, so every check answers a real or feinted kick.
- No downed opponent may be attacked.

Law: the legal techniques are punches (jab, cross, hook, uppercut, to head or body), round kicks to legs, body and head (including the switch kick and the Brazilian kick), the teep to the stomach and the clinch knee to the body. A clinch holds for one knee and releases at once. After a knockdown neither fighter attacks.

## Knockdown and count {#knockdown}

Sections 5.05.B.2–5 and Appendix 3.D require the standing opponent in the farthest neutral corner, facing center, before the count begins; leaving the corner pauses it. Readiness is assessed at eight. An uninterrupted ten-count ends the contest by knockout, followed by the match-ending bell. Section 5.06.E.2 distinguishes that outcome from an immediate-assistance early stoppage.

Law: the count is one number per elapsed second from the moment the standing fighter is in the farthest neutral corner facing center. The count starts a full second before "One", so "Ten" closes the tenth elapsed second. At "Ten", a fighter who has not regained standing balance and a ready guard loses by knockout; the referee then signals the result and attends to him. That exact one-number-per-second rhythm is this film's implementation of the ten-count, not a quoted timing formula from the rulebook.

## Medical uncertainty {#uncertainty}

[CDC's TBI explanation](https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/about/index.html) describes head impact and rapid head movement as possible concussion causes. It does not diagnose this fictional outcome, establish an impact threshold or prove carotid obstruction. The film shows a descending blow behind the ear and loss of support; the exchanges cease, the standing fighter goes to the neutral corner and the referee counts to ten before declaring the knockout. No injury mechanism is asserted.
