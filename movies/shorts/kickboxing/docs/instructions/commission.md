# Kickboxing commission

## Required film {#commission}

Complete [issue 3040](https://github.com/samchon/automovie/issues/3040) under the latest instructions: 180 seconds, two highly trained adult men, both 180 cm and 80 kg, fighting at championship level. They step into an actual glove touch and immediately exchange combinations, feints, checks with counters, slips, parries, rolls and blocks, with varied footwork under GLORY rules: a clinch only for one immediate knee and then release, no elbows, throws or sweeps. At 150.000 A's right Brazilian kick lands: his instep and lower shin come down over B's lowered left arm onto the area behind B's left ear at the junction of jaw and neck. B has learned to drop his left arm whenever A's right knee rises; A exploits that tell with a feint and then with the finish. B falls on his right side. The count starts at 157, with One at 158 through Ten at 167; the knockout is signalled at 167.6, A celebrates from 168.1, the match-ending bell rings at 170.1 and the film ends at 180. During every round kick the kicking-side hand reaches forward at the protective head line while the opposite hand guards, through chamber, delivery, recoil and replant. The visible referee is an adult man. No downed attack and no asserted injury mechanism.

## Direction {#direction}

Write the screenplay in the production's notation: each H2 scene is the next fifteen-second phase of one continuous ring scene, headed `INT. KICKBOXING RING - NIGHT` and then `CONTINUOUS`, with no time jump; each H3 is an exchange beat with its beat record, short present-tense action and separate speaker and dialogue blocks. Change pressure, angle and initiative across the round; do not loop the first minute. Use the whole ring: angles, pivots, circling, cutting off and the ropes. The referee is always active: positioning, side view, clinch breaks, signals and the knockdown procedure. The camera reacts to the action and relates the fighters. Body and glancing contacts interrupt breath and guard without early knockdowns.

## Image and sound {#presentation}

Keep feet, targets and heads readable together on the same screen axis. Show the decisive kick and articulated fall without a cut. Keep the entire count in real time with A in the neutral corner and B down. Start and match-ending bells, English referee words, breaths, mat friction and distinct contacts carry the film. The winner's raised gloves, supported victory steps and excited breath remain visible beside the downed opponent in the wide ending. The referee is a visible participant with readable signals, gaze, expression and support. No replay, time compression or commentary. Production owns officiating policy; Human owns reusable anatomy and motion.
