# Developing direction

Identify fixed conditions, delegated choices and the preservation boundary for supplied material. Research the intended audience, nearby works and relevant format expectations where they inform those choices. Use the findings to understand the viewing experience rather than adopt another work's scale or style by default.

Compare materially different directions when the central premise is open. Distinguish them by the situation, pursuit, forces encountered, progression or ending, then choose the one that best serves the commissioned experience. Resolve these together as far as the delegation permits:

- Who or what the audience follows, the starting situation, consequential choices and costs, and destination.
- The question, reward or progression that sustains attention across the requested duration.
- The audience's access to information and the intended image, sound, tone, rhythm and form.
- What closes, what remains open and which decisions later scene authors may make afresh.

An observation, demonstration, ensemble, abstract film or music video can use its own progression. Make the direction concrete enough to select and discard material while preserving supplied events and meanings.

Record why the chosen direction serves this work through [requirements](brief.md#requirements). When scale is open, choose a provisional duration or release plan from the consequential turns, parallel progressions and material to enact. Revisit it against completed event design and performed scenes; text length does not establish running time. Author choices remain revisable within delegation; changing a user-fixed condition requires the user's authority.
