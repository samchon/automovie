# Realized speech principles

Each script and final screenplay H2 answers from its actual speech and neighboring action. Treatment plans and settings voice descriptions do not establish the quality of an executed exchange.

## Do these words belong to these speakers? {#speaker-plausibility}

Do age, status, situation, knowledge, relationship, vocabulary, address, register, rhythm, dialect, avoidance, and silence make each consequential line plausible and distinguish the speakers? Read the exchange itself, not an explanation about the speaker. Ordinary replies can be shared; a speaker label cannot make implausible expression plausible. Authoring jargon and procedural codes belong in speech only when they exist in the world and this speaker would use them. Preserve the inherited exchange's purpose and outcome across naturalistic, theatrical, ritual, and other adopted forms. Choose social and regional signals deliberately rather than reducing a community to an unexamined stereotype.

## Is the relation between action and speech legible? {#action-and-line-order}

Does adjacency communicate whether an action prepares, interrupts, contradicts, completes, or follows a line, including simultaneous action and meaningful uncertainty? Written order alone must not accidentally imply a decision the scene did not make. Repeated ordering is a failure when it hides or misstates the required relation, not merely because it repeats. Delivery notes, speaker cues, and neighboring action should clarify the exchange rather than explain again what the line has already done.
