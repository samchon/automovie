# The brooms

## Form, material and markings {#form}

### A school broom {#broom}

Fourteen identical school practice brooms, one per rider. Each is 2.10 m long: a straight, smooth, honey-brown ash shaft 1.65 m long and 3.2 cm thick, slightly tapered at the nose, and a bundle of dark brown twigs 0.45 m long, bound to the shaft's tail and spreading to 0.25 m across at its end. A 3 cm band of house colour, green or scarlet, rings the shaft just behind the nose so a rider's broom matches the team. There is no saddle, footrest, strap or handle. A broom weighs about 3 kg. Its form is the production's choice for a school broom.

### Zones along the shaft {#zones}

Measured from the nose: the hands hold the shaft between 0.15 and 0.50 m; the rider's seat point is at 0.85 m; the clear rear section from 1.05 to 1.60 m is bare wood, long enough for a second rider to sit astride 0.45–0.55 m behind the first ([broom-riding study](../../../research/flight/broom-riding.md#tandem)); the twigs begin at 1.65 m.

## Use {#use}

### Riding {#riding}

A rider mounts a broom hovering level at 0.7 m above the ground by swinging a leg over and sitting at the seat point, then kicks off. The seat, steering, speeds, banking and reach follow [broom flight](../world/flight.md). The rear section can carry a second rider or be grasped from below by a hanging rider. In flight a broom makes a rush of air and a low twig vibration, which these silent films do not play.

## State changes {#state}

### Ridden, tandem and riderless {#states}

A broom is ridden by one rider, ridden in tandem by two ([two riders](../world/flight.md#tandem)), carries a hanging rider beneath it ([falling and hanging](../world/flight.md#falling)) or flies riderless ([riderless brooms](../world/flight.md#riderless)). A riderless broom lands nowhere in these films while they are watched; it does not break.
