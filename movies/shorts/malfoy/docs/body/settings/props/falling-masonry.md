# The support's cap and its debris

## Form, material and markings {#form}

### The cap {#cap}

One rectangular block of grey limestone, 0.80 m wide, 1.00 m deep and 0.25 m thick, about 500 kg, that sits as the projecting cap of the [stone support](../sets/training-court.md#support): its rear edge rests on the bearing ledge at the upright's south face and the rest of it projects south over open air, its underside 2.70 m above the floor. Its faces are plain and weathered, darker than the light grey mortar of its joints.

### The debris {#debris}

Everything the cap's failure can release, and nothing more: two angular chips of mortar no larger than 4 cm, a short stream of sand-sized grit, three angular stone fragments no larger than 8 cm, and one low puff of grey dust. All are the same grey stone or lighter grey mortar ([falling-masonry study](../../../research/physics/falling-masonry.md#fragments)).

## Use {#use}

### Nobody handles it {#no-handling}

The cap is architecture, not a tool: nobody touches, climbs or uses it, and neither student knows its joint is cracked ([Carina's knowledge](../cast/carina-malfoy.md#knows)). Only a struck joint sets it moving.

## State changes {#state}

### How it can fail {#failure}

The cap's states, in the only order they can occur, are those of the [falling-masonry study](../../../research/physics/falling-masonry.md#stages): in place; struck, when a spell or blow breaks the cracked lower mortar fillet and the two chips drop beside the base; tipping, its front edge dipping 5–15° for 0.8–1.5 s while the rear still rests on the ledge, the upper mortar web holds and the grit runs; released, when the upper web breaks; falling, 0.74 s to the floor, rotating forward 60–90° through the free air in front of the upright and never through it; fallen, flat on a broad face where it lands, with the three fragments thrown up to 2.5 m away from the upright and the dust below 0.8 m, thinning in 1.5–2.0 s. Nothing else follows: no second collapse, no rolling block, no continuing shower. The fallen cap, chips and fragments stay where they land for the rest of the film.

### What can stop it {#stoppers}

Nothing in these films: a shield stops the fragments but cannot hold the cap ([wand magic](../world/magic.md#practice-only)). A person survives it only by being outside its danger region ([falling-masonry study](../../../research/physics/falling-masonry.md#clearance)).
