# The Beaters' bats

## Form, material and markings {#form}

### The bats {#bats}

Four short plain wooden clubs, one per Beater: 0.55 m long, a 20 cm grip 3.5 cm thick widening to a blunt rounded head 8 cm across, in mid-brown wood. Canon calls them short wooden clubs ([practice-play study](../../../research/quidditch/practice-play.md#roles)); their dimensions are the production's choice, short enough to read apart from a 2.1 m broom shaft.

## Use {#use}

### Striking {#striking}

A Beater holds the bat by the grip in the batting hand and steers with the other; a swing from the shoulder meets a Bludger within about 1.2 m of the shoulder and drives it away ([practice-play study](../../../research/quidditch/practice-play.md#beaters)). Between swings the bat rests across the Beater's thigh or rides held upright. A bat never strikes a person, a broom or the Quaffle ([conduct](../world/quidditch.md#conduct)).

## State changes {#state}

### None {#none}

N/A: each bat stays with its Beater the whole practice and does not break.
