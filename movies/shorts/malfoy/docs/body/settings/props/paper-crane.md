# The paper crane

## Form, material and markings {#form}

### The crane {#crane}

One 15 cm square of thin cream writing paper folded into a classic paper crane: about 11 cm from beak to tail, 10 cm across the spread wings and 6 cm high, weighing under 2 g. Its folded faces are blank cream.

### The writing {#writing}

The inside of the square carries one line in blue-black ink in Carina's neat upright hand: "Would you have tea with me?" The folds hide it completely; it can only be read with the sheet opened and its writing facing the reader. This inscription is the production's invention.

### The opened sheet {#opened}

Unfolded, the square shows the crease grid of the crane's folds; folded once in half along the writing, it makes a 15 × 7.5 cm rectangle with the writing inside.

## Use {#use}

### Handling {#handling}

It is held between thumb and index finger. Carina has charmed it to fly once ([paper charm](../world/magic.md#paper-charm)): a fingertip push launches it level along its line at 1.0–1.5 m/s, and a hand that closes on it in flight catches it without crushing it. Unfolding it takes two hands and about 2 s, and folding the sheet once about 1 s (production choices). It passes from hand to hand under the [ownership rule](../../../research/everyday/handover.md#ownership).

## State changes {#state}

### Its stages {#stages}

Folded crane → flying (once) → caught or landed → opened sheet → folded once. Paper under two opposing pulls creases at the first buckle and tears on a second sustained pull ([handover study](../../../research/everyday/handover.md#pulls)); a torn sheet cannot be mended in these films. It does not fly again, unfold itself or change its words.
