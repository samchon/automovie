# The two tea cups

## Form, material and markings {#form}

### The cups {#cups}

Two matching plain cups of white glazed ceramic with straight sides, 8 cm across and 8.5 cm tall, each with one round handle that stands 3 cm out from the side with a finger hole 2 cm across. No saucer, spoon, pattern or lid. Each holds warm amber tea to 2 cm below the rim and weighs about 0.45 kg full. The tea is comfortable to hold and drink, never scalding, and shows no steam.

### Where they start {#start}

Carina has brought them before the corridor film begins; they stand upright on the [window sill](../sets/school-corridor.md#sill), 25 cm apart.

## Use {#use}

### Holding and carrying {#holding}

A cup is held by the handle with the index finger through it and the thumb on top, or around the body in the palm. One person can carry one in each hand. It is carried upright within 10°, at no more than 1.0 m/s and for no more than six steps without spilling ([handover study](../../../research/everyday/handover.md#cups)). Handing one over, the giver lets go only after the receiver supports its body or handle ([ownership](../../../research/everyday/handover.md#ownership)). A cup set down stands on its flat base. Ceramic set on stone makes a small dry click, which these silent films do not play.

## State changes {#state}

### Tea level {#tea-level}

The tea level drops only when someone drinks. A dropped cup breaks and spills; no film does this.
