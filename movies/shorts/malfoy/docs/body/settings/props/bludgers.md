# The two Bludgers

## Form, material and markings {#form}

### The balls {#balls}

Two jet-black iron balls, 25 cm across, matte, with no markings; canon makes them iron ([practice-play study](../../../research/quidditch/practice-play.md#roles)) and the size is the production's choice. They are told apart only by where they fly; their authoring identities are B-1, which starts over the west half of the pitch, and B-2, over the east half.

## Use {#use}

### Behavior {#behavior}

A Bludger flies by itself at 8–12 m/s toward the nearest rider in its half of the pitch, never stops, and is driven away only by a Beater's bat: struck within bat reach, it leaves at 10–15 m/s in the swing's direction, travels 20–40 m and then turns toward another rider ([practice-play study](../../../research/quidditch/practice-play.md#beaters)). A struck Bludger gives a hard knock, which the silent films do not play. Nobody catches or carries a Bludger.

## State changes {#state}

### None {#none}

N/A: the Bludgers fly the whole practice unchanged; no film shows one hitting a rider.
