# The side courtyard

## Shape and dimensions {#shape}

### The recess {#recess}

A shallow paved recess beside a side wing of the castle, off every through route: a level rectangle 8.0 m east to west and 4.0 m north to south, paved with flush pale-grey limestone slabs whose narrow joints leave a flat, dry, matte surface on which a sheet of paper lies flat. Distances below run east from the west wall and north from the south edge. The recess is the production's invention.

### Walls {#walls}

The north wall is 8.0 m long, 2.4 m high and 0.25 m thick, dull beige plaster over stone. A matching west wall 4.0 m long meets it at the north-west corner and has one unroofed passage gap 1.2 m wide, centred 1.4 m north of the south edge, leading to a side passage out of view. The east side is the side wing's plain grey stone wall, windowless at this level. The south edge is open to level, unused lawn.

### The bench {#bench}

One backless bench cut from a single block of matte medium-grey limestone, 1.6 m long, 0.4 m deep and 0.45 m high, its edges slightly eased, with no legs, cushion or fittings ([sitting study](../../../research/everyday/sitting.md#seat-height)). It stands immovably with its back against the north wall, centred 2.2 m from the west wall, so its ends are 1.4 m and 3.0 m from it. In front of it lies 3.6 m of clear paving to the open south edge.

## Zones and landmarks {#zones}

### Zones {#zone-list}

| Zone | Where | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| West gap | The passage gap in the west wall | The only built way in or out |
| Bench | The seat | Sitting, or addressing an empty seat |
| Bench front | The paving south of the bench, 1.4–3.0 m from the west wall, up to 2.0 m south of the seat's front | Standing face to face, handing things over |
| Clear paving | The rest of the open floor | Walking, waiting, stepping back |
| Open south | The lawn beyond the south edge | Light; nobody arrives that way |

### Landmarks {#landmarks}

The bench's centre front; the west gap's middle; a starting place two steps (about 1.3 m) south of the bench's front.

## Routes, entrances and levels {#routes}

### The west gap {#entrance}

People come and go through the west gap; from it a straight line of clear paving runs east to the bench front. A person standing at the bench front facing the bench has the gap behind her left shoulder and cannot see someone entering. One level throughout.

## Dressing, lights and acoustics {#dressing}

### Dressing {#bare}

The walls, the bench and the paving, and nothing else.

### Light {#light}

Late afternoon on a weekday, in the hour after the last lesson: low sun from the south-west lights the beige north wall and throws soft shadows north-east across the paving, so dark-robed figures and a white note stand out against the wall. The air is still.

### Acoustics {#acoustics}

N/A: the films are silent ([sound](../sound/silence.md)).

### Occupancy {#occupancy}

At this hour the recess is empty and nobody passes through it; it is a public corner, not locked, and anyone may arrive or leave by the west gap.

## Use {#use}

### How people use it {#people-use}

Students wait or sit on the bench, and a person can stand at the bench front and speak to its empty seat as if to someone sitting there. Two people face to face at the bench front stand 0.8–0.9 m apart for an arm's-length exchange ([walking study](../../../research/everyday/walking.md#transitions)).

## State changes {#state}

### None {#none}

N/A: nothing in the recess changes; only the [note](../props/invitation-note.md#dropped) may come to rest on the paving.
