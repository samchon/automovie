# Clock Tower courtyard

A stone courtyard enclosed on three sides at the foot of the castle's clock tower, with a covered walk along two sides, a bench and a great clock; the place of `paper-crane-invitation` and `three-oclock`. Production's invention within canon's Hogwarts; the two films use the same courtyard, each from the same baseline.

## Shape, zones and landmarks {#shape}

### The courtyard {#courtyard}

A square open court 20 m by 20 m, paved in grey flagstones, open to the sky. Its north and west sides are lined by an arcade (a covered walk) under a single-storey roof whose ridge is about 5 m above the court; at the north-east corner, above the end of the north arcade, rises the clock tower. The east side is the castle's main wall, about 15 m high, with windows above head height. The south side is open to the grounds behind a stone parapet 1 m high, over which the slopes run down toward the lake.

### The arcade {#arcade}

A covered walk 3 m deep along the whole north and west sides, under a stone vault 4 m high, open to the court through a row of round arches, each 2.5 m wide with stone piers 0.6 m square between them. The arcade's floor is 0.3 m above the court.

### The bench {#bench}

A plain, backless stone bench inside the west arcade at its middle, against the back wall and facing east into the court: 2 m long, 0.4 m deep, its seat 0.45 m above the arcade floor. Two people can sit on it with about a hand's width between them, or closer.

### The fountain {#fountain}

An octagonal stone basin 3 m across in the centre of the court, its rim 0.6 m high and 0.3 m wide, wide enough to sit on. No fountain jet runs; a few centimetres of still rainwater lie in the bottom.

### The clock face {#clock-face}

On the clock tower at the court's north-east corner, a large round clock face 3 m across, its centre about 12 m above the court, set on the tower's south-west face so that it looks across the court: a black face with gold Roman numerals and gold hands, readable from anywhere in the court. From the bench (about 20–22 m away, a seated eye about 1.5 m above the court) it is seen looking up about 27° and to the left (north-east), through the arches: that sightline crosses the arch plane at about 3.0–3.3 m, under the 4 m vault. It shows the real time of each film, between half past two and half past four ([afternoon](../world/four-films.md#afternoon)).

## Routes, entrances and levels {#routes}

### Entrances {#entrances}

An archway passage at the south end of the west arcade, from the main castle corridor, is the way in and out used in both films. The open south parapet is not a way in; nobody climbs it. A small door at the east end of the north arcade leads to the clock-tower stairs and is not used.

### The arcade walk {#arcade-walk}

The west arcade is a straight 20 m walk from the archway passage, past the bench at its middle (10 m in), to the north-west corner, where it turns along the north arcade. Someone walking it passes the bench at arm's length.

### Steps between arcade and court {#steps}

Along the arcade's open side, between each pair of piers, two shallow steps lead down to the court: each 0.15 m high and 0.35 m deep. Anyone crossing from the bench to the court steps down them.

## Dressing, practical lights and acoustics {#dressing}

### Dressing {#dressing-items}

A few fallen brown and yellow leaves in the court's corners and on the arcade steps; ivy on the lower east wall; worn hollows in the steps. Nothing else.

### Light {#light}

The low afternoon sun ([afternoon](../world/four-films.md#afternoon)) comes over the open south parapet from the south-south-west to south-west and lights the court's south and east parts, the fountain and the north arcade, slanting in through the north arcade's arches so that a person walking it passes from light to shade arch by arch. The west arcade, where the bench is, is roofed and has the sun behind its back wall, so it lies in soft shade all afternoon, with the bright court seen through its arches. The roof of the west arcade casts a shadow that grows eastward across the court as the afternoon goes on: about 3–5 m out from the arches at 14:30, and most of the way across (15–20 m) by 16:30, when only the court's east side, the fountain's east rim and the north arcade's eastern arches are still in sun (inference from the sun's height and the 5 m roof). No lamps are lit.

### Acoustics {#acoustics}

Stone on three sides: footsteps and voices carry and echo softly; the clock ticks audibly when the court is quiet. Recorded for the world; the hints are silent ([silence](../sound/palette.md#ambience)).

## Use of the space {#use}

### How the two use it {#bench-use}

Harry sits on the bench to read in the shade of the west arcade. Anyone coming from the archway passage approaches him along the arcade from his right (south) and can walk past and on to the north-west corner, then turn and come back. From the bench Harry can see the whole court, the fountain and the clock; someone standing in the court is seen through the arches. The bench seats two side by side, so moving from standing in front of it to sitting on it is the closing move the [crane premise](../../../instructions/commission.md#paper-crane-invitation) names.

### Occupancy {#occupancy}

In both films the court is empty except for the two: it is out of the way, and at that hour other students are at lessons, at the lake or in the common rooms. No one enters during either film.

## State changes {#state-changes}

### Baseline and change {#baseline}

At the start of each film the court is as described and the bench empty or with Harry on it, as the screenplay decides. Within a film a note or a crane can fall on the flagstones or the steps; leaves can be scuffed. Nothing carries over.
