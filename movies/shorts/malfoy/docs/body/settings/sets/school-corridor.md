# The corridor window bay

## Shape and dimensions {#shape}

### The bay {#bay}

A quiet window bay beside a castle corridor: a level rectangle 6.0 m along its window wall and 4.0 m deep, with a flat pale-grey plaster ceiling 3.0 m high. The walls are flat warm-grey stone and the floor matte grey flagstones. Its plain side walls close the left and right ends; its front edge lies open to the corridor along its whole width. Left and right are as seen facing the window; distances along the wall run from the left wall. The bay is the production's invention.

### The window and sill {#sill}

One rectangular window 3.6 m wide and 1.4 m tall, its left edge 0.8 m from the left wall, in a plain stone frame with a single fixed pane. Its flat stone sill, 0.30 m deep, runs the window's width with its top 0.95 m above the floor. Through the pane shows only pale sky.

### The bench {#bench}

A fixed backless bench of plain brown wood: a flat seat 1.8 m long, 0.45 m deep and 5 cm thick, its top 0.45 m above the floor ([sitting study](../../../research/everyday/sitting.md#seat-height)), on four square legs. Its long back edge stands 0.55 m out from the window wall and its left end 1.8 m from the left wall, so it runs 1.8–3.6 m along the wall and the sill's last 0.8 m (3.6–4.4 m) lies beyond its right end. It never slides or tips, even when someone shifts along it, and two students fit on it side by side.

## Zones and landmarks {#zones}

### Zones {#zone-list}

| Zone | Where | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bench, left half and right half | The seat's halves, 1.8–2.7 m and 2.7–3.6 m along the wall | Sitting |
| Sill right end | The 0.8 m of sill beyond the bench's right end | Where the two cups stand at the start |
| Right-end standing area | The floor from the bench's right end to the right wall, from the window wall to the front route | Standing at the sill's right end; coming round the bench |
| Front route | The floor in front of the bench, 3.0 m deep from the seat's front edge to the bay's open front | Walking, meeting, handing things over |
| Open front | The bay's mouth onto the corridor | Arriving and leaving |
| Gap behind the bench | The 0.55 m strip between the bench and the window wall | Nobody's route; too narrow to use |

### Landmarks {#landmarks}

The bench's front-right corner; the bench's right end; the sill's right end; the bay's open front at its middle.

## Routes, entrances and levels {#routes}

### One way in {#entrance}

The only way in or out is the open front from the corridor. Both ends of the bench connect to the front route, so a person can walk around its right end to the sill. One level throughout.

## Dressing, lights and acoustics {#dressing}

### Dressing {#bare}

The bench, the window and the sill, and nothing else: no door, curtain, painting or other furniture.

### Light {#light}

Late afternoon on a weekday, in the hour after the last lesson: warm low sunlight comes through the west-facing window and falls across the floor toward the open front, lighting the sill and the bench from behind; soft cooler light from the corridor fills the front of the bay, so faces turned toward the window and hands at the sill stay readable.

### Acoustics {#acoustics}

N/A: the films are silent ([sound](../sound/silence.md)).

### Occupancy {#occupancy}

After lessons the bay lies off the busy route; nobody passes or looks in during the film. It is not locked, and either student may leave by the open front at any time.

## Use {#use}

### How students use it {#students-use}

Students sit on the bench to talk or wait, stand at the sill and set things on it, and walk in and out along the front route. A person seated on the bench can turn at the waist to look back at the sill.

## State changes {#state}

### What it holds {#holds}

The bay itself never changes; only what rests on the sill or the bench does, as the [cups](../props/tea-cups.md#start) and [paper crane](../props/paper-crane.md#stages) records give.
