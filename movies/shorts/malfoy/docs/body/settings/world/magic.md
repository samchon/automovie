# Magic

The magic the films show: what a wand does, which spells appear and how they look, how charmed paper and brooms fly, and what magic cannot do here. These are canon used as an adopted choice ([canon frame](four-films.md#canon-frame)) or the production's inventions where marked. Duelling rules are in [practice duelling](practice-duelling.md); the wands themselves in [wands](../props/wands.md).

## Rules {#rules}

### A spell needs a wand in hand {#wand-in-hand}

A witch or wizard casts by holding their wand in one hand, making a short movement of wrist and arm that ends with the wand tip pointing at the target, and speaking the spell's word. Fifth-years cast spoken spells only; none of the films' people casts silently or without a wand. A person without their wand in hand cannot cast.

### Spells seen in the films {#spell-catalogue}

| Spell | Effect | Look (invented where canon is silent) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Expelliarmus* (Disarming Charm) | Tears the target's wand from their hand and sends it flying toward the caster; at practice strength it also jolts the target's arm and can push them back about half a step | A thin scarlet jet |
| *Protego* (Shield Charm) | Raises an invisible barrier in front of the caster that stops a jet; it lasts 1–2 s | A faint silver-blue shimmer, a disc about 1.2 m across, 0.3–0.5 m in front of the caster |
| *Avis Charta* (paper-flight charm; invention) | Makes a folded paper figure fly to the person named while casting | No light; the paper simply begins to move |
| *Finite* (counter-charm) | Ends a charm on the object the wand tip touches; here, the paper-flight charm | No light; the paper goes still |
| Slowing Charm (Madam Hooch's; invention, unnamed in canon) | Brakes a falling body to a gentle descent of about 2 m/s within about half a second | No light; the falling body checks sharply and then sinks slowly, limbs loose |

No other spell is cast in any film. The leads cast spells only in `look-at-me-duel` (the two duelling spells) and `paper-crane-invitation` (the paper-flight charm and its counter-charm, as its screenplay decides); the Slowing Charm belongs to Madam Hooch in `snitch-before-you`, under the range limit in [what magic does not do here](#limits).

### How a jet travels {#spell-jet}

Production's invention: a duelling jet leaves the wand tip at the end of the cast and travels in a straight line at about 15–25 m/s, crossing a 4–5 m practice distance in 0.16–0.33 s. It is a glowing streak about 5 cm thick and 0.5–0.7 m long that lights nearby surfaces faintly in its colour, and it ends where it strikes a person, a shield or a wall.

### Charmed paper flies to its addressee {#paper-flight}

Production's invention, after canon's flying paper memos: a folded paper figure charmed with *Avis Charta* flies at a walking pace (about 1–1.5 m/s) at head height (1.4–1.8 m) toward the person named, flapping if it has wings, and settles on that person's hand or shoulder. Until the addressee opens it, it keeps pulling toward them if anyone else holds it, with a force of about 0.2–0.5 N — the weight of 20–50 g in the hand: a pinch holds it without effort, and it drifts toward the addressee only when a grip loosens. Opening or unfolding it ends the charm. The caster can also cancel it by touching it with her wand tip and saying the counter-charm *Finite*; cancelled, it stops flying and pulling and stays wherever it is — in the air it drops, in a hand it stays in that hand. Cancelling does not bring it back to the caster.

### Brooms fly for their rider {#broom-flight}

A broomstick flies when someone sits astride it and wills it: it rises, hovers in place, accelerates, turns with the rider's lean and the hands' pressure on the handle, and descends. A racing broom in this practice cruises at 8–15 m/s and dives at up to 30 m/s; with two riders it cruises at no more than 10 m/s and needs about twice the room to turn, though for the seconds of a rescue it carries two at the speed it already has while its rider brakes ([saving a rider in the air](quidditch.md#air-rescue); [riders in a practice match](../../../research/flight/broom-riding-and-snitch-catch.md#riders-in-a-practice-match)). The broom, not the rider's arms, carries a rider's weight: anyone sitting on the shaft is held up by it. A rider who comes off falls under ordinary gravity; nothing slows the fall except Madam Hooch's charm within its range ([limits](#limits)). A riderless broom slows and hangs in the air within a few seconds. A broom whose tail is broken bucks and spins, loses lift and sinks in a descending spiral. The [broom record](../props/broomsticks.md) gives the brooms themselves.

### What magic does not do here {#limits}

Neither lead knows any charm that stops or slows a falling person; such magic is beyond fifth-year teaching in this world. During Quidditch play no rider carries a wand ([Quidditch](quidditch.md#no-wands)). Madam Hooch, the only adult present in any film, knows the Slowing Charm ([catalogue](#spell-catalogue)), but it works only on someone she can see within about 10 m of her wand. A fall from the Seekers' height lasts 1.7–2.5 s ([pitch levels](../sets/quidditch-pitch.md#levels)); she needs about 0.5 s to react and 0.3 s to cast, and from a hover she covers about 10 m in her first second of flight and 20 m in each second after. The charm itself needs about half a second to brake the body, which must be done before the ground. So she can slow a free fall only if it begins within about 15 m of her wand (for a fall from 15 m) to about 25 m (for a fall from 30 m), measured through the air from her post at mid-pitch, 10–15 m up; a fall that begins farther from her is over before she is in range. A rider who first hangs from a broken broom for 1–2 s gives her that much more time, about 30–60 m of reach ([who can reach a rider in time](quidditch.md#who-can-reach)). Away from her, and in the other three films, a fall is stopped, if at all, by a hand or a broom. No spell heals, summons objects, reads minds or changes anyone's feelings in these films.

## Tone {#tone}

### Magic is everyday {#everyday-magic}

For the characters, magic is schoolwork and ordinary life, not spectacle: a spell is cast as casually as a ball is thrown, nobody marvels at a flying crane or a hovering broom, and the films never pause to show magic off. Its feeling follows the films' [tone](four-films.md#register).
