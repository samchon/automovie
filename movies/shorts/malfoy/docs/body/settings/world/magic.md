# Wand magic

## Rules {#rules}

### Casting {#casting}

A spell needs a wand held in the hand, a target the caster is looking at, and the cast's three stages: a still aim of at least 0.25 s, a gesture of 0.15–0.25 s and the release at the gesture's end, at most one spell per 0.8 s per caster ([duel study](../../../research/duelling/practice-duel.md#casting)). Fifth-year students speak each spell's incantation aloud as they gesture; the films are silent, so the words exist only in the screenplays. A student with no wand in hand casts nothing, except through the one pre-charmed paper crane ([paper charm](#paper-charm)).

### Visible physical cue {#visible-cue}

Every spell shows as something physical the camera can follow: a streak of coloured light travelling from the wand tip, a translucent surface, or an object moving. A spell's effect always has a visible cause and a visible result, and it never stands in for what a body does ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#readable-action)). Spell light is plain: no halo, sparkle trail or glow on the caster. These visual rules are the production's invention, not a claim about canon's appearance.

### Practice only {#practice-only}

The cast know and use only these practice spells on each other: the practice jinx, the disarming charm and the shield charm, plus the levitation charm on objects. Their names and broad effects follow Harry Potter canon (the Disarming Charm, the Shield Charm and the Levitation Charm); their timing, colours and limits below are the production's. The incantations are canon's *Expelliarmus* for the disarming charm, *Protego* for the shield charm and *Wingardium Leviosa* for the levitation charm; the practice jinx is spoken *Flipendo*, a knockback jinx's word from the wider franchise that the production adopts for it.

| Spell | Visible cue | Speed and size | Effect on arrival | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Practice jinx | A thin blue-white streak, 0.4 m long and 2 cm thick, that vanishes on impact with a small flash | 5 ± 1 m/s | Stings: knocks the struck arm, wand or aim aside; chips weakened mortar or brittle stone | Cannot injure seriously, lift or throw a person, or break sound stone |
| Disarming charm | A scarlet streak of the same size | 5 ± 1 m/s | The struck hand opens and its wand flies 2–4 m on a rising arc, away from the target and toward the caster's side | Affects only what the hand holds |
| Shield charm | A translucent pale-blue oval surface; form in [the shield](../props/shield.md) | Forms 0.25–0.4 s after the gesture | Stops spells and light thrown or flying objects, which drop on its far side | Cannot hold up or deflect a heavy falling mass such as the stone cap; lasts while the caster keeps the wand raised, at most 3 s |
| Levitation charm | A swish-and-flick gesture; a faint pale-gold glow on the lifted object | Rises at 0.3 m/s; follows the wand's movement at up to 1 m/s | Lifts and holds one loose object of up to 5 kg | Not a person, not anything fixed or heavier; drops when the wand drops or the caster looks away |

The streak speed is a deliberate slowing that lets a 24 fps frame show a two-metre shot ([duel study](../../../research/duelling/practice-duel.md#casting)).

### Deflection {#deflection}

A wand swept across an incoming streak turns it aside without any spell, by up to 60° from its line, and the streak flies on at its speed until it strikes something. Deflection is possible only when the defender is watching the caster's wand hand and the streak's travel time covers the defender's reaction plus the sweep, which at these speeds needs a casting distance of 1.6–2.0 m or more ([duel study](../../../research/duelling/practice-duel.md#deflection)). A deflected jinx keeps its effect: it can still chip weakened stone where it lands.

### Paper charm {#paper-charm}

Carina knows a small charm, cast with her wand beforehand, that lets one folded paper fly once: launched from a fingertip, it glides level along its launch line at 1.0–1.5 m/s for at most 6 m, stops dead in a hand that closes on it, and settles to the floor if nobody catches it ([handover study](../../../research/everyday/handover.md#crane)). Contact or landing ends the charm; it neither steers, evades a hand nor flies again. The charm is the production's invention, after canon's flying paper memos.

### What magic does not do {#limits}

In these films magic does not heal, move or lift a person, cushion a fall, summon someone, read a mind, stop a falling heavy mass or carry anyone through the air except on a broom ([broom flight](flight.md)). Nobody can apparate inside the school's grounds (canon).
