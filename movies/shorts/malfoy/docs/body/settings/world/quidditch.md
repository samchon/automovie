# Quidditch

The rules and conduct of the practice match in `snitch-before-you`. Quidditch is canon used as an adopted choice; distances are canon's where it gives them, converted to metres, and the production's inventions where marked. The players are recorded in [Carina](../cast/carina-malfoy.md), [Harry](../cast/harry-potter.md), [Madam Hooch](../cast/rolanda-hooch.md) and [the riders](../crowds/quidditch-riders.md); the balls in [Quidditch balls](../props/quidditch-balls.md); the brooms in [broomsticks](../props/broomsticks.md); the field in [the pitch](../sets/quidditch-pitch.md). How the bodies ride and catch comes from the [riding study](../../../research/flight/broom-riding-and-snitch-catch.md).

## Rules {#rules}

### Two teams of seven {#teams}

Each team has seven riders: three Chasers, two Beaters, one Keeper and one Seeker. The practice match flies Gryffindor against Slytherin, full teams, fourteen riders in the air ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#snitch-before-you)).

### The balls and the score {#balls-and-score}

Chasers pass the Quaffle and score ten points by throwing it through one of the opponents' three hoops; the Keeper defends the hoops. Two Bludgers fly by themselves at the nearest riders, and Beaters knock them away with bats. The Golden Snitch flies freely about the pitch; the Seeker who catches it in the hand ends the match and wins 150 points for the team. In a real match only a Seeker's catch ends play; in this practice the referee may also end it ([practice match](#practice-match)).

### A practice match {#practice-match}

Production's invention: before the season Madam Hooch referees a practice match between the two teams to test new players and brooms. It is played to the same rules as a real match, without spectators and without the points mattering beyond the day. It ends at a Snitch catch or when Madam Hooch ends it.

### No wands in play {#no-wands}

Players leave their wands with Madam Hooch before play begins; nobody in the air carries a wand. Only Madam Hooch has hers.

### Stopping play {#stopping-play}

Madam Hooch stops play with a long blast of her whistle when a rider is in danger or a foul is committed; at the whistle every rider stops where they are and hovers — except a rider already near enough to save the one in danger ([saving a rider in the air](#air-rescue)), whom the rule of the air expects to help rather than hover. The Quaffle and the Bludgers are charmed to answer the referee's whistle: at the long blast they stop and hang where they are within about a second. The Snitch does not answer the whistle and flies on. Play restarts at a short blast. A Bludger already striking when the whistle sounds completes that strike.

### Riding two to a broom {#two-to-a-broom}

A broom may carry a second rider in an emergency; a rider on a broken broom may climb onto a teammate's or any rider's broom. The match does not continue with a rider doubled up: play stops.

### Seeker craft {#seeker-craft}

Seekers fly above the main play, 15–30 m up, circling at 8–15 m/s and watching for the gold glint of the Snitch; when one sees it, they dive or sprint for it at up to 30 m/s, and the other Seeker follows the first's line. A Seeker may block the other's line but may not grab another rider or their broom, except to save them.

### An almost certain catch {#almost-certain-catch}

Players call a catch "almost certain" when a Seeker's open hand is within about one forearm's length (0.25–0.3 m) of the Snitch's path in the last half-second before it would arrive ([catching](../../../research/flight/broom-riding-and-snitch-catch.md#catching)). Even then the Snitch can dodge; nothing but the closing hand makes it a catch.

### Saving a rider in the air {#air-rescue}

A rider can save another only with their broom: by taking the wrist or arm of someone still holding onto a broom and steering so they can swing a leg over the shaft, or by diving onto a falling rider's path, matching their fall so that they weigh almost nothing in the hand, and guiding them onto the shaft before levelling out ([reaching a falling rider](../../../research/flight/broom-riding-and-snitch-catch.md#reaching-a-falling-rider)). A rescuer must be within about 3–5 m and dive at once; nobody catches a falling body at arm's length without matching it. The match and the take must happen within the first 1–1.5 s of the fall, when the faller has dropped 5–11 m and is falling at 10–15 m/s; braking from that speed to a level two-rider flight then costs another 8–12 m of height. So a free fall can be saved only if it begins about 25 m or more above the grass — in the Seekers' upper air — and a rider hanging from a sinking broom, which descends at only 2–5 m/s ([broken tail](../props/broomsticks.md#broken-tail)), can be taken from any height. Once on the shaft, the broom carries both ([broom flight](magic.md#broom-flight)).

## Time, weather and season {#time-weather-season}

### Afternoon practice in calm air {#practice-hour}

Practice matches are flown after lessons in daylight, between 14:30 and 16:30 in mid-October ([four films](four-films.md#afternoon)), in the calm, dry air the [weather record](four-films.md#weather) gives. Low south-western sun makes riders flying west squint, and the Snitch glints brightest when it is between a rider and the sun.

## Tone {#tone}

### Brisk and competitive, never cruel {#match-tone}

The practice is competitive — both teams want to win — but sporting: no deliberate fouls, no taunting that matters. A fall stops everyone at once. The film's warmth comes from the two Seekers, not from the match ([tone](four-films.md#register)).
