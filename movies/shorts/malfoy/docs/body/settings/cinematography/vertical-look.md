# Vertical look

The shared look of the four films: lenses, framing, camera movement, light and grade for a 60-second, 9:16 frame at 720 × 1280 ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#vertical-sixty-seconds)). It is drawn from the [vertical short coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/vertical-short-coverage.md). Each screenplay chooses its shots within this look.

## Lens kit {#lens-kit}

### Four fields of view {#fields-of-view}

Lenses are named by the vertical field of view they give in the tall frame, so they mean the same thing on every camera:

| Name | Vertical field of view | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wide | 60–70° | A whole set or a whole pitch; both people full-length at a distance |
| Normal | 40–45° | Full figures and two-shots |
| Medium-long | 28–32° | Medium shots and over-the-shoulder shots |
| Long | 18–24° | Close-ups of faces and inserts of hands and objects |

No fisheye, no extreme telephoto.

### Camera height {#camera-height}

Default eye level, about 1.5 m for standing people and about 1.2 m for people seated on the courtyard bench; a high angle looks down on the duelling platform's line; low angles are used in the air to show altitude against the sky.

## Framing grammar {#framing}

### Faces and contacts in the safe centre {#safe-centre}

Eyes sit 25–40 % down from the top edge; every contact a film depends on — a hand on a wrist, fingers on a crane or a note, a hand closing on the Snitch, hands at a waist — sits inside the central zone (x 110–610 px, y 190–830 px at 720 × 1280); nothing essential sits in the bottom quarter ([the tall frame](../../../research/cinematography/vertical-short-coverage.md#frame)).

### Two people in a tall frame {#two-people}

Two people within about 1 m share the frame side by side, full-length or from the hands up. Farther apart, they are staged in depth along the lens — one near, one far — or covered in singles and over-the-shoulder shots. A contact is framed from the hands to the faces, so the hands and both responses share one frame.

### Inserts {#inserts}

An insert frames hands and the object or wrist they hold so that they fill the central zone, at the long field of view, from a side that shows the fingers closing. A contact two-shot frames from the hands to both faces at the medium-long field of view. These are the two shapes in which the films show their contacts, as the [seen-contacts choice](../../../instructions/direction.md#seen-contacts-and-pace) requires.

## Camera movement {#movement}

### Motivated and steady {#motivated}

The camera is mostly still or moving slowly — a slow push in toward a held look, a slow drift with a walk. It moves faster only when the action does: tracking alongside a rider in flight at a constant size, whipping to follow a falling body. No handheld shake. In the air the camera flies with the riders, beside, behind or below them.

## Lighting style {#lighting}

### Natural afternoon light {#natural}

All light comes from the afternoon sun, low in the south-west, warm and directional, with soft fill from sky and stone ([afternoon](../world/four-films.md#afternoon)). Indoors it enters through windows as bright bars with visible dust ([duelling room light](../sets/duelling-room.md#light)); in the courtyard the court is sunlit and the arcade in shade ([courtyard light](../sets/clock-tower-courtyard.md#light)); over the pitch the riders are side-lit against open sky. Spell jets add brief coloured light ([spell jet](../world/magic.md#spell-jet)). The sun does not visibly change within a film's minute, so a change of brightness comes only from where people and camera are: from the shaded arcade into the sunlit court, from the room's shade into a window's bar of light, from the low play into open high air.

## Grade {#grade}

### Warm and soft {#warm}

Warm, golden highlights; soft contrast with open shadows; natural skin; house colours (Slytherin green and silver, Gryffindor scarlet and gold) kept rich but not neon. On the delivered hint, which carries no textures, the grade applies only to light and shade; colour is for the later reference images.
