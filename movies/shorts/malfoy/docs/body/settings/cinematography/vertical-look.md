# The vertical look

## Lens kit {#lens}

### Normal lenses on a tall frame {#normal}

Every film is framed 9:16 at 720 × 1280 ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#format)). Ground scenes use a vertical field of view of 40–55°, which on the tall frame sees only 23–33° across, so faces, hands and paper read at medium size without wide-angle stretch ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/vertical-coverage.md#geometry)). Flight uses 55–70° vertically to hold two riders, their brooms and the lawn below in one frame.

## Framing grammar {#framing}

### Two people in a tall frame {#two-people}

Two people are shown in profile facing each other across the frame or on a diagonal in depth, the nearer lower in frame and the farther higher, because side by side at full figure they do not fit ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/vertical-coverage.md#geometry)). A full two-shot fills 55–80% of the frame height with the figures, a wide shot 35–55% and includes the landmark they relate to: the support, the bench, the sill, the goals ([sizes](../../../research/cinematography/vertical-coverage.md#sizes)).

### Hands and objects {#hands}

When a hand or a small object carries the beat, a grip, a handover, a crane in flight, a note on the paving, the frame includes both people's hands and the object for the whole action, within the margins: faces below the top 10% of the frame, hands and held objects above the bottom 12% and 6% in from each side ([margins](../../../research/cinematography/vertical-coverage.md#margins)).

### In the air {#in-the-air}

Riders are seen from a three-quarter side view at their own height or within 20° of it, with the lawn or a goal in the frame as the reference for height and speed ([movement](../../../research/cinematography/vertical-coverage.md#movement)).

## Camera movement {#movement}

### An operator who follows {#operator}

The camera moves like a careful operator: it follows a walk, arcs to keep a pair in profile as they turn, pushes in when the hands take over and pulls back when a danger or a team must be read; it never shakes. In flight it travels alongside at the riders' speed, 4–10 m away ([movement](../../../research/cinematography/vertical-coverage.md#movement)). Standing scenes are seen from 1.2–1.6 m high and seated ones from 0.9–1.2 m ([eye height](../../../research/cinematography/vertical-coverage.md#eye-height)).

### Cutting {#cutting}

Shots average 3–7 s; one shot runs unbroken from a contact's first touch to its release; screen direction holds across every cut ([cutting](../../../research/cinematography/vertical-coverage.md#cutting)).

## Lighting style {#lighting}

### Natural daylight {#daylight}

Each film is lit by its set's daylight only, as the set records it: [court](../sets/training-court.md#light), [corridor](../sets/school-corridor.md#light), [courtyard](../sets/side-courtyard.md#light), [pitch](../sets/quidditch-pitch.md#light). Spell light adds no illumination to the scene. Light never changes within a film.

## Grade {#grade}

### True house colours {#grade-colours}

A clean grade: neutral for the court and the pitch, slightly warm for the two late-afternoon films. Skin tones, the pale and dark hair, the white collars and the green, silver, scarlet and gold of the houses stay true so the two students stay distinct in every frame.
