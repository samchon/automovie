# docs/body/settings/props/broomsticks.md#form

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet gives the brooms' common build with a position table along the shaft, the stirrups that carry the feet, each named broom's appearance, the team and school brooms, and the hint appearance: "A long thin rod with a fan-shaped tail and four stirrups hanging on short struts below the shaft".

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

The build is designed for this film's tandem flight: "Rear stirrups (one each side, a second rider's)" at 0.35 m and a "Second rider's seat (hips)" at 0.40 m ahead of the binding, with the second rider's "thighs and knees close on the outside of the front rider's hips, the way a motorcycle pillion's do, so neither rider's legs cross the other's"; the named brooms are the leads' own (Harry's Firebolt, Carina's Nimbus 2001).

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

Every contact point has a distance in the table: rear stirrups 0.35 m, second rider's hips 0.40 m, front stirrups 0.70 m, rider's hips 0.75 m, hands 1.00–1.10 m and 1.10–1.20 m, tip 1.40 m. The stirrups are placed in three dimensions ("hung on a strut about 0.45 m below the shaft and about 0.15 m out to the side"), and the resulting pose is stated so a solver can meet it: "knees bent to about 110°" with "hip to sole about 0.45 m with thigh and shin of about 0.40 m each". The hint limit is stated: "a broom is identified by its rider".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

All brooms share one file because they share one build; flight rules are routed to the magic record and riding to the study in the opening paragraph and the build fact's links.

# docs/body/settings/props/broomsticks.md#handling

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet states how a broom is ridden, mounted on the ground and in the air, and ridden by two.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

It includes this film's in-air mounting: "a second rider can climb onto a hovering or slowly moving broom from close alongside", and the two-rider limits "cruises at no more than 10 m/s and needs about twice the room to turn".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

Riding is given as contacts: "inner thighs pressing it, soles in the front stirrups, both hands on the handle ahead of the body — the forward hand 0.35–0.45 m ahead of the seat"; the passenger "holds the front rider's waist, closes the knees on the outside of the front rider's hips, keeps both feet in the rear stirrups". Control is stated: "Folding forward speeds the broom; leaning turns it; lifting or lowering the handle climbs or dives."

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

The postures link the riding study and the limits link the magic and Quidditch records; no flight or mounting event is decided here.

## @evidence instructions/motion.md#effectors-to-targets

Riding, mounting and two-up riding are each a set of effectors on targets: seat to shaft, soles to the front or rear stirrups, hands to the handle at stated distances, a passenger's hands to the front rider's waist, knees to the outside of the front rider's hips and a leg over the shaft.

# docs/body/settings/props/broomsticks.md#state-changes

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet gives the baseline ("Every broom is whole at the start of the film") and the one change a broom can suffer, the broken tail, with its consequences.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

The change is the one this film's danger can use: "A Bludger striking a broom's tail can snap the binding", after which "the rider may be thrown off or left hanging from the shaft by the hands".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

The broken broom's behaviour is quantified ("sinks in a descending spiral at about 2–5 m/s") and the rider's two outcomes have stated limits ("can hold on for several seconds; one thrown off falls freely"); "A broken broom does not mend within the film".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

How a rider is saved is routed to the Quidditch rescue rule; whether a broom breaks is left to the screenplay.
