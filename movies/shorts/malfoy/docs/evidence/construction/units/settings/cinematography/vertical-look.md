# docs/body/settings/cinematography/vertical-look.md#lens-kit

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet gives the lens kit and the camera heights: four named fields of view with their uses, "No fisheye, no extreme telephoto", and default eye levels.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

Lenses are named "by the vertical field of view they give in the tall frame", which suits these 9:16 films, and camera heights follow the leads' measured eye heights ("about 1.5 m for standing people and about 1.2 m for people seated on the courtyard bench"), with angles named for the duel platform and the air.

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

Each lens has a checkable range (Wide 60–70°, Normal 40–45°, Medium-long 28–32°, Long 18–24°) and a use; the exclusions are explicit.

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

The cinematography record owns the look; the opening paragraph leaves each film's shots to its screenplay ("Each screenplay chooses its shots within this look").

# docs/body/settings/cinematography/vertical-look.md#framing

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet gives the framing grammar for the tall frame: where faces and contacts sit, how two people share it, and the two shapes used to show contacts.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

It lists the films' own contacts as what must sit in the safe centre — "a hand on a wrist, fingers on a crane or a note, a hand closing on the Snitch, hands at a waist" — and solves two people in a tall frame by staging "in depth along the lens".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

The rules are measurable: "Eyes sit 25–40 % down from the top edge"; the central zone is "x 110–610 px, y 190–830 px at 720 × 1280"; two people share the frame side by side "within about 1 m"; an insert is "at the long field of view, from a side that shows the fingers closing"; a contact two-shot is "at the medium-long field of view".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

The framing shapes are the look; the requirement that contacts be seen and the shot pace are owned by the direction instruction, which the inserts fact cites rather than restates. The safe zone links its research section.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#vertical-sixty-seconds

The framing grammar is designed for the 9:16, 720 × 1280 frame the commission fixes, giving pixel bounds for faces and contacts in that raster.

## @evidence instructions/direction.md#seen-contacts-and-pace

The inserts fact defines the two shapes — an insert and a contact two-shot — in which the films show their contacts, as the seen-contacts choice requires; the safe-centre fact keeps every such contact inside the frame's central zone.

# docs/body/settings/cinematography/vertical-look.md#movement

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet states the camera's movement style: "mostly still or moving slowly", faster "only when the action does".

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

The moves named are these films': "a slow push in toward a held look, a slow drift with a walk", "tracking alongside a rider in flight at a constant size, whipping to follow a falling body", and in the air the camera "flies with the riders, beside, behind or below them".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

The style is checkable: still or slow by default, fast only with motivating action, and "No handheld shake."

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

It sets the movement style; which move a shot uses is left to each screenplay.

# docs/body/settings/cinematography/vertical-look.md#lighting

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet states the lighting style for all four places and the only cause of brightness change within a film.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

It ties light to the films' own sets and hour — window bars in the duelling room, the sunlit court and shaded arcade, the corridor's "slanting panels across the floor and up the north wall", riders "side-lit against open sky" — and to spell jets.

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

The single-source rule ("All light comes from the afternoon sun") and its consequence are checkable: "a change of brightness comes only from where people and camera are", with the three named transitions, "from the shaded arcade into the sunlit court, from the room's or the corridor's shade into a window's bar of light, from the low play into open high air".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

Sun height and each set's light are linked to their owners (four-films, duelling room, courtyard, corridor, magic) instead of restated.

# docs/body/settings/cinematography/vertical-look.md#grade

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet states the grade: "Warm, golden highlights; soft contrast with open shadows; natural skin".

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

It names the house colours of these films ("Slytherin green and silver, Gryffindor scarlet and gold") and states how a textureless hint carries the grade.

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

The limit is explicit: "On the delivered hint, which carries no textures, the grade applies only to light and shade; colour is for the later reference images."

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

The grade is part of the look owned here; no edit decision is made.
