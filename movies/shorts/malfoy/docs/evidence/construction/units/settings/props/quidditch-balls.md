# docs/body/settings/props/quidditch-balls.md#form

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet gives the form of all four balls and their hint appearance: Quaffle, two Bludgers and the Golden Snitch, "A gold ball the size of a large walnut, about 4 cm across".

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

The Snitch's look is tied to this film's light ("most brightly against the low south-western sun") and the hint fact states the film's reading condition: it "must be shown large in frame or near a hand to be read".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

Sizes are given in centimetres for each ball (Quaffle "about 30 cm", Bludgers "about 25 cm", Snitch "about 4 cm" with wings "to about 10 cm"), with material and colour.

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

The four balls share one file as one set used together; scoring rules are routed to the Quidditch record.

# docs/body/settings/props/quidditch-balls.md#handling

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet states how the Quaffle and Bludgers are handled in play, how the Snitch flies, and how it is caught.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

The Snitch's flight gives the window this film's near catch needs: "It can hover still for a second or two, which is when a Seeker can close on it."

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

Speeds and heights are stated (Bludgers "at 10–20 m/s"; the Snitch "at up to about 20 m/s" and "mostly 2–30 m above the grass"); the catch is given as an effector and target: "the open hand is brought to the Snitch's path and closes around it at the contact moment".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

The catch law links the riding study and the broken tail links the broom record; whether anyone catches the Snitch is left to the screenplay.

# docs/body/settings/props/quidditch-balls.md#state-changes

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

It states what this film's Snitch does if not caught and what a late catch is worth: "it flies on, even after Madam Hooch stops play, since it does not answer her whistle, and a catch made while play is stopped still ends the match and wins".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

The start state has two stated options ("released and in play, or in their crate at mid-pitch if the screenplay begins with the release"), the catch rule is unconditional, and the reset is stated.

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

It links the stopping-play rule that owns the catch rule rather than restating its reasons, and leaves the start option to the screenplay.
