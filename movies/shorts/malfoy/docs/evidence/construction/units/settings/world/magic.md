# docs/body/settings/world/magic.md#rules

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet gives every magical rule the films use: casting needs a wand in hand, the closed catalogue of spells seen (two duelling spells, the paper-flight charm, its counter-charm and Madam Hooch's Slowing Charm), the jet's travel, charmed paper flight, broom flight, and what magic does not do.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

The rules serve these premises and close the obvious ways out of them: cancelling the crane's charm "does not bring it back to the caster", so the tug must be by hand; neither lead knows a charm that slows a fall; and Madam Hooch's charm is bounded by range, so a fall far from her is stopped "if at all, by a hand or a broom".

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

Values and limits are checkable: the jet travels "at about 15–25 m/s, crossing a 4–5 m practice distance in 0.16–0.33 s"; paper flies "(about 1–1.5 m/s) at head height (1.4–1.8 m)" with "a force of about 0.2–0.5 N"; brooms cruise "at 8–15 m/s and dives at up to 30 m/s", two riders "no more than 10 m/s", carried "at the speed it already has while its rider brakes" during a rescue; the Slowing Charm "Brakes a falling body to a gentle descent of about 2 m/s within about half a second", and her reach is derived from her reaction, casting, flight and that braking: "within about 15 m of her wand (for a fall from 15 m) to about 25 m (for a fall from 30 m)", and "about 30–60 m of reach" for a rider who first hangs for 1–2 s. Inventions are marked in the catalogue and the jet and paper-flight facts.

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

Spell rules are owned here; their use in a duel, the rescue branches and the who-can-reach comparison are linked to the duelling and Quidditch records rather than restated. No spell's use in a film is decided beyond which spells can appear.

# docs/body/settings/world/magic.md#tone

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet states how magic feels in these films: "magic is schoolwork and ordinary life, not spectacle".

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

It names the films' own magic — "nobody marvels at a flying crane or a hovering broom" — and states that "the films never pause to show magic off", which follows from the films' bodies-first tone.

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#usable-facts

The fact is checkable in a screenplay: a cast is as casual as a thrown ball, no character reacts with wonder, and no beat exists to display magic.

## @evidence shared/principles/settings.md#record-and-boundary

It links the work's tone owner (`docs/body/settings/world/four-films.md#register`) rather than restating it, and adds only what is specific to magic.
