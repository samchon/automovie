# docs/research/flight/broom-riding-and-snitch-catch.md

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice

Broom flight is invented; the study names its analogues (a saddle ridden astride, motorcycle pillion riding, one-handed catching) and states where each departs (no ground, no handlebars, a self-propelled Snitch). It gives the law of riding astride (seat position, knee grip, hand positions, folding for speed, leaning to turn, climb and dive) and its effector targets; the pillion law (body alignment, holds, knee grip, chosen shoulder, following the lean, mounting order) adapted to tandem flight with its targets; and the catching law (closure onset before contact, peak closing speed at contact, continued closing, difficulty) adapted to an intercepting hand with an aperture and an "almost certain" catch condition. "Riders in a practice match" gives speeds and heights by position for everyone in the air, including Seeker cruise and dive speeds and the two-rider limit; "Reaching a falling rider" gives the free-fall law and the speed-matching condition under which a rider can be saved, and states that an arm's-length catch without matching is not possible. "Level markers", "Filming" and "Measurable targets" give the skilled-versus-insecure rider and catcher, the tracking and angle grammar, and the tabulated values.
