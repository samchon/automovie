# docs/research/behavior/proxemics-and-gaze.md

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice

The depicted practice is how two schoolmates keep distance and hold eye contact. Each has a law (distance shrinks with closeness and a change of zone reads as approach or withdrawal; gaze has a comfortable length whose breaking or extending reads as shyness or intensity), reported values with their conditions, and a use table that turns the values into the films' zones (acquaintance 0.9–1.3 m at every film's start, approach, contact range, shared bench or broom) and look lengths (ordinary, nervous, charged), marked as the production's choice. "Conduct, level and filming" states who is present and how distance and looks must be framed to read; "Measurable targets" lists the four values with their bases. The study does not decide which crossing happens; it says so.

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#traceable-sources

Distances come from sorokowska-2017 (accepted manuscript pp. 4, 11, 12–13) with the method's limit stated — a preference marked on a 0–220 cm diagram, not a measured standing distance. Gaze values come from binetti-2016 (pp. 3–4 and Fig. 1b) with its sample, stimulus and the stranger-on-video condition stated. Every adopted range in the use tables is labelled the production's choice built on those values.
