# Walking, stopping and turning

What a fifteen-year-old's ordinary walk looks like in the corridor, courtyard and training-court films: how fast, how many steps, how a walker stops at a person or a bench and turns back. Sources are in the [index](../index.md).

## Law {#law}

### Gait {#gait}

A walk alternates single support (one foot planted, the other swinging) with brief double support when both feet are down. Each stride is two steps; each step moves the body forward by one step length while the pelvis rises and falls once and the arms swing opposite the legs.

| Measure, 14–21 years (VOSS Table 2, self-selected; Table 3, fast) | Self-selected | Fast without running |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed, m/s | 1.28 ± 0.15 | 1.65 ± 0.17 |
| Cadence, steps/min | 115.4 ± 8.2 | 132.7 ± 8.8 |
| Stride length, m | 1.34 ± 0.16 | 1.50 ± 0.18 (girls 1.42 ± 0.14, boys 1.62 ± 0.16) |
| Double support, % of cycle | 17.9 ± 3.3 | 14.1 ± 3.3 |
| Swing, % of cycle | 41.1 ± 1.6 | 43.1 ± 1.8 |

These are reported measurements from six inertial sensors on a 25 m walkway, two minutes per pace. The 11–13 group walks at 1.34 ± 0.14 m/s with 122 ± 11 steps/min, so the 14–21 values bracket a fifteen-year-old.

Step length scales with leg length. The production derives each walker's step length from the self-selected stride (0.67 m per step for the group mean) times the ratio of the walker's stature to the group's implied mean stature, a production inference: VOSS does not report stature by group.

### Pace by intent {#pace}

| Intent | Speed, m/s | Cadence, steps/min | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hesitant or rehearsing walk | 0.9–1.1 | 95–110 | Production choice below the self-selected range |
| Ordinary walk | 1.15–1.40 | 107–124 | VOSS self-selected mean ± 1 SD |
| Hurried walk, catching up | 1.50–1.80 | 124–142 | VOSS fast mean ± 1 SD |
| Run | Above 2.0 | Above 150 | Production choice; walking ends where double support vanishes |

### Starting, stopping and turning {#transitions}

Production inferences, since no retained source measures them for this age: a walk reaches its pace in the first step and stops in the last, so a walk of n steps is n step periods long with no extra ramp; the final step is a half step that brings the trailing foot beside the planted one. A turn in place of up to 180° takes two steps, pivoting on the ball of the planted foot while the other foot steps around; a turn of up to 45° happens inside an ordinary step. A walker who stops facing a person stands at conversational distance, 0.8–1.2 m between chests (production choice for an arm's-length exchange), and at 0.5–0.9 m when about to hand something over. A walker stopped by someone stepping into the path stops a hand's breadth short, about 0.1 m between the two fronts, so about 0.32 m between the two pelvis points for chests about 0.2 m deep (production choice). A walk whose straight line would pass within 0.5 m of another standing person bends around that person, passing 0.6 m from the person's centre on the side the walk already favours (production choice; people keep about that clearance when overtaking or crossing at close quarters).

## Measurable targets {#targets}

- An ordinary walk shows 1.15–1.40 m/s and 107–124 steps per minute; a hurried walk 1.5–1.8 m/s.
- No foot slides along the floor while planted: planted-foot drift under 2 cm per step.
- Every walk between two landmarks is a whole number of steps, ending with both feet planted.
- Arms swing opposite the legs unless a hand carries something or is held out.
