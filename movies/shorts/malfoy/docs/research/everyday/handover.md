# Handing over, taking back and carrying small things

How two people pass a paper crane, a note or a tea cup, how an offer is withdrawn, what happens when both pull, how a dropped sheet falls and is picked up, and how a filled cup is carried. Sources are in the [index](../index.md).

## Law {#law}

### Phases of a handover {#phases}

KOP (abstract; Section 4) divides a handover between a giver and a receiver into four phases: reach and grasp (the giver takes the object), object transport (the giver brings it toward the receiver while the receiver reaches), object transfer (both hold it while the load passes from one to the other) and end of handover (the giver withdraws). The giver's start of release is planned ahead, but the release itself is feedback-controlled during the transfer phase: the giver opens only as the receiver's grip takes the load, and adapts to the receiver's intention.

| Value | Range | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer phase, both hands on the object | 0.50 s for 90 g; 0.64 s for 1.8 kg | KOP Section 4.4, reporting Mason and MacKenzie (2005) and Döhring et al. (2020); measured |
| Transfer phase for a 5 g sheet of paper | 0.35–0.50 s | Production inference: lighter than the lightest measured object |
| Transfer phase for a 0.5 kg filled cup | 0.50–0.64 s | Interpolated between the two measured objects |
| Transport and reach | 0.5–1.0 s over a 0.3–0.6 m reach | Production choice; ordinary unhurried arm movement |
| Handover point | Near the midpoint between the two chests, lower as they stand farther apart | KOP Section 3.5, reporting Hansen et al. (2017); direction measured, no value retained |

### Ownership {#ownership}

An object changes hands only when the receiver's fingers have closed on it before the giver's open. A grasp is "closed" when the receiver's thumb and fingers meet the object on opposite faces. The rule follows KOP's feedback-controlled release and is the production's test for every transfer.

### Offer and withdrawal {#withdrawal}

An offer is the transport phase without a transfer: the object travels to the handover point and the giver's grip stays closed. Withdrawing it reverses the transport, 0.3–0.6 s back to the giver's chest or hip, faster than the offer because it is not aimed (production choice). A withdrawal that starts after the receiver's fingers touch the object becomes a pull.

### Opposing pulls {#pulls}

Paper under two opposing grips creases at the first pull and tears if both keep pulling. A person who feels the sheet buckle stops pulling within one reaction time, 0.21–0.25 s (the reaction-time range in the [duel study](../duelling/practice-duel.md#reaction)). Production rule: a sheet survives one buckle; a second sustained pull tears it.

### Dropped paper {#dropped}

A released sheet of 10–16 cm does not fall straight: it flutters side to side and lands flat. Production choice, since no measured source was retained: it falls 1 m in 1.2–1.8 s, drifting up to 0.3 m sideways, and lies still where it lands in still air.

### Picking up from the floor {#pick-up}

A person reaching to the floor in front of the feet plants the feet about shoulder-width, bends the knees and hinges at the hips with the back inclined forward, reaches with one hand and pinches the object at an edge. Lowering takes 0.8–1.2 s and rising 0.8–1.2 s (production inference from the [sitting study](sitting.md#stand-to-sit), a shallower movement). Two people bending toward the same point between their feet from 0.8–0.9 m apart bring their heads to meet at about forehead height before their hands reach the floor, because each head travels forward over the toes as the hips hinge: with each person's head about 0.35 m ahead of the feet at full hinge, two heads 0.85 m apart leave no gap (production inference from the cast's body measures).

### The enchanted paper crane {#crane}

Closest real analogue: a hand-launched paper glider. The production departs from it on purpose: the charmed crane leaves the fingertip at 1.0–1.5 m/s and glides level along its launch line for at most 6 m, much slower than a dart-shaped paper glider, so the audience can follow it. It stops dead in a hand that closes on it.

### Carrying a filled cup {#cups}

MAYER (summary only) reports that in an ordinary mug the coffee's lowest sloshing mode is excited by a normal walking pace, so each step feeds it; spills typically come between the seventh and tenth step; walking slowly, accelerating gently and watching the cup all reduce spilling. The production's carry rule therefore keeps a cup carried at chest-to-waist height, upright within 10°, at a walk no faster than 1.0 m/s, for no more than six steps, starting and stopping over a full step. A cup is handed over the same way as any object but never released into an open palm: the receiver supports its body or handle first.

## Outcome table {#outcomes}

| Giver | Receiver | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offers and opens after the receiver closes | Closes on the object | Transferred |
| Offers and withdraws before contact | Reaching or open palm | Withdrawn; the giver keeps it |
| Opens before the receiver closes | Reaching or open palm | Dropped; paper flutters to the floor, a cup breaks |
| Keeps pulling after contact | Keeps holding | Paper creases; a second sustained pull tears it |
| Lets go of a charmed crane in flight | Closes a hand on its path | Caught; the charm ends |
| Launches a charmed crane, nobody catches it | None | Glides its 6 m and settles to the floor |

## Measurable targets {#targets}

- In every transfer both hands touch the object for 0.35–0.64 s, and the giver's grip opens only after the receiver's closes.
- A handover point lies within 0.15 m of the midpoint between the two chests, at waist to chest height.
- A carried cup tilts under 10° and is never carried faster than 1.0 m/s or for more than six steps.
- A dropped sheet takes 1.2–1.8 s per metre of fall and lands flat.
