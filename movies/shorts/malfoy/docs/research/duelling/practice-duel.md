# Practice duelling with wands

How two fifth-year students duel for practice: stance, distance, footwork, casting, deflection, shielding, disarming, levitation, and a wrist grip and its escape. A wand duel is invented; its closest real analogue is fencing for stance, distance and attack timing, and a thrown projectile met by a reaction for the spells. Where the production departs from those analogues it says so. Sources are in the [index](../index.md).

## Law {#law}

### Stance {#stance}

Fencing's on-guard stance places the trailing foot at an angle to the lead foot (CHEN Section 1, qualitative). The production adapts it: the wand-side foot leads and points at the opponent, the rear foot turns out 60–90°, the feet stand 1.2–1.5 shoulder widths apart with the knees softly flexed and the weight between them. The wand arm is half extended with the tip at the opponent's chest height; the free hand hangs loose beside the hip, ready to balance or grip, instead of fencing's raised rear arm.

### Distance {#distance}

| Band | Chest to chest | What it allows | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Casting distance | 2.0–4.0 m | Shots, shields, disarming, deflection | Production choice; see [deflection](#deflection) for the lower bound |
| Wand reach | 1.0–1.6 m | Wands click together tip to shaft | Arm plus wand length of the cast |
| Grip range | Under 0.9 m | A free hand closes on the other's wand wrist | Forearm-plus-hand reach of the cast |

### Footwork {#footwork}

Advances and retreats are steps of 0.3–0.5 m, the lead foot first when advancing and the rear foot first when retreating, each 0.3–0.5 s, so the stance width never collapses. Circling is a sidestep of the lead foot followed by the rear foot, keeping the chest square to the opponent. A practiced duellist moves the body with the arm: at the touch, elite fencers' body centres still move at 0.41 ± 0.20 m/s against novices' 0.04 ± 0.10 m/s (CHEN Section 4.2.3, measured), which is the production's marker of a skilled attack.

### Attack timing {#attack-timing}

A fencing attack action lasts under one second: a lunge attack takes 0.92 ± 0.05 s in national-level fencers (WANG, measured), most of its gain comes in its first 200–300 ms (CHEN Section 4.2.3), and a simple lunge's peak velocities are 1.72–1.92 m/s at the body's mass centre, 2.49–2.91 m/s at the weapon and 4.10–4.56 m/s at the front foot (CHEN Section 4.2.1, measured). The production uses these for a stepping thrust of the wand and for a run at the opponent from casting distance.

### Casting {#casting}

Every spell follows one shape: aim settle, gesture, release. The wand tip holds still on its target for at least 0.25 s; the gesture (a short flick or a swish) takes 0.15–0.25 s; the spell leaves the tip at the gesture's end. A student casts at most one spell per 0.8 s. These are production choices: canon gives no timing. Each spell's visible cue and limits belong to [wand magic](../../body/settings/world/magic.md).

The spell's streak travels at 5 ± 1 m/s. This is a deliberate stylized departure: real projectiles are far faster, and the chosen speed lets a 24 fps film show a two-metre shot over about ten frames.

### Reaction {#reaction}

Simple visual reaction time in young adults is 0.21–0.25 s: the four UK samples aged about 18–30 in WOODLEY Table 1 average 208.5, 225, 230.9 and 237.8 ms after the corrections the authors apply (measured). A choice between two answers adds about 0.1 s (production inference). No defensive movement may begin earlier than 0.21 s after the caster's gesture becomes visible.

### Deflection {#deflection}

A defender who is watching the caster's wand hand can sweep their own wand across the streak's path in 0.10–0.15 s (production choice: the 2.5–2.9 m/s weapon velocities above carry a wand tip through a 0.3 m sweep in that time). A shot is deflectable only when its travel time is at least the defender's reaction plus the sweep, 0.31–0.40 s, which at 5 m/s means a casting distance of 1.6–2.0 m or more. A deflected streak leaves at up to 60° from its incoming line, keeps its speed and continues until it strikes something.

### Wrist grip and escape {#grip}

A grip is the gripper's free hand closing around the opponent's wand wrist from the outside, turning the wand tip away. Coaching text on wrist-grab escapes teaches a quick, continuous rotation of the trapped arm with the body stepping to the outside of the gripping wrist, and names the thumb as the weak link of a grip pulled against straight back (CAILE). The production's escape is a partner-dance underarm turn built on that principle: the held person raises the joined hands above her head, steps under them and turns her body a full circle beneath the raised arm, so the gripper's wrist rotates toward its limit; the gripper opens the hand before the wrist is forced, 0.8–1.2 s after the turn starts. The underarm turn itself is a production choice; no retained source measures it.

### Running at a person {#run-in}

A run from casting distance into grip range takes two to four running steps, 0.6–1.2 s, reaching 2–3 m/s (production inference from the lunge's 1.7–1.9 m/s body speed plus a running start). A person run at inside 1 m who is facing the runner responds after one reaction time.

## Outcome table {#outcomes}

| Attacker intent | Defender intent and condition | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shot | Deflect, watching, travel time ≥ reaction plus sweep | Deflected, new line up to 60° away |
| Shot | Deflect, travel time shorter | Hit |
| Shot | Shield standing, planted | Blocked |
| Shot | None, or not watching | Hit, or miss if the aim was off the body |
| Disarm | Shield standing | Blocked |
| Disarm | None or deflect too late | Disarmed: the wand flies from the hand |
| Grip on the wand wrist | Underarm turn | Escaped: the gripper releases |
| Grip on the wand wrist | Straight pull back | Held |
| Levitation of a loose object | None | The object rises |
| Levitation aimed at a person | Any | Not admitted at this level: see [wand magic](../../body/settings/world/magic.md) |

## Measurable targets {#targets}

- Duellists stand 2–4 m apart while casting; every cast shows a still aim of at least 0.25 s and a gesture of 0.15–0.25 s.
- A streak moves at 4–6 m/s and is visible from tip to target.
- No defensive movement starts within 0.21 s of the caster's gesture becoming visible.
- A deflection happens only at casting distances of 1.6 m or more.
- Advance and retreat steps are 0.3–0.5 m long; the stance width stays 1.2–1.5 shoulder widths.
- A grip escape by underarm turn lasts 0.8–1.2 s and ends with both people facing each other again.
