# Vertical short coverage

How a sixty-second, 9:16 film about two people and their contacts is cut and framed: how long shots last, where faces and hands sit in the tall frame, and how the shooting of the depicted practices fits it. The [cinematography settings](../../body/settings/cinematography/vertical-look.md) adopt the look from this study. Source keys refer to the [research index](../index.md).

## Shot pace {#pace}

### Law

Popular films have cut faster across the decades, and within a film the pace follows its structure. Reported (cutting-2016, Methods): the mean shot duration across 180 English-language films was 7.46 s; for "more contemporary movies" (1990–2015) it was 4.3 s. Across a contemporary film, shots lengthen and motion falls through the setup, while "in the climax, shots shorten and motion rises", then both reverse at its end (Discussion); luminance shows "darkening across the complication and development followed by brightening across the climax". Conversations are cut at about the film's average pace: "the average shot duration during a conversation is typically about the average shot duration of the movies" (Discussion).

### Use

Inference and production's choice: a 60-second short cut at the contemporary mean holds about 14 shots; short-form vertical video is cut faster, so each film targets a mean of 2.5–4.5 s (13–24 shots), with longer holds (5–8 s) for the beats where a contact or a look must be watched, and the shortest shots (1–2 s) at each film's climax. Each film brightens toward its ending, following the climax pattern.

## The tall frame {#frame}

### Safe zones

Reported (reap-safe-zones, table "A practical 1080x1920 safe-zone starting point", an editorial recommendation, "not official organic-post specifications"): keep about the top 15 % clear of faces, the left and right 10–15 % clear of key action, and the bottom 25–35 % clear of essential content; "Keep the main message, face, product and CTA" in the centre. At 720 × 1280 those margins are about 190 px at the top, 70–110 px at each side and 320–450 px at the bottom.

Production's choice: faces sit with the eyes 25–40 % down from the top edge; every contact the film depends on (a hand on a wrist, fingers on a crane, a hand closing on the Snitch) sits inside the central zone (x 110–610 px, y 190–830 px at 720 × 1280); nothing essential sits in the bottom quarter.

### Framing grammar

Production's choice for a frame twice as tall as it is wide:

- A standing figure fits whole in the frame's height, so the vertical frame suits full-body shots of a single person, and of two people only when they are within about 1 m of each other.
- Two people farther apart are staged in depth along the lens axis (one near, one far, overlapping in the frame), seen from above, or covered in singles and over-the-shoulder shots.
- A two-shot of a contact frames from the hands up to the faces, so the cause (the hands) and the response (the faces) share one frame.
- Inserts of hands and objects fill the central zone and last 1–3 s.
- A seated pair on a bench, or riders on one broom, fit side by side or one behind the other in a medium shot.

## Shooting the practices {#shooting-the-practices}

- **Duel** ([practice duelling](../duel/practice-duelling.md#filming)): the line between duellists runs in depth; spell jets cross the frame toward or away from the lens.
- **Flight** ([broom riding](../flight/broom-riding-and-snitch-catch.md#filming)): tracking alongside or behind riders at a constant size; high and low angles for altitude; the catch in a shot holding hand and Snitch.
- **Everyday movement** ([teen movement](../movement/teen-bodies-and-everyday-movement.md#conduct-level-and-filming)): wides that show feet landing; medium two-shots for handovers; inserts for paper in hands.
