# Vertical-format coverage

How a 9:16 frame shows two people, their hands and the objects between them, and how its camera moves and cuts, for four 60-second films at 720 × 1280. Sources are in the [index](../index.md).

## Practice {#practice}

Full-screen vertical placements are 9:16, delivered at 1080 × 1920 (minimum 720 px wide), and Meta asks advertisers to keep the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% at each side free of text, logos and key elements, because the app's interface covers them (META-SZ, spec and safe-zone tables; a secondary report of Meta's guidance). Contemporary features change shots often: average shot lengths of 1997–2016 releases run about 4 s for action, 5.1 s for adventure, 6.2 s for science fiction and 15.7 s for horror (FOLLOWS, text figures from the Cinemetrics database).

## Law {#law}

### Frame geometry {#geometry}

A 9:16 frame is tall and narrow: its horizontal field of view is 2 · atan(tan(v / 2) · 9 / 16) for a vertical field of view v, so a 50° vertical lens sees only 29.5° across. At the distance where a 1.65 m standing figure fills 70% of the frame height, the frame is 2.36 m tall and only 1.33 m wide. Two people standing side by side therefore do not fit at full figure without shrinking to under half the frame; two people facing each other in profile at handover distance (0.5–0.9 m between chests) do. The production stages two-person action in profile across the frame or on a diagonal in depth, one person nearer and lower in frame, the other farther and higher (derivation from the geometry above).

### Sizes {#sizes}

| Size | Shows | Fill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wide | Both full figures and the landmark they relate to, such as the bench, the support or the goals | Figures 35–55% of frame height |
| Full two-shot | Both people head to feet, in profile or in depth | Figures 55–80% |
| Medium | One or two people from the head to the hips | Head to hip 60–85% |
| Close | A face and one raised hand, or the joined hands | Production use only where hands carry the beat |

### Margins {#margins}

The delivered films carry no interface, but they will be watched on phones. Production choice, softer than Meta's advertising rule: faces stay below the top 10% of the frame, hands and the objects they hold stay above the bottom 12% and 6% in from each side, and feet may reach the bottom edge only in a full figure.

### Movement {#movement}

The camera moves only for a reason the action gives: it follows a walk, arcs to keep a pair in profile as they turn, pushes in when the hands become the subject and pulls back when a danger or a crowd must be read. It never shakes. In flight it travels beside the riders at a three-quarter side view, 4–10 m away, matching their speed, within 20° of their height. Production choices.

### Cutting {#cutting}

Average shot length 3–7 s, between the action average and slower genres, since these films carry both action and quiet comedy. One uninterrupted shot covers every contact action from its first touch to its release (a grip, a handover, a mount, a forehead brush), so the contact is never cut away. Screen direction holds across a cut: the line between two people is not crossed without a shot that moves across it. Production choices.

### Eye height {#eye-height}

Standing scenes are shot from 1.2–1.6 m, the subjects' chest to eye height; seated scenes from 0.9–1.2 m; flight from within 20° of the riders' level. Production choices.

## Measurable targets {#targets}

- Every frame is 720 × 1280.
- Full figures fill 55–80% of the frame height in a full two-shot and 35–55% in a wide shot.
- Faces never enter the top 10% of the frame; held objects and hands in a contact action stay inside the margins for the whole action.
- Average shot length per film 3–7 s; no cut falls between a contact's first touch and its release.
- No frame crosses the action line without a moving shot that crosses it.
