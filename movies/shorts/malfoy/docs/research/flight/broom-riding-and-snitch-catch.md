# Broom riding and the Snitch catch

How a body sits a flying broom alone and with a passenger, and how a hand catches a small fast ball. Broom flight and Quidditch are canon used as an adopted choice under the [canon boundary](../../instructions/commission.md#canon-boundary), with their rules fixed in the [Quidditch settings](../../body/settings/world/quidditch.md) and the brooms in the [broom settings](../../body/settings/props/broomsticks.md). This study supplies the bodies: the broom's closest real analogue is a saddle ridden astride, where the passenger's role is studied in motorcycle pillion riding, and the Snitch catch's analogue is one-handed catching of a thrown ball. Source keys refer to the [research index](../index.md).

## Where the analogues hold and where they depart

Holds: a rider astride a narrow seat grips with the knees and steers with the hands; a passenger behind holds the rider and follows the rider's lean; a one-handed catch closes the fingers around a ball at a timed moment. Departs: a broom has no engine sound, no handlebars and no ground under it; it steers by the hands' pressure on the shaft and the rider's lean, and a fall drops the rider through air. The Snitch flies under its own power and changes direction, so the catching hand must intercept a path rather than wait at a fixed point.

## Riding astride {#riding-astride}

Production's choice (no retained source covers a front rider's posture): the rider sits astride the shaft with the hips about 0.75 m ahead of the tail binding, inner thighs pressing the shaft, soles in stirrups hung about 0.45 m below the shaft almost under the hips, knees bent to about 110°; both hands hold the shaft ahead of the body in a power grip, the forward hand about 0.35–0.45 m ahead of the seat and the rear hand 0.10–0.15 m behind the forward hand. For speed the rider folds forward until the chest is 0.10–0.25 m above the shaft, elbows bent and close to the body; for hovering or slow flight the trunk is upright. A turn is a lean of the whole body into the turn with the shaft, the outside knee pressing; a climb lifts the handle end, a dive lowers it. Effector targets: seat to the shaft, both soles to the stirrups, both hands to the handle — all held for the whole ride.

## Riding pillion {#riding-pillion}

### Law

Reported (nsw-pillion, "Instructions for pillions"): "Align your body with that of the rider." "Use the grabrail, or hold the rider's waist." "Grip with your knees when braking." "Pick a shoulder to look over and stick with it." "Keep your feet on the footpegs at all times." Reported (begin-motorcycling): the rider gets on first and steadies; the passenger mounts by swinging a leg over and settling both feet on the pegs ("Getting on the motorcycle"); with "hands around the waist of the driver … adopt a relaxed but firm grip and move with the bike naturally" ("What do I hold?"); in corners "follow the natural lean of the driver and bike. Don't try to fight the lean nor lean too far" ("Cornering").

### Use

Production's adoption for tandem broom flight: the front rider steers; the passenger sits directly behind with the hips about 0.35 m behind the front rider's, chest 0.05–0.15 m from the front rider's back, knees closing on the outside of the front rider's hips, feet in the rear stirrups, both hands on the front rider's waist at the hip bones or one hand on the waist and one on the shaft; the passenger looks over one chosen shoulder of the front rider and leans with the front rider, never against. Effector targets: passenger's seat to the shaft behind the rider, hands to the rider's waist, chin over the chosen shoulder.

## Catching {#catching}

### Law

Reported (cesqui-2016, 11 adults catching balls launched from 6 m with flight times of 0.55, 0.65 and 0.75 s): finger closing starts a near-constant interval before impact, 91 ± 8 ms, 104 ± 10 ms and 110 ± 16 ms (mean ± SE) for the three flight times (p. 12); "the hand-ball collision occurred approximately at the time of peak finger closing velocity" (p. 13); closing lasts about 230 ms, and "The fingers continued to close around the ball after impact to tighten the hand's grip" (p. 10). Catching is difficult: success rates ranged from 2 % to 85 % across participants although over 90 % of balls were touched (Results).

### Use

Production's adoption for the Snitch catch: the catching hand is an effector whose target is the Snitch's predicted position at the contact time; the arm extends to intercept the path during the last 0.3–0.5 s, the hand opens to an aperture a little wider than the Snitch (about 6–8 cm for a walnut-sized ball), the fingers start closing about 0.1 s before contact and are at peak closing speed at contact, and they keep closing after it. An "almost certain" catch is one where the open hand is within about one forearm length (0.25–0.3 m) of the Snitch's path inside the last half second. Turning away at that moment is a decision the screenplay makes, not a miss.

## Riders in a practice match {#riders-in-a-practice-match}

Production's choice (canon adopted): Chasers fly at 5–15 m/s in passing patterns at 5–15 m altitude near the hoops; Beaters hover near their teammates and swing their bats at incoming Bludgers; Keepers patrol 1–3 m in front of their hoops; Seekers circle higher (15–30 m) at 8–15 m/s and dive at up to 30 m/s when they see the Snitch; a broom carrying two flies at no more than 10 m/s; the referee hovers at mid-pitch and flies to an incident at up to 20 m/s. These are performance ranges for a hint, not measurements.

### Reaching a falling rider {#reaching-a-falling-rider}

Inference from free fall (no retained source): a body falling from rest drops 4.9 m in the first second and 19.6 m in two, reaching 9.8 m/s after one second and 19.6 m/s after two. A rider who reaches a falling person does it by diving on a path that meets the faller's, then matching their downward speed so that the two move together; while the speeds match, the faller weighs almost nothing in the rescuer's hand, and the rescuer's grip only has to guide them onto the shaft before the broom levels out and takes their weight. A rescuer 3–5 m from the faller when the fall begins, diving at once, can match within about 1–1.5 s, 5–11 m below the start, at 10–15 m/s; a rescuer farther away, or slower to start, cannot before the ground. Braking two riders from 10–15 m/s to level flight at a production-chosen 8–10 m/s² of deceleration, while still descending, costs a further 8–12 m of height, so a matched rescue needs the fall to begin about 25 m or more above the ground. Catching a falling body at arm's length without matching speed is not possible for a teenager.

## Level markers {#level-markers}

A secure rider keeps the hands on the handle and the knees on the shaft through turns, and moves the whole body with the broom; an insecure one stiffens upright and grips with the hands alone. A skilled catcher's hand arrives already open and closes once; a poor catch claps at the ball late.

## Filming {#filming}

Flight reads in tracking shots that keep the rider at a constant size while the background streams past, and in shots from above and below that show altitude; a catch needs a shot that holds the hand and the Snitch together through the closing ([vertical short coverage](../cinematography/vertical-short-coverage.md)).
