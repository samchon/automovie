# Teen bodies and everyday movement

The measured bodies and ordinary movements of two fifteen-year-olds: how tall they are, how they walk, reach, take and give a small object, sit and stand, and bend to the floor. Every effector target in the [effector-targeting study](../motion/effector-targeting.md) takes its timing and reach from here. Source keys refer to the [research index](../index.md). Values are marked as a **reported measurement**, an **inference** (with its translation) or the **production's choice**.

## Stature {#stature}

Reported measurement (who-2007, row 180 months = 15.0 years): girls' median height 161.7 cm, 25th–75th percentile 157.0–166.3 cm; boys' median 169.0 cm, 25th–75th percentile 163.7–174.2 cm. At 15.5 years (row 186) the medians are 162.2 cm and 171.1 cm.

Production's choice: Carina 163 cm and Harry 167 cm, both inside the interquartile range for their sex at fifteen. The 4 cm difference puts their eye lines within a few centimetres of each other when both stand, so neither looks steeply up or down at the other.

## Walking {#gait}

### Law

A walk is a chain of foot targets: each foot lands heel first on a point ahead on the path, carries the body over it, and leaves ball then toe while the other foot swings to the next point. Both feet are on the ground for a short double-support interval at each change of support; the arms swing opposite the legs. The pelvis rises over the stance leg and drops at double support.

### Values

Reported measurement (ito-2022, Table 2, group C, aged 11–12, median height 147.8 cm, leg length 0.75 m, comfortable self-chosen speed): cadence 121.8 steps/min (range 91.1–151.2), speed 1.22 m/s (0.84–1.77), step length 0.60 m, stride length 1.20 m, step time 0.49 s, stride time 0.99 s, single support 0.41 s, double support 0.16 s. The same study states that children aged 7–11 already "exhibit adult-like gait patterns" (Introduction).

Inference (translation to the depicted age): step length scales with leg length, and leg length with stature, so scaling the 12-year values by 163/147.8 and 167/147.8 gives step lengths of about 0.66 m for Carina and 0.68 m for Harry. Cadence falls slightly as stature rises (ito-2022 groups A → C: 130.4 → 127.2 → 121.8 steps/min), so a comfortable cadence of 112–120 steps/min is adopted, giving comfortable speeds of about 1.2–1.35 m/s. These are translations, not measurements of fifteen-year-olds; no open study of that age was retained ([unavailable](../index.md#unavailable)).

Production's choice for performed variants, built from the same law:

| Walk | Step length | Cadence | Speed | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Comfortable | 0.62–0.70 m | 112–120 steps/min | 1.2–1.35 m/s | Ordinary crossing of a space |
| Hesitant | 0.45–0.55 m | 90–105 steps/min | 0.7–0.95 m/s | Approaching someone one is nervous to reach |
| Brisk | 0.70–0.78 m | 125–135 steps/min | 1.5–1.75 m/s | Hurrying, leaving |
| Stop | One shortened last step (about half a step), feet ending side by side | — | to 0 within one step | Arriving at a mark |

## Reach to grasp {#reach-to-grasp}

### Law

A reach carries the hand to the object along a smooth path with one velocity peak, opening the hand on the way and closing it around the object at arrival: "a smooth bell-shaped velocity profile with one velocity peak occurring approximately halfway through the movement", with grip aperture "reaching a maximum at approximately 60 to 70% of the reaching phase, followed by hand closing around the object" (valevicius-2018, Introduction). Clearing an obstacle on the way produces "a double velocity peak and typically a longer deceleration phase" (valevicius-2018, Abstract).

### Values

Reported measurement (valevicius-2018, Table 2, 20 adults aged 25.8 ± 7.2 years, reaches of 464–701 mm to a box): movement time 0.68–0.97 s; peak hand velocity 1.0–1.47 m/s, reached at 39–43 % of the movement; maximum grip aperture 107–117 mm, reached at 72–79 %; peak deceleration at 58–74 %.

Inference: fifteen-year-olds' reaches are taken to follow the same profile; the aperture for a small paper object is smaller than for the box (a pinch opens only a few centimetres more than the object's thickness), which is the production's choice.

## Handing an object over {#handover}

### Law

A handover between two people runs in four phases (kopnarski-2023, abstract and §4.1, p. 10): **reach and grasp** (the giver takes the object); **object transport** (the giver carries it toward the exchange point; "Typically, the receiver starts their action during the object transport phase"); **object transfer** ("the receiver builds up grip force until they alone hold the object in their hand, while the giver simultaneously reduces their grip force"); and **end of handover** (the giver's hand returns). The exchange happens "in a horizontal plane at the center of the actors" — midway between them — and lower when they stand farther apart (§3.5, p. 9). At short distance the arms work mainly at the elbow; at longer distance the shoulder must extend the arm (§4.3, p. 12). The giver's transport accelerates to a peak and then decelerates longer than in a solitary placement (§4.3, p. 12).

### Values

Reported measurement (kopnarski-2023, §4.4, p. 12): the transfer phase "lasts on average about 500ms with an object weight of 90g". Inference: a slip of paper or a paper crane weighs a few grams, so its transfer takes about 0.3–0.5 s, the light end of that report.

### Interrupted and contested handovers {#interrupted-and-contested-handovers}

Production's choice, built on the four phases: an **offered-and-withdrawn** handover stops during object transport, before the receiver's hand makes contact, and the giver's hand returns along its approach path; if the receiver has already started reaching, their hand arrives at an empty exchange point. A **contested** hold — two people gripping one light object, as in a tug — is a transfer phase in which neither grip force falls: the object stays at the midpoint of the two hands and moves only as the hands move.

## Sitting down and standing up {#sit-and-stand}

### Law

Sitting is a controlled lowering of the seat onto its support: the trunk leans forward over the feet, the hips and knees flex, the seat lands, and the trunk returns upright. Standing reverses it: the trunk leans forward to bring the weight over the feet, the seat lifts off, and hips and knees extend. Reported model (wairagkar-2021, p. 12): a three-segment sagittal body (shank, thigh, back) whose thigh angle is "0° while standing and 90° while sitting".

### Values

Reported measurement (wairagkar-2021, p. 14, 10 younger healthy adults, 37.4 ± 9.9 years): sit-to-stand 1.44 ± 0.36 s, stand-to-sit 1.55 ± 0.33 s. Inference: teenagers move at least as quickly; the production adopts 1.1–1.6 s for an unhurried sit or stand, and a hesitant sit may pause briefly with the seat just above the support, a pause the same study reports only for older groups (p. 14) and the production uses as a deliberate performance choice.

## Bending to the floor {#bending}

### Law

Picking something up from the floor is done with a **stoop** (knees nearly straight, trunk folding at the hips) or a **squat** (hips and knees flexing, trunk staying more upright), or a mix of the two. Reported measurement (hwang-2009, Results, 26 young men lifting boxes): in stoop lifting "the knee joint ROM was nearly zero"; the knee range of motion "showed the largest difference" between the two techniques; mean lifting speeds were 0.59 ± 0.14 m/s (squat) and 0.60 ± 0.10 m/s (stoop).

### Values

Inference: for a light object such as a dropped note, the hand travels from hanging height (about 0.7 m above the floor for a 163–167 cm teenager) to the floor; at the reported 0.6 m/s that takes about 1.1–1.3 s for the whole lowering. Production's choice: Carina bends with a squat-dominant mix, knees together, one hand reaching; Harry with a stoop-dominant mix and a quick bend. Two people bending toward one point from facing positions bring their heads toward each other: with each head travelling forward about 0.3–0.4 m in a stoop, two people standing less than about 0.8 m apart can meet head to head over the object.

Seated bend (inference from the sitting model above, no retained measurement): a person seated on a 0.45 m seat reaches the floor in front of their feet by folding the trunk forward over the thighs (about 60–80° of trunk flexion) and dropping one arm, without rising; the head travels forward and down about 0.4–0.5 m to above the knees. A seated person and a standing person in front of them, 0.6–0.9 m apart, bending at once toward a point between them bring their heads together at about the seated person's knee height plus a head's height (about 0.7–0.9 m above the floor).

## Conduct, level and filming {#conduct-level-and-filming}

Everyday movement has no officials or crowds here; the level marker is ordinariness — weight that shifts before a step, hands that open before they arrive and close after, and no movement starting from a frozen pose. Walking reads in a wide shot that shows the feet landing; reaches and handovers read in a shot that holds both hands and the object at the contact frame (see [vertical short coverage](../cinematography/vertical-short-coverage.md)).
