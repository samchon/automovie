# Give It Back, Potter: creative brief

## Commission and form {#commission-and-form}

The user commissioned a separate producer and production under `movies/shorts` for this accepted paper-crane romance direction, requested the scaffold's settings-to-screenplay procedure, and then required everything in English. This is one complete 60-second short for a general teen-and-adult audience, with a clear opening problem and resolved invitation. Use compact settings followed by final screenplay; omit unused treatments, scripts and outline populations. The title is the producer's delegated choice, naming Carina's immediate pursuit rather than a canonical episode.

## Source boundary {#source-boundary}

Carina Malfoy and Harry Potter are the user-adopted pairing. This is an original school-age alternate-universe fan scene, not an adaptation of an identified passage of The Villainess Malfoy or another published text. ## Physical comedy and care {#physical-comedy-and-care}

The adopted situation is Carina regretting a paper invitation, Harry receiving it, a short reach-and-yield exchange near a bench, his return of the note, recognition of two waiting cups, and an accepted invitation. Make the comedy the collision of her urgent retrieval with his effort to avoid tearing the paper and give her room. Harry must not prolong her distress or use reading as leverage. No grabbing bodies, confinement, mockery or hot-drink chase. The final shared seat and voluntary cup handoff must change the meaning of the earlier movement.

## Motion and material limit {#motion-and-material-limit}

The user's production aim is visibly useful motion conditioning before later video-to-video diffusion. At the selected low-detail Human representation, paths, hand targets, turning, balance and object transfers must reveal intentions without depending on facial detail. Use two people, a single sparse corridor, a bench, two cups and one folded sheet. Keep the note's travel brief; the main performance is embodied interaction. Keep every cup supported until a deliberate handoff, and identify paper ownership through every transfer.

## Audience experience and language {#audience-experience-and-language}

Let the audience see the two cups at the opening while Harry initially sees only the paper, creating affectionate anticipation. Keep the note's exact words unreadable until its return: “Would you have tea with me?” Use plain contemporary English and short lines, without imitated archaic British speech or voice-actor impersonation. Carina's clipped demands soften through action; Harry's dry questions give way to a direct welcome. Retain real pauses for the changed distance and final acceptance. Use no explanatory narration or subtitles that disclose the invitation early. The screen note is the only required readable insert; a final title card is unnecessary.
