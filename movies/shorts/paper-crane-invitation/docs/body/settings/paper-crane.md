# Paper crane

## Invitation and single paper charm {#paper}

One cream square of ordinary writing paper, approximately 16 centimetres across when open, folds into a recognizable small crane. Its only writing is the invented English invitation “Would you have tea with me?” in a dark hand. Folded faces hide those words; unfolding exposes the same sheet without a second note, added caption or changing inscription. The sheet can crease and tear under opposing pulls, so one person must release it before a transfer finishes.

Carina knows a harmless, wandless, one-use paper charm that sends this crane slowly along a chosen short route within the corridor. A fingertip push starts it; a hand can catch it without injury. Contact ends the charm, so it neither flies again nor unfolds itself. It cannot evade a hand, alter its words, read minds or move people or cups. This explicitly invented local magical rule supplies no other power. Unfolding and refolding are ordinary hand actions with a soft paper rustle; a fold need not restore the finished crane.
