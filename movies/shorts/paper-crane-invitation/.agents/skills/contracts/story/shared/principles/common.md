# Common authoring principles

Each authored H2 answers these questions from its own body and identified parents. Population coverage belongs to obligations, not to an individual H2.

## Does this unit fulfill its assigned scope? {#scope-preservation}

Does the body deliver the facts, constraints, events, relationships, interval, or result promised by its title, instructions, and lineage? A heading, placeholder, research list, or promise that another author will decide the essential content fails. Distinguish a decision deliberately left to the next layer from an unfinished decision owned here. An answer about the whole population cannot replace this unit's missing content.

## Do its choices come from this work? {#specificity}

Do the chosen facts, decisions, actions, images, and details follow this work's circumstances rather than familiar labels or habitual gestures? Specificity means useful distinctions, not more detail. The author's process vocabulary, such as checking, reporting, and judging, must not become the world's institutions, objects, or character interests without a creative reason owned by this work. Even a work about records must realize events and relationships rather than substitute its management procedure for them. Settings and treatments require specific decisions, not literary decoration.
