# Scene and performance notation

Unless the commission adopts another form, use conventional screenplay blocks: a scene heading such as `INT. LOCATION - DAY`, short present-tense action paragraphs of what can be seen or heard, and a consistent speaker cue before each dialogue block, as the [BBC format](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/writersroom/scripts/screenplay.pdf) and [Final Draft elements](https://www.finaldraft.com/learn/screenplay-formatting-elements/) show. Break paragraphs at changes of action, response or attention, and mark off-screen speech, voiceover, overlap, screen text and time shifts where they would otherwise be ambiguous. Include camera, edit and performance directions where they determine the experience.

H1 and H2 headings are navigation and evidence units; they prescribe neither shot boundaries nor duration. A long continuous scene may use H3 subheadings for meaningful shifts of action under H2 scene headings.

The screenplay decides the complete visible and audible action, speech, reactions, timing and result; implementation precision lives in the code that consumes it under [source layout](../../development/layout.md). Read the blocks as a continuous sequence, each entry following the preceding exit; final duration comes from the performed action, speech, pauses and edit.
