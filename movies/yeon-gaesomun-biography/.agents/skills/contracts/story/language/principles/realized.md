# Heard Korean in the historical drama

Every script and screenplay scene H2 answers for its realized speech, narration, or purposeful silence. A silent scene establishes its own heard boundary; it does not invent dialogue to answer this question. Treatments and settings do not answer as realized scenes.

## Does the heard register carry the scene's actual relationships? {#korean-register}

Make the scene's heard Korean intelligible, including what speech or silence lets its people express, withhold, or misunderstand. Social distance appears through honorifics, address, permission to interrupt, and changes under pressure. A Goguryeo subject addresses the king as 「폐하」, an adopted departure that does not prove historical imperial titulature. Keep rank, family intimacy, local language knowledge, and the later narrator's voice distinct. The audience's shared Korean does not make all characters historically speak one language or remove a translation barrier. Silence can preserve a relationship or information boundary without pretending that unspoken dialogue was heard.

Preserve each actual voice rather than giving every general the same aphorisms. Internet idiom, modern technical concepts, and the narrator's modern satire do not belong in a period character's mouth. Treat numeral notation in the written production text separately from its natural spoken realization. Basis: instructions 002, 005, 009 and 010. Calling the king 「전하」 or letting a resident explain an undisclosed distant plan are representative failures.
