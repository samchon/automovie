# Taking up the work of the king he killed

## Time requested by the boy who shared his bow {#eulbo-after-withdrawal}

After Tang withdraws in 645, Gaesomun checks passage along the roads and ferries and travels to Ansi. He did not enter the fortress to command it during the siege; he comes as the central official responsible for assessing the damage to the surviving fortress and its future defense. The castellan also has him hear about the burden on the soldiers assigned to transport and repairs in the military office. Gaesomun recognizes Eulbo first, hearing his name and voice; Eulbo, too, recognizes the boy who once competed with him in the courtyard, now the man demanding his labor ([Eulbo in 645](../settings/040-agents/115-eulbo.md#eulbo), [the castellan's authority](../settings/050-systems/210-ansi-defence.md#ansi-defence), [the military office](../settings/060-places/110-ansi.md#ansi)).

Eulbo does not ask for the name or military honors he wanted as a child as the price of their reunion. The war is over, yet transport and repairs continue; he asks how long he must stay. He speaks for his own share and exercises no authority to release other soldiers. Gaesomun does not send his old friend home alone at once. He asks the castellan to distinguish essential repairs from maintenance that can wait. They decide to postpone the latter and assign those workers to rotate through the essential repairs, reducing the continuous labor imposed on the same soldiers. The castellan and Gaesomun both accept the risk of leaving some sections less fully maintained.

The next day Eulbo actually receives relief from the work he has been doing. He does not go straight home, and repairs remain to be done. But orders that had seemed to add work without end now define an assigned section and a rotation. Gaesomun sees both the help Eulbo receives and the price he still pays. The reunion reveals no separate feat of arms or settlement of a debt, does not establish whether Eulbo is a slave, and leaves his fate after 645 unresolved. Affection for a recognized old friend and the power to demand more labor from that same man remain together in the room.

Other postwar reports make overlapping demands for the same people and supplies: fortress defense, return home, and arms for the next army. Gaesomun knows that adjusting one rotation at Ansi cannot answer them all. He must decide what to delay. He maintains the immediate defense first, making the localities bear the cost of not sending everyone home at once ([the postwar situation](../settings/050-systems/230-first-war-outcome.md#first-war-outcome), [household burdens](../settings/030-society/070-household-burdens.md#household-burdens)).

Alone after this decision, Gaesomun recalls Go Geonmu's last words. He now understands that the king's caution was not simply a loss of courage. Yet he does not abandon his own judgment that Tang would have invaded whatever diplomatic course they chose. He concludes that its will to conquer must first be broken if recovery is to be protected. He tells no one of his private regret that the king had to die; do not turn it into a public confession of remorse for the killing itself ([the debt to the king](../settings/040-agents/001-yeon-gaesomun.md#yeon-and-yeongnyu)).

Gaesomun changes the conduct of military affairs: fortresses and local commanders must buy time while mobile forces cut enemy communications and supply. Rather than trying to oversee everything directly, the center delegates areas of responsibility and reporting duties, while holding commanders more strictly accountable for results. He does not revere a single remark by Go Jeongui as the secret to the next war. Even sound judgment needs actual roads, provisions, and people to work.

The historian compares the present work with the second reconstruction Go Geonmu undertook after the Sui wars. Do not give Gaesomun a finished judgment from posterity within the scene; let the choices he faces now, with the same resources in demand, bring him to understand the king. What he has yet to do is deliver the promised support to the Malgal bereaved. The next event begins in the gap between a declaration and its transport.
