Bojang accepts the throne judging that the royal family’s future requires the country to survive. Gaesomun establishes no new dynasty; he uses the king’s name and legitimacy to control military and state affairs. He assumes the work’s chosen highest office, daemangniji, and directly decides necessary tasks and resources ([office boundary](../settings/040-agents/001-yeon-gaesomun.md#yeon-origin)). The king retains ceremonial and formal orders; Gaesomun needs his cooperation but possesses independent military strength.

The historian briefly explains the new king’s genealogy: he is the son of Daeyang, Yeongnyu’s younger brother, explaining why his name preserves royal legitimacy. Do not turn Daeyang’s title into a separate reign or the new king into a child. Bojang is an adult with royal rights but lacks troops to exercise them immediately ([genealogy](../settings/040-agents/014-daeyang.md#daeyang), [adult king’s position](../settings/040-agents/016-bojang.md#bojang)).

Bojang avoids immediate public conflict, not because he forgets his murdered uncle, but because he cannot yet take Gaesomun’s force. Gaesomun begins to read submission as certain cooperation. Do not reveal a completed 666 restoration plot now; show only the king choosing to survive and retain influence ([Bojang](../settings/040-agents/016-bojang.md#bojang)).

As Tang’s preparations become actual invasion, Gaesomun uses the failure of royal diplomacy to justify his rule. Yet responsibility for obtaining troops and provisions to stop it now falls to him. He demands shares from regions and nobles and connects mobilization through royal orders. Coercion and practical administration work together.

The historian connects northern strength once sought by Northern Zhou and Northern Qi to reports of Tang’s founding. In 617 Li Yuan traded horses and negotiated military support with Turks. The proposed exchange promised Chang’an’s land and people to Li and wealth to Turks. Do not imply all promised troops arrived or Tang’s whole army came from Turks. This earlier context is for the audience, not a report just received by Goguryeo command in 645 or a new Chinese court scene ([founding negotiations](../settings/020-world/110-tang-and-steppe.md#tang-and-steppe)). Tang later subdues Eastern Turks and incorporates northern and western forces into eastern expeditions. The historian connects earlier conquests including Gaochang to this changed threat: the reversal from seeking help to using those powers as expeditionary strength is the point, not a separate Chinese history ([Tang’s actual ability](../settings/050-systems/010-tang-military-confidence.md#tang-military-confidence)). Goguryeo command trusts its victory over Sui and large relief force. The audience sees Gaesomun shares that confidence and enters Jupilsan.
