# Roads that feed horses, camps that starve

During preparations to march south from the Sinseong area early in the twelfth lunar month, Gaesomun does more than receive reports that the mobile army moves quickly. He has the grain and hay needed for roughly 50,000 horses, and food for the people, checked at each successive feeding area. He separates the shares provided by central and noble storehouses from local carts, oxen, and labor, and excludes seed and the minimum food for displaced households from the inventory ([the finite supply model](../settings/050-systems/100-counterattack-concentration.md#interior-supply-capacity)). Families with storehouses worry about what will remain for them if they give supplies now. Gaesomun demands that they also bear responsibility for maintaining horses equipped at private expense, and orders release quantities and passage deadlines for each storehouse. The families comply because they must if they are to send their troops and horses south; after releasing stock, they still bear the cost of delayed work in their own districts.

Supplies are allocated for 22 feeding areas along the march, an additional two days waiting to cross, and reserve stocks in ten areas north of Pyongyang. Troops to guard the routes are assigned from local defenses, not added as a new field army. Supplies for a separate 7,000 light cavalry marching south are secured apart from the central mobile army's quantities. The allocations make the next action possible, but tie up local labor and storehouses, delaying other recovery. Do not make this a success in which loyalty creates feed.

That twelfth lunar month, Pyongyang's defenders take two Tang strongpoints. They achieve this with the fortress's own troops, without adding the central mobile army or Namgeon's heavy cavalry, still marching south from the north ([the calendar's fixed state](../settings/020-world/030-second-war-calendar.md#second-war-calendar)). Go Hwi, too, regards it as an achievement of holding the fortress.

Some call for an attack on the remaining positions, but the defenders lack the strength for continuous attacks on their own and stop. The siege remains. The items returned to Haeun for repair bear signs of wear and damage, and Yu Hye accepts that her husband must remain in the fortress. The small victory cannot be announced as the liberation of the whole capital. The need for the northern mobile army to arrive becomes more concrete.

## Two different journeys through winter {#march-and-starvation}

Gaesomun's mobile army travels roughly 550 kilometers at an average of 20–25 kilometers a day. He does not send the entire column before receiving confirmation of the next area's stocks, ferry, and access. Two days waiting at a river are not an uplifting tale of survival on carried grain alone; they consume hay and food allocated in advance. After 22 days moving and two waiting, the army reaches north of Pyongyang early in the first lunar month. Horses and support personnel also operate within the limits of that pace.

In the Tang siege camps, by contrast, winter stocks dwindle with the land connection cut. Su Dingfang knows names and fighting spirit cannot feed his men and asks Silla for provisions. Ren Yaxiang and Pang Xiaotai separately calculate their own camps' supplies and prospects of relief. Proximity does not solve one another's hunger. The audience sees that the difference between an army able to move and armies held in place in the same winter comes from earlier preparation and access routes.
