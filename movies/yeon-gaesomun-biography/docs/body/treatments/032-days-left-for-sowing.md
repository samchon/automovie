# Reconstruction that leaves days for sowing

## Miran's request changes the order of work {#work-yields-days-to-sowing}

From 664 to early 665, the results of reconstruction are visible in the eastern allocation of labor. Miran tells Nyuil that returning injured people cannot take the same share as before the war, and that sending Nuri continuously to construction and transport will make them miss the next sowing. Their narrow fields cannot feed them alone; they also need hands to obtain food from the streams and prepare forest products. She is not asking that construction cease, but negotiating for time for the household to recover by continuing its different livelihoods. Nuri helps her mother, but objects to having her labor defined solely as a share her mother bargains with the state ([a season of overlapping livelihoods](../settings/030-society/060-regional-livelihood.md#regional-livelihood)).

Nyuil knows that calling everyone at once, as for a front-line mobilization, could finish construction quickly. Nevertheless he divides the workforce and first leaves days for household labor. He prioritizes stretches connecting routes home and centers of production, accepting a later completion for the rest. He reports to Namsaeng that the original work order cannot be met and takes responsibility rather than blaming residents' negligence. Nyuil does not unilaterally change distribution and education as well.

Namsaeng accepts the delay and maintains the reconstruction principle of separating seed for planting from food to eat now. Miran actually gets workers back for sowing, and Nuri takes on household work without promising all future transport at once. Construction is delayed, and the household gains the next season. Even after victory, state protection remains a relationship to be negotiated and fulfilled each time. The achievement reaches Gaesomun as a country retaining not only troops who obey orders but members who will protect their homes.

## The same recovery leaves different regional debts {#unequal-regional-costs}

In Liaodong and Gungnaeseong, return home and the recovery of roads increase trust in Namsaeng. Seokbu joins reconstruction, guiding people along routes and to workers, and sees that the commander who endured the same cold at the front protects residents' time in recovery too. Namsaeng accepts this cooperation as a basis for his rule. Yet even within regions receiving support, the discontent of households repeatedly supplying troops and transport does not disappear.

Around Pyongyang and in the south, resistance grows from people who sent food and materials and see their own defenses deferred. A report that Silla forces captured Dolsaseong in the seventh lunar month of 664 strengthens that opposition. It names Kim Inmun, Pumil, Gungwan, and Munyeong as joint commanders who moved troops from Ilseonju and Hansanju and the troops and horses of Ungjin's subordinate fortresses. Gungwan is a person's name here, not the common noun for a military officer; do not merge the four names into one general or invent separate credit for who crossed the gate first ([Pumil](../settings/040-agents/081-pumil.md#pumil), [Gungwan](../settings/040-agents/082-gungwan.md#gungwan), [Munyeong](../settings/040-agents/083-munyeong.md#munyeong)). Do not invent a direct siege scene with a fixed location or new casualty figures. Namgeon argues that a larger offensive must prevent local losses, while Namsaeng recognizes the need for border defense but will not turn all reconstruction workers into an expeditionary force because one southern fortress fell. Recovery remains the priority, and opposition at the center remains too.

The narrator connects the timescales on which cessation of war and local fighting coexist, and the difference between regions receiving support and regions bearing burdens. Do not bundle Pyongyang's discontent into a universal popular desire for royal restoration. The next event inherits a situation in which growing local trust in Namsaeng increases central suspicion that the benefits and power of reconstruction are concentrating in one person.

## The brothers' abilities give their opposition weight {#competence-strengthens-opposition}

Citing his experience commanding the central heavy cavalry during the campaign, Namgeon demands that Namsaeng keep and use the army now. His actual ability to see urgent danger and move troops lends weight to his argument among the central military establishment. He knows what the delaying action contributed, but does not believe it justifies the Yalu defeat or his elder brother's cautious course. He gives the production and cost of households maintaining the army less weight than his own experience.

Namsan coordinates royal and noble demands and conveys the center's opposition to his brother. Beyond simple support for war, he advances the idea that royal and Yeon cooperation could preserve power while reducing their father's coercion. Namsaeng still needs both men's work, but hears their demands for attack as words ignorant of real costs. Believing more explanation will make them understand, he sets out only the authority he will delegate and does not adequately consider where they want recognition.

King Bojang sees the difference at close hand. He judges that the more successful reconstruction is, the more firmly Namsaeng's local cooperation will take root without the royal house, and opens a separate channel for the younger brothers to receive recognition for their abilities. He has not yet reached the point of ordering a coup. While Gaesomun's authority prevents the offensive and advances reconstruction, the king and brothers approach one another through the judgment that after he is gone they need not remain bound to the same policy.
