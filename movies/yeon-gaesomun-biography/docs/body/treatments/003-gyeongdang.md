Around 620 Gaesomun attends [Pyongyang Gyeongdang](../settings/060-places/260-pyongyang-gyeongdang.md#pyongyang-gyeongdang). He tries to take his fine bow from home first; Buru points out the turn and sends him to the end of the yard. Eulbo hits the target first with a shared bow. Instead of reclaiming his turn through family status, Gaesomun tries to win the next contest. Buru makes the noble child keep the same line; the children follow Eulbo, who shoots best now, rather than family rank ([school operation](../settings/030-society/030-gyeongdang.md#gyeongdang)).

When Gaesomun boasts of his bow, Eulbo retorts briefly that owning a bow and hitting a target are different things. The children laugh at acting victorious too soon, and Gaesomun tries to answer with his next arrow. This contest is ordinary life in a shared yard where children can boast and answer back, not solely foreshadowing a life-or-death contest. When another child’s arrow falls short after an unsteady draw, a waiting child mocks that archer’s lack of strength, enacting the [social pressure attached to martial ability](../settings/030-society/020-martial-status.md#martial-status). Buru calls for the next attempt without making the ridicule a moral lesson; the exchange exposes the yard’s disregard for weakness, alongside its challenge to inherited privilege.

On a day Eulbo is late finishing cart work, Gaesomun resents the absence of the rival he wants to beat. Eulbo explains that his father’s injured leg and household debt required him to carry loads, revealing different demands on their days. Gaesomun knows but does not yet think waiting for him is his duty. Eulbo recognizes a friend who wins by skill while remembering that the winner does not understand his circumstances ([Eulbo](../settings/040-agents/115-eulbo.md#eulbo)).

Eulbo cites Ondal, a poor man recognized through military achievement, and tells Gaesomun he hopes to earn a name and escape debt the same way. To Gaesomun military merit offers a way to expand family power; to Eulbo it can change his household’s position. The two learning the same bow want different things from war from the start.

The historian briefly adds Ondal’s other ending to the advancement story the children choose. The man who earned his name in the vanguard against Northern Zhou died under Adanseong from an arrow on a Silla expedition. Courage alone did not remove walls, difficult terrain, and the connection between defenders and relief; even a captured fortress required troops to hold the next stronghold and supply routes. Do not open Ondal’s battle as a separate recollection. Distinguish the individual success children admire from the country’s expeditionary limits. This additional knowledge does not immediately make the children abandon dreams of merit ([achievement and a soldier’s different conditions](../settings/040-agents/084-ondal.md#ondal)).

In the same long view the historian connects the report that Go Seung attacked Bukhansanseong in 603 but withdrew outnumbered when King Jinpyeong’s relief arrived. Personal courage attacking a fortress differs from an expedition’s capacity to face additional forces outside it. This is an earlier example distinct from the 661 siege at the same fortress; do not make it a lesson in which children learn every campaign and revise their conclusion on the spot ([Go Seung’s withdrawal](../settings/040-agents/102-go-seung.md#go-seung), [Jinpyeong’s relief](../settings/040-agents/103-jinpyeong.md#jinpyeong), [the two distinct sieges](../settings/060-places/190-bukhan.md#bukhan)).

Buru tells how Yeongyang and prince Go Geonmu defeated Sui’s proclaimed two million. To the children, the number is the scale of the enemy their inherited hero overcame. His story of Geonmu’s 612 fight keeps them holding bows longer. Though he experienced the outer-fortress ambush, he emphasizes a heavy-cavalry charge and 500 defeating 43,000. Gaesomun is captivated that the prince went forward first and wants to stand at the front himself. Buru’s two missing fingers are visible, but the boy sees victory before injury ([Buru’s knowledge and motive](../settings/040-agents/114-gyeongdang-teacher.md#gyeongdang-teacher)).

The historian distinguishes oral tale from record for the audience. [The adopted distinction](../../instructions/003-era-and-restoration.md#repeated-invasions-and-war-memory) gives 1,133,800 in the record and two million in Sui’s proclamation. Distinguish the recorded figure from both that threat and a modern independently verified mobilization count; the children continue to hear the larger heroic number. Briefly add that Sui’s naval troops were dispersed looting before the ambush and Zhou Fashang’s force remained at the ships. Do not perform the fight as a separately settled flashback at the children’s hearing, or have the boy learn and correct every error. He gains a desire to resemble his hero; the audience knows the battlefield conditions absent from it.

Around 626 Gaesomun surpasses Eulbo in archery and reading. When Buru orders formation movement, Gaesomun goes alone for the target and splits the children behind him. Buru does not end the contest with an individual win; he makes them line up again. This time Gaesomun waits for those behind and they finish together, winning the children’s recognition of his ability. He learns cooperation is useful but remembers his own judgment was right before he remembers waiting.

After hearing his son won, Taejo asks whether the losing child will follow next time. Mother first asks about Eulbo’s household; Jeongto stays with the children who lost. One victory gives the family different questions. As years pass Gaesomun retains his expectation that military achievement can make the country great; that expectation carries into Taehak.
