# The Paegang force is cut off first

## Choosing the weaker defense first {#first-target}

On the fourteenth day of the second lunar month, Gaesomun attacks Ren Yaxiang's camp first. Hunger is not the only reason: eliminating the camp whose defense and relief connections are easiest to cut will narrow the next enemy's escape at Sasu. Of at most 50,000 personnel, he commits 30,000 to frontal combat, separating 16,000 for interdiction and combat reserve and 4,000 for horses and baggage support ([the attack's limits](../settings/050-systems/110-ren-yaxiang-defeat.md#ren-yaxiang-defeat)). Do not use support personnel as additional charging troops.

While local infantry holds the narrow front, cavalry pressures open ground and connecting routes. Namgeon commands a group of central heavy cavalry, sustaining the attack on the outer formation. When the lead troops become entangled toward the narrow entrance, he regroups them toward open ground, leaving room for infantry to continue pressing. His actual ability lies in preserving their momentum while pulling them out of a front where they cannot immediately be used. These troops belong to the existing mobile army that returned in the first lunar month; they are not separate reinforcements. Pyongyang's defenses are not emptied for everyone to pour through one entrance. Ren Yaxiang requests relief from Su Dingfang and separates his fighters and carriers in an effort to hold the camp. The enemy commander, too, knows what he must protect.

The route from Su Dingfang to Ren Yaxiang's camp is about 18 kilometers, including a detour and crossing. A messenger can convey the crisis sooner than a large force can assemble and cross. Su Dingfang must deal with pressure on his own camp and prepare to withdraw at the same time; the arrival of supplies does not allow him to send everyone. Sokro's interdiction delays relief movements and forces the enemy to check each other's situation again.

When the connection between the assembly ground before Ren Yaxiang's camp and the storehouse breaks down, fighters and carriers scatter in different directions. The rear that sustained the fighting ahead is cut off, and even those receiving orders cannot return to the same formation. Gaesomun commits reserves then to prevent the organization from re-forming. This is not the result of enemy cowardice or discovery of a single secret in their formation.

Ren Yaxiang dies in camp that day. Do not settle who killed him or with what weapon as a new heroic tale. Although soldiers survive or are captured, the Paegang army loses the organization needed to resume operations that night. Rather than pursue every person, Gaesomun regroups the remaining mobile forces and infantry and prepares to move to Sasu.

Through Ren Yaxiang's last reports and the interruption of his command, the audience sees how delayed information separated life from death and preserved organization from collapse. The historian distinguishes the death recorded in history from the work's organizational collapse, without imposing a numerical ending. Four days are needed before the next attack because this victory did not leave the troops unscathed.
