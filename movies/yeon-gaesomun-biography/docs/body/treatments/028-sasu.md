On the night of the fourteenth, the force that destroyed Ren Yaxiang's camp reorganizes. On the fifteenth and sixteenth it moves and assembles along a 22-kilometer connecting route that avoids enemy roads and the water's edge, rather than taking the direct 12 kilometers to Sasu. On the seventeenth it completes reconnaissance and blocks the land rendezvous points ([the Sasu timetable](../settings/050-systems/120-sasu-battle.md#sasu-battle)). The result at the Imjin does not reach this force instantly and cause it to change its movement.

Pang Xiaotai knows of Ren Yaxiang's crisis but must check whether his own troops and baggage can pass through the blocked exits. An urgent message takes half a day to reach Su Dingfang; assembling and crossing with a relief force takes at least a day. He receives reports of the positions of his thirteen sons and the state of his forces, but one son's death is not used to paralyze the whole army. He holds his camp and watches for relief to the end.

On the morning of the eighteenth day of the second lunar month, Gaesomun places heavy cavalry on the open front to shake the enemy's outer formation and uses local infantry to press through the narrow entrance into the camp. Sokro's light cavalry cuts outside communications and scattered troops. The heavy cavalry formation does not enter the narrow lanes between barracks intact; horse and baggage support maintains the connection behind ([the Sasu camp's layout](../settings/060-places/100-sasu-ground.md#sasu-ground)).

Pang Xiaotai holds the entrance and waits for help from another camp. Drums and shouts can be heard inside, but barracks and baggage block each junior commander's view of other formations, so their responses do not come together at the same time. Ren Yaxiang's camp has already fallen, narrowing the escape route, and the interdiction on the seventeenth delays relief. With the elite troops deprived of conditions in which to use their tactics, Gaesomun connects the divisions at the front and inside. Both combat ability and exhaustion act on both sides.

Pang Xiaotai, his thirteen sons, and tens of thousands die, and the Okjeo army loses its organization. The water's edge and narrow exits obstruct the formation's retreat, but individual survivors escape. Weak ice does not swallow the whole army at once, and a perfect encirclement with no open direction does not guarantee every person's death. Gaesomun's soldiers also suffer losses and fatigue.

After victory at Sasu, he intends to finish the campaign by attacking Su Dingfang too, but that enemy has already received provisions and prepared to leave. The six-part plan the audience heard at the first council now reaches its final threshold: three organizations collapse, two forces depart, and one gets home. Preserve the emotion of a great victory without granting a complete victory yet.
