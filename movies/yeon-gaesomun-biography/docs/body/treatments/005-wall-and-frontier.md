News of the autumn 629 defeat at Nangbiseong reaches conversation at Taejo’s house. Seogu and Taejo, already divided over Tang’s reunification, again give different answers. When Seogu describes envoys and investiture as the price of peace, Taejo replies that they yielded first and still lost a southern fortress. Rather than staging all 619–629 diplomacy separately, the historian connects the 622 return of people and 624 investiture and calendar as accumulating grounds for the two arguments ([record and readings](../settings/020-world/090-second-restoration-and-tang.md#tribute-years-619-629)). Gaesomun remembers continued tribute despite the loss as humiliation.

When news of Eastern Turk collapse arrives in 630, Taejo admits the time to strike Liaoxi has passed. He now urges spending people and grain first on elites and cavalry instead of the wall. Seogu says both are needed; Taejo replies the same resources cannot support both. The audience sees the offensive argument inherited by the boy change with circumstances. Gaesomun’s accusation that the elders missed their chance grows stronger.

In the work, news that the victory memorial has been dismantled reaches Pyongyang at an advanced date in 631, before wall construction begins. The king decides to preserve peace while buying time for defense because Tang pressure may turn east. Gaesomun reads the king as weak for not attacking first even after the victory marker is destroyed ([adopted dating and conflict with records](../settings/020-world/090-second-restoration-and-tang.md#tang-unification-and-wall)). The historian distinguishes the earlier destruction and report as reconstruction without adding the actual later mission as a second event.

Only after this debate does the boy learn Seogu fought with Go Geonmu at the outer fortress in 612. He first discovers that a man arguing against fighting knows how to fight. Even so, Gaesomun preserves his belief by deciding Seogu has grown old and passive. Do not give him an early realization of the king’s wisdom. The distance between Geonmu and the boy grows not after admiration disappears, but as the boy demands a stronger decision from his admired hero.
