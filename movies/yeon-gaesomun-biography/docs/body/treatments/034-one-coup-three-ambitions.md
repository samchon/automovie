# Different aims gathered under one royal order

## A successor who must visit the regions leaves the center {#restoration-requires-absence}

In 665, Namsaeng must turn the position gained through his father's authority into real cooperation for reconstruction. Gathering reports in Pyongyang alone cannot coordinate household labor and road conditions, so he tours Liaodong and Gungnaeseong. He continues to entrust central military affairs to Namgeon and contact with the royal house and nobles to Namsan. This is not a case of brothers secretly occupying a vacuum because they were excluded entirely from power; real delegated authority is used during their elder brother's absence.

Attendants and baggage accompany him. A one-way journey between Gungnaeseong and Yodongseong takes nine days under normal conditions, twelve in winter; days spent at refugee villages are additional. Even an urgent messenger with relay horses needs four days for that section. Do not apply these figures uniformly to the entire journey to Pyongyang. The audience understands that stays during the tour and round-trip reports prevent Namsaeng from immediately knowing relationships that change daily at the center.

Namsaeng accepts the cost of leaving his post while negotiating the order of work with local people. At the center, the royal house and nobles read that negotiation as a sign that coercive power has weakened. Performing real duties, Namgeon and Namsan gain people and channels through which their orders work. As the story enters 666, the secret unknown to their elder brother is not his succession itself but their decision not to return the authority he entrusted to them.

## The king and two brothers decide to remove the elder {#royal-command-enables-removal}

King Bojang judges that if Namsaeng's reconstruction succeeds, the Go royal house will be left with ceremony alone. He gives Namsan grounds for the royal house and Yeon family to rule together, and recognizes Namgeon's military ability as a force for correcting his elder brother's policy. Namsan knows the king desires restored power, but believes he and Namgeon can control that force. Namgeon accepts that a royal order would let him remove his brother without violating loyalty to family or country.

The three agree to summon Namsaeng to the center, strip him of effective power, and take control of his remaining residence and guards. They do not plan to kill their elder brother and nephew. The king expects coercive conflict between the brothers to diminish the Yeon family's prestige and lead to restored royal authority; the younger brothers seek the king's approval to begin their own administration and offensive. The order actually approved by the king gives their bargain political power beyond the family.

Reveal the difference in their aims to the audience before Namsaeng learns it. Do not postpone the cause of the coup into a mystery about who forged an order. Stop, however, at the point where all believe they can still control events after removing Namsaeng. Only troubling signs at the center reach him, creating a need to establish the facts and making his next act, sending an investigator, possible.
