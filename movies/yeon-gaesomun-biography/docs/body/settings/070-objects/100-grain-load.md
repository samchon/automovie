Canon guide: [Location of this subject](../outline/index.md#070-objects--100-grain-load).

## Grain loads and carrying equipment {#grain-load}

**Definition and scope**

Grain loads divide total food into units people can move and guard. The transport records of rice, fine grain, and over 2,000 carts ground the quantity. The work uses cloth sacks, woven packages, balanced loads on either side of animals, and human backloads. One sack is not a fixed seok; wounded people get smaller loads. Unequal weights on either side affect an animal’s gait and securing ropes.

**Established facts**

Where mountain paths lack cart-wheel width, grain is unloaded and moved by other means. Do not establish that every cart and abandoned load is later recovered. If snow or water enters packaging, inspect it separately rather than hiding it in dry grain. Delivered food is Tang’s share; transport workers need their own meals separately. One grain reserve does not solve all food for horses and people. See [the transport column](../050-systems/150-transport-column.md#transport-column) for assumed seok volume, [grain and own rations](../050-systems/160-grain-and-rations.md#grain-and-rations) for grains, milling, textual variants, and delivery target, and [the transport column](../050-systems/150-transport-column.md#transport-column) for the work's transport personnel.

Recurring sacks are thick gray-brown cloth about 70 centimeters long and 40 wide laid flat, with the opening folded and tied twice. Filled weight varies with carrier and grain condition; dimensions alone do not establish one seok. Packs on an animal hang below a wooden cross support on equally long cords for balance. The wooden two-wheeled cart has wheels about one meter in diameter, width 1.6 meters, and a two-meter bed. Cloth and rope use the light tawny to gray-brown [baseline range](../010-work/010-work.md#visual-world); wet sections appear darker. These dimensions are not an ancient standard confirmed by the record of over 2,000 carts.
