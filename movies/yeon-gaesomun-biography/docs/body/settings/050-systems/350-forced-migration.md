## Lives divided by forced migration and remaining {#forced-migration}

**Definition and scope**

Use the forced relocation of residents into Tang’s interior after Goguryeo’s fall. For 669, present the Samguk Sagi text’s 38,300 households and distinguish variants of 38,200, 28,200, and 30,000 households. Do not read households as people and claim 38,000 people moved. Do not extrapolate the exact national population or people per household from this account.

**Established facts**

Do not divide the outcome simply into all Pyongyang residents taken away and all Liaodong and Gungnaeseong residents who submitted left behind. Some movements disperse the central population and command groups; other residents remain. A cooperating commander’s connections can protect some families, but do not guarantee every household against relocation. Haeun’s household moves inland together; Miran and Sokro’s community consists of those remaining in the east who are again asked for military service and subjected to rule.

Haeun’s destination is a rural area of Henan. Do not claim a precise modern village is a residence preserved on an actual relocation list. Mending tools and a few pieces of cloth brought from Pyongyang remain, but the same customers and work do not. Seokbu survives fighting for Goguryeo, is disarmed, and is required to work in his own region. Do not frame the survivors as feeling their country was preserved, or only the displaced as feeling its fall.
