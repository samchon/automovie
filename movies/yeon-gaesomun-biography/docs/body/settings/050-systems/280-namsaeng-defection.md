## Submission at Gungnaeseong and the routes Tang gains {#namsaeng-defection}

**Definition and scope**

After refusing the summons and his younger brothers’ seizure of the center, Namsaeng enters Gungnaeseong and gathers local supporters and surviving troops. At first he seeks the strength to protect his own survival and remaining family. After confirming Heonchung’s death and the royal order for his execution, he accepts Tang’s military power as a means to overthrow the new regime. Local nobles’ longstanding grievances and trust forged in fighting alongside Namsaeng strengthen this choice.

**Established facts**

Heonseong explains the direction in which to seek outside aid and travels as envoy. Some around Namsaeng oppose him, including Seokbu, who does not follow. Tang gains not merely a family to spare but information with which to conquer Goguryeo. Namsaeng’s knowledge of fortress approaches, regional commanders, storehouses, and mobilization relationships is used to bypass and divide the existing defense network.

Gungnaeseong’s submission alone does not open every Liaodong fortress to Tang at once. Some commanders cooperate and others hold out; the trust attached to Namsaeng’s name depends on who receives the order. Tang forces widen those gaps through actual operations. The feeling that Namsaeng ignored his homeland’s future survival and Tang’s policy of planned conquest are different motives.
