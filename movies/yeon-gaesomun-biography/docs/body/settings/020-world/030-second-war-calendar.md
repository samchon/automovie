# The campaign calendar, 661–662

Canon guide: [where to find this subject](../outline/index.md#020-world--030-second-war-calendar).

## The campaign calendar, 661–662 {#second-war-calendar}

**Definition and scope**

In the second Goguryeo–Tang war of 661–662, Tang attacks by land and water. The main work begins in Pyongyang in late spring 661, before Tang's full invasion. People know Baekje's capital and royal house fell in 660, but also hear that restoration forces have recovered fortresses and continue fighting. In the work, a frontier scout returning from Liaoxi gathers news that Tang appointed commanders for six directions—Liaodong, Paegang, Pyongyang, Nufang, Buyeo, and Okjeo—and will divide 35 armies among routes, along with news of recruiting in Hebei and elsewhere in the new year, and reports to a Yodongseong officer. Goguryeo does not know Tang's exact total and estimates roughly 350,000 from the 35-army organization. The officer reports to Pyongyang in late spring. Yeon Gaesomun reads the original; Go Hwi read a copy at military business the day before. Tang's appointments and recruiting are recorded. Samguk Sagi records more than 44,000 recruited from 67 prefectures in Henan, Hebei, and Huainan in the first lunar month of 661 for the Pyongyang and Nufang field headquarters, and in the fourth month commanders advancing by divided water and land routes in 「凡三十五軍」. Scouting, the report route, and arrival time are the work's construction. In records the six directional names appear successively between winter 660 and the fourth month of 661; the 35 armies first appear in the summer fourth-month entry. The work combines the six names and 35 armies in one late-spring report. Goguryeo learning the 35-army organization then is a fictional advance of the fourth-month entry's information. Actual departures, forces, and routes cannot be known from this report. Go Hwi's household departure and gate dispute are invented in the information gap.

This record owns the recorded calendar for the second war. It gives only dates and facts conveyed by records and dates chosen by the work. Dates differ among sources and cannot be merged into one definitive calendar. Work instructions determine the campaign's major stages; the corresponding treatments determine character choices, results, and the order in which news arrives between them.

The fictional northern-diversion timetable places central dispatch on days 11–15 of the ninth lunar month. This does not establish actual dispatch dates of the three edicts. Adding [northern diversion](../050-systems/090-northern-diversion.md#northern-diversion) transmission times of 18, 20, and 28 days and preparation of 2, 2, and 3 days yields 20, 22, and 31 days from dispatch to departure respectively. Xiao Siye departs late month nine to early month ten, Ashina Zhong early month ten, and Qibi Heli in mid-month ten after the ice crossing, all by day 20 of the tenth lunar month. Do not convert differing month lengths into a historically fixed single day. Do not attach these long delivery values to a scholarly late dispatch reconstruction, produce a departure in the eleventh lunar month, and then say the forces were already gone late in the tenth lunar month.

Zizhi Tongjian's placement of the ninth-month recall supports considering an earlier order but does not prove ninth-month dispatch of all three. The work moves northern danger reports and redeployment decisions earlier than the scholarly reconstruction, adopting a fictional timetable that accommodates the dispatch window and fixed Nufang counterattack. Do not present this as a confirmed Tiele uprising date or Lee's conclusion. Goguryeo does not know the dispatch window beforehand. Confirmation of departure from Buyeo after two days, reporting another two days to Namsaeng and eight more to Pyongyang, conveys only movement and remaining camps. The first report does not disclose all three departures or the center's reasons.

Silla's annals connect receipt of provisions to immediate withdrawal; Zizhi Tongjian connects withdrawal to events after Ren's death and Pang's defeat. The work adopts instruction 007's order: delivery, Sasu, then Su's withdrawal alive. Do not attach unchanged the Silla annals' account that Kim Yusin returned after hearing of Tang's withdrawal. [The Silla main body's return](../050-systems/170-return-and-pursuit.md#return-and-pursuit) uses separate fictional dates, not advance news of Tang's later withdrawal.

[Three organizations destroyed and people remaining](../050-systems/130-second-war-losses.md#second-war-losses) determines which armies lose operational capacity and their losses. Place canon handles detailed location identifications and terrain at Yango and Sasu.
