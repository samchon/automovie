# Households providing grain and people

Canon guide: [Location of this subject](../outline/index.md#030-society--070-household-burdens).

## Households providing grain and people {#household-burdens}

**Definition and scope**

A household supplies tax grain and labor for military service and transport from the same livelihood. The Book of Sui tax account lists burdens borne by people and households and different quantities of grain, but interpretations differ on who bore them and how to read the units. Do not calculate those figures as a modern annual tax applying to every household. The work constructs central and local collection of grain, cloth, and labor according to actual mobilization units.

**Established facts**

Local mobilizers gather household grain together with supplies from local storehouses. When assigning people they try not to take soldiers, carriers, and busy-season farm workers from one household at once; urgent war conditions can break this allocation. Show who benefits and who must do another person’s share when it does.

A great family’s private storehouse and public military storehouse are separate places with different custodians. To use private stores requires the owning family’s consent or power to compel it to contribute.

Tax reductions and exemptions from service need an actual period and people to take on the reduced burden. Yeon’s encouragement and Namsaeng’s declaration of reconstruction do not themselves provide rations. Policy results require showing when households receive which supplies and what they do in return.

Do not leave household life solely to dialogue exchanging ledger numbers. Judge it through whether seed for the next crop can be kept, who has returned home, and whether the next requisition can be endured.
