## Supported findings by cause

### Final realization sometimes preserves the script's entire local choreography

The clearest example is `t021-december-sortie`. Script 003 at 290 already specifies: the strongpoint defenders repel the initial attackers; the next Goguryeo rank holds; the first attackers regroup; together they force the entrance; those inside call back; the rear keeps the route open. Screenplay 003 at 333 renders the same sequence: defenders thrust attackers out, the waiting troops hold beyond reach, the groups regroup for another push, weapons give way, and an inside cry calls the rear forward. The paragraph supplies no distinguishable new allocation of audience access, sound, emphasis or time. It is a paraphrase of executable choreography, not merely a shared fact that two strongpoints were taken.

The strongest counterreading is that preserving an intelligible causal attack is necessary. That permits preserving the result and mechanics, but the parent-differentiation contract separately rejects borrowed execution sequences. The treatment owner, `021-winter-reserves.md:19-21`, fixes the two captured strongpoints, the exclusive use of garrison troops and the inability to continue; it does not prescribe this entrance choreography. The earliest owner of this duplication is final screenplay construction. Recompose from those event and execution decisions with an actual viewing progression; no new battle outcome is needed.

This is a concrete surviving failure, not a claim that all 115 H2s lack transformation. Whole paragraphs of inherited fixed action elsewhere require care: the four guards' roles and corridor in `t037-wounded-heonchung-carried-inside`, for example, are adopted constraints, so preserving them is not by itself copying. Likewise, the tool-versus-cloth negotiation in `t041-four-migrate-to-henan-together` is largely treatment-owned event design; screenplay 200 adds a specific halted reply and the daughter's unyielding grip. I withdraw those as standalone copying findings.

### A new attention plan introduces an unmarked return to a group scene

Screenplay 001 at 162 establishes the empty shooting place at sunset, Gaesomun waiting and Eulbo arriving from cargo work. Their exchange continues through 174. At 176, "The next child receives the shared bow from Eulbo," and children behind him make room. At 180 another child returns the bow; 184 and 186 continue the group turn-taking. No new day, return of the group, or transition is established. The reader has just been placed with two boys after the shooting place emptied, then suddenly must invent a group contest to support the historian's commentary.

The strongest counterreading is an intended montage or later practice session. The text gives neither temporal boundary nor transition, while repeatedly using "next" as continuous action. Script 001 at 152-168 keeps the late-day two-boy contest and its historian voice together without reintroducing the queue. The earliest owner is final screenplay construction, `t003-shared-bow`. Make the intended temporal and participant boundary legible while retaining the boys' class difference, the bow and the historical comparison.

### Research and edition management enter the performed historian voice

The adopted historian can compare records, expose heroic exaggeration and know the later outcome. That is not an automatic defect. However, several lines tell viewers how the current author has reconciled or staged historical material, interrupting the experience rather than giving a historian's consequential judgment.

The clearest example is screenplay 001 at 350: "Receiving the destruction report first and beginning the wall afterward is the work's reconstruction. The destruction in the later embassy record is treated as the same single event." It arrives immediately after the king's wall order and before the boy confronts Seogu. The audience is taken from the boy's crisis into instructions about avoiding duplication of an embassy record. Script 001 at 352 already says this work places the report before the wall and does not duplicate the embassy. Treatment `005-wall-and-frontier.md:15`, `memorial-and-wall`, explicitly assigns the historian to distinguish the advanced report as reconstruction without treating the later mission as a second destruction. This is therefore an upstream design cause, not something to fix solely by polishing final speech.

Related concrete members found in the full reading are screenplay 003:177 ("The river's freezing at this point is this story's reconstruction"), 004:124 ("The course by which orders and roads were cut ... is the work's reconstruction"), and 004:282 ("The extended collapse of three armies beyond Tang's brief postwar record is the work's reconstruction"). They inherit corresponding script narration at 003:166, 004:100 and 004:254. Treatment `026-ren-camp.md:31` assigns the recorded-death versus reconstructed-collapse distinction to the historian; treatment `029-bitter-victory.md:31` assigns the comparison of Tang's record with the work's interpretation to that voice. By contrast, treatment `018-yalu.md:19` fixes the early freeze as story chronology without assigning its source status to spoken narration, so the earliest visible owner of that particular voiced qualification is scripts 003:166. These share the cause of carrying the production's certainty bookkeeping into the finished voice, but do not all have the same earliest owner. Some source comparisons legitimately do dramatic work, such as the exaggerated two million over children acting out a heroic charge; those must retain their function.

Repair starts at the treatment or script that first prescribes authorial process as narrator text, keeping uncertainty and the distinction between record and reconstruction true. The repair question is where that distinction must be heard and what it lets the audience recognize, rather than whether every research qualification can be deleted. The adopted chronicle form allows direct commentary; the failure here is the specific interruption by edition-management language, not narration itself.
