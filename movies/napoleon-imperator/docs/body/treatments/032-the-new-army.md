# Victorious Recruits and Experience That Does Not Return

## A newly assembled army wins again {#spring}

Factual parents: [the war of 1813](../settings/100-history/020-life-and-memory.md#russia-and-coalition), [the cost of conscription](../settings/030-society/010-power-and-cost.md#recruitment), [Ney](../settings/050-people/200-ney.md#ney), [Duroc](../settings/050-people/210-duroc.md#duroc).

In 1813 Napoleon wins at Lützen and Bautzen with newly assembled forces. The army lost in Russia has not simply returned; veterans and recruits fight in different proportions. Ney's courage at the front and the Emperor's command remain real forces. Do not make these achievements false victories from the start merely because the audience already anticipates downfall.

To exploit victory fully requires cavalry for pursuit, experience, horses, and time. An announcement of victory in battle does not replace what is lacking. The opponent can withdraw again and preserve an army, while France is asked for more people to fight. The achievement the state gains through recruits' courage cannot return losses to the homes that sent them. Do not use an unconfirmed age of fourteen as a universal conscription age.

Additional factual parent: [additional historical decisions](../settings/100-history/030-decisions-and-results.md#spring).

At Lützen, when Ney's troops encounter unexpected pressure in the villages, Napoleon shifts to concentrated artillery and a Young Guard counterattack to drive the enemy back. At Bautzen, frontal pressure and Ney's advance on the flank do not complete the cutting of the retreat, allowing the enemy to withdraw in order. Give the distinct execution of the two victories, then move to the limits on pursuit from a shortage of cavalry and Duroc's separate death.

## The man who guarded the spaces between orders disappears {#duroc}

Follow the adopted account in which Duroc is wounded on May 22 and dies that night. Do not turn the variant date of the 23rd into an additional scene of survival. With the loss of a man who since Italy had sorted requests, protected working time, and managed close access, Napoleon loses another continuity outside the administration of victory. Do not repeat the same words or consolation used at Lannes's death.

The audience's loss is more than the departure of a kind character who rebukes the Emperor. A long-known, trusted relationship diminishes around power in the future. The commander's work of keeping the army moving does not stop, so grief and the next order overlap in one person. The armistice briefly pauses this exhausted army and its relationships, but is not time that restores former strength.
