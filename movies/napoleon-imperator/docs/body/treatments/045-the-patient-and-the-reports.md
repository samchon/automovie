# The Person Who Knows the Body and the Person Who Grants Permission

In spring 1818, after Gourgaud leaves, O'Meara still sees and treats the patient closely. He stands between information demanded by the governor and his relationship as physician. Do not combine his writing on Napoleon's side and opposing statements from Lowe's side into one neutral report. What the audience needs is not authentication that everything either side says is true, but the condition that health reports also pass through power and interests.

After his dismissal in July 1818 O'Meara can no longer treat him. Do not keep him at the bedside until Antommarchi's arrival in 1819 to smooth over the gap in care. As Napoleon's body worsens, who may admit and dismiss a needed person creates problems of both health and pride. The island's restrictions now touch even the possibility of recovering strength to walk and speak.

## A new physician cannot repair time already past {#antommarchi}

Antommarchi arrives on September 20, 1819. He did not witness earlier treatment and is not a physician who arrives already knowing a definitive modern diagnosis. Although exercise, daily life, and efforts to care for the body briefly make activity possible, they cannot fully reverse worsening illness. Garden, dictation, meals, and rest are not a simple exile montage, but a daily life changing the scope of what can be done that day.

For Bertrand and Marchand, helping the Emperor do what he wants overlaps with accepting what is no longer possible. The time available to care for a body today shrinks, rather than a story of recovering power. Do not fix the new physician as one incompetent culprit who arrived late; retain contemporary diagnosis and knowledge and the limits of patient and care. The next will arises as the future in which he can live and command again diminishes.
