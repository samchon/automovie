Ajaccio, August 15, 1769. While the audience awaits the birth of an emperor, the family welcomes one child who must be cared for. Survival and the continuation of the family come first for Letizia; no one prophesies Napoleon's future victories. The historian notes only the island's incorporation into France, then withdraws to the family's life. The power gathered under one name in the first battle is now traced back through the home and institutions on which that person depended.

For the boy, pride in Corsica and the path of a French education still coexist. Do not make the fierce words he later used about his father's choice of France a fixed statement by a child. Leave a gap between his attachment to Paoli and the reality his family chose, so the later political break opens as a matter of actual choice rather than inborn destiny.

## Marked by his accent, he finds a place through mathematics {#school}

After learning the language at Autun in 1779, he moves to Brienne and in 1784 to the military school in Paris. Education is the gain of leaving home; separation from family and familiar speech is the cost. The student who must learn a language his fellows understand does not answer shame about his origins with submission, but holds on through study and pride. Mathematics and reading are practical means of passing through the gate of rank, not signs of a genius born ahead of others.

First establish the snow-fort episode as an anecdote Bourrienne related later. Mock attack turns to snowballs mixed with stones and injuries, leaving a small fracture in the pleasure of command: other people's bodies follow it. Do not end it with a prohibition or the Emperor's own recollection. After his father's death he completes the course and becomes an artillery second lieutenant in 1785; the result leads less to glory than to pressure to help support the family again.
