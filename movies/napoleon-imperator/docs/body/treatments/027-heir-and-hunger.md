# A Dynasty to Continue and Homes Growing Empty

## A child's birth becomes the nation's promise {#heir}

The heir's birth in 1811 gives Napoleon two results: family joy and continuity of the dynasty. Marie Louise is a person who has given birth and must recover, not just a symbol gained by the state; the baby is not a little emperor with a will to inherit victory. Distinguish the child his parents care for physically from the position the empire gives him. Even at the distance of a historian who knows his later life, let the audience fully receive that day's relief.

The desire that the established order continue so this child can be safe connects to a larger logic of national security. Yet the heir's existence does not make allies more obedient. The difference between a future made certain for the father and Europe's uncertainty remains as pressure toward the next war.

Additional factual parent: [the heir's title](../settings/050-people/590-napoleon-ii.md#napoleon-ii-vienna-role).

The child receives the title King of Rome. Make that title known separately from the child cared for by his family, without turning it into actual command of a state by the baby.

## A state able to demand more meets a society less able to give {#grain-and-conscription}

At the same time, a grain crisis and the cost of the blockade press on daily life. A state that demands food, money, and soldiers through administration gathers resources for war, but the households receiving those demands need provisions and labor left for themselves. The difference between a household able to buy a substitute and one that must send a son remains as unequal burdens under the same law. Carry this asymmetry through the current process of mobilization without inventing a newly named family or particular incident of harm.

Russian and French interests also diverge over the demands of blockade and security. For Alexander, the need to protect his country's interests becomes greater than continuing to follow the relationship at Tilsit. Napoleon seeks to solve through war the problem of whether to accept an ally's departure or restore order by force. Carry forward the promise of the future opened by celebrating a birth and the shortage of people to send out as two sides of the same period.
