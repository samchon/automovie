# Boundaries of Records and Adoption

[Outline](../outline/index.md#120-research--010-source-policy)

## Four layers and reconstruction {#layers}

Contemporary documents are letters, orders, reports, and diaries written at the time. The existence of a document differs from a judgment that every claim in it is true. A witness's memoir is memory written later; identify the recorder or say the account is reported. Place later biographies, paintings, and legends alongside their later reception. Preserve a story whose source cannot be found as one whose first transmitter and original wording are unknown, and do not reproduce it as a confirmed event on the scene. Even within one file, utterances can belong to different layers. When filling dialogue, inner thoughts, stage dimensions, or weather absent from records, distinguish it as a choice of the work that does not alter historical outcomes. This carries forward original `120-research/030-sources-and-records#record-layers` and `#imagination-in-gaps-rule`.

## Bulletins and recorders in exile {#weight}

Troop counts, losses, and self-assessments in Grande Armée bulletins are propaganda claims. Do not fix numbers not compared with opposing reports, contemporary documents, and research as neutral screen captions. Las Cases's Memorial is a diary written by Las Cases and an edited publication, distinct from campaign memoirs Napoleon separately dictated. Gourgaud's resentments, Bertrand's dated notebooks, Montholon's distrusted memoirs, and the devoted Marchand's view produce different records. Madame de Rémusat's memoir was reconstructed from memory and materials starting in 1818 after her diary was burned; do not use it as a contemporaneous transcript. This adopts the original research domain's explanation of how sources were formed, not an assertion that the current producer compared every manuscript.

## Editions of correspondence and translations {#editions}

The 32-volume Correspondence published on Napoleon III's orders and the Napoleon Foundation's 15-volume General Correspondence underwent different selection and editing. Attach the actual volume, document number, and translation used to a quotation. The source prioritizes the 15-volume edition and identifies the 32-volume edition when used. Do not expand reading a catalog, title, or preface into reading the whole body. Distinguish Stutterheim's Austerlitz account in the London 1807 English edition translated from French by John Pine Coffin. For this authorship the title, translator's preface, and introduction were opened and read; the full French original and other complete editions were not newly compared. The [English edition used](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66641/66641-h/66641-h.htm) includes different assessments by the translator and the Austrian author.

## Denominators and clocks {#numbers}

For the same question, one owner holds the adopted value, range, attribution, and conditions for change. Do not mix nominal organization with present strength, an army crossing a border with troops fighting that day, killed with wounded, prisoners, or stragglers, or soldiers with civilians. If times differ, first check whether they refer to the same act and place. Do not combine different arrivals of advance elements and the main body as a margin of error. Distinguish dispatch and receipt of orders, ship clocks and mean time, and calculated sunrise and observed sunlight. When the calendar of a Russian report's date is unknown, do not fix the report date solely by the conversion rule for the battle date.

## Narrow adoption of conflicting details {#collisions}

A narrower prohibition or unknown condition in the same source group takes precedence over a broad anecdote's permission. When Monge 211's intimate meal and mediation anecdote conflicts with Berthollet 219's scope allowing only an institutional relationship, do not adopt a scene of Berthollet meeting the Emperor privately. Change scope in that owner only after verifying the personal relationship with direct sources.

For the Bank of France's founding date follow January 18, 1800 adopted by [institution 096](../../../references/novels/settings/060-institutions/096-bank-of-france.md#bank-of-france). Preserve an older summary in that record of Gaudin's biography giving February 18, 1800 as an unconfirmed value whose stage may differ. This carries forward the source's narrow adoption; the original law or founding assembly record was not newly compared.

The February/March variants for Beresford's appointment in institutions 057 and 104, the unfinished 1815 section of [history 130](../../../references/novels/settings/100-history/130-neapolitan-campaigns.md#neapolitan-war-1815), and conflicting dates for Smoliani remain unresolved between sources. Commonly accepted years, campaigns, and organizational changes may be used, but do not use exact dates dependent on these conflicts or unfinished events as scene hinges before verification. Do not use 084's phrase “after Moreau's death” as the sequence of the August 27 battle.

[Institution 083](../../../references/novels/settings/060-institutions/083-french-army-germany-1813.md#french-army-germany-1813) and [history 069](../../../references/novels/settings/100-history/069-lutzen.md#lutzen) state that Girard was killed at Lützen, but 083 also records his command in August that year. Do not solve this contradiction as two people of the same name. Jean-Baptiste Girard survived his wound at Lützen and was mortally wounded at Ligny. He is distinct from Étienne-Maurice Gérard, commander of IV Corps at Ligny. The complete displayed texts of the [Senate career biography of Girard](https://www.senat.fr/pair-de-france/girard_jean_baptistepf1041.html) and [Napoleon Foundation account of Ligny](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/la-bataille-de-ligny/) were opened in this authorship to compare continuity of career and the two commanders. The Senate text is an OCR republication of an 1889–1891 biographical dictionary, so its year and position at Lützen and exact death date are not grounds for this correction. Distinguish Girard's division and Gérard's corps in the [retained Ligny record](../../../references/novels/settings/100-history/081-ligny.md#ligny) too. This correction adopts only continuity of identity and survival; it selects no new performer, detailed biography, appearance, voice, or battle count.

The 50, 70, and 82 cavalry squadrons of institution 098's fifth column are records with different dates and tables of organization. Do not combine them as the minimum and maximum of one observation, and do not use an unsupported ceiling of 5,400 men. Institution 099's ceiling of 5,700 grenadiers and exact handover time of command between Oudinot and Duroc are also unresolved. Do not calculate the two guns that crossed in 073 and twelve left later in 074 as one date and population. If printed totals and row sums in 1813–1814 tables differ, do not alter numbers to make them agree and put them in captions. Realize currently verifiable unit existence, mission, and approximate state; when exact numbers become necessary, resolve them with support in the relevant institution, person, or history owner. These military numerical restrictions preserve conflicts discovered in the handoff report, not a declaration that the present producer resolved them from primary sources.

## Handoff of facts and new verification {#verification-boundary}

This adaptation carries forward statements in retained source settings and the limits those statements identify. A previous author's statement in a file that they “actually read” something is that author's record, not a reading achievement transferable to the current producer. If an external original is newly opened in the current production, record the section read at its factual owner or in the [research record](../../../analysis/source-intake.md). Unknown values in handed-off material not newly verified remain unknown. If a decisive scene depends on one unknown value, realize it without the value or research and repair its factual owner first. When an example conflicts with a verification result, follow the research result. Do not silently repair a conflict between a fixed user interpretation and material; reveal the status of each.

## Commentary on literature actually consulted {#bibliography}

Author commentary introduces only editions and sections actually consulted. Korean-edition bibliographies for Roberts, McLynn, Horne, Mikaberidze, Lentz, On War, and Chandler recorded in the source library do not mean each book was read in full. If the current production has not read those Korean books, do not call them “books consulted.” Do not settle the existence or current availability of translations of Las Cases, Bourrienne, Marbot, Bourgogne, correspondence collections, and others from the source's old research. Verify publisher or library bibliography anew before providing needed reading guidance.
