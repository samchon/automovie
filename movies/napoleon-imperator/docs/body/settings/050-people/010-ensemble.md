## The freedom of Toussaint and Delgrès {#colonial-agency}

Toussaint and Delgrès act for their own freedom, power, and people's survival. They do not remain only victims' names for judging Napoleon's reputation. When direct records from the opposing side are thin, do not invent secret dialogue for its command as historical fact. [Slavery and colonial people](../030-society/010-power-and-cost.md#slavery) owns concrete conditions of law and expeditions; this section establishes the two men's basic agency.

## Unrecorded residents and soldiers {#invented-people}

Unnamed veterans, recruits, cantinières, conscript families, and residents may be invented. Set place, age, status, possible knowledge, and period of survival as choices of the work, distinguishing whether the same person appears in another period. Invented residents do not take the recorded words or decisions of historical people. The source's elderly Moravian winegrower is a fictional character of unknown name and age, with the role of showing resistance after requisition. Do not add an unrecorded death or a named speech to the Emperor as historical fact.

## Relationships heard in English {#registers}

The source expressed relationship distance through Korean speech levels. In this English edition, convey those distances through forms of address, diction, and the exchange, while preserving the underlying relationships and the distinction between private and formal settings. French tu and vous are not mechanically interchangeable with fixed English pronouns.

Do not say law forced all French people to use familiar speech during the Revolution. Distinguish citizens' forms of address and attempts to erase distance in party and office from relationships in general society. Do not turn foreign accents into English regional dialects. Dialogue outside the table follows the individual's original voice and authority. Use the source `020-writing-rules#name-forms` and individual H2 titles as reference while rendering names consistently in conventional English or French forms. Francis is “Francis II” in 1805 and “Francis I” after August 6, 1806. Do not merge different people under one shortened name, such as Auguste de Caulaincourt and his older brother, or valet Louis Marchand and General Jean-Gabriel Marchand.

Nicknames have dates of origin. Do not bring forward Ney's “bravest of the brave” as his own description before 1812 or Davout's Iron Marshal as a fixed title before 1806. “The Eaglet” is a later name from a work in 1835, not what the son called himself in life. Catherine Hubscher's “Madame Sans-Gêne” is the name of an 1893 play. The verb for betrayal derived from Marmont is also reception after 1814. Place these later titles beside earlier events only when the historian reveals their formation.
