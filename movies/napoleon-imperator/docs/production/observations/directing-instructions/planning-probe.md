### N01: Arrivals and joint request

Brief identification: “Murat, French marshal.” His hat's contact is readable without cutting away from his entrance. Soult approaches the table with heavy audible steps; identify “Soult, French marshal.” Newly arrived Lannes stops on the opposite side and looks down at the map they offer him; identify “Lannes, French marshal.” The three settle around the table within the same view, with the map and their directed looks readable. Murat and Soult address Lannes, rather than assume he has witnessed their outposts.

MURAT: If we stay scattered, the enemy will take us one by one. Let us ask to withdraw together.

SOULT: My outposts have been pushed back too.

Hold Lannes with both reporting men through the end of Soult's report. No narration or extra report words.

### N02: Lannes takes authorship

Cut tighter along the table to include Soult, Lannes and the sheet between them. Soult slides the paper toward Lannes after reporting, and addresses him across the movement.

End with the petition written. The next cut advances to its presentation rather than suggesting simultaneous writing and delivery.

### N03: The Emperor asks Soult

Establish the petition audience in a medium shared view: Napoleon at the table, Lannes handing him the petition, Soult and Murat present, with an unobstructed exit. Identify “Napoleon, Emperor of the French.” Napoleon receives and reads the joint petition. He then lifts his gaze from the paper to Soult.

Cut on that directed look to a medium two-shot of Soult and Lannes. Soult remains oriented toward Napoleon, whose position has been established.

Let Lannes fix his eyes on Soult as the answer lands. Soult lowers his attention to the map; retain both in frame so his avoidance is an observed relation, not an inferred reaction supplied by a cutaway. A brief silent interval lets that change register before Lannes speaks.

### N04: The challenge

Remain on the same two-shot. Lannes directs each line at Soult. Soult keeps looking at the map rather than meeting Lannes's eyes.

LANNES: You asked me to write it, and now I am the only one asking?

SOULT: He asked about my corps.

### N05: Withdrawal and unresolved departure

Their individual departures must remain distinguishable; three figures disappearing on one cut would lose the result and order. Napoleon stays. The exit order and Lannes's gaze are the relevant actions; no added conciliatory gesture or response.

## 2. Twelve-second cup short with delegated filming

### Final screenplay draft: The Offer

Brief: A twelve-second short. Two stick figures, A and B, occupy a plain shared space. A offers a cup; B hesitates and then takes it. Filming is delegated. No dialogue, captions, music or upstream settings, treatments or scripts are needed. The direction is patient, observable hesitation followed by acceptance; no reason for the hesitation is asserted.

0-2 seconds: A stands at screen left holding an upright cup at chest level. B faces A at screen right. A's free hand and B's hands are empty. A medium two-shot includes their heads, torsos and hands, with the cup clear against the background. Both are still long enough to establish who has it.

2-4 seconds: A extends the cup into the gap. B turns its head toward the cup. A reaches the offering position and waits there.

4-8 seconds: Cut closer on both upper bodies, with the cup and B's receiving hand in the lower center. B lifts one hand toward it, stops short and draws that hand slightly back. A continues holding the cup without chasing B's hand. Keep B's head and stalled hand together so the hesitation belongs to B.

8-10 seconds: B reaches again and closes its hand around the cup. Only once B supports it does A release and withdraw its hand. The cup remains upright throughout.

10-12 seconds: B draws the cup toward its own chest. A's hand is visibly clear. Hold their shared frame until the end, with the cup in B's sole possession. Cut to end at twelve seconds. Sound is silence.
