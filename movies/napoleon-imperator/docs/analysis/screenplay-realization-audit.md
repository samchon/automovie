### 2. Post-Waterloo political action remains a result without its execution

At screenplay `009-waiting.md:139–151`, Davout states a real refusal/distinction, but the next causal operation is: “At Fouché's place, words pass about the next order and Parliament decides separately. Results differing from support Lucien sought accumulate.” London news then intervenes, followed by the second abdication. The filmmaker is not given what anyone says, what motion or vote is decided, what result reaches whom, or what observable refusal defeats Lucien's attempt. “Words pass” and “results ... accumulate” cannot be performed without inventing the political event that the scene says has happened.

The script already leaves the same gap at `009-waiting.md:173`: “Fouché's papers head toward other political forces and the next order. Parliament does not wait for the Emperor's next command either.” The final wording does not repair it. Treatment `042-the-second-abdication.md:9–13` establishes competing responsibilities, Fouché's interest, parliamentary independence, relinquished authority, and the promise that the audience receives remaining negotiations and dangers. It does not decide the local parliamentary operation or the concrete negotiation that now blocks Napoleon. The earlier treatment `039-a-different-returned-emperor.md:9–15` decides constitutional limits but likewise cannot supply this later event.

The strongest defense is the adopted distant historian and compressed historical form. A concise historian bridge could be sufficient. Here, however, neither an actual historian statement nor an actual political operation carries the missing event; the text instead tells production that undisclosed decisions and results exist. A camera distance or an extra close-up would not fix that. This differs from the Malet sequence, whose treatment intentionally assigns the episode to a distant report and whose screenplay actually states the lie, recognition, arrest, returning troops, and stopped orders.

**Root cause and earliest responsible owner:** treatment 042's abdication event leaves its promised political causal operation underspecified. Settle what political decision/negotiation changes the available authority and how its result reaches the relevant participants, within adopted historical limits; then script its executable speech/action or an explicitly chosen adequate historian bridge; then compose the final experience. Where existing settings cannot support that event, research belongs upstream before deciding it. Do not invent a private Fouché meeting or parliamentary vote merely to fill this report's blank. A pure screenplay rewrite would otherwise make the next layer invent an absent event.

**Recipient loss:** the audience is told to register independent political judgment but receives no defined judgment to understand, hear, or watch. Napoleon's abdication follows an abstract collection of outcomes instead of the particular loss of authority the episode means to dramatize.
