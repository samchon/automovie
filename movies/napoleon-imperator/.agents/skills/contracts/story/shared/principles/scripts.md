# Script principles

Each script H2 answers for an executable scene or inseparable audiovisual progression within the script's chosen partition. Scripts already contain full action, actual dialogue, staging, and a viewing position.

## Can the scene be performed in its written order? {#executable-scene}

Does it start in an executable block rather than a planning summary, establish time, space, participants or forces, access, inherited pressure, and the initiating condition, then perform action, process, exchange, reaction, and an observable exit state? Blocks follow meaningful changes of action, response, process, time, space, form, or view. Separate speakers and place interruption, silence, and simultaneous action beside the operation they change. Their length and division carry urgency, duration, humor, or stillness rather than a fixed line count or a gesture on every line.

Consequential questions, lies, bargains, refusals, interruptions, and silences need the actual words, tactics, actions, and reactions that change choice, knowledge, power, or relationship. Specify delivery only where tone, volume, pace, or interruption changes meaning. Include the movement, objects, environment, physical limits, and time that affect the exchange. A reactionless beat list, reported exchange, buried decisive quotation, unexplained transition, or result without its execution fails. Observational, ritual, nonhuman, or formal processes need their actual operation rather than invented conflict. Do not have another person verbally summarize a lesson or realization the action already delivered; repeating it through several mouths turns the scene into a lecture. The exit follows from the final operation; do not append a redundant image, gesture, or conclusion to restate it.

## Can the established scene be seen and heard consistently? {#audiovisual-staging}

At the opening and each change of place, does the scene establish the used area, interior or exterior, story day and time, light sources and color, weather, and mood through visible and audible conditions? At each block, locate bodies and movement and establish what the view sees, from what scale and angle, with what movement and point of focus; a declared fixed view may supply that rule. Add performance, expression, gaze, appearance, clothing, possessions, and nonverbal sound when they change or carry meaning. Use canonical names and appearance terms; carry unchanged state forward instead of reciting it in every block. Meaningless directions that bury an execution decision fail even when each is physically possible.

Distinguish who speaks, whether the speaker is visible, what is audible within the world or only to the audience, and consequential overlap with action or sound. Dimensions, sightlines, props, light, costume, and sound must fit settings and inherited state. A missing view, impossible position or sightline, unsupported core appearance, or habitual composition that conceals the scene's changing force fails. Express a change of mood through its conditions; an interpretive label alone supplies neither mood nor performance. Inner meaning needs visible action, sound, or an explicitly adopted voiceover, caption, or inserted form. Declared theatrical, experimental, and fixed-view forms may express these decisions through their own notation.

Track each participant's knowledge, belief, doubt, and error through an actual path such as speech, observation, document, silence, or absence; a camera view cannot grant the participant knowledge. Scripts establish meaningful emphasis and viewing positions; final screenplay still owns their finished audiovisual realization, transitions, attention, and duration. Lens numbers, music composition, and inconsequential acting detail remain production choices unless the work requires them.
