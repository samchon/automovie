# Final screenplay principles

Each final screenplay H2 answers for the finished audiovisual experience specified by that unit. In a long work it follows already executable scripts; a short work authorized to start here realizes its actual brief and owns the necessary scene decisions directly. Neither path replaces finished audiovisual realization with planning or polished wording.

## Does it realize its material as a finished audiovisual experience? {#audiovisual-realization}

Does the unit transform its established action, performance, staging, view, speech, silence, and result through deliberate images, sound, attention, duration, juxtaposition, and transitions? Inherit these from actual scripts in a long work, or decide them here within the actual brief for an authorized short path. Direct entry must establish its used time and space, participants or forces, consequential staging and conditions, actual action, spoken words, response, and resulting state to the extent its chosen form uses them. Preserve the settled events, choices, relationships, consequences, world values, and reveal order, while selecting image and sound relationships, performance emphasis, motifs and temporal experience as needed for this moment. These are means of realization, not required additions to every paragraph. Important choices and exchanges must be experienced rather than reduced to synopsis, and micro-procedures or sensory decoration that displace them fail even when each detail has a function. Merely transcribing script blocks, expanding their directions, listing shots, summarizing the story, or filling it with commentary fails. An already complete short utterance or inserted artifact may be preserved without decorative expansion; the layer's contribution is the resulting experience, not extra words.

Can the audience follow who, when, where, what is happening, what a reference means, and what information its chosen access permits? Supply usable orientation when a name, relationship, world fact, or spatial arrangement first matters, without a lecture that stops the action. Across joins, preserve consequential position, direction, gaze, object and bodily state, and the source and temporal relation of sound, or make their deliberate change legible. Distinguish visible and off-screen speech, audience-only narration, screen text, and simultaneous or successive actions where that changes meaning. The camera may show what a character cannot see; that does not grant the character knowledge. Changes of access need legible boundaries, and withholding needs a reason within the declared form rather than conveniently hiding an already visible fact. Deliberate ambiguity or fragmentation still gives the audience a way to distinguish its rule from an accidental omission. Necessary concise connections and summary may serve the selected experience; sustained tension, humor or loss must still receive the time needed to be felt.

## Do selected detail and time earn their effect? {#attention-and-duration}

Do image, sound, performance, speech, silence, and transition allocate attention and time according to the moment's importance, pressure, and intended audience experience? Let important exchanges and sustained moments occur, and compress or omit repetition that adds no experience while preserving required action and results. Mark duration estimates as provisional until actual performance, recorded sound, and playback establish them. Dialogue density, cuts, or a matching word count cannot prove pace.

Choose concrete details from the inherited scene that change action, relationship, atmosphere, understanding, or thematic force; realize emotion through their context before demanding its effect. A static appearance inventory, sensory checklist, generic mood label, unnecessary factual explanation, or after-the-fact interpretation cannot replace the audience's experience. Restrained detail, nonemotional atmosphere, and deliberate gaps work when their cues let the audience experience their function. Inward meaning must reach the audience through performance, image, sound, or an adopted narration or caption form.

Create the performance and sensory emphasis, images, motifs and relationships between image and sound the scene needs; these are choices that make an experience, not categories to fill. Important choices and exchanges fail when replaced by synopsis or displaced by a chain of micro-procedures, even if each detail has a function. Places, objects, appearance, capacities, and other world values arrive through its parents; new realization cannot silently add those facts, close an open relationship, or change a cause or outcome. The distinction is between inherited facts and the experience made from them, not between old and new visible details. If the script leaves no room for this layer, repair the earliest owner instead of manufacturing length.
