# @automovie/human

Procedural anatomical face construction. Components fit to one immutable host, share their skin boundaries and finish internal geometry against the refined surface. The package contains no named person, photograph or fitting runtime. Construction coordinates use millimetres, +Y up, +Z anterior and anatomical left +X; the model boundary converts them to metres.

Optional numerical hair cards and skin-following eyebrow ribbons belong to the same static facial asset.

The eye component under `face/anatomy/eye` separates shape contracts (`IPortraitEyeShape` and its records under `structures`), copied input admission (`resolvePortraitEyeInputs`), transverse tissue formulas (`portraitEyeLidRows`), shared ring attachment (`appendPortraitEyeMargins`), optical shell construction (`buildPortraitEyeCornea` over `buildPortraitCornea`), final skin contact (`createPortraitEyeSurfaceContact`) and refined interior construction (`buildPortraitEye`). `createPortraitEyeComponent` orchestrates their fit/attach/finish lifecycle. Both skin constraints and emitted lid rings call the same section calculation; drawing and contact call the same optical shell builder. Upper lashes (`face/anatomy/lash`) and eyebrow fibres (`face/anatomy/brow`) are built against the refined lid and forehead skin by `buildPortraitEye`. Keep those numerical owners shared when adding a shape control, and test section arithmetic separately from whole-face fitting.

Node consumers require Node 22.23.2 or later. Older Node 22 loaders can expose an unevaluated ES-module dependency when a CommonJS package and an external ES-module glTF reader share the same import graph. Consumer import order must not be used to mask that runtime defect.

## Face documents and editing

### Anatomical parameter contract

`IAutoMovieHumanFaceAnatomicalParameters` is the type-first contract for a future measured editor. Its nested component types name neutral craniofacial, neck, ocular, brow, lash, nasal, cheek-compartment, layered soft-tissue, oral, primary/permanent dental, tongue and auricular measurements; scalp and facial hair biology, optical appearance and performed motion have separate owners. Individually observed jaw and eye motion capacities are distinct from a requested pose, and simultaneous endpoint motion still needs joint validation. Performed expression has measured displacements and separately named FACS appearance categories with ordinal intensity, not artist morph weights. The dental stage and jaw reference agree at type level when dentition is known. Clinical skin-condition grades keep each facial region and its own validated observation scale, including distinct resting and maximum-contraction upper-face lines; they are ordinal observations, not millimetre wrinkle controls. Hair biology includes measured scalp regions and categorical whorls, while numerical parting, gathering, bangs and regional flow compose without authoring individual guides. JSDoc states units, landmark pairs, sides and acquisition limits. Missing values remain unknown. The type admits no personal vertices, free curves, groom guides, card lists or mesh references. Its source references include the [3D Facial Norms measurements](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4841054/) and [3D landmark-definition study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/joa.12407); each component documents its additional primary source.

Lower-eyelid bag contributors distinguish orbital-fat prolapse, orbicularis thickness, skin laxity and transient fluid from an infraorbital hollow. Oral shape distinguishes Cupid's-bow peak geometry, tooth crowns, the palatal vault and contrast-CBCT oral space. The latter protocol requires lower-incisor contact and cannot be supplied for an edentulous jaw. Observed regional skin colour uses calibrated D65/CIE L*a*b* measurements; authored PBR linear RGB albedo has a separate appearance owner. The connected editor still consumes `IAutoMovieHumanFaceBasisDocument`, whose 151 shape coordinates are basis-authored weights. It does not consume anatomical parameters yet. A future resolver must measure matching landmarks on the exact shared basis, establish supported physiological and population domains, solve the coupled inverse without losing independently authored detail, and validate the rendered surface before offering a field as editable. The schema alone does not certify an anatomical range or a likeness.

### Connected facial priors

Connected documents now author skin reflectance numerically. `materials` stores linear RGB and roughness. Optional `skin` maps shared basis surface IDs to compact `IPortraitColourField` arrays: named neutral-space centres and radii in metres, RGB gain and strength. The builder evaluates the common C2 compact-support field on reference vertices and transports its colours through shape, expression and UV/material separation. No individual skin image is loaded. Omitted/null/empty records add no buffer; an empty array gives its surface white multipliers. Fields narrower than the source vertex spacing need additional sampling before fine detail can be claimed. The editor's **Colour and surface** section edits global values through the same transaction and undo history; the **Pigmentation regions** section adds, renames and removes fields and edits centres/radii in millimetres, linear RGB gains and strength. Both sections preserve the numerical document and share undo/redo.

Connected `hair` now accepts `IAutoMovieHumanFaceHair`, whose layers contain numerical growth, length, styling and finish parameters. A basis surface supplies shared `hairDomains` and optional query-only `hairContactClosure`. `createHumanFaceHairBuilder` samples owned neutral area correspondence, seats the same barycentric roots on the current surface, integrates metric curves against a closed signed-distance query and meshes those exact stations. The first curve bend establishes a strip's initial transverse plane; minimal rotations transport it thereafter. Colour and fibre pixels are generated from numerical finish inputs. Generated guides, private bitmaps and personal resource keys are absent from this document schema.

Optional `rootRegion` localizes a layer's roots with six Gaussian coefficients, `center` and positive `spread` triples in neutral metres. Count remains the requested number of accepted strips, with relative density weighted over the shared scalp and hairline. The editor exposes **Localize roots** and each metric coefficient. This supports separately placed populations with independent lengths or curl; `part.region` independently limits the combing field. Rejected candidates retain their original sequence identities so common roots keep their seats and variation after an edit.

Optional `guides` turns a layer into a hierarchy: `fraction` of the roots, chosen by their own sample identity, are integrated through the fields and surface contact as guides. The remaining roots blend their nearest guides on their own side of the part (`interpolateHumanFaceHairStrands`). Each strand grows its own canonical rooted transition before its interpolated remainder is projected. Placement must preserve the post-interpolation metric length and the free-chord bound within the existing rounding allowance; otherwise the same metric walk continues that root. A previous hierarchy measurement built three published subjects 2.2 to 2.5 times faster with an eighth of the roots as guides and no ribbon vertex inside the skin. That timing does not cover the increased fallback that a stricter launch and metric check can require. Optional `clump` in [0, 1] gathers a placed strand toward its nearest guide, nothing at the root and that fraction of the way at the tip. A strand returned to integration uses the authored regional field instead. The playground's **Guide hierarchy** toggle exposes the fraction, neighbour count and clump.

Optional `gather` makes a tied hairstyle from the same numerical layer. An anchor specified by polar and azimuth angles is ray-attached to the shared scalp and follows shape edits through that triangle's barycentric coordinates. Curves descend a current-surface distance field to the authored tie radius, then continue along a tail direction. Optional `tail.spread` gives a target tube radius and a metric transition reach; stable root-sample coordinates distribute the locks through that cross-section. The tie is an event in each curve, so gathered layers integrate every root and refuse a guide fraction under one rather than blending mismatched pre- and post-tie intervals. Unreachable ties and too-short lengths refuse. These controls create no personal strand or guide asset, and the result still needs front, quarter, profile and back review: the shared generator does not infer a hairstyle or accept photographic likeness by itself.

`humanFaceHairEmergence` supplies a unit direction above the scalp tangent plane: 30 degrees in the mid-scalp and 15 at the frontal hairline, with the shared hairline ramp between them. These are the lower ends of the surgeon placement guidance in Shapiro & Shapiro (2013), used as conventional targets rather than population measurements or biological bounds. The direction follows the combed field's tangential part; a field without that part leaves the hair on its normal. Temporal and occipital figures are not modelled separately.

[createHumanFaceHairCurveStart](src/face/anatomy/hair/createHumanFaceHairCurveStart.ts) supplies the initial field direction and the caller's exact metric/contact instance. `integrateHumanFaceHairCurve` preserves that direction on its first discrete chord, then grows a curved rooted transition against the current host before the ordinary free walk. [createHumanFaceHairExteriorInterval](src/face/anatomy/hair/createHumanFaceHairExteriorInterval.ts) certifies each rooted chord on the same closed collider and original sampler support. Root tangency does not require an entire straight ray to reach the free clearance. The derived `freeFrom` index marks the first full-clearance station; all rooted stations are meshed, and simplification starts only in the free remainder. The rooted rows subdivide the existing surface attachment, with their positive width limited by the actual skin gap. This is a ribbon coverage proxy, not a measurement or replacement of shaft diameter or buried follicle anatomy. Free rows keep the existing requested-clearance fit and free chords retain the half-step margin. Stem and free distances both spend the same total metric, while the gathering clock follows their actual cumulative distance. A blocked exterior step, insufficient metric or exhausted count refuses with its cause.

Before a scalp tie is entered, the requested direction remains the normalized ordinary/gather direction blend. Geometric transport uses the actual first-free skin offset as a derived datum and advances its requested normal intent by actual chord metric. Strength continuously corrects only offset residual: zero and already tied tails retain their ordinary path, and flat-surface normal motion is preserved. Rooted trials read current and predicted contacts before admission; a changed contact can supply a two-normal outward goal under the same turn bound. Every actual station still needs its original exterior/floor and source/Float32 coverage proof, without a new authored waypoint, host-support exception or budget.

Direct `integrateHumanFaceHairCurve` callers supply `raycaster`, original sampler `rootBoundary` and a caller-owned `budget` alongside the signed query, all from the same current collider. Returned curves and rooted handoffs carry required boundary metadata; missing or malformed metadata refuses rather than being guessed by the mesher. These are derived numerical context, not new hairstyle document fields. A non-guide attempts its interpolated remainder after the same canonical rooted transition; rejected placement resumes that one walk with its remaining budget rather than starting the root again. The immutable-ray `launchHumanFaceHairCurve` remains a numerical ray query using the same exterior-interval owner, not the anatomical definition of the entire rooted transition. Hair-to-hair contact and physiological validity require separate qualification.

Direct `buildHumanFaceHairMesh` callers also supply the same source and Float32 resident separation readers, each curve's remaining budget and its original sampler attachment. [fitHumanFaceHairRibbonRows](src/face/anatomy/hair/fitHumanFaceHairRibbonRows.ts) qualifies complete transverse rows and swept convex cells in both representations. It keeps the centreline, paired rows, UV stations and nominal density coverage, and fits the actual positive row widths where the whole surface requires it. Outside corners alone do not prove a ribbon interior clear. Root fans require the original canonical seat and bounded contact on its incident supports; every other host face remains subject to strict separation. Non-root stem cells require strict positive separation and free cells retain the requested clearance. Unsupported centre chords, collapsed represented widths and exhausted shared budgets refuse. The emitted positions determine the fitted width; the nominal density width cannot stand in for that measurement.

`limitHumanFaceHairTurn` uses a 6 mm reference radius as a numerical regularizer: the requested direction turns at most `h / 0.006` radians for a nominal metre step `h`. Loussouarn et al. (2007) classified washed, dried 6 cm hairs by two-dimensional curve diameter and other descriptors; the below-1.2 cm class boundary does not establish a minimum local radius or a biological maximum curvature. Contact projection and shortened steps can change the realised polyline, so its actual chords and turns are verified separately from this direction rule.

A ribbon has no authored width. It stands for the whole neighbourhood of one root, so `humanFaceHairDensity` measures the scalp that root is responsible for from the population's own local density: with `d` the distance to a root's fourth neighbour, the density is 3 / (pi d^2) and the ribbon is `d sqrt(pi / 3)` wide, falling back on the area the sampler accepted over the population when a layer is too small to have neighbours. On the published documents that reads 8.1 mm where a fixed 4 mm was authored, and roots thinned by a hairline or a root region widen their ribbons exactly as far as they thinned. The fibre path is no longer paid for the ribbon either: `humanFaceHairContact` keeps half a sampling step and the requested clearance, which lowered the published hair from 4.0 mm to 2.0 mm above the scalp, and `buildHumanFaceHairMesh` instead measures each ribbon's own corners against the same surface and narrows the station where they would enter, both sides together so the ribbon stays centred on its fibre. A half width within the station's own free distance less the requested clearance needs no query, since the nearest surface point is that far away. Thinner hair is a smaller `count` or a lower `finish.coverage`; there is no width to widen until gaps close.

`finish.grey` paints a greying head as the admixture it is. Greying is a follicle's own switch, so a greying head carries white fibres beside pigmented ones rather than one faded colour (Tobin & Paus 2001; Jo et al. 2018), and the largest fibre-level survey classified its 3,343 hairs as pigmented or white against a black-and-white card (Van Neste 2004). The proportion in [0, 1] is how many of a card's painted fibres are left unpigmented; each takes one side of that switch by its own coordinate, and the unpigmented fibre is painted thicker, 67.68 against 57.41 micrometres, as that survey measured. The finish's `color` is then the pigmented fibre's own colour, the base finish becomes the unpigmented one, and the scalp under the hair sees the mixture. A fibre part-pigmented along its length is documented but has no published frequency, so none is invented. Greying runs temples first and the occipital region last, so a head grey only at the temples is a layer of its own with its own `rootRegion`. Omitting the field paints every fibre pigmented, byte for byte as before.

Optional `iris` gives each articulated eye its own pigment: `{ left, right }`, the subject's own eyes, each an `IPortraitIrisPigment` with the constructed portrait eye's band meaning (`base` is the limbal ring, the stroma takes `base + (i/7) * variation` along deterministic radial fibres). The basis eye texture paints sclera, iris and pupil together, so a material colour could only tint the whole eye; `createHumanFaceIrisPigment` instead repaints only the anatomical iris disc. It finds each globe as the textured region fully bound to that eye owner, takes the optical axis from the corneal protrusion over a two-pass sclera sphere fit (`locateHumanFaceIrisDisc`), sets the limbus at asin(11.71 / 24.2) from the adult visible iris and globe diameters and the pupil at a 3.5 mm light-adapted diameter, rasterizes the disc's texels through the neutral surface (`rasterizeHumanFaceIrisTexels`) and paints them (`humanFaceIrisTexelColour`), blending both edges over 0.3 degrees so the sclera keeps its painted detail. The texture is decoded and re-encoded by `decodePng` and `encodePng`; the first pigment costs about 0.7 s on the published basis and a repeated pigment reuses its bytes. Omission or null leaves the texture byte for byte. The editor's **Iris pigment** section adds, edits (both eyes together or one at a time) and removes the field. The pigments are authored colours under the renderer's light, not reflectances recovered from a photograph.

A material override may also carry `pigment` (the fibres' linear RGB albedo, each component in [0,1]) and `density` (a coverage factor in [0,4]) for a material whose embedded texture holds fibre coverage in its alpha, the brow and lash cards cut by a mask or blended. The source cards paint one dark brown for every subject and a colour override can only darken them, so `createHumanFaceFibrePigment` repaints each covered texel as the pigment times its luminance over the median luminance of the texels the card draws, held at one (a darker texel keeps its shade, the source's brighter coverage fringes take the pigment itself), sets the base-colour factor to white, and multiplies coverage by the density, so a factor below one thins fibres under the material's own cutoff. Omission leaves the card byte for byte; an out-of-range value or a material without coverage is refused. The editor's **Brow and lash fibres** section adds, edits and removes both values per card. They are authored colours under the renderer's light, not reflectances recovered from a photograph.

Under a hair document the scalp takes the hair's colour (`createHumanFaceScalpTint`): each growth-domain vertex is covered fully inside the hairline, ramps up across the 1 cm transition zone at it (`humanFaceHairlineCoverage`, the first 0.5 to 1.0 cm of the hairline in the hair restoration literature; the 2 to 3 cm often quoted beside it is the whole frontal hairline region) and follows the layer's root region, and the builder multiplies the skin finish toward the hair colour by that coverage as a vertex colour, clamped so a hair lighter than the skin leaves the skin. Nothing new is stored: the rule reads the hairline, root region and colour every document already has, so skin no longer shows between ribbons of dark hair.

The playground's **Numerical hair** section edits every layer field through the document transaction and history. Mean length scales the six canonical regional lengths by one ratio, preserving their authored differences. Adding a layer selects a resident shared growth domain. Strip count, static combing/lift and procedural fibre coverage are authoring quantities; they do not calibrate biological density or elastic mechanics, and the derived ribbon width states coverage, not a fibre's diameter. The free-strip clearance argument excludes root fans and hair-to-hair contact. Closed, oriented manifold queries do not prove that a deformed head is embedded or physiologically valid.

`createHumanFaceBasisBuilder(basis)` compiles a caller-supplied `IAutoMovieHumanFaceBasis` once and returns a builder for compact `IAutoMovieHumanFaceBasisDocument` edits. `createHumanFaceBasisBuilder(basis, { occlusion: { rays, size } })` also bakes the evaluated face's ambient occlusion (`bakeHumanFaceOcclusion`), the first layer of the surface maps a document's own geometry determines: from every vertex of each opaque material whose meshes all carry UVs, `rays` cosine-weighted directions about its normal are cast from 0.05 mm off the surface to the model's bounding diagonal against every opaque triangle (the engine's `createAutoMovieMeshRayCaster`), and the share that escapes is rasterized over the material's UVs into a `size` square texture (four rings of seam filling) set as its `occlusionTexture`. A renderer applies it to indirect light only, so a lid crease's cleft, the mouth's interior, the nostrils and the ear's concha darken under the sky and fill light as a real head's do, while direct shadows stay the lights' own. The editor bakes it with 32 rays at 1024 texels, about 2.5 to 5 s for a new pose; without the option nothing is baked and the materials stay as they were. GLB export carries the map and its strength.

`createHumanFaceOcclusionCache` retains the baked images for one posed face in a fixed-option builder. The bake precedes hair generation, and document colour, roughness, iris, fibre and hair changes leave its opaque facial positions, normals and UVs untouched; they reuse the same image bytes in an owned map. Shape or expression changes replace the pose and rebake. Hair cards were never occluders in this AO stage, and cache reuse does not add them.

A basis may declare `landmarks` (the source's joint-cube centroids with their own sparse shape rows), an `articulation` (one mandible: pivot landmark plus condylar offset, unit axis, opening degrees with a coupled translation, protrusion, both laterotrusions and a sagittal translation limit; two globes: centre landmark and gaze channels, each an axis, degrees and an eccentric translation) and per-surface sparse `attachments` (`[vertex, weight]` rows per owner, the cranium as the complement). `createHumanFaceBasisBuilder` then evaluates the rest layer on surfaces and landmarks (`evaluateHumanFaceRest`), resolves the joint motions from the shaped landmarks (`resolveHumanFaceArticulation`), poses each attached surface by linear blend skinning (`poseHumanFaceSurface`) and only then reconstructs normals. Expression rows on an articulated basis are rest-space residuals over that motion, which is what the offline preparation measures from the source (`test/scripts/face-review/prepare-articulated-basis.ts`, with `unposeHumanFaceSurface` as the exact inverse). A mandibular arch or globe attached at unit weight is rigid by construction and on the arc at every fraction of opening; a document whose opening and protrusion together exceed the budget is refused by name. `summarizeHumanFaceArticulation` states the requested degrees and millimetres for an editor. Landmarks, weights, angles and translations are measured from the shared source and cited in the study receipt; they establish no clinical trajectory and no contact freedom, which the crossing census measures separately.

### Procedural face documents

`basis.recipe.skinColour` and `detail.skinColour` hold optional named skin-colour regions. `eye.sphereFit` selects how the reference ocular cap is placed. Omission or `"aperture-plane"` keeps the original canthal-plane fit. `"observation-ray"` keeps the reference rim mean's image position while fitting centre depth along the recorded observation ray; tangent canthal support instead fixes that position at the canthal midpoint. `eye.opticalFrame` independently selects `"head-plane"` (the omission default) or `"radial"`. `eye.canthalSupport: "tangent"` separates the declared optical radius from the full aperture width. ### Skin condition and expression folds

`recipe.skin` and `detail.skin` contain optional `IPortraitSkinShape` settings. `laxity` runs from zero (taut underlying shape) to one (full authored resting condition). `wrinkleDepth` and `wrinkleWidth` set persistent crease depth and transverse support in millimetres. Independent `forehead`, `glabella`, `crowFeet`, `lowerLid`, `nasolabial`, `marionette` and `perioral` amounts weight the anatomical families. `cheekSag`, `jowlSag`, `underEyeBag` and `volumeLoss` control separate soft-tissue movements, weighted by laxity. These are visible-shape authoring envelopes, not a biological-age predictor, recovered tissue measurements or an elasticity simulation.

`expressionCreasing` is independent of laxity. Current brow elevation/furrowing, lid closure, smile and pucker drive transient folds, so a taut face can crease while performing without acquiring permanent folds. Both sides share skin settings but follow their own current anatomical attachments and expression. Default laxity and expression creasing are zero; earlier documents retain their shape. `portraitSkinParameters` supplies the defaults, units, bounds and editor meanings, and `resolvePortraitSkinShape` refuses invalid values without clamping.

`createPortraitSkinLayer` samples actual anterior skin depth for curved crease valleys and broad tissue supports. Creases request local surface sampling through `refinePortraitSurfaceSampling`, while broad tissue-only changes retain the existing sampling. Shared tangent-guided midpoint insertion retains original vertices, material ownership and linear open rims before displacement and common normals. Faces incident to exactly coincident free-rim samples keep their common sampling until contact sealing, so local detail cannot split a closed lip's two sides differently. This interpolates the existing curvature; it does not recover unseen anatomy. Fine creases cost additional vertices and construction time. Strong combinations can still fail the engine's orientation checks. The numerical skin region supports scalar editing, whole-profile replacement, reset, history and portable JSON through the same editor transaction as other anatomy.

## Layout: face, body and what they share

The package has three source folders and one dependency direction: `face -> common <- body`. `face/` and `body/` are the two anatomies; `common/` holds what both need (material-region splitting, mesh normals and buffers, the PNG codec, the document text envelope, the glTF document and extensions, the endpoint-scale type) and imports neither. The two anatomies never import each other. `boundaries/element-types` in `lint.config.ts` enforces this at error severity, so a wrong-way import stops the build at its import line. `src/index.ts` re-exports all three folders, and the package exports `./face/*`, `./body/*` and `./common/*` subpaths symmetrically. Each anatomy keeps its static export in its own `export/` folder and its document reading and writing in `document/`; the body's `anatomy/` groups its types by what they declare (`identity`, `generated`, `articulation`, `measurements`) beside the regional folders. A file in `common/` is named for what it does (`areaWeightedNormals`, `float32MeshBuffers`, `createGltfDocument`, `encodePng`), not for the face prototype it came from, and `git mv` keeps its history.

`buildPortraitHead(host, components, rounds, layers, anatomy)` fits all selected components to the same input host, rejects overlapping cut ownership, joins their surfaces, subdivides common skin and resolves final contact. Shape arrays are copied at admission rather than retained as mutable caller state.

`preparePortraitHead` owns the shared surface phase used by `buildPortraitHead`: fitting, attachment, continuation, refinement, surface layers, paired replacements, final proposals and reference colour sampling. Reference coordinates serve colour sampling; they are not a physical tissue rest shape.

`blendPortraitSkin` resolves all component attachments together on the original host connectivity. Exact pins and skin outside geodesic reach form the boundary of a positive inverse-distance graph interpolation. ### Surface hair and eyebrow cost

`recipe.hairLayers` carries up to eight additional source `{ id, profile }` populations; `detail.hairLayers` may copy those exact cards, add a guide-free layer or clear the population while changing numerical styling. Each `IPortraitHairLayer` has a unique nonblank id and a complete `IPortraitHairShape`, so dense inner coverage and fine outer locks can use independent guides, coverage, pigment masks and normals. `buildPortraitHairGroom` assembles these layers with optional legacy hair, and `buildHumanFace` calls it directly. Additional parts use `scalp-hair-layer:<id>` and finishes use `human-hair-layer:<id>`; resident collisions refuse. A layer's base finish must belong to the input palette, never to a preceding generated layer. Empty profiles emit nothing but still validate their settings. The limits bound authoring cost, not biological density.

Select `hairLayers` and a named layer in the editor to adjust that profile through the existing hair scalar controls. `setHumanFaceHairLayerDetail(document, layerId, "hair.coverage", 0.8)` writes one field into a complete additional-population override while preserving the other authored profiles, guides and omitted fields. Applied profiles display normal strength zero, taper start zero and shade strength one when omitted; those display defaults are not written into unrelated layers. The `hair` region still edits the separate legacy owner. Individual layer scalars do not have an inheritance button because array inheritance restores the whole population.

The complete-region JSON editor may retain source guides, add guide-free layers or clear the additional population; it cannot introduce new card guides. Arrays replace completely, `[]` removes only additional layers, and inheritance restores the basis. Appearance controls expose each named resident base finish used by either population; sharing a base intentionally shares those base colours, while the layers' generated masks remain independent. Layer ordering does not change either layer's geometry or finish values. Omitted layers preserve the existing single-profile output.

Numerical setters detach containers along the edited path. Reusing one JavaScript profile object for several layers, sides or the basis does not link their edits: changing or removing a field on one owner retains every other owner's authored values. Shared material identities still intentionally refer to the same resident finish.

`hair.fibreShadeStrength` separates the procedural RGB pattern from the base finish's pigment. Omission or one preserves the original PNG bytes; zero uses white RGB without changing alpha, normals, guides or triangle count. Intermediate values interpolate the encoded RGB pattern towards white, not physical pigment concentration. The hair-region scalar editor and portable document preserve the [0,1] control. `createPortraitHairTexture` accepts the same strength as its fifth argument. Reducing it can retain pale hair colour without filling the gaps between fibres; it does not repair the groom or simulate light scattering.

`recipe.hair` supplies the complete guide-bearing `IPortraitHairShape`; `detail.hair` may vary scalar styling, inherit source cards by omission or clear them. Each `IPortraitHairCard` has root-to-tip guide stations, paired transverse width directions and an authored width in head millimetres. The author places those guides against the current scalp; changing cranial proportions requires inspecting and refitting their attachments. `buildPortraitHairCards` emits two triangles per longitudinal interval regardless of painted fibre count. Width, tip taper, coverage and segments have scalar editor controls; the complete guide array belongs to the immutable source recipe. An empty card array removes the hairstyle, while omission adds none. The supplied finish must exist and the derived `:hair-cards` finish identity must be free.

`createPortraitHairTexture` creates a deterministic 128 by 256 PNG with multiple painted fibres, sparse roots and staggered tips. The builder embeds that mask without a source photograph, network request or native image library. This is a bounded static card renderer, not a physical hair-scattering model, dynamic strand simulation or automatic hairstyle fitter. Inspect the front, sides and back for scalp gaps and card intersections.

`hair.fibreCurl` opts into a 512-square curled-fibre alpha and normal pattern. Supply all three values, for example `{ amplitude: 0.16, cycles: 4.8, aspectRatio: 0.57 }`. Amplitude is the maximum transverse excursion as a fraction of UV width, cycles is the nominal wave count along UV length, and aspect ratio is the nominal card width divided by its length for the normal direction. Seeded fibres vary their phase, amplitude and frequency. These are pattern controls, not measured hair diameters or a physical scattering model. Different card proportions can require different authored profiles; one nominal aspect does not infer every guide's metric. Zero amplitude or zero cycles can describe a straight pattern, but only omission preserves the original mask bytes. `assertPortraitHairFibreCurl` admits complete profiles, including for empty grooms. The hair-region editor saves the complete optional profile and exposes its three scalar controls once present. Pattern edits change rendered coverage and shading without adding triangles or changing the authored guides.

`hair.taperStart` holds a lock's full geometric width until the authored root-to-tip fraction, then narrows it linearly to `tipWidth`. Its [0,0.95] envelope leaves a positive taper interval. Omission or zero preserves the earlier full-length taper. A later start keeps the body of long hair covered without changing the root width, guide, UVs, painted mask or triangle count. The scalar editor and complete hair region both retain it; this control does not repair incorrect guide paths or an unsuitable hairstyle silhouette.

`hair.fibreNormalScale` adds a scalar editor control in [0,1]. `createPortraitHairNormalTexture` derives a linear RGB normal PNG from the same fibre centres and radii as the colour mask. Positive strength replaces the normal binding on the owned card finish; zero or omission retains the base finish's binding unchanged. One uses the full circular cross-section, while intermediate values scale the transverse normal. The texture and strength survive GLB/glTF export. This changes shading without adding triangles or changing the silhouette; it cannot repair scalp gaps or guide intersections.

Use base-colour alpha, not normal RGB, to determine painted coverage. A fibre centre can encode the same nearly flat normal as empty space. Conversely, a softened edge can retain a transverse normal when its small coverage rounds to zero in the 8-bit alpha channel. The normal texture describes orientation, not occupancy.

`createPortraitHairMaterial` supplies the builder's independent card finish and retains an explicitly authored base finish's `alphaCutoff`; omission uses 0.45. Set `alphaMode: "mask"` on that base finish when supplying a cutoff. This threshold changes coverage after texture filtering, not card geometry or the painted fibre population. Inspect the intended viewing distance: fine, sparse tips can disappear and reappear as their projected width changes. A lower threshold is an appearance choice, not a geometric collision repair.

`eye.browProfile.representation: "ribbon"` uses skin-following strips instead of the default eight-sided tubes. At the same longitudinal sampling it uses one eighth of the triangles. Both paths keep the current brow boundary and surface contact, so brow elevation moves the attached hairs. Omission preserves earlier documents.

`eye.browProfile.flow` authors two through 32 medial-to-lateral direction witnesses. Each section has `at`, `lower` and `upper`; each direction gives a `tip` across the supporting brow (lower zero, upper one) and signed `outwardBend` in millimetres. Declare the complete flow profile in the source recipe; eye and independent side edits may retain or clear its array but cannot author new direction witnesses.

Set `eye.browProfile.densitySeed` to an unsigned 32-bit integer, including zero, to thin faded ends independently of root height. Different seeds select different retained strands without changing each strand's dimensions; no random state enters replay. With no endpoint fades the seed changes no geometry. Omission retains the original coupled density pattern for existing numerical documents, including the time-capped study; that pattern can leave root-height bands empty near faded ends. New authored profiles can select the independent pattern explicitly.

The extraction initially retains the `Portrait` symbol names so the existing measured subject and its regression tests replay the same calculations. Subject coordinates and settings live in the test studies, outside this package. A successfully constructed or exported face is not by itself an accepted likeness.

## Whole people

`createHumanPersonBuilder({ face, body })` compiles a connected numerical face basis and body basis, then evaluates an `IAutoMovieHumanPersonDocument`. Both bases use metres, +Y up, +Z anterior and anatomical left +X. The face's skin colour supplies the body's cheek albedo; a person body document carrying its own `skinColour` refuses. Optional `population: "linked"` takes age and sex from the body macros and refuses duplicate face axes. Omitting it keeps the two documents independent, so anatomical compatibility is the caller's responsibility.

The body follows the face's neck cut. `stitchHumanPersonBoundary` subdivides each incident skin triangle onto the union of both boundary partitions, using one face polyline for positions and normals and each material region's own UV and appearance interpolation. It emits no joining ribbon part. A subdivision that reverses an incident triangle refuses, and the ordinary model and Float32 gates remain active. This geometric join does not establish clinical neck motion or admissible head-to-stature proportions. The result preserves face and body part identities under `face:` and `body:` prefixes, carries the body and facial joint frames, and `exportHumanPerson` exports its static resident model.

Two skin surfaces carrying `sourcePartition` use `createHumanPersonSourceNormals` in the `human/build/` subpath. The offline compiler supplies one original parent tree, ordered affine cut table and canonical sample identities to both halves. `validateHumanPersonSourcePartitions` checks domains and complete positive chart coverage; a shared generation label alone certifies neither the source geometry nor anatomy. The builder evaluates the original-parent area-weighted normal field from the current performed cells and interpolates that field through shared cuts or two-coordinate affine `refinements` through `common/basis/interpolateHumanBasisSourceTriangle`, preserving shading across complementary clipping. Optional `parentNormalDomains` split source corner stars, while each vertex's `normalParents` selects actual cell incidence for a contact crease at an unchanged geometric sample; ambiguous unselected incidence refuses. Missing halves, incompatible trees, changed cell populations, disagreeing shared Float32 positions, collapsed or reversed emitted Float32 cells, and undefined used normals refuse. This path cannot quietly discard source-bound cells through an additional runtime cut. Bases with neither record retain the earlier joined-adjacency fairing. Source provenance does not establish supported physiological motion or head-to-stature proportions.

With `normalTransport` on both partitions, `createHumanPersonNormalTransport` consumes that ancestral field and its weighted-key identity from `createHumanPersonSourceNormals`. The person builder obtains the reference from the same face shape and other expressions at omitted, zero `mouthClose`, after final contact. Reference and current then use `createHumanPersonSourceSkin` with the same actual body build and bones, in head-skin, cut and collar order. Every fixed reference/current cell needs actual performed points and a nonsingular frame, including zero displacement. Required stars include unchanged neighboring source cells; zero displacement and unaffected families retain the exact reference field. A transport binding supplies its ancestral parent selector when `normalParents` is absent, and must agree when both are present. Raw bindings retain the existing ordered source chart. The cofactor's transverse extension is the numerical shading convention described by `IAutoMovieHumanBasisNormalTransport`, rather than a tissue law.

Source corner aliases retain their emitted geometry, UV and normal incidence. The seam alone projects actual used vertices through canonical sample identity to one physical topology; unused aliases and merely coincident distinct samples do not supply that identity. Chart coverage reads each actual emitted cell's bindings, independently of reference-cell point admission. The compiled plan exposes `sampleCount` as its safe integer namespace extent and memoized, read-only `chart(sample)` and `preimage(sample)` lookups from copied metadata. Wide sparse or orphan namespaces therefore do not allocate their extent. All geometry and resident-model admission remains active; a closed-contact geometry refusal is not repaired by changing the normal field.

### One-skin source generations

`createHumanPersonGenerationBuilder({ generation })` evaluates the same `IAutoMovieHumanPersonDocument` against one source generation read as one connected skin with a head/body partition (`IAutoMovieHumanPersonGeneration`): the face basis is the head partition view, the body basis is the body partition view, both register `sourcePartition` on the generation's id, and `headSkin` supplies the generation's one weight map on the head cells. This is the adopted person path; `createHumanPersonBuilder` and its runtime seam remain for the published two-basis pair until a generation is published in its place. `createConnectedPersonRuntime` selects this builder when its input carries `headSkin`.

- **Ownership.** The face owns the whole head partition's shape, neck stub included. The body gives the head only a rigid carry by the displacement of the body's eye-joint midpoint, the anchor the face basis is defined around (`humanPersonEyeCentre`, also used by the two-basis builder). Body endpoints that shape the head once over the skin (the source's age, sex, weight and muscle macros, bound part rows and face landmark rows) reach the face view through `drivers`: driver-only face channels whose weight is the body's own gain for that endpoint, applied before expression and articulation.
- **Neck band.** Channels without a counterpart across the cut are continued only on the body side (`band`: face rows and the jaw attachment carried down, body rows returning to the anchor carry at the cut). The face producer evaluates the band through an added, never-emitted view (`createHumanPersonBandFaceView`). There is no cut evaluation, collar conform, boundary subdivision or normal fairing: shared cut samples have one rest value and one skinning row, and `createHumanPersonSourceNormals` evaluates one normal field over both halves. `boundary` reports the largest face- and body-field displacement at the cut.
- **Refusals.** Views not registered on the generation id, a head weight map that is not four influences per head vertex or names an undeclared joint, a missing driver channel, a face document stating an aliased face channel (`aliases`) or a driver channel, unavailable body source targets, and every existing face, body, source-normal and resident-model admission refuse by name.
- **Known gaps.** The jaw attachment reaches the cut, so jaw opening combined with a head turn can still invert a few submental triangles (owners #2736, #2731). Eyes, teeth and tongue are attached rigidly and do not follow a change of head size (#2728, #2738, #2739, #2704). Stature enlarges the head as the source macro does; head-to-stature proportion and the standard viewer people that pair an adult face with other bodies without `population: "linked"` are identity questions (#2704). The two-basis seam path folds submental skin under head turns that its current guards do not detect; it is not the adopted path. None of this establishes clinical neck motion.

The resident development viewer lists `person:connected-reference` and `person:reference:<body state>` alongside the individual domains. These previews use the same person builder and precision boundary as the static export.

## Browser editor and studies

Run `pnpm --filter @automovie/playground dev` from the repository and open `/face.html`. The development command builds the human browser entry before starting Vite. Both face page entries mount the connected-basis editor with committed numerical studies, anatomy-nested fine controls, simple projection, hair/skin/iris fields, expression presets, orbit views and clay; historical procedural guide-array editing is a source-level tool, not a page control. Model construction runs in a worker. Failed geometry keeps the last valid model; history and JSON/GLB/glTF downloads refer to that committed state. A JSON glTF download also needs all its sibling buffer and image files in the same directory.

The [reference studies](../../test/src/subjects/README.md) own photo selection, numerical fits and observation status. Their files are examples, not defaults shipped by this package. Reconstruction remains provisional: single-view depths, posterior anatomy and material choices are estimates; matching landmarks and passing topology checks do not establish photographic identity. Reproducibility should name the runtime and compare both numeric geometry and serialized bytes; separate JavaScript runtimes can differ at floating-point rounding even for the same document.
