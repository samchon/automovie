### Rules every body part follows

1. Each part is one `IAutoMovieHumanViewPart` lofted by `AutoMovieHumanViewLoft.create`, under its existing chain name where the chain survives (`trunk`, `leg.<side>`, `arm.<side>`, `foot.<side>`, `toe.<side>.<n>`, `hand.<side>.1`, `finger.<side>.<name>`, `thumb.<side>`). The left side is canonical and the right is its reflection (J6).
2. Naming map. The kernel names a part's views by its chart axes: `front` holds the extremes along `u`, `side` the extremes along `v`. Every body part takes `u` forward (anterior, or dorsal on the hand and foot) and `v` lateral (+Z on the left side), so the kernel's `front` pair is the anatomical side view (front and back profiles) and its `side` pair is the anatomical front view (lateral and medial outlines). Every table below names the anatomical view.
3. Knots stand at published sites, and each knot's value is an input read there, or a law with a published source or a derivation. Where no breadth or depth is published at a tape site, the box comes from the girth and a width-to-depth law plus a law placing the bone or the axis inside the box. Those laws are the input and laws worker's (owner rule 3 lists the priors they replace); each one this map needs is listed as "law needed" with its candidate source, and none is fitted on f1, f2, m1 or m2.
4. Fullness. Where tapes exist the part's fullness is pre-solved at each tape site by `AutoMovieHumanViewSection.solve` on that station's box, and the part is handed to the operator as `kind: "shoulder"` with those solved shoulders as knots plus a sourced shoulder law at the stations between and beyond the tapes. This keeps one owner kind per part while a trunk or limb has both tapes and untaped stretches. Quarter departures only where a source gives them.
5. Joins by role. The merge traces its curve as a graph over the part's phase (`AutoMovieHumanSolidIntersection` premise), so the part is the solid whose station lines leave the host across the contact: the limb onto the trunk, the leg (shank end) onto the foot, the neck onto the trunk, each digit onto the palm's or forefoot's distal cap, a breast or a gluteal mass along its own outward axis, a pit cutter along its inward axis. The union is symmetric; only the operator's roles follow this rule.
6. Joints inside one bone chain are walk stations, not merges: the knee, the elbow, the wrist, and the interphalangeal joints of fingers and toes. At the neutral pose those segments are within a few degrees of collinear, so the two tubes' surfaces cross nearly tangentially, where the trace's Jacobian `[A_s, -B_y, -B_theta]` is singular; #3048 and #3050 already allow this reduction for the knee and the elbow ("the C2 station rule being its blend"). The part's axis is then two or more straight bone segments joined by a C2 turn over the joint's skin depth (`IAutoMovieHumanViewAxis.IAxis` is an interface, so the jointed axis is the limb's own piece, carrying today's eased-turn law of `AutoMovieHumanLegAxis`). This departs from #3049 and #3051 (phalanx onto phalanx, palm onto forearm); the supervisor is told.
7. Blend widths from the parts' own sizes. A fold between two solids whose outward normals turn by `beta` across the curve (read from both surfaces' jets at each traced point) is the fillet of radius `R` whose tangent points stand `R tan(beta / 2)` from the curve on each side (two faces turning by `beta` touch a circle of radius `R` at that distance from their edge, a derivation). `R` is the soft tissue that rolls into the fold: the subcutaneous layer at the site (`IAutoMovieHumanSubcutaneousFat`, uncompressed layer thickness: `thighFront` at the groin, `thighBack` at the gluteal fold, `upperArmBack` and `chest` at the axilla, `chest` under a breast, `abdomen` at the navel's rim), so a lean person's folds are crisp and shallow and a high-fat person's wide, as `testing.md` requires; for a web between digits `R` is the smaller digit's half breadth at the contact, since the web spans the gap between the two digits' surfaces. Each width is capped at half the gap to a neighbouring merge's host rail (crotch, cleavage, gluteal cleft, webs; `AutoMovieHumanSolidMerge` JSDoc) and at the part's own length to its first section clear of the host.
8. Every part runs into its host far enough that every phase's station line crosses the host inside the part's own layers (`AutoMovieHumanLoftJet.at` clamps outside them): a limb continues to its proximal joint centre and closes there by a pole inside the host; a mass or pit starts inside its host.

### Operator gaps found by this map (to the supervisor for the kernel worker; each blocks the rows named)

1. Merge onto a host that is already a merge. The gluteal fold (thigh against the gluteal mass), the gluteal cleft and the cleavage (a mass against its pair), the malleoli (on the shank-foot blend) each need a curve that crosses from one face of the host (the base, a blend, a merged part) to the next. `AutoMovieHumanSolidJoin` takes one `IAutoMovieHumanSolidSurface` as the host. Needed: a host made of faces with trim loops, the trace switching face where its host point leaves a face's trimmed domain.
2. A part that lies along its host. The trace parametrizes the curve by the part's phase and needs each phase's station line to cross the host once; a groove or a mass lying along its host (the spinal groove, the gastrocnemius heads, vastus medialis, the deltoid heads, the digit creases, nail folds) has station lines that run along the host and cross it twice or never. Needed: the curve traced by another parameter (the angle about the footprint's centre in the host's tangent plane, or arclength), with the rails and blend unchanged.
