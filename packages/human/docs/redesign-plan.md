# Human redesign plan

Plan for [#2993](https://github.com/samchon/automovie/issues/2993), built on [survey.md](survey.md). The skin is composed from anatomical modules whose smoothness follows from their formulas; nothing is smoothed after a fit. It replaces the world-height section stack, its five repair rules and the layer-indexed basis.

It is step 2 of the Human skill's [work order](../../../.agents/skills/human/SKILL.md#work-order): no code changes before it is complete, then section 8 in one pass, then the rounds of section 9 until section 10 holds, each round's findings added here grouped by cause.

## 1. Modules

Every anatomical part is a module with its own frame, parameters, feature curves, construction and fit. A composer joins modules by the rules of section 3. A module owns its shape; a boundary shared by two modules has exactly one owner and both consume it.

| Module | Kind | Frame | Parameters it reads |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cranium and face mass | base | the saved head rows' planes, tilted over the lower face and horizontal above | head length, cephalic index, height, face height and width, minimum frontal width |
| Neck | the cranium and face base's lowest keys, no base of its own | the head base's horizontal keys down to its neck ring | neck circumference |
| Trunk (thorax, abdomen, pelvis) | base | horizontal sections, the saved rows' planes | stature, cervicale, suprasternale and acromial height; chest circumference, breadth and depth; underbust circumference; natural waist circumference, height, breadth and depth; buttock circumference and depth; biacromial, bicristal and hip breadth |
| Arm (upper arm, forearm) | appendage | humerus and forearm axes | radiale-stylion length, span; upper arm, forearm and wrist circumference |
| Hand (palm) and digits | base and appendages | metacarpal and phalanx axes | hand length and breadth, palm length, thumb length |
| Leg (thigh, shank) | appendage | femur and tibia axes | thigh and shank length, ankle height; thigh, calf and ankle circumference |
| Foot and toes | base and appendages | foot axis, toe axes | foot length and breadth |
| Nose | relief on the face | the face base's chart, its axis by section 2's rule | nose width, height, protrusion |
| Lips | relief on the face | the face base's chart, its axis by section 2's rule | mouth width, upper and lower vermilion height |
| Breasts | relief on the thorax | the trunk base's chart, its axis by section 2's rule | chest and underbust circumference, chest depth |
| Buttocks | relief on the pelvis | the trunk base's chart, its axis by section 2's rule | buttock circumference and depth, hip breadth |
| Auricles | relief on the face, the auricle set's choice, pending the step 2 batch (section 8) | the face base's chart, its axis by section 2's rule | ear length, breadth, protrusion |
| Eye openings | trims with lids | globe frame | palpebral fissure length and height, intercanthal width |
| Nostrils | trims with pockets | nostril axis | nose width |

Genitals and nipples stay outside the modelled skin. Every parameter is a measured quantity of the identity (`IAutoMovieHumanBodyIdentity` with ANSUR II's definitions, `IAutoMovieHumanHeadIdentity` with the Farkas landmarks'); the authoring controls and construction supplements with no measurement behind them are removed (section 5). The brow, cheeks and chin are the face base's own shape, fitted to the rows, and change with the face parameters the base reads; they become reliefs only if an inspection round finds a defect only a relief removes. Every frame and region comes from the source: the head base's planes from the saved head rows, the face's regions from the reference head's landmarks, the auricles' from their landmarks, the trunk, limb and digit axes from the reference body's bind skeleton (`body-observations.json`, registered like the meshes). The measurement law acts on these parameters and on module coefficients (section 6).

## 2. Representations

### Base

A base is a family of sections in its own frame along its axis, stored as key sections only (`IAutoMovieHumanFeatureChain`): periodic cubic B-spline sections of N control points at shape events, interpolated over station by the natural cubic spline of each control point (C2, least `integral f''^2`). N is set per module, 64 for the limbs, digits, toes, palm and foot and 96 for the head and the trunk, not 256, the sections' fairness in phase being the thin-plate energy's (section 4); two bases sharing a key (section 3) have one N, since the key is copied whole and a chain has one control count, which the pairs that share keys (the head and the trunk, the palm and the arm, the foot and the leg) already have. Each key carries its plane, an origin and two in-plane axes, and the origins and axes are interpolated over station by the same natural cubic spline as the control points, so the surface `P(s, t) = o(s) + x(s, t) u(s) + z(s, t) v(s)` is C2 in station and, between two keys, a polynomial in station and a B-spline in phase whose first and second derivatives are exact; the axes are not renormalised between keys (the fit sees the same surface the generator evaluates). Every expanded key carries its values' first and second station derivatives, and each interval is the quintic Hermite of its two keys, which reproduces the spline's cubic exactly. A key shared with a neighbouring base belongs to its owner: the other base copies it, and its interval ending there takes the owner's first and second station derivatives at it instead of its own, in its own station's direction (both stations in millimetres), so the join is C2 and the owner is untouched. A closed end (the vertex, a digit tip) is a cap that continues its base's own chain past the base's end key, its station the angle `theta` from the pole axis, as in spherical coordinates, carried onto the base's station by `s = s_e + sigma rho (pi / 2 - theta)` (`s_e` the end key's station, `sigma` the sign that runs the station on past it), so the base's and the cap's keys are one chain, one chart for its reliefs and trims, and their control points one natural spline over station: the cap's keys lie in planes parallel to the end key, at origins `C + rho cos(theta) n` moving with the exact derivatives of that formula (C the end key's origin, n its plane normal pointing out of the base, rho the pole's distance from the end plane), from the end key (`theta = pi / 2`) to the pole (`theta = 0`, every control point at the pole, the chain's end station on the cap's side, its first for a bottom cap and its last for a top one). At the end key the formula's origin moves along n at unit speed per station with no second derivative, as a base's planes do where they reach the end key straight (the trunk's and the crown's horizontal keys, the palm's and a digit's straight last bone), so the planes are C2 there too. A section's size goes as `rho sin(theta)`, which a spline holds, where a height station would need a square-root profile no spline holds, and the origin's zero slope at the pole leaves no cone point there.

### Relief

A relief is a displacement field over a chart of its base: `S(u, v) = B(u, v) + d(u, v) e(u, v)`, with `(u, v)` the base's station and phase restricted to the relief's window, `e` the relief's fixed axis, the direction within 70 degrees of the base's mean normal that maximises the 5th percentile of `n . e` over the feature's observed normals (forward and down for the nose and the breasts), so the whole feature, underside included, is a single height and cannot fold back, and `d` a tensor-product cubic B-spline whose three outer rows of control points are zero (a uniform cubic takes three control points at a knot), so `d` and its first and second derivatives vanish at the window's edge. The sum is C2 wherever the base is. A transformation is an affine map of the window and a gain on `d` (a smaller nose, fuller breasts, a stylised face); the lips', whose window holds the cutaneous lips too, maps the window's station by `u + D(u, v)`, with `D` a tensor-product cubic B-spline over the window, zero with its first two derivatives outside the vermilion borders' chart box and zero on the oral line, set so the borders reach the target vermilion heights (small enough that the map stays monotone in `u`), so the vermilion heights change without the cutaneous lips and the map is C2 across phase as well as station.

### Appendage

An appendage is a family of sections along its own spine (the bone for a limb or digit), stored and interpolated as a base is. Its root boundary meets a trim curve on the base through a strip (section 3). The auricle is no appendage: the auricle set made it a relief (section 8).

### Auricle

The auricle is not a tube: it is a thin flap, front face (concha, antihelix, scapha) and back face, attached to the head along a closed line and curled at its free rim, and a station measured away from the head folds back on itself at the concha. Its representation is decided by one experiment set run on f1 outside the live source before the auricle is built, each variant fitted to the observations listed below and compared offline by its surface distance to the rows, the G0, G1 and G2 residuals of its join and the curvature combs of its sections (renders come in the inspection round, after the choice):

1. a ribbon swept along the rim arc whose sections run from the front attachment round the rim to the back attachment, its two long edges forming the root loop;
2. a smooth shell over the ear's footprint, carrying the folds as a relief;
3. a smooth implicit union of a fitted ear surface with the head.

The set runs once, after the head base's final fit and its region check (section 4), in one process on f1 for both auricles:

- region and root loop: the auricle's region by section 4's rule, from its landmark curves, its boundary in the base chart a periodic cubic B-spline least-squares fit with as many control points as hold one chart cell, printed with its anatomical extent before any variant is fitted;
- observations: every saved point that projects into the region and stands off the base by more than the robust scale either way, the concha lying below the base (the head rows and every `ear-observations` family: the horizontal exterior with its helix, lobe and lower parts, the closed horizontal and the tilted lower), plus one new private family cut through the observation program in the planes normal to the rim arc every 3 mm, the rim arc being the outer helix landmark curve fitted as an open cubic B-spline in space;
- variant 1, ribbon: a clamped tensor-product cubic B-spline surface over (the plane's share of the rim arc, the section's arc-length share from its anterior end), fitted by least squares to the rim-normal sections inside the root loop, each the run of its plane's observed loop through the plane's rim point, with a small second-difference penalty on the control net; its long edges are the sections' ends, where they leave the root loop;
- variant 2, shell and relief: one relief over the base along the axis of section 2's rule, its window the region's chart box grown by its zero rings and its net at 1 mm spacing, finer than section 4's relief spacing of 1.5 mm because the helix and antihelix folds, a few millimetres across, are the finest the head carries, so the smooth shell and the folds lie in one net (a coarse net's space is contained in a fine one's), fitted to the outermost projected observation per chart cell with a 1 mm thin-plate length;
- variant 3, implicit: a signed distance field fitted to the observations thinned to one per 3 mm cell by the biharmonic radial basis (Carr et al. 2001) with off-surface points 1 mm along the observed normals and smoothing of the rows' error, its union with the head base's offset distance by a smooth union of 2 mm blend radius; its distance to the rows is the first-order estimate `|f| / |grad f|`, and its zero set is sampled by Newton steps from the observations for the join and section measures;
- comparison, recorded in [experiments.md](experiments.md) per auricle and variant: the 50th, 90th and 99th percentile of the observed rows' distance to the surface; the largest position, normal angle and normal curvature jump across the root loop (G0, G1, G2 residuals) sampled every 1 mm; the number of sign changes of curvature along each rim-normal section of the surface beyond the observed section's own (curvature below 1/50 mm ignored), so the anatomical folds the rows carry are not counted; and whether the rim's curl is held, the section's self-crossings in its plane against the observed section's;
- choice, stated before the set runs: the variant whose 99th percentile distance is within twice the rows' error (0.23 mm) and whose G1 residual is below 1 degree, among those the one with the fewest excess curvature sign changes; if none meets both, the one with the lowest 99th percentile, and the shortfall is reported in the experiments with its cause before the plan changes; both auricles take one variant, the one whose worse side scores best by this rule, since the two are one module mirrored;
- the join's measure traced, after the first run gave every variant a G1 of 85 to 90 degrees, the relief's included though it has no boundary at the root loop (the [experiments](experiments.md)): the measure pairs each root loop point with the variant's nearest sample however far (G0 4 to 17 mm), so it can compare normals away from any join. One set, run once: per root loop sample, the paired sample's distance and normal angle, binned by distance, showing whether the large angles come from far pairings; then each variant's join measured where its construction meets the base (the ribbon along its long edges against the base there, the relief across its window's edge, where it is C2 by construction, the implicit variant across the edge of its blend, where the union's value turns from the ear's to the head's), and section 2's choice applied to those measures. Its remaining choices (`runAuricleSet.ts`, whose trace was written at 14:27 and ran once at 18:56, section 8): the pairings binned at 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 mm; each join sampled along its construction's edge, the ribbon's two long edges at 241 points each, the relief's window edges every 0.5 mm of station and every 0.5 mm of skin at its 75 mm phase scale, and the implicit blend's edge where the ear's distance lies within 0.1 mm of the 2 mm blend radius, on the base's rings every 0.5 mm of station at 200 phases across the relief's window; at each sample G0 the base's offset, G1 the angle between the variant's and the base's normals, and G2 the difference of their normals' change 1 mm across the edge; the variants refitted by the set's code as it now stands, which lies outside version control, so that the 14:27 edit left its fitting unchanged is not shown, and the relief's library has changed since the first run (its normals, projection and the cross-validation, the last unused at the fixed 1 mm length), so the run's own distances and root loop measures are recorded beside the first run's and any difference is reported, not taken as the same fits; the report written to `.shots/claude-scratch/auricle-trace.json`, beside the first. The set ends there: its choice stands, its shortfall and cause recorded.

### Openings

An opening is a closed trim curve in its module's chart (eye margins on the face base, nostril margins on the nose relief, the oral line on the lips). Inside, the closure (lids on the globe, nostril pocket) is built from the trim curve and the surface's analytic cross derivative there, not from mesh jets.

## 3. Composition

- **Relief:** added to its base before tessellation; no boundary exists.
- **Appendage to base:** the base is trimmed along the root curve `C(t)` (a curve in its chart); the appendage ends at `A(t)`; between them a strip `T(t, w)`, a clamped cubic B-spline in `w` whose control rows are curves in `t`: its knots uniform, the three rows at each edge are exact functions of `t`, set by that edge's position and its surface's first and second cross derivatives at `t` by arc length (the first scaled by the reach of section 4 times the chord between the edges, the second by that length's square), so position, tangent plane and curvature match both surfaces (G2) by construction and not by a fit of the edge, and the interior rows are periodic cubic B-splines in `t`, free and fitted (section 4), since the skin between a trim and its root loop (the shoulder, the axilla, the groin) is centimetres wide and has its own shape. The interior rows are stored as offsets from the quintic Hermite blend of the two edges' position and cross derivatives at each row's Greville abscissa in `w`, in that blend's local frame there (its unit tangent along `t`, its unit normal, and their cross product), so whatever moves an edge (the law, a transformation) carries them. Cross derivatives come from the module surfaces analytically.
- **No contact rings:** a base that carries appendages is closed (the trunk down to the perineum, the palm and the sole at their distal ends), and every appendage roots in a closed loop on it. The axilla is the arm's strip with the trunk under it, the crotch the trunk between the two leg roots, a web the palm between two finger roots, so no junction needs a patch of more than two surfaces and no horizontal contact ring exists anywhere.
- **Tessellation:** each module's chart is sampled adaptively by its own chord tolerance; a base leaves out every grid cell with a corner inside a trim curve, and the hole's edge is zipped to the trim, the strip's base edge, by the share of perimeter walked in the chart; boundaries are sampled once by their owner and shared by index, so the mesh is watertight. A trim that winds once round its base's cap pole (the regions section 4's pole rule bounds, a middle or ring finger's on the palm and toes' on the sole) bounds the chart beyond it toward the pole: its phase is stored as its winding times the loop parameter plus a periodic cubic B-spline remainder, a cell is left out where a corner lies past the trim's station at its phase, that station interpolated between the trim's samples ordered by phase, the pole's fan is not built, the hole's edge is the ring of the left-out cells' vertices round the pole, the zipper walks it and the trim in phase order from their nearest pair, and the strip's side away from the hole is the side away from the pole. Shading normals are the analytic surface normals. The auricle set chose the relief (section 8), so every module has a chart and no tessellation of an implicit surface is needed.

Every join is classified before its evaluator is built ([source-domain admission](../../../.agents/skills/human/reverse-analysis.md#source-domain-admission)):

| Join | Class | Construction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cranium and face base to trunk | regular loft boundary | one shared key section, the head base's horizontal neck ring (1425 mm on f1), owned by the trunk |
| Trunk to arm | appendage root | closed root loop round the shoulder, G2 strip; the axilla is the strip over the trunk |
| Trunk to legs | appendage roots | closed root loops on the closed trunk, G2 strips; the crotch is the trunk between them |
| Forearm to palm, shank to foot | regular loft boundary | one shared key section at the wrist and the ankle, owned by the palm and the foot, the planes turning by section 4's joint rule |
| Palm to digits, foot to toes | appendage roots | closed root loops on the closed palm and sole, G2 strips; the webs are the base between them |
| Face base to auricle | relief, the auricle set's choice, pending the step 2 batch (section 8) | displacement over the face base's chart, no boundary |
| Face base to nose and lips; thorax to breasts; pelvis to buttocks | relief | displacement, no boundary |
| Inframammary fold | crease inside a relief | the breast relief's own shape, curving with anatomy |
| Gluteal fold | relief meeting an appendage root | the buttock relief's lower edge meeting the leg's root strip, which runs in the femur's normal plane, not a horizontal ring |
| Eye margins | opening | trim, lids on the globe |
| Nostrils | opening and inferred closure | trim on the nose relief, pocket from the analytic cross derivative |
| Oral line | crease (closed mouth) | the lips relief's contact line; the interior stays with #2971 |

## 4. Fit

The saved Recovery/Hermite rows stay the primary evidence. Where a module's frame is not the rows' (the auricle across its rim, limbs and digits along their bones) or the rows are too sparse for its features (the trunk's folds), the observation program cuts the reference mesh in the module's planes into an observation file of the same Hermite form, private under `test/.shots/<alias>/`, with its source and plane recorded; the generator never reads it.

An offline fitter per module under `test/src/executable/reference-experiments/human-calibration/` (private, ignored) reads the observations of its region and sets the module's coefficients:

- objective: squared surface distance (Wang, Pottmann and Liu 2006): each surface sample pairs with its nearest observed point in space, and the residual is the offset along the surface normal (tangent-distance minimisation), so any plane of observation and any frame of the module fit alike and rows that graze the skin do not drag it, the surface sampled for the pairing every 0.5 mm of station at 2N phases, each sample counting by the surface area it stands for so dense rows do not outweigh sparse ones, with a tangential term of a twentieth of the normal one's weight holding each sample where it lies along its section, since the normal offset alone leaves the phases free to slide, the surface being the composed chain the generator evaluates (section 2's planes and caps, `HumanModuleFitting.composed`, which every fitter of step 2 of the pass sets, the head base's refit with its cap included, while the head base fitted for the auricle set and the auricle set keep the expansion they were fitted with); plus the thin-plate energy `L^4 integral (D_ss^2 + 2 D_st^2 + D_tt^2)` of the correction D from the stage's starting keys, its station integrals by three-point Gauss quadrature of the keys' cardinal splines and its phase derivatives by differences of the control points over their mean spacing (on the coordinates themselves it counts a closed section's own curvature as bending and shrinks it, run A of the [experiments](experiments.md));
- universal rules, not reproduction (the user's order, section 8): no fit has an error to reach, neither the rows' own error (`0.2 / sqrt(3) = 0.115 mm`, their fitting tolerance 0.2 mm spread evenly, which the head base fitted for the auricle set was fitted to and keeps) nor any tolerance per layer, since a surface chasing every saved row reproduces one specimen's sampling rather than a body's form, and the fairer surface a rule draws shows that form better; the shape is carried by few keys at the sites where the measurements are defined (below), which the measurement law moves (section 6), and each fit's root mean square offset is reported, never targeted;
- smoothing by generalized cross-validation (Craven and Wahba 1979): the final `L` minimises `n RSS / (n - tr A)^2` over the inliers, `RSS` their squared offsets weighted by the biweight (a relief's unweighted, having none), `A` the influence matrix of the last linearised weighted least-squares step and its trace estimated by Hutchinson's randomised estimator (20 Rademacher probes, each one more solve), each length fitted from the stage's starting keys and searched by golden section in `log L` between 1 mm and 40 mm to a hundredth of the bracket (each probe's solve by the fit's own solver: conjugate gradients for a base or an appendage, and for a relief or a strip, whose unknowns enter linearly, the Cholesky factor of its normal equations, `n` there the relief's projected heights or the strip's paired samples); the criterion predicts the offset of observations left out, so the data and not a set number decide the stiffness, and a stiffer surface wins wherever more freedom would only follow the rows' noise;
- keys at the measurement sites, not at even spacing: a base's or an appendage's keys are its end keys, the planes of the joints its axis crosses between them, and the planes through the sites where the identity's measurements of that module are defined (section 1's parameters: on the trunk the suprasternale's, the acromia's, the chest's, the underbust's, the natural waist's, the iliac crests' and the buttocks' levels; on a limb its girths' sites; on the hand and the foot their breadths'; on the head the levels of gnathion, zygion, nasion, glabella, frontotemporale and eurion, where its face height, its widths, its length and its breadth are measured, a pair of points found side by side, the frontotemporalia's, at their mean height), each site found on the specimen by its measurement's definition (the identity measurement program, below), its key at the site's station, on the trunk and the head its height (on the head in the current calibration's plane at that station, tilted over the lower face) and on a limb the arc length of its origin's foot on the axis, a limb's key at a site or placed by the radius rule taking the family plane nearest that station (the planes lie 4 mm apart, a digit's 2 mm, and turn across a joint by the joint rule, which a site's own bone-normal plane would cross), but for the palm's and the foot's end key, the heads' plane, cut as a site plane, its station its origin's foot on the polyline of the family planes' origins, a site whose station falls outside its module's end keys left out with its reason printed, and keys within one plane spacing of each other (a limb's 4 mm, a digit's 2 mm, the trunk family's 2 mm rows, the head rows' 0.5 mm), keys on neighbouring planes included, being one key, an end key's kept before a joint's and a joint's before any other, else the first in station order; and, where two neighbouring keys stand farther apart than the smaller of their sections' mean radii (a section's mean radius, here and in the caps' end-key tests below, its distance from its area centroid averaged over its length, the starting section's: the calibration's on the trunk and the head, the observed loop's on a limb), keys evenly between them, each on the family plane nearest its station on a limb, so that none does, or until no plane farther than one plane spacing from both is left between them, since a tube's shape changes over lengths of its own radius and two keys alone interpolate their interval linearly (the foot's arch between the ankle and the ball, the palm, the skull above eurion), a rule of the body's scale and not of the rows; no key is added where the offset is large; each key starts as the current calibration's section in its plane where the calibration has one (the head, the trunk) and otherwise the observed loop of its plane (the limbs, digits, toes, palm and foot, whose bone-normal planes the world-height calibration lacks); a cap's keys, at equal steps of `theta`, as many as its base's mean key spacing fits in its arc `rho pi / 2` and at least one, start as its end key's section drawn toward the pole by `sin(theta)` (each control point `p + sin(theta) (c - p)`, p the pole's place in the plane, c the end key's point), the cap's own formula, since the sections the calibration or the observations hold in those planes carry what the cap does not (the thighs below the trunk's end key, which the leg roots trim away);
- strips: each appendage's reach (its strip's fullness), one number per strip and so a rule's free parameter rather than a reproduction, is the value minimising the strip's surface distance to the observations over the strip, a one-dimensional search, not a set constant; then the strip's interior rows, spaced across the strip's width at its appendage's mean base key spacing, are fitted to the observations between its edges (the parent's observations inside the root region, which the parent's fit masked, and the appendage family's loops proximal of its first key, which are joined to the parent) by the objective, thin-plate energy and cross-validation above, its edge rows held;
- bases by robust fitting: a base is fitted with Tukey's biweight on the normal residual, its threshold `c = 4.685 s` with `s = 1.4826` times the residuals' median absolute deviation from their median (the standard 95 percent efficiency constant, not a tuned value), from keys smoothed to 4 cycles per section, its end keys held where they are not its own to fit (a key shared with a neighbour that owns it, section 3, as that neighbour fitted it, so owners fit first: the trunk, then the head, the palm and the foot before their limbs; a cap's pole key at its pole; the head base fitted for the auricle set, before its neighbours exist, holds its neck ring and crown as the current sections smoothed to half their control count), a key the base owns fitted with the rest, at the thin-plate length the cross-validation chooses, with its reliefs' windows, its appendages' root regions and its openings' outlines (the eyes, whose observations are the globes and lids) masked, so those regions stay without data and the base runs under them by its thin-plate energy alone (runs D and E of the [experiments](experiments.md) showed a relaxing base climbing onto an unmasked protrusion from its edges);
- regions from the source's own landmarks, not from thresholds: three detection sets showed that no threshold on a stand-off map holds the features (a symmetric robust fit absorbs them, a baseline sinks into the cranium as it stiffens, and an auricle's concha and scapha break it into pieces, the [experiments](experiments.md)). The reference carries the anatomical definitions instead: landmark curves and points on the head (`head-observations.json`: the lid margins and inner eye corners, the medial brow ends, every ear curve and the lobule connection point, the upper and lower outer lip contours, the philtrum, the nostrils, the nasolabial and mentolabial folds) and measurement courses and the bind skeleton on the body (`body-observations.json`: the Underbust, Bust Span, Hip, Waist, Rise and Neck Base courses, every joint). Each landmark point is projected onto its base's starting keys (nearest point of a dense sampling, a k-d tree), so no region waits for the fit it masks, and each region is a closed curve in the base's chart:
  - lips: the perioral region, the convex hull of the upper and lower outer lip contours, both philtrum curves, the mentolabial fold, both nasolabial folds and both mouth corners, less the nose region, since the cutaneous lips run on past the vermilion border to those folds (the first check's misses, the [experiments](experiments.md));
  - nose: the convex hull of the landmarks' nasion estimate (not the identity's measured nasion; the mean of the medial brow ends and the inner eye corners, `pt_browmiddle_ls`, `pt_browmiddle_rs`, `pt_eye_corner_inner_l`, `pt_eye_corner_inner_r`, on the midline between brow and eyes; the skull midline curve does not reach the face's skin), both inner eye corners, the upper ends of both nasolabial folds (the alar creases) and both nostril outlines;
  - each auricle: the convex hull of every ear landmark of its side (the outer and inner helix, the central upper and lower curves, the tragus, antitragus, concha and heel curves and the lobule connection point); the hull, taken about the ear's mean phase, is the ear's footprint seen along the base normal, whose boundary is the root loop; the landmark curves run inside the ear's outline and its rim stands beyond them (the first check's misses), an overhang the user left to the auricle set (section 8);
  - each breast: the disc about the Bust Span course's end on that side (the apex) reaching the Underbust course below it;
  - the buttocks: the posterior trunk chart between the Waist course and the leg root regions, either side of the midline;
