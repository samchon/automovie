# Upper limb

The arm part runs from the mid upper arm cut to the wrist; the deltoid, armpit and the arm's upper half stay in the trunk (`trunk.md`). The hand is its own part (`hand.md`).

The ANSUR arm girths are contracted; a relaxed rest-pose arm must use NHANES mid-upper arm circumference. Per BMI unit the flexed biceps girth gains about 7 mm and the wrist about 1.2 mm (`body-composition.md`), so the arm tapers more steeply as adiposity rises.

## Carrying angle

Kaewpornsawan et al. 2018 (`../sources/kaewpornsawan-2018-carrying-angle.md`) supplies `carryingAngle` per arm: slightly varus at birth, about 6 degrees at 2 to 6 years and 11 from 15, girls 8.5 against boys 6.9. No adult series by sex was read, so adults hold the 16-year value (12.3 women, 10.0 men).

## Superficial muscles

From Gray 1918 and Bridgman (`../sources/gray-1918.md`, `../sources/bridgman-1920.md`), conventional.

| Muscle | Course and surface |
| --- | --- |
| Biceps brachii | From the coracoid (short head) and supraglenoid tubercle (long head) to the radial tuberosity and lacertus fibrosus; spans the front from the anterior axillary fold to the cubital fossa; globular when contracted; its tendon sinks into the cubital fossa, the mass ending in a wedge that turns outward |
| Brachialis | Lower half of the anterior humerus to the ulnar tuberosity; visible on both sides of the lower biceps, more laterally |
| Triceps brachii | Long head from the infraglenoid tubercle, lateral head above and medial head below the radial groove, to the olecranon; long head a longitudinal eminence below the deltoid, flattening into the tendon plane in the lower back of the arm; lateral and medial heads stand out when active |
| Deltoid insertion | Apex sinks into the lateral groove of the arm at mid-length; a depression marks it |
| Brachioradialis with extensor carpi radialis longus | From the lateral supracondylar ridge (upper two thirds and lower third) to the radial styloid and second metacarpal; starts above the elbow between triceps and brachialis, broadest at the lateral epicondyle, tendinous below mid-forearm |
| Extensor group (extensor digitorum, extensor carpi ulnaris) | From the lateral epicondyle; a tapering mass separated from anconeus by a furrow and from the flexors by the ulnar furrow |
| Flexor-pronator group (pronator teres, flexor carpi radialis, palmaris longus, flexor carpi ulnaris) | From the medial epicondyle; a fusiform mass pointed at the epicondyle and tapering to the wrist; pronator teres runs obliquely to mid lateral radius |
| Anconeus | Triangular area lateral to the olecranon, apex at the lateral epicondyle dimple |
| Abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis | An oblique swelling across the lower dorsal radius to the thumb |

## Form rules

- Staggered masses at the elbow: the lateral (brachioradialis and extensor) mass starts higher, above the elbow, and stands higher than the medial (flexor-pronator) mass, which starts at the medial epicondyle; the two converge below to bound the cubital fossa. On the arm the deltoid apex reaches the lateral side at mid-arm while the biceps peak lies lower and in front.
- Interlocking wedges (Bridgman): the forearm overlaps the arm laterally by the brachioradialis wedge that rises a third of the way up the arm and points to the thumb, and medially by the flexor-pronator wedge pointing to the little finger; flexor tendons point to the medial epicondyle, extensor tendons to the lateral one.
- The upper half of the forearm is rounded and muscular, the lower half flattened and tendinous; the ulnar border is a subcutaneous furrow from the olecranon to the ulnar styloid.
- With the elbow straight and the hand turned in, the inner line of the forearm continues the arm's inner line.
- Muscle size grows toward the trunk: forearm muscles are larger than the hand's, arm muscles larger than the forearm's, the shoulder's largest.
- Elbow crease: a fold convex downward from one epicondyle to the other; the joint lies slightly distal to its centre (Gray).

## Open questions

- The longitudinal girth profile (where each girth peaks between elbow and wrist, and between the cut and the elbow) has no measured source; it must follow from muscle placement and be checked against the ANSUR and NHANES girths.
- Relaxed versus contracted biceps girth difference: not read.
- Forearm pronation and supination twist the forearm cross-section; how the radius crossing the ulna changes the surface needs a source.
