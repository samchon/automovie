# Ear

Each value holds for its cited population and protocol. Use the landmarks and measurements as anatomical evidence when judging ear form.

## Parts

From Gray 1918 ([gray-1918](../sources/gray-1918.md)), established account.

- One plate of fibrocartilage under thin adherent skin, ovoid with the larger end up; the lateral surface is concave and faces slightly forward.
- The helix is the curled outer rim. The antihelix runs inside it and divides above into two crura around the fossa triangularis; the scapha is the groove between helix and antihelix.
- The concha is the deep bowl in front of the antihelix, split by the crus of the helix into the cymba above and the cavum below; the external acoustic meatus opens from the cavum.
- The tragus projects over the meatus from the front; the antitragus faces it across the intertragic notch.
- The lobule hangs below the cartilage and has none.

Both sources record greater protrusion at the middle of the auricle than at the top. Inspect the cranioauricular sulcus along the attachment and behind the concha.

## Horizontal sections

Use the upper and lower attachment landmarks to choose the planes. A cut through a free lobule or an overhanging helix can meet the auricle separately from the head. At the attachment, trace how the auricle and scalp outlines meet. Inspect the concha and scapha in close views so the section read preserves the projection of the helix, the openness of the bowl and the notch behind the ear. [Gray's Anatomy](../sources/gray-1918.md).
