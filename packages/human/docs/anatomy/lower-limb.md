# Lower limb

The leg part runs from the mid-thigh cut to the ankle; the gluteal region, groin and upper thigh stay in the trunk (`trunk.md`). The foot is its own part (`foot.md`).

## Girths

ANSUR II means (women / men, mm): thigh at the gluteal furrow 616.1 / 625.1; lower thigh at suprapatella 400.7 / 409.3; calf maximum 373.3 / 392.3; ankle minimum 215.7 / 229.3. Women's thigh girth equals men's in absolute terms at 66 mm less stature (thigh / stature 0.378 against 0.356). Per BMI unit the gluteal-furrow thigh gains about 13.9 mm, lower thigh 6.8 to 8.0, calf 6.1 to 6.2, ankle 2.3 to 2.5 (`body-composition.md`). Calf depth / calf circumference is 0.31 (F) and 0.32 (M) at 15 to 18 years; upper thigh depth / circumference 0.29 and 0.31 (`../sources/snyder-1977.md`), against 1/pi = 0.318 for a circle: the calf is near round, the upper thigh slightly wider than deep.

## Superficial muscles

From Gray 1918 (`../sources/gray-1918.md`) and Bridgman (`../sources/bridgman-1920.md`), conventional.

| Muscle | Course and surface |
| --- | --- |
| Tensor fasciae latae, iliotibial band | Broad elevation below the anterior iliac crest; the band makes a groove down the lateral thigh to the lateral knee |
| Sartorius | From the ASIS across the thigh to the upper medial tibia behind the medial femoral condyle; a ridge when active, otherwise a groove between quadriceps and adductors |
| Rectus femoris | From the anterior inferior iliac spine to the patella, appearing in the angle between sartorius and tensor |
| Vastus lateralis | A long flattened plane, crossed by the iliotibial band groove, high on the lateral thigh |
| Vastus medialis | A prominence in the lower medial thigh, increasing toward the knee and ending abruptly with a full curve; "bulges only in the lower third of the thigh, and overhangs the knee on the inside" (Bridgman) |
| Adductors and gracilis | Fill the upper medial thigh; gracilis smooths the medial outline; adductor longus tendon a ridge from the pubic tubercle when active |
| Hamstrings | Emerge narrow below gluteus maximus and divide into two tendon ridges bounding the popliteal fossa: biceps femoris laterally to the fibular head, semitendinosus (sharp cord) and semimembranosus (rounded) medially |
| Gastrocnemius | Two heads from the femoral condyles; "those of the medial head being thicker and extending lower than those of the lateral"; with the muscle active, the medial border is the more prominent |
| Soleus | Edges show on both sides of gastrocnemius, "of which the lateral is the longer" |
| Calcaneal tendon | Calf mass ends abruptly in it; tapers in its upper three quarters and widens slightly below |
| Tibialis anterior | Fusiform bulge lateral to and beyond the tibial crest; tendon across the front of the ankle to the medial foot |
| Peroneus longus and brevis | Upper lateral leg, between extensor digitorum in front and soleus behind |

## Form rules

- Staggered contour peaks: on the thigh the lateral mass (vastus lateralis, tensor) is high and the medial mass (vastus medialis) low; on the calf the medial gastrocnemius belly is fuller and lower than the lateral, while the lateral soleus edge runs longer; at the ankle the medial malleolus is higher and further forward than the lateral. Inner and outer contours therefore never peak at the same height.
- Tibial crest and medial tibial surface are subcutaneous for most of the leg: the medial shin is flat, the lateral shin muscular.
- The knee "as a whole is bent convex toward its fellow" (Bridgman): a slight inward angle at the knee in the frontal plane.
- Patella: subcutaneous; medial border a little more prominent in extension; in flexion it sinks between the condyles. Fat pads bulge on both sides of the patellar tendon opposite the joint line in extension.
- The popliteal fossa is a diamond between the hamstring tendons above and the gastrocnemius heads below.
- The greater trochanter shows as a depression in standing; the gluteus maximus ends in a hollow behind it.

## Open questions

- The femoral shaft angle in the frontal plane (bicondylar angle) and its sex difference, which set how far the knees come together: no source read.
- Knee valgus angle by sex: no source read; only Bridgman's qualitative rule.
- Calf maximum height and gluteal furrow height for adults: only the 1977 adolescents (15 to 18) were read.
- The ANSUR-derived thigh is 20 mm shorter than de Leva's (see `skeleton.md`).
- No source quantifies the vertical offsets between the vastus medialis and lateralis peaks or between the gastrocnemius heads; the stagger must come from attachment geometry and be checked on renders.
