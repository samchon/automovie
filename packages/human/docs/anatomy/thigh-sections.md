# Shared thigh section correspondence

The shared mid-thigh ring is owned once by both trunk and leg. Its plane is perpendicular to the hip-to-knee axis at the midpoint. Its final periodic cubic curve, rather than its control polygon, is normalized to the existing thigh-girth curve. The prior quarter-axis shape was an offline fit to another synthetic reference cohort. It strongly favors anterior and medial reach, with the same normalized shape for every identity.

On the committed 717abbc7 consumer, muscular female and male sections extend 7.844760 and 3.332686 mm across the midsagittal plane. Their intended ring curves already extend 7.990753 and 3.483050 mm across it. This is principally a source section/stance compatibility issue, not a subdivision convergence defect. No solver tolerance, bridge-only compensation or arbitrary flesh gap can establish injectivity while those rings remain fixed.

Cause isolation keeps the quantities separate. Reference sections are cut perpendicular to each native rig's own hip-to-knee axis at its midpoint; mapped sections use the Human frame. Each shape-only counterfactual normalizes its own measured perimeter to Human's unchanged intended girth and places it on Human's unchanged axis. Exact polyline support yields muscular female/male minimum x of -3.537731/+6.937795 mm for native shapes and -3.438355/+7.014854 mm for mapped shapes. Mapping changes normalized medial reach by less than 0.100 mm in these four gate comparisons. Merely copying the female reference's shape would not solve her compatibility conflict. Changing the native midpoint cut from perpendicular to horizontal changes perimeter by only 0.036895/0.506032/0.307484/1.826380 mm in female-model/male-model/female-muscular/male-muscular order. These plane and mapping sensitivities do not identify a clinical error.

The revised shape is a four-coefficient fit of the actual twelve-span ring producer, including its inverse radial stencil, 64 samples per cubic span for the existing perimeter normalization, and 144 positive radial probes. Calibration uses the four provided meshes already used for the campaign's gate correspondence, two of each sex. Native rig axes and dimensionless own-perimeter radial shapes are used; Human girths or stance angles are not fitted. Fixing the posterior ratio to one removes the common-scale ambiguity. The fitted front/back/medial/lateral ratios are 1.265042/1/1.103695/1.045752. All four leave-one-reference-out radial RMS values improve; all eight other default bodies held out from calibration improve. No mesh coordinates, topology, landmark indices or morph arrays enter Human. Synthetic cohort transfer remains an inference; the references are not clinical ground truth and there is no equally muscular female reference in this set.

The change leaves the girth interpolation, anatomical levels, joint centres, stance, stature, topology and published UV definition unchanged. It corrects a cohort-specific normalized section shape at its shared source. It does not certify final skin girth or global nonintersection, which require fresh native generation, actual consumer sections and whole four-person images with construction.

The measurement posture issue remains explicit. Gordon et al. 2014, NATICK/TR-15/007, section 6.4.79, printed page 155, takes upper-thigh circumference with equal weight on both feet, legs spread until thighs do not touch, tape perpendicular to the long thigh axis, muscles relaxed, and the tape outside the furrow. Section 6.4.61, page 137, takes lower thigh horizontally at suprapatella with feet about 10 cm apart and relaxed knees. The canonical rest stance instead has vertical hip/knee/ankle axes. The references' approximately 2.7 to 3.1 degree hip-to-knee frontal rig inclination is a different posture and is not adopted as anatomy or a new rest constant. A one-section minimum-abduction calculation would ignore bone length, ground support, stature, whole-leg contact and soft-tissue deformation; it is therefore not a justified posture conversion.

Native build and affected checks establish admission only. Visual acceptance still requires the four gate identities in fourteen directions, defect closeups, true generating construction and raking light with fine material normals off. Source-shape calibration, clinical measurement, numerical feasibility and appearance remain separate judgments.
