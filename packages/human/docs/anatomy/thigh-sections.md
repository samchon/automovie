# Shared thigh section correspondence

The shared mid-thigh plane is perpendicular to the hip-to-knee axis at its midpoint. Girth constrains the final periodic cubic curve, not its control polygon. Section shape and stance must be checked together: changing bridge interiors cannot resolve fixed boundaries that cross the midsagittal plane.

## Reference-fit protocol

Four selected synthetic adult bodies, two of each sex, supplied native mid-thigh sections perpendicular to their own rig axes. Radial shape is dimensionless and normalized by each section's own perimeter. The fit evaluates the twelve-span ring producer, inverse radial stencil, 64 samples per cubic span for perimeter normalization and 144 positive radial probes. Fixing posterior ratio to one removes common-scale ambiguity. Fitted front/back/medial/lateral ratios are 1.265042/1/1.103695/1.045752. Grade: synthetic-reference fit, not a clinical norm.

All four leave-one-reference-out radial RMS comparisons and eight other default bodies held out from calibration improved. Human girths and stance angles were not fitted. No equally muscular female reference exists, so transfer to that body remains an inference.

Shape-only comparisons normalize reference perimeters to the same intended Human girth and place them on the same Human axis. Native versus mapped muscular female/male sections gave minimum x of -3.537731/+6.937795 versus -3.438355/+7.014854 mm: changing registration altered normalized medial reach by less than 0.100 mm. Switching the native midpoint cut from perpendicular to horizontal changed perimeter by 0.036895/0.506032/0.307484/1.826380 mm, in slim-woman/slim-man/muscular-woman/muscular-man order. These sensitivity observations do not identify clinical error or establish global nonintersection.

## Measurement posture

[Gordon et al. 2014, NATICK/TR-15/007](https://tools.openlab.psu.edu/publicData/ANSURII-TR15-007.pdf), section 6.4.79, printed page 155, measures upper-thigh circumference with equal weight on both feet, legs spread until thighs do not touch, tape perpendicular to the long thigh axis, muscles relaxed and tape outside the furrow. Section 6.4.61, page 137, measures lower thigh horizontally at suprapatella with feet about 10 cm apart and relaxed knees.

The canonical rest stance has vertical hip/knee/ankle axes. References' approximately 2.7 to 3.1 degree frontal hip-to-knee inclination is another posture, not a new anatomical constant. A one-section minimum-abduction calculation ignores bone length, ground support, stature, whole-leg contact and soft-tissue deformation, so it supplies no justified posture conversion. Source-shape calibration does not establish final displaced-skin girth or appearance.
