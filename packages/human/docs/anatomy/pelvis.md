# Pelvis boundary constraints

The pelvis belongs to the continuous trunk mass and shares its mid-thigh boundaries with the legs. A bridge cannot establish nonintersection when its fixed shared rings already cross the midsagittal plane. Stance, joint axes and section reaches must be checked together; a hidden flesh gap would change the problem. Measurement-posture limitations and the section-fit protocol are in [thigh sections](thigh-sections.md).

Four selected adult reference bodies were compared in matched physical planes. At the authored buttock level, mapped references had midline recesses of 0.098/0.069/0.103/0.078 of their own full section depth; the compared generated surfaces had 0.007/0.005/0.005/0.004. Order: slim woman, slim man, muscular woman, muscular man. Grade: synthetic-reference observations, not clinical norms. The differing depths prohibit treating these fractions as absolute clinical errors or replacing measured depth with a reference's depth.

Paired posterior masses need to preserve physical posterior extrema and distinguish convex-hull tape girth from skin perimeter. Bridge interiors, collar feasibility, gluteal masses, infragluteal transitions and posed contact require separate checks; local triangle-pair counts cannot establish whole-surface injectivity or appearance.
