# Zhuang 2010, NIOSH facial anthropometric differences

Zhuang Z, Landsittel D, Benson S, Roberge R, Shaffer R. "Facial Anthropometric Differences among Gender, Ethnicity, and Age Groups." *Annals of Occupational Hygiene* 54(4):391-402, 2010, doi:10.1093/annhyg/meq007. [CDC Stacks PDF](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/187926/cdc_187926_DS1.pdf), Tables 1 to 4 and text.

## Licence

Journal article with NIOSH authors, published by Oxford University Press.

## Population and protocol

3,997 US civilian workers measured by NIOSH in 2003 with traditional instruments (spreading and sliding calipers, tape, pupillometer): men and women, Caucasian, African-American, Hispanic and "other" (mainly Asian), ages 18 to 66. Table 4 is a linear model: the baseline is a Caucasian male construction worker aged 18 to 29 of average weight (78.7 kg) and height (1762.2 mm); each column is the average change for one factor holding the others fixed. Grade: measured, then population-fitted (regression).

The text states that women are smaller in every dimension, African-Americans have "shorter, wider, and shallower noses", the mainly Asian group has wider faces and noses, and workers 45 and older have "larger and longer narrower faces".

## What Human takes

The size of ancestry and age effects relative to the sex effect with stature and weight held fixed: the female effect on head circumference is only -5.4 mm once stature is fixed, while neck circumference differs by 50 mm; nose breadth carries the largest ancestry effect; face length and nose length grow by about 2 mm after 45.

## Limits

Regression coefficients, not group means; "other" is a mixed group; working-age adults only.
