# Hara et al. 2016, hip joint centre by age group and sex

Hara R., McGinley J., Briggs C., Baker R., Sangeux M. "Predicting the location of the hip joint centres, impact of age group and sex." *Scientific Reports* 6:37707, 2016. ## Population and protocol

CT scans of 157 deceased individuals in Melbourne: children 5 to 11 years (24 male, 13 female), adolescents 16 to 19 and adults 25 to 40 (30 per sex each); ages 12 to 15 and over 40 excluded; no pelvic or limb disorders. One assessor located the femoral head centre (accuracy 1.1 mm). Pelvic frame: origin at the midpoint of the anterior superior iliac spines (ASIS); X posterior-anterior (negative posterior), Y medial-lateral (positive lateral), Z inferior-superior (negative inferior). Leg length LL is ASIS to medial malleolus through the medial femoral epicondyle; IA is the inter-ASIS distance; PD is the distance from the ASIS midpoint to the posterior superior iliac spine (PSIS) midpoint. Grade: measured, then population-fitted.

Sex changed the cross-validated error by at most 0.2 mm, so the authors propose one equation for both sexes; with LL as predictor, age group improved only Y and by tenths of a millimetre. Adolescents and adults differed in no pelvic dimension except IA (effect size 0.03). The authors note the pelvis differs between the sexes, but the hip centre in this frame is not one of the major differences.

## What Human takes

The hip joint centre in the pelvis frame from leg length alone, one equation for both sexes from 5 to 40 years, which suits a generator that already knows leg length. Ages under 5 and over 40 are an extrapolation that needs its own check.
