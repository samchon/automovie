# Ludewig et al. 2009, motion of the shoulder complex

Ludewig P.M., Phadke V., Braman J.P., Hassett D.R., Cieminski C.J., LaPrade R.F. "Motion of the shoulder complex during multiplanar humeral elevation." *Journal of Bone and Joint Surgery (American)* 91(2):378-389, 2009, doi:10.2106/JBJS.G.01483. Read at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2657311/ (methods, Table I, Results text; the per-angle appendix was not read).

## Licence

Open access via PMC. Numbers are facts.

## Population and protocol

Twelve healthy adults (7 men, 5 women, 22 to 41 years). Transcortical pins in the clavicle, scapula and humerus with electromagnetic sensors at 100 Hz; active flexion, coronal-plane abduction and scapular-plane abduction, analysed at 30, 60, 90 and 120 degrees of humerothoracic elevation. Grade: measured.

## Values

At rest (Table I): sternoclavicular retraction 19.2, elevation 5.9; scapulothoracic internal rotation 41.1, upward rotation 5.4, anterior tilt 13.5 degrees. Across the raise (Results, from the 30-degree to the 120-degree position): clavicle retraction +16 (23 to 39), elevation +6 (11 to 17); scapula relative to the clavicle internal rotation +8, upward rotation +11 (5 to 16), posterior tilt +19 (-4 to 15); scapulothoracic upward rotation +39 (11 to 50), posterior tilt +21, internal rotation -2. Scapulohumeral rhythm 2.1:1 in abduction. "Little clavicular elevation occurred during the raising of the arm." The text's starting values disagree with Table I's rest values (retraction 23 against 19.2, elevation 11 against 5.9), because the text starts at 30 degrees.

## What Human takes

The shoulder girdle's motion in the hanging-to-T-pose conversion: the 30-to-120 changes, over 90 degrees of elevation, taken as the change from hanging to 90 degrees.
