# Bridgman 1920, Constructive Anatomy

Bridgman G.B. *Constructive Anatomy*, Bridgman Publishers, Pelham NY, 1920 (ninth printing 1928). Read: the full OCR text of the Cornell copy, https://archive.org/download/cu31924014504371/cu31924014504371_djvu.txt (the drawings were not examined).

## Form rules stated

- "Think in masses, define them in lines." Masses of different proportion wedge into each other; the effective conception is wedging and interlocking.
- The head, chest and pelvis are unchanging blocks; "ideally... balanced symmetrically over each other. But rarely in fact, and in action never"; they bend in the sagittal plane, twist in the horizontal and tilt in the frontal plane, limited by the spine.
- Front of the torso in profile: "the long curve of the front, broken by depressions at the border of the breast muscle and at the umbilicus... into three lesser curves, almost equal in length." Back: "the sharp anterior curve of the waist, opposite the umbilicus, bending into the long posterior curve of the chest, and the shorter curve of the buttocks."
- From behind, the torso is a great wedge, apex down, base at the shoulder corners, apex between the buttocks, buttressed by the waist muscles; the trapezius is a diamond with lower apex below the shoulder blades.
- In bending or turning, the front centre line "bends always to the convex side", paralleled by the rectus borders; the straight front wedge becomes two wedges.
- Arm: the deltoid is a wedge whose apex sinks into the outer groove of the arm half way down; the forearm overlaps the arm on the outside by the supinator (brachioradialis) wedge that rises a third of the way up the arm and points to the thumb, and on the inside by the flexor-pronator wedge pointing to the little finger.
- Muscles grow larger toward the trunk as the levers lengthen: fingers, forearm, arm, shoulder.
- Thigh: vastus internus (medialis) "bulges only in the lower third of the thigh, and overhangs the knee on the inside"; the gluteus medius forms a wedge with apex at the femoral head, overlaid in front by tensor fasciae latae.
- The knee "as a whole is bent convex toward its fellow".
- Foot: the outer side is lower and shorter, the inner side higher; the inner arches are successively higher, forming half of a transverse arch completed by the other foot.
- Hand: wrist width is twice its thickness; "there is always a step-down from the back of the arm, over the wrist, to the hand"; the knuckles arch concentric around the base of the thumb; the middle knuckle is largest and highest; fingers spread radiate from the base of the thumb and their tips lie on a curve centred there; each finger segment steps down to the next, bridged by a wedge; the palmar web reaches the middle of the first phalanx; finger pads on the palm side are of equal length, so creases do not all sit opposite the joints.
- Wrist extension and flexion each almost a right angle, "total movement... slightly less than two right angles"; the fingers cannot close when the wrist is fully flexed.

## What Human takes

The block-and-wedge reading of the figure (rib cage and pelvis as rigid masses that tilt, twist and bend against each other), the profile rhythm of the torso, the overlap wedges of arm and forearm masses, the arch and step-down rules of hand and foot.
