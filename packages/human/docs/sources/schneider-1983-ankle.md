# Schneider et al. 1983, adult ankle sections in a driving posture

Schneider L.W., Robbins D.H., Pflug M.A., Snyder R.G. *Development of Anthropometrically Based Design Specifications for an Advanced Adult Anthropomorphic Dummy Family, Volume 1: Procedures, Summary Findings, and Appendices*. UMTRI-83-53-1, December 1983; DOT HS 806 715. [Primary PDF](https://mreed.umtri.umich.edu/mreed/downloads/anthro/amvo/AMVOvol1.pdf): section 3.8.1, page 73; section 3.8.3, page 77; Table 3.13, page 89; Appendix H-81 to H-83, PDF pages 327 to 329. US government-sponsored report.

## Population and protocol

Twenty-five selected subjects in each small-female, mid-sized-male and large-male group, intended to span ages 18 to 74 and about 10% non-Caucasian subjects per group. Selection was around stature, weight and sitting-height targets, so these are selected size groups, not an unrestricted population sample.

The subjects sit in a contoured hardseat in a relaxed driving posture, feet supported on the toeboard. The ankle landmark lies on the lateral lower leg at the estimated minimum circumference. Breadth and depth use an anthropometer perpendicular to the lower-leg axis at that landmark; girth uses steel tape in the same plane. Grade: measured.

## Limits and use

The same-level breadth and depth establish a section deeper than broad under this supported seated protocol. They do not establish a standing section or its minimum-girth height. An ellipse at those breadths and depths has a perimeter about 6 to 7% below the measured tape girth, so all three cannot be imposed exactly as one ellipse. The summary data do not establish whether that difference comes from section shape, caliper compression, sample composition or posture.

Applying the seated ratio to a standing section lacks evidence from a matching posture.
