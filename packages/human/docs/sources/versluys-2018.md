# Versluys et al. 2017 and 2018, limb proportions and male attractiveness

Versluys T.M.M., Skylark W.J. "The effect of leg-to-body ratio on male attractiveness depends on the ecological validity of the figures." *Royal Society Open Science* 4:170399, 2017 ([PMC5666244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5666244/), CC BY), abstract-level evidence. Versluys T.M.M., Foley R.A., Skylark W.J. "The influence of leg-to-body ratio, arm-to-body ratio and intra-limb ratio on male human attractiveness." *Royal Society Open Science* 5:171790, 2018 ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5990728/), CC BY).

## Population and protocol

Heterosexual US women rated rendered male figures (Daz Studio "Michael 3", 175 cm) whose leg-to-body ratio (LBR), arm-to-body ratio (ABR) and intra-limb ratio (IR) were set from -3 to +3 SD of the 1988 ANSUR male database (about 9,000 US Army personnel). Definitions: leg length = trochanter height minus lateral malleolus height; LBR = leg length / stature; arm length = acromion to radial styloid; IR = lower segment / upper segment (lower leg = popliteal height minus ankle height; forearm = medial epicondyle to styloid; upper arm = acromion to medial epicondyle). Grade: measured preferences on population-defined stimuli.

## Values

| Ratio (ANSUR 1988 men) | Mean | SD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LBR | 0.491 | 0.015 |
| ABR | 0.349 | 0.010 |
| IR, legs | 0.743 | 0.033 |
| IR, arms | 0.817 | 0.032 |

The most attractive LBR was about +0.5 SD above the mean (replicating the 2017 study, where silhouettes peaked fractionally below the mean and detailed renders about 0.5 SD above). ABR had no effect; IR had a weak effect peaking at the mean.

## What Human takes

Leg length slightly above the population mean, by about half a standard deviation, is the measured attractive optimum for men; arm length and limb segment ratios should stay at the population mean. Departures beyond about +-2 SD read as less attractive, which supports narrow admitted ranges for proportions.
