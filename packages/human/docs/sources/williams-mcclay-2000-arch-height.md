# Williams and McClay 2000, measurements used to characterize the foot and the medial longitudinal arch

Williams D.S., McClay I.S. "Measurements used to characterize the foot and the medial longitudinal arch: reliability and validity." *Physical Therapy* 80(9):864-871, 2000. Read from the journal's PDF (Figure 1, methods, Table 2).

## Definitions

Foot length: the most posterior point of the calcaneus to the end of the longest toe. Truncated foot length: the most posterior point of the calcaneus to the centre of the first metatarsophalangeal joint. Dorsum height: floor to the top of the foot at 50 percent of foot length. Navicular height: floor to the most anterior-inferior point of the navicular. Measured with calipers to 1 mm, standing on one foot with 10 and 90 percent of the body's weight on it.

## Table 2, 102 feet (measured)

| Dorsum height, cm | 5.62 (0.44) | 5.23 (0.45) |
| Foot length, cm | 24.20 (1.69) | 24.40 (1.69) |
| Truncated foot length, cm | 17.83 (1.13) | 17.94 (1.14) |

| Dorsum height / truncated foot length (arch height index) | 0.316 (0.027) | 0.292 (0.027) |

Dorsum height over truncated foot length was the most reliable and valid clinical measure across both loads. Truncated over total foot length is 0.735 at 90 percent weight bearing, consistent with ANSUR II's first metatarsal head at 0.739 to 0.741 of foot length.

## Derived bands

Standing (90 percent weight bearing), mean plus and minus 2.326 SD: arch height index 0.229 to 0.355; navicular height over foot length 0.082 to 0.202; dorsum height over foot length 0.137 to 0.291.

## Limits

One clinical sample of adults (sex and population not broken down in Table 2); a flat foot sits at the low end and a cavus foot at the high end of a wider population than 2.326 SD of this sample covers.
