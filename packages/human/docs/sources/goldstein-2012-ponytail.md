# Goldstein, Warren and Ball 2012, the shape of a ponytail

Goldstein R. E., Warren P. B., Ball R. C. "Shape of a ponytail and the statistical physics of hair fiber bundles." *Physical Review Letters* 108(7):078101, 2012; arXiv:1204.0371. The arXiv full text was read for the definitions and values below.

## Population and protocol

Switches of straight European hair about 25 cm long, about 9,500 fibres in one image, photographed hanging under gravity; single fibres characterised by their diameter, Young's modulus and density.

## Values (stated by the authors)

- Fibre diameter `d` = 79 (SD 16) micrometres along its major axis; Young's modulus `E` about 4 GPa; density about 1.3 g/cm^3; linear mass density `lambda` about 65 micrograms per cm.
- Bending modulus `A = E pi d^4 / 64`, about 8e-9 N m^2.
- The elastogravity length `l = (A / (lambda g))^(1/3)`, about 5 cm, "on which gravity bends a hair".
- The Rapunzel number `Ra = L / l`, the fibre length over that length; a ponytail of `Ra` about 5 takes a shape dominated by gravity.

## Use

The hair rules read a strand that stands off the head as a cantilever under its own weight: a free length of hair longer than `l` is bent down by gravity, so straight to wavy hair lying over the vault cannot stand clear of the scalp by more than `l` unless something holds it there.

## Limits

The values are for straight European hair; tightly curled hair (curl classes above II) forms a self-supporting bundle through fibre contacts, which the elastogravity length of a single fibre does not bound. `l` scales with `d^(2/3)` (as `A / lambda` scales with `d^2`), so the 2.326 SD ends of the diameter, 42 and 116 micrometres, give `l` from about 3.3 to 6.5 cm.
