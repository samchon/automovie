## One fat scale

Mecherques-Carini et al. ("Validity and agreement between dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry, anthropometry and bioelectrical impedance in the estimation of fat mass in young adults," *Front Nutr* 11:1421950, 2024, PMC11198126, Table 2) measured 161 men and 104 women aged 18 to 35 on a Hologic Horizon DXA, a Tanita MC-780 segmental bioimpedance analyser and skinfolds. Tanita read men 3.96 points above DXA (17.34% against 13.38%), and the Durnin and Womersley skinfold equation read women 4.27 above it (28.62% against 24.35%). The paper reports the biases only as means and in Bland-Altman figures, so a lean person's bias is not given; as a mean shift or as the ratio of the means, both conversions agree within 1.5 points:

Ralph-Nearman's scale is a consumer Innerscan, not the MC-780, so the man's conversion transfers between two eight-electrode Tanita devices. Both converted values lie at or below adiposity -1 at 20 years (women 20.7%, men 10.0%), the converter's floor of two NHANES standard deviations below the median, so the model pair takes that floor; the bound is not widened. On this one scale the male models (6.8 to 8.3%) are leaner than the sprinters and jumpers (9.2% at the 25th percentile, 11.1% median), and the female models (19.9 to 20.6%) carry about 1.5 to 2 points more than the female athletes (18.4%).

## Muscularity and BMI

Low BMI constrains the lean mass available to the muscular identities. Among NHANES 2017-2018 women aged 18 to 30 (our reading of the public DXX, BMX and DEMO files), the 21 with BMI 16 to 18.5 had an appendicular lean mass index of 4.85 at the median and 5.96 at the 90th percentile, against 6.70 for all women of that age, and those with BMI 21 to 22.6 reached the overall median only at their 90th percentile. Holding even the median woman's fat-free mass index (16.4) at BMI 17.5 needs 6% body fat. That is below the 12.6% DXA that the female fitness competitors of Hulmi et al. (*Front Physiol* 7:689, 2017, PMC5222856, Table 3) reached on stage, at BMI 20.7. Elite female endurance runners in Pettersson's study, at BMI 19.6 and 16.7% fat, held only the median lean mass (ALMI 6.82). The lowest BMI at which measured, non-competing women carry above-median limb muscle at low fat is that of female sprinters and jumpers: 21.3 in Santos's sample and 22.0 in Pettersson's.

Women use the female-athlete BMI reference, where the female athletes carry 18.4% fat and ALMI 7.66 against the model woman's 19.9 to 20.6% at BMI 17.1. Men's sprinters and jumpers sit at the male models' BMI (22.4 against 21.8) with limb lean mass (ALMI 9.21) above the NHANES median man's 8.87, but on one DXA scale they carry more fat than the male models (9.2 to 11.1% against 6.8 to 8.3%): no measured, non-competing men at this BMI are leaner than male fashion models, so the muscular man differs from the model man by lean mass, not by fat.

## Tolerances and resolution

The identity stores body fat and the appendicular lean mass index, not BMI, so the muscular pair is resolved to those two and BMI follows; the model pair, whose lean mass was not measured, is resolved to body fat and BMI.

Tolerances: BMI within 0.05 for the model pair, and body fat at the adiposity floor where the converted value lies below it; ALMI exact and body fat within the sample's interquartile range for the muscular pair; girths within the sample's standard deviation. Both athletes' fat lies beyond adiposity -1, two NHANES standard deviations below the median person at 20 (women 35.1 - 2 × 7.22 = 20.7%, men 23.4 - 2 × 6.68 = 10.0%), so all four take that floor: at 20 years the converter's range cannot separate the pairs by fat, only by limb lean mass (7.66 against 5.58 in women, 9.21 against 8.20 in men). The converter's BMI for the muscular pair reads 2.1 above the athletes' measured BMI: it derives the fat-free mass index from limb lean mass at the median person's ratio (2.43 in women, 2.39 in men), while these athletes carry more of their lean mass in their limbs (17.30 / 7.66 = 2.26 and 19.62 / 9.21 = 2.13). The girths read fat and limb lean mass, so this costs them only the fat mass index the extra BMI implies (0.4 kg/m² in women, 0.2 in men).

The model woman's natural waist and hip resolve 4 mm above and 9 mm below Park's models. On the same DXA scale the converter places Park's own control women (161.2 cm, BMI 20.6, 23 years, 28.5% skinfold fat, 24.2% converted) within 3 mm of their measured natural waist (66.3 cm) and hip (90.5 cm), and from the controls to the models it narrows the waist by 34 mm (measured 41) and the hip by 16 mm (measured 8).
