# ISO 8559-1:2017, landmarks and surface lengths

ISO 8559-1:2017(E), *Size designation of clothes — Part 1: Anthropometric definitions for body measurement*, first edition 2017-03; the same standard as `iso-8559-1-2017.md` and `iso-8559-1-2017-trunk.md`. Read on 2026-11-03: the publisher's sample, https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/61686/d6f1d29d3ae94fd79871997a574038b9/ISO-8559-1-2017.pdf, cover to page 7 (contents, scope, clauses 3.1.1 to 3.1.13 with their figures); the measurement transcription at https://docs.bodygram.com/platform/measurement-definitions. Grade: landmark definitions verbatim; measurement definitions at second hand or by clause title only, as marked.

## Landmarks (verbatim, clause 3.1)

| Clause | Landmark | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1.1 | shoulder point | "most lateral point of the lateral edge of the spine (acromial process) of the scapula, projected vertically to the surface of the skin"; "Identical to acromion in ISO 7250-1" |
| 3.1.6 | back neck point | "tip of the prominent bone at the base of the back of the neck (spinous process of the seventh cervical vertebra) in the mid-sagittal plane, and projected posteriorly to the surface of the skin"; identical to cervicale |
| 3.1.7 | side neck point | "crossing point of the neck base line and the anterior border of the trapezius muscle"; "Side neck points are determined when locating neck base line (3.2.1)" |
| 3.1.8 | front neck point | "crossing point of the line connecting medial superior boarders of the left and right clavicles and front median line" |
| 3.1.10 | elbow point | "most prominent point of the olecranon of ulna" |
| 3.1.11 | bust point | "most anterior point of the bust when wearing bra" |
| 3.1.13 | armpit front fold point | "front fold point of the armpit on arm scye line determined using a scale placed under arm"; "The level is decided as the upper edge of a rule placed under the armpit" |

## Measurements

| Clause | Title (contents, verbatim) | Definition and grade |
| --- | --- | --- |

| 5.2.3 | Bust point width | Clause text not read. It sits in 5.2, "Breadths, widths and depths", measured with calipers (4.2.4), so it is the straight breadth between the bust points |

| 5.4.7 | Across front width | Clause text not read; in 5.4, "Distances measured following the surface of the body", between the armpit front fold points (3.1.13) by its landmarks |
| 5.4.8 | Front neck point to waist | Clause text not read |
| 5.4.10 | Side neck point to bust point | Clause text not read; in 5.4, following the surface, between the landmarks its title names |
| 5.4.14 | Upper arm length (shoulder to elbow, elbow bent) | Transcription: "distance from the shoulder point to the elbow point, right side", elbow bent |
| 5.4.15 | Lower arm length (elbow to wrist – elbow bent) | Clause text not read |
| 5.7.6 | Inside leg length | "vertical distance from the inside leg level to the outer ankle point, right side" (transcription), a calculated measurement |
| 5.3.3 | Neck base girth | "measured over back neck point (seventh cervical vertebra) and the front neck point" (transcription, `iso-8559-1-2017-trunk.md`) |

The standard holds no neck length measurement in its contents (5.1 to 5.7).

## Use

Every surface distance of 5.4 is a tape on the skin; a tape held taut bridges hollows between its landmarks. Since 5.3.3 lays the neck base girth over the back neck point, the neck base line's rearmost point is the back neck point.

## Limits

Clause 4 (posture, tape tension), 3.2 (lines, including the neck base line and arm scye line) and every clause text of 5.2 and 5.4 other than the transcribed ones were not read; the bust point is defined on a breast in a bra, which raises and moves it toward the nipple, while a bare breast's most anterior point lies lower and nearer the midline.
