# NHANES anthropometric reference data, 2015 to 2018

Fryar C.D., Carroll M.D., Gu Q., Afful J., Ogden C.L. *Anthropometric Reference Data for Children and Adults: United States, 2015–2018.* National Center for Health Statistics, Vital Health Stat 3(46), January 2021. [Report](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/100478/cdc_100478_DS1.pdf), methods and Tables 7 to 29.

## Licence

The report and [NHANES public-use microdata](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/) are public domain; cite NCHS. These values are published summary statistics.

## Population and protocol

A multistage probability sample of the civilian noninstitutionalized US population, 18,061 examined persons of all ages, weighted to national estimates; pregnant women excluded from weight and girth tables. Single measurement per person by trained technicians in mobile examination centres. Definitions:

| Measure | Protocol |
| --- | --- |
| Recumbent length | Birth to 47 months, digital infantometer |
| Stature | From 2 years, wall-mounted stadiometer |
| Head circumference | Birth to 6 months |
| Waist circumference | "at the uppermost lateral border of the hip crest (ilium)", the NIH protocol, not the natural waist and not omphalion |
| Upper arm length | Posterior border of the acromion to the tip of the olecranon |
| Mid-upper arm circumference | At the midpoint of upper arm length, relaxed |
| Upper leg length | Seated, inguinal crease to the distal end of the femur, from age 8 |

## Interpretation and limits

The tables are cross-sectional, so age columns mix ageing with birth-cohort differences. Upper arm length (bone between palpable landmarks) stays flat across adult ages while upper leg length falls about 3.5 cm from the twenties to 80+: the leg measure ends at a soft-tissue landmark (inguinal crease), so the fall is not bone shortening and must not drive femur length. The 6 cm stature fall from 30–39 to 80+ in both sexes mixes ageing (disc height loss, kyphosis, see `koele-2020.md`) with taller recent birth cohorts; The cross-sectional fall is not a longitudinal estimate. Mid-upper arm circumference peaks at 40 to 49 and falls about 3.5 cm by 80+, while waist keeps rising to 70 to 79: old age moves mass from limbs to trunk.
