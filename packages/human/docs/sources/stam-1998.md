# Stam 1998, exact evaluation of Catmull-Clark surfaces

Stam J. "Exact Evaluation of Catmull-Clark Subdivision Surfaces at Arbitrary Parameter Values." SIGGRAPH 1998, pp. 395-404. Read from the author's copy at https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/public_user/stam/reality/Research/pdf/cc.pdf.

## Licence

## What it says

- The surface and all its derivatives can be evaluated at any parameter value from eigenbasis functions that depend only on the scheme. On the regular part, where Catmull-Clark surfaces are bicubic B-splines, the eigenbasis equals the power basis. The cost is comparable to a bicubic spline.
- A bicubic B-spline patch is defined by 16 control vertices; a face next to a valence N extraordinary vertex by 2N + 8.
- Assumption: the mesh is subdivided until each face is a quad with at most one extraordinary vertex.
- Curvature near an extraordinary vertex does not exist: Gaussian curvature takes arbitrarily large values nearby and is infinite at the vertex. Catmull-Clark surfaces are therefore in general not C2 there, and a sphere cannot be represented exactly.

## What Human takes

Limit-point and limit-normal evaluation for vertices and semantic landmarks; the patch-support fact that makes a junction's appearance depend on both parts; the reason to keep extraordinary vertices out of broad lit fields (`../techniques/subdivision-and-cage.md`, `../techniques/junction-and-seams.md`).
