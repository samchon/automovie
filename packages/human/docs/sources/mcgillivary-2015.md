# McGillivary 2015 thesis, thigh-calf contact in deep knee flexion

McGillivary T.L. *Relationship between the Magnitude and Location of Thigh-Calf Contact Force in High Flexion and Anthropometric Measures.* MSc thesis, Kinesiology, University of Waterloo, 2015, https://dspacemainprd01.lib.uwaterloo.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/f72b7a91-1ef0-4bac-be6f-80e92f4784ab/content. Read: abstract, literature review on Zelle et al., results sections 4.7 and 5.5. Zelle et al. 2007 and 2009 were not read; their figures below are as this thesis reports them.

## Population and protocol

30 healthy young adults (16 male, 14 female); opto-electronic leg tracking and a pressure-mapping mat between thigh and calf during dorsi-flexed kneeling, plantar-flexed kneeling and plantar-flexed (heels-up) squatting. Grade: measured.

## Values

- Thigh-calf contact started no lower than 126 deg of knee flexion in any man and 128 deg in any woman; men made contact at a lower angle than women on average (p = 0.02).
- Mean maximum flexion reached: 146.5 +- 8.2 deg squatting, 149.4 +- 7.5 deg kneeling; Zelle et al. 2007 reported 151.8 +- 4.4 and 156.4 +- 3.4.
- Maximum contact force at maximum flexion: 1.1 +- 0.6 N/kg squatting, 2.0 +- 0.7 dorsi-flexed kneeling, 2.2 +- 0.9 plantar-flexed kneeling; centre of pressure 13.7 to 14.9 cm from the epicondylar axis along the tibia.
- Starting angle correlated with maximum flexion (R 0.60 to 0.70): later contact allowed deeper flexion. Most girths correlated negatively with both.
- Fitted starting angle: squatting SSA = 151.96 + 5.38 SX + 0.0432 MTC - 1.102 PCC + 11.19 HT; kneeling SKA = 171.87 + 4.15 SX + 0.33 MTC - 1.47 PCC (MTC maximal thigh and PCC proximal calf circumference in cm, HT height; the sex coding is not stated in the sections read). External validation R2 0.71 (RMSE 5.1 deg) and 0.60 (4.6 deg) on 10 held-out participants. PCC alone explained most kneeling variance (partial R2 0.39).
- Zelle et al. 2007, as reported: thigh and calf circumference correlated with the contact starting angle (R -0.54 and -0.79).

## What Human takes

Knee flexion past about 126 deg is limited by thigh-calf soft tissue, not bone, and the onset angle falls as proximal calf girth rises, by about 1.1 to 1.5 deg per cm. Human derives the knee flexion limit from its own thigh and calf cross-sections by collision, using this as the check.
