# ANSUR II handbook, foot and neck datums

Hotzman J et al. *Measurer's Handbook: US Army and Marine Corps Anthropometric Surveys, 2010–2011*. NATICK/TR-11/017, 2011. Approved for public release, unlimited distribution. [Primary handbook](https://mreed.umtri.umich.edu/Anthro/Hotzman_2011_ANSURIII_Measurements_a548497.pdf).

Page numbers below are printed page numbers. The PDF viewer numbers are ten pages higher.

## Foot datums

The Brannock device aligns to the foot's long axis. Foot length, p. 113, projects pternion to the longest toe. Foot breadth, p. 112, takes the transverse support width at the metatarsal heads. The two extremes can occur at different along-foot stations. Ball length, p. 84, locates the first metatarsal head with a pointer. Ball girth, p. 83, separately passes a tape through the first and fifth metatarsophalangeal protrusions.

Heel breadth, p. 126, is the maximum horizontal caliper distance at or posterior to the lateral malleolus. The ordinary procedure locates the protruding heel points just above the table and keeps the fixed blade parallel to the foot's medial side. For a heel without protruding points the protocol takes a reading at the level of the most protruding point of the lateral malleolus. This definition supplies a heel-specific caliper.

Heel-ankle circumference, p. 125, passes through the heel's first table-contact point and the front-ankle dorsal juncture. The photograph and diagram show a diagonal course. A model of taut skin contact can include straight bridges over hollows. Its length is the sum of the contact-arc and bridge lengths, using the specific course under study.

## Neck tapes

Section 5.2.18, p. 40, puts infrathyroid at the inferior midsagittal point of the thyroid cartilage. The measurer palpates down to the gap above the cricoid while the head stays in Frankfurt position. Neck girth, p. 139, passes through that point perpendicular to the neck's long axis. The figure shows the tape higher posteriorly. It gives a qualitative direction. The site still requires the palpable landmark and the axis for that person.

The separate base tape, p. 140, follows the drawn anterior and lateral marks. Section 5.2.29, p. 51, marks the tape's lower edge at the front and sides of the neck. Section 5.2.43, p. 67, finds the trapezius point where its palpated anterior border crosses the lateral neck line. Mark the actual tape or the specified base course in world space and use its full three-dimensional length. Position the posterior landmark using the definition of the base path being measured.

In a model, a positive planar scaling of a template curve with length `G0` reaches tape length `G` at scale `G/G0`. That equation closes the chosen curve length. The plane and anterior point still come from the selected site, and the separate nonplanar base course still requires its own landmark path and contact check.

## Hand calipers

Section 5.2.36, p. 60, defines stylion at the inferior radius and transfers its mark to the dorsal and ventral wrist. Hand length, section 6.4.45, p. 121, runs from stylion to dactylion III. The caliper beam is parallel to the arm. The palm rests on a table, with the distal phalanges 8 mm higher. The fingers touch and digit III aligns with the forearm in extension.

Palm length, section 6.4.66, p. 142, runs from ventral stylion to the centre of digit III's most proximal base crease. The forearm is horizontal and the straight hand faces palm up. The caliper beam is parallel to the forearm axis. Use the skin crease for the distal endpoint.

Hand breadth and circumference, sections 6.4.43–44, pp. 119–120, use the metacarpale II and V landmarks in the hand-length table pose, with the fingers together and thumb away. The caliper reads the distance between the landmarks and the tape passes through both. Forearm girth in section 6.4.39, p. 115, is flexed with a clenched fist. Apply the protocol for the declared input posture.

## Bideltoid source constraint, campaign #3062

Section 6.4.12, p. 88, finds the greatest horizontal skin breadth over the deltoid muscles. The caliper moves vertically to find the maximum and lightly contacts both sides. Read at the maximum point of quiet respiration and exclude the region below the deltoids. Section 6.1.2, p. 74, describes the seated posture with shoulders relaxed, upper arms loosely down, elbows at 90 degrees and straight hands with palms inward.

In a symmetric upper-arm-down model with lateral glenohumeral coordinate `gZ` and deltoid support `r(s)` relative to the joint centre, the deltoid breadth is `2*(gZ+max r(s))`. The shape and position of the deltoid support have to come from the same measuring pose. The handbook procedure allows contact on the finite caliper blades. A same-height section gives the same width only when the two support extrema align at a shared height.

## Head circumference

Section 6.4.47, p. 123, seeks the maximum above the ear attachments, passing just above the brow ridges and around the back of the seated head while compressing the hair. The illustration shows a plane higher anteriorly than posteriorly, without side tilt.
