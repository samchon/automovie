## Definitions and use

The Brannock device aligns to the foot's long axis. Foot breadth, p. 112, is the maximum across support width at the metatarsal heads; its opposite extrema can occur at different along stations. Foot length, p. 113, projects pternion to the longest toe. Ball length, p. 84, uses the first-head pointer; ball girth, p. 83, is a separate tape through heads I/V. Toe splay is excluded from the head landmarks. BODY's independent breadth normalization uses those support extrema, not IBV's oblique ball chord.

Neck girth, p. 139, is infrathyroid and perpendicular to the neck axis. The base tape, p. 140, follows drawn anterior/lateral landmarks; that paragraph does not explicitly name cervicale. Forearm girth, p. 115, is flexed with a clenched fist, so it cannot replace Human's relaxed palm-up definition.

### Tilted-cylinder design and mean-site convention

HEAD's subsequent design chooses the retained mean height y0 as an axis point, not an anterior thyroid skin landmark. The following is a geometric construction, not a published axis/site law or completed source result. In a fixed horizontal X/Z chart, let `C(y)=[x0+m*(y-y0),y,0]`, `h=sqrt(1+m*m)` and R be constant. Horizontal source ellipses with AP half extent R*h and lateral half extent R form a right circular cylinder about C. Its normal-plane course through C(yc) is `P(phi)=C(yc)+R*cos(phi)*[1,-m,0]/h+R*sin(phi)*[0,0,1]`, with ideal real-arithmetic length `2*pi*R`. Its vertical range is `yc +/- R*abs(m)/h`; the whole course must remain inside the constant-cylinder band. Keeping that band below clinical C does not alone preserve the connecting C/Me curves or their jets.

If the site were instead the anterior skin point at height yf, the same plane's centre would be `yc=yf+m*R/h`. With the selected axis-centre convention the front proxy is at `yf=yc-m*R/h`. These are coordinate relations, not a new anatomical height offset. m is dx/dy in equal coordinate units and is dimensionless; R, G and coordinates take the same calibration scale once. At the 10:29 read, HEAD's proposed m is NeckSource.head.backSlope, the secant between phase-pi skin points on its last two retained harmonic rows. Adopting that slope for a new axis is a pose-transfer convention; it is not a measured neck axis or the distinct secant between generating-row means.

The native section owner read at 09:50 gives the ideal ellipse when its four extents are `[R*h,R,R*h,R]`, all shoulders are ELLIPSE and departures vanish at that section: adjacent own curvatures agree, so no G2 end correction is required. Actual source functions, not just knots, must keep R constant and Cx affine across the band. Normalized endpoint jets alone do not make a nonpolynomial composition exact: with angle parameter theta, affine physical height gives a sin-squared Cx that is not quintic when m is nonzero. Physical-station origin coordinates avoid that mismatch. Native admission, binary64 evaluation, final periodic lofting and anatomical tape contact remain separate verification boundaries. The independent named-height base course must use the same emitted source and its full world-space speed, including axis drift and radius/phase changes.

For a fixed named height/phase course, a radius family has convex length if its actual world derivative is `P'(s,lambda)=A(s)+lambda*B(s)` throughout the same domain: integrate the norm of that affine expression. Linear knot/control values alone do not establish this premise after nonlinear section preparation or parameter composition. Axis drift also prevents importing the earlier constant-centre monotonicity argument. A continuous finite sign bracket may select a bisection branch; it proves neither uniqueness nor absence of all positive solutions when that branch fails. A numerical length primitive closes only the declared curve, not the anatomical tape's site/contact.
