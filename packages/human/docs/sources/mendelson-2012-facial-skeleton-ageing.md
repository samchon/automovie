# Mendelson 2012, facial skeleton with ageing

Mendelson B, Wong CH. "Changes in the facial skeleton with aging: implications and clinical applications in facial rejuvenation." *Aesthetic Plastic Surgery* 36(4):753-760, 2012, doi:10.1007/s00266-012-9904-3. Read as PMC3404279 (NCBI efetch).

## Findings

- The facial skeleton expands with age overall (vertical height increases unless teeth are lost), while specific sites resorb.
- Orbit: the aperture enlarges in area and width; the superomedial and inferolateral rims recede most, the inferolateral by middle age and the superomedial in old age. Consequences named: prominent medial fat pad, medial brow elevation, a longer lid-cheek junction.
- Midface: the maxilla retrudes in dentate adults; the maxillary angle (orbital floor to anterior maxilla) fell by about 10 degrees from under 30 to over 60 years (Mendelson et al., CT at the orbital mid-axis). The zygoma resorbs less than the maxilla.
- Piriform aperture: enlarges, with the greatest posterior recession at its lower part, so the alar base moves back relative to the medial canthus and the nasolabial region deepens; the nose lengthens and the tip droops.
- Mandible: a 3D CT study of 120 subjects found no change in bigonial width or ramus breadth, an increased mandibular angle, and decreased ramus height and body height and length; the prejowl region becomes concave and contributes to jowls.
- Growth from infancy (Enlow): the face lengthens, deepens and widens; the glabella protrudes and supraorbital ridges expand.
- Males have more prominent brow ridges, a larger piriform aperture and a larger jaw.

Grade: measured in the cited studies, relayed by a review.
