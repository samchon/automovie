# Attractiveness studies read as abstracts only

Each entry below was read as its abstract through the Europe PMC API; the full texts were not read, so only the abstract's claims are used.

| Study | Population and protocol | Finding used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tovée M.J., Maisey D.S., Emery J.L., Cornelissen P.L. "Visual cues to female physical attractiveness." *Proc. R. Soc. B*, 1999 (doi 10.1098/rspb.1999.0624) | Ratings of images of real women | BMI, not WHR, is the primary determinant of female bodily attractiveness; earlier WHR effects were inflated by covariation with apparent BMI; the perimeter-area ratio of the silhouette is a visible cue to BMI |
| Sell A., Lukazsweski A.W., Townsley M. "Cues of upper body strength account for most of the variance in men's bodily attractiveness." *Proc. R. Soc. B*, 2017 (doi 10.1098/rspb.2017.1819) | Three photograph sets of men's bodies rated for strength or attractiveness | Rated strength explained over 70% of men's bodily attractiveness; with tallness and leanness, 80%; no nonlinear peak, the strongest men were most attractive |
| Brown W.M., Price M.E., Kang J., Pound N., Zhao Y., Yu H. "Fluctuating asymmetry and preferences for sex-typical bodily characteristics." *PNAS*, 2008 (doi 10.1073/pnas.0710420105) | 3D optical scans of 77 people (40 male, 37 female), 360-degree videos of body shape rated by 87 evaluators | Strong negative correlations between fluctuating asymmetry and bodily attractiveness in both sexes; sex-typical size and shape rated attractive and associated with lower asymmetry |
| Kiire S. "Effect of Leg-to-Body Ratio on Body Shape Attractiveness." *Archives of Sexual Behavior*, 2016 (doi 10.1007/s10508-015-0635-9) | 22 3D stimuli rated by 40 women and 40 men in Japan; LBR = crotch height / stature | Inverted U with its peak at the average LBR; higher LBR rated more attractive in female figures, lower in male figures |
| Karastergiou et al. 2012 | see `karastergiou-2012.md` | |
