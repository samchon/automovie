# Ashwell 2012, waist-to-height ratio as a screening tool

Ashwell M., Gunn P., Gibson S. "Waist-to-height ratio is a better screening tool than waist circumference and BMI for adult cardiometabolic risk factors: systematic review and meta-analysis." *Obesity Reviews* 13(3):275-286, 2012. Read at second hand (abstract and summaries); the full text was not read.

## Population and protocol

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 31 studies, about 300,000 adults of several ethnic backgrounds, comparing waist circumference, BMI and waist over stature as screens for diabetes, dyslipidaemia, hypertension, metabolic syndrome and cardiovascular disease.

## Value (derived by the authors)

A waist over stature of 0.5 is the first boundary of raised risk, the same for men and women and across the ethnic groups studied ("keep your waist to less than half your height").

## Use

The catalog's fold rules read a person as lean when waist over stature is under 0.5 and the `fat` control is at or below zero: central adiposity, the source of abdominal, flank and back folds, begins at that boundary.

## Limits

Risk screening, not a measure of skin folds; the waist in the pooled studies was taken at several sites (natural waist, iliac crest, umbilicus), while the person's `waist` input is the natural waist.
