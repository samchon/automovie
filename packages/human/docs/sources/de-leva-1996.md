# de Leva 1996, adjusted Zatsiorsky-Seluyanov segment inertia parameters

de Leva P. "Adjustments to Zatsiorsky-Seluyanov's segment inertia parameters." *Journal of Biomechanics* 29(9):1223-1230, 1996. Read from the scanned copy at https://ebm.ufabc.edu.br/wp-content/uploads/2013/12/Leva-1996.pdf (Tables 1 to 4 and Fig. 1).

## Licence

Journal article, copyright Elsevier. Numeric parameters are facts; Human may cite them as small named constants with this citation.

## Population and protocol

Zatsiorsky et al. (1990a) gamma-ray scanned 100 men (mean age 24) and 15 women (mean age 19), ethnically Russian, mostly physical-education undergraduates. De Leva re-referenced their centre-of-mass positions and radii of gyration from bony landmarks to joint centres, scaling joint-centre offsets from Western survey means. Table 4 reference subjects: women 61.9 kg, 1.735 m; men 73.0 kg, 1.741 m. Grade: measured on young athletic adults, then derived (joint-centre re-referencing).

Table 1 notes that acromion-radiale measured with the arm abducted 90 degrees (235.9 F, 244.8 M mm) is markedly shorter than with the arm adducted, because the acromion moves toward the radiale: a joint centre placed at a fixed offset below the acromion only holds in the adducted rest posture.

## What Human takes

Segment mass fractions, centres of mass and radii of gyration for physics outputs (Mission 3 backlog), scaled by the identity's own segment lengths and mass. Joint-centre offsets from landmarks give a second, independent placement of shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee and ankle centres to check the skeleton against ANSUR landmark heights.

## Limits

Young, lean, athletic Russian sample; 15 women only. Mass fractions do not follow adiposity or age; a heavier abdomen or an infant's large head needs another source (see `anatomy/body-composition.md` and `anatomy/growth.md`). Hip joint vertical offset from trochanterion is small but its mediolateral position is not given here; use the pelvic regressions in `harrington-2007.md`.
