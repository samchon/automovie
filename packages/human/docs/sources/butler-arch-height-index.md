# Butler et al., the arch height index

Butler R.J. et al. "Arch height index measurement system: establishment of reliability and normative values" (PubMed 18347117). Read: the abstract-level record at https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/openlb/publication/OLB-PM-18347117, with the Duke scholars record https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/791022 as a cross-check on the measures compared. The paper itself was not read: its other authors, journal, year and pages are unverified, and only the values below were read. Grade: measured, abstract level.

## Population and value

100 recreational runners. The arch height index averaged 0.340 with a standard deviation of 0.030, and men's and women's indices were similar though men's feet were larger.

## Definition

The index is the dorsal height at half the foot's length divided by the truncated foot length, the heel to the first metatarsal head (the abstract names a measurement system built on these two lengths; the exact landmarks, the footprint of the weight-bearing condition and the instrument were not read).

## Use and limits

The test reads the skin's section height at half the foot's length over 0.74 of the foot length (ANSUR II's ball-of-foot length, 0.739 women and 0.741 men, standing for the truncated length) within 2.326 SD of the mean, 0.270 to 0.410. The population is runners, not a general sample, and the dorsal height there is the skin's highest point of the section, not a ruler's reading at the navicular. Only the index is taken from the paper; its classification bands for low and high arches were not read.
