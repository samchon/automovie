# Gray's Anatomy, 20th edition (1918)

Gray H., Lewis W.H. (ed.) *Anatomy of the Human Body*, 20th ed., Lea & Febiger, 1918. Read at Bartleby (https://www.bartleby.com/lit-hub/anatomy-of-the-human-body/): sections 4.6 to 4.8 on the muscles of the trunk and limbs, and chapter XII on surface anatomy of the thorax, abdomen, upper and lower extremity. The Bartleby rendering drops the first paragraph of several surface-anatomy pages (the bony landmarks of the back and pelvis), so those are taken from `chakraverty-2007.md` instead.

## Protocol

Classical descriptive anatomy from dissection and living surface inspection; no quantities beyond a few centimetre distances. Grade: established anatomical account (conventional for attachments, measured nowhere). Attachments are stable knowledge and agree with modern texts; surface descriptions refer to a lean, muscular adult.

## Surface form statements used by Human

- Pectoralis major overlaps the sternum most at its middle, so the sternal furrow is wide above and below and narrow in the middle; its lower border forms the rounded anterior axillary fold.
- Serratus anterior with the arm by the side shows from the fifth rib down; its digitations interlock with the external oblique in a zigzag line.
- The external and internal obliques form "a thick oblique roll" over the iliac crest that defines the iliac furrow. The linea semilunaris marks the lateral rectus border. Rectus abdominis carries three tendinous inscriptions: at or just below the xiphoid tip, midway to the umbilicus, and at the umbilicus (the last indistinct).
- The umbilicus lies above the body's midpoint, 2 to 2.5 cm above the level of the iliac tubercles in a well-nourished adult.
- The deltoid is "rounded and fuller in front than behind"; its insertion shows as a depression at mid lateral arm. The greater tubercle is the most lateral bony point of the shoulder, beyond the acromion, when the arm hangs.
- Trapezius' lower border runs from the root of the scapular spine to the T12 spine; latissimus' lateral border slants from the iliac crest to the axilla, and with teres major forms the posterior axillary fold.
- Biceps spans from the anterior axillary fold to the elbow; brachialis shows on both sides of its lower part, more laterally. The long head of triceps shows below the deltoid and flattens into the tendon plane in the lower posterior arm.
- At the elbow, the lateral (brachioradialis and extensors) mass starts above the elbow between triceps and brachialis and stands higher than the medial (pronator and flexors) mass, which tapers from the medial epicondyle; together they bound the cubital fossa.
- With the elbow fully flexed, olecranon and both epicondyles form an equilateral triangle; in extension the olecranon tip is level with, and nearer to, the medial epicondyle.
- Tensor fasciae latae makes a broad elevation below the anterior iliac crest; the iliotibial band makes a groove down to the lateral knee. Vastus medialis forms a prominence in the lower medial thigh ending abruptly at the knee; vastus lateralis a long flattened plane.
- Gluteus maximus forms the buttock outline, more prominent behind, ending in a depression behind the greater trochanter; its lower border crosses the gluteal fold obliquely downward and lateralward. The greater trochanter shows as a depression standing (gluteus medius and minimus bulge above it) and as an eminence in flexion and adduction.
- The calf: gastrocnemius borders converge on the calcaneal tendon, "the medial border is the more prominent"; soleus edges show on both sides, "of which the lateral is the longer". Tibialis anterior bulges lateral to and beyond the tibial crest.
- The medial malleolus is higher and further forward than the lateral malleolus. The fibular head is slightly above the level of the tibial tuberosity.
- The knee joint line in extension lies above the apex of the patella.
- The dorsum of the foot is convex, highest along the line of talus head, navicular, second cuneiform and second metatarsal, falling slowly laterally and steeply medially. The navicular tuberosity is 2.5 to 3 cm in front of the medial malleolus.
- Hand: the palmar web reaches the middle of the proximal phalanges, so the fingers look shorter from the palm than from the back. Of the three wrist creases the middle marks the wrist joint. The thenar eminence is larger and rounder than the long narrow hypothenar. The third metacarpal head is the most prominent knuckle.
- Thorax: the angles of the ribs lie on an oblique line diverging downward on each side of the spine; the inferior angle and vertebral border of the scapula are palpable under latissimus and trapezius.

The medial palpebral ligament is about 4 mm long and 2 mm broad; the lacrimal part of orbicularis oculi about 6 by 12 mm. The sternocleidomastoid divides the side of the neck into the anterior triangle (midline, mandible, SCM) and the posterior triangle (SCM, middle third of clavicle, trapezius).

External ear: an ovoid auricle, larger end up, lateral surface concave and directed slightly forward; helix rim with an occasional Darwin tubercle; antihelix parallel inside it, dividing above into two crura around the fossa triangularis; scapha between helix and antihelix; concha divided by the crus of the helix into cymba (upper) and cavum (lower); tragus in front over the meatus, antitragus opposite across the intertragic notch; lobule of areolar and adipose tissue without cartilage. One plate of yellow fibrocartilage; skin thin and adherent. The external acoustic meatus is about 2.5 cm from the bottom of the concha.
