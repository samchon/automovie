# Koelé et al. 2020, age-related hyperkyphosis

Koelé M.C., Lems W.F., Willems H.C. "The Clinical Relevance of Hyperkyphosis: A Narrative Review." *Frontiers in Endocrinology* 11:5, 2020. Read at https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fendo.2020.00005/full (the first 100,000 characters: definitions, epidemiology and causes).

## Licence

Frontiers, CC BY. A narrative review; the values below are its citations of primary studies, which were not read themselves.

## Values (as cited by the review)

| Statement | Value | Cited study |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thoracic Cobb angle, younger adults | 20 to 29 deg on average, rising after the fourth decade | Fon et al. 1980 |
| Adults 65 and over | 35 to 38 deg (35 to 42 deg across cohorts) | Lorbergs 2017, Katzman 2014, Goto 2019, Bartynski 2005 |
| Women 65+ with osteoporosis | 47 rising to 52 deg with age | Ensrud 1997 |
| Progression in older women | +2.6 deg in 3 years; +7 deg in 15 years; +3.9 deg in 4 years | Katzman 2011; Kado 2014 |
| Per vertebral fracture | +3.8 deg | Kado 2013 |
| Proposed hyperkyphosis threshold | Cobb 50 deg or more standing; 40 to 50 deg a pre-stage | the review |
| Prevalence at 50 deg | 20 to 40% of older adults; up to 55% in a geriatric population | several |
| Heritability | 54 to 61% | Stone 2015; Yau 2016 |

Named causes: vertebral fractures (present in at least 40% of hyperkyphosis), disc degeneration with loss of anterior disc height, and back extensor weakness.
