# FAA 1965 and Japanese surveys, shoulder slope

Shoulder slope, the angle of the line from the side neck point to the shoulder point (acromion) below the horizontal in the frontal plane. Read at second hand (search summaries of the reports); the reports' tables were not read.

## Values (measured)

| Source | Sample | Angle | Spread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FAA Office of Aviation Medicine technical report, 1965 (report 65-14) | 55 male air traffic trainees | 67.5 degrees from the body's vertical axis, so 22.5 degrees below horizontal | SD 5.0 degrees |
| Japanese women, silhouette measurement (Hiroshima Bunkyo repository) | adult women | 22.3 (right) and 22.9 (left) degrees below horizontal | not read |
| Japanese women students (same repository) | 337 students | 22.3 (right) and 23.6 (left) degrees | not read |
| Japanese women aged 18 to 20 | | means 19.9 to 22.1 degrees across groups | not read |

## Derived band

The men's mean plus and minus 2.326 times their SD, which also holds the women's means: 10.9 to 34.1 degrees below horizontal.

## Limits

Second-hand values; the method (goniometer or silhouette) moves the reading by a few degrees; the women's spread was not read, so the men's SD stands for both sexes.
