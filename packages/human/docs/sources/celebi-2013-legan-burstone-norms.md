"Comparison of Soft Tissue Cephalometric Norms between Turkish and European-American Adults." *The Scientific World Journal* 2013:806203, 2013, doi:10.1155/2013/806203. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3606791/). Read: Table 1 (the European-American norms of Legan and Burstone, and the pooled Turkish sample), Table 2 (Turkish men and women) and the figure legends defining the measures.

The European-American norms are those of Legan HL, Burstone CJ, "Soft tissue cephalometric analysis for orthognathic surgery," *Journal of Oral Surgery* 38:744-751, 1980. The values below were initially read through Celebi; a later, limited primary reading is recorded separately.

## Population and protocol

96 Turkish adults (48 men, 48 women) aged 20 to 27 with normal occlusion and balanced faces, from the orthodontic department of the Kirikkale dental faculty; lateral cephalograms in natural head position, soft-tissue landmarks traced by one operator. Legan and Burstone's norms come from European-American adults. Grade: measured.

Facial convexity is the angle between the glabella-subnasale and subnasale-pogonion lines, positive for a convex profile; lip protrusion is the lip's distance ahead of the subnasale-pogonion line. The mentolabial sulcus is printed with opposite signs in the two columns, so its convention is not settled by this source.

## Limits

Young adults of two ancestries only; North Indian and Colombian samples are reported elsewhere to be more convex. Cephalometric distances are projections on the lateral film, not 3D distances. The ratios without an SD in the European-American column cannot bound a range from that column alone.

## Primary definition follow-up, campaign #3062

Directly read the 1980 article's landmark definitions, Table 1 OCR and ratio paragraph on printed pp. 744-746 in this [public scan transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/702292016/Burstone-y-Legan-Blandos). Figure images were not opened; OCR and projection notation remain a verification limit.

C is the neck/submental tangent junction; Gn' is constructed between Pg' and Me'. The text describes Sn-Gn'/C-Gn' as distances, supporting straight segments on the lateral film rather than a 3D or explicitly HP-projected ratio. That interpretation is not a new population law. The read definitions impose no C-below-Me' inequality: Me' is the lowest chin point, not the lowest neck point. A height-ordered construction therefore needs its own valid branch domain; these clinical definitions do not justify moving C to satisfy an interpolation branch.

## Later image reading, 2026-11-05

The earlier OCR-only checkpoint above remains historical. The face owner subsequently opened printed pp. 744 and 747 of the same [1980 public preview](https://www.scribd.com/document/702292016/Burstone-y-Legan-Blandos), including Figure 3, and [Celebi 2013 Figure 2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/cms/asset/04edf90d-17a1-4c48-94dd-dccaba0cc1dd/tswj806203-fig-0002-m.png).

The 1980 sample comprised 40 white adults, 20 men and 20 women, aged 20–30, orthodontically untreated with Class I occlusion and selected vertical facial proportions (N–ANS/ANS–Me between 0.75 and 0.85). Its text measures lip protrusion perpendicular to Sn–Pg' at the most prominent lip contour, and sulcus depth perpendicular to Li–Pg'. Page 744 defines Ls/Li at the mucocutaneous borders. A generated border endpoint therefore does not automatically locate the outermost positive lip bulk. The figures also distinguish Stms from Stmi and permit a resting interlabial gap.

These lateral-film norms and FaceBase's three-dimensional border-to-stomion chords have different cohorts, landmarks and measurement conditions. Their means do not establish a personal chord direction or a joint statistical relation. No ratio between those population means, or additional hard 3/2 mm peak constraint, is adopted. An explicit geometric direction convention must remain separate from the public chord and from the authored section relief.
