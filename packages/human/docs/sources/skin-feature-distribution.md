# Skin-feature distribution sources

These sources justify regional skin rules within the populations and protocols below; they do not supply a universal procedural skin preset.

## Visible depressions and follicles

[Flament et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4337418/) examines cheek-visible depressions in 2,585 women ages 18–80, using polarized 35 mm imaging at 25 micrometre resolution and a 0.25 mm detection threshold. Main-cohort densities of roughly 10–80/cm² are counts of visible depressions, not every microscopic follicle. Wider reported depression areas of 0.05–0.37 mm² correspond to circular equivalent diameters about 0.25–0.69 mm, a derived conversion rather than measured circular shape. No depth distribution was found. The enlarged-pore group comprises 840 selected participants from the main cohort plus 120 additionally recruited Brazilian complainants; its 960 participants and wider observations must remain distinct from the unselected main cohort.

[Otberg et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14962084/) uses cyanoacrylate biopsies at seven body sites. The cited abstract supports site-dependent follicle density and opening size, but supplies no numeric whole-body table. [Jönsson et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-02308-9) measures forearm follicles, not cheek depressions, and shows why body size confounds a simple sex-based rule.

## Regional eligibility

[Barrett et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.archoralbio.2004.09.012) studies lower-lip midline tissue from 31 cadavers ages 47–102. Vermilion lacks hair follicles and sweat glands in those samples, while sebaceous structures can occur toward its cutaneous side. Separate vermilion from ordinary appendage-bearing perioral skin; do not claim every vermilion gland is absent or use this elderly sample to calibrate young-lip optics.

[Ridged and non-ridged skin microscopy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6328512/) compares palmar/dorsal hand and plantar/dorsal foot samples from six donors ages 25–70. Palmar and plantar skin need a distinct ridge/eccrine microrelief family. Excluding ordinary follicular acne and pits does not erase sweat structures, normal pigment or every possible lesion.

## Pigment and acne

[Barón et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4257874/) follows 477 white Colorado children ages 6–10, measuring facial freckling categories and exposure/genotype associations. It supports susceptibility and exposure, not dots/cm² or an adult-wide size distribution. Its introductory 1–2 mm description cites earlier work and is not a new cohort measurement. Keep ephelides separate from nevi and solar lentigines.

[Choi et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22176122/) studies 914 acne patients using photographs, lesion counts and Sebumeter readings. Associations depend on lesion class, facial zone and demographics; oiliness is not a deterministic lesion generator. [Dréno et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdv.12757) finds multi-zone involvement in most of 374 adult women attending dermatology visits. Neither a fixed T-zone nor a jawline-only rule follows. Neither study provides a universal count, diameter or elevation prior.

[Yamaguchi et al. 2004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2172049/) compares one palm and four sole samples with matched trunk samples from five Asian adults ages 31–47. Lower melanogenesis and marker-positive melanocyte density justify a distinct palmoplantar pigment field, not the current quarter-melanin multiplier. Cell number, melanosome volume fraction, pigment concentration and rendered RGB are different quantities.

## Modeling boundary

Regional intensity, physical size, colour and height are independent quantities. Missing depths, density laws, transition widths and age-driven correlation scales remain conventions. Clinical sample rates and country labels cannot determine an individual's appearance.
