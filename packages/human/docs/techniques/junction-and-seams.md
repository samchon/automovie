# Junctions and seams

Joining cages before subdivision makes a shared ring an interior region of one limit surface. This avoids independently smoothed boundary normals. It does not establish anatomical flow, absence of folds or injectivity.

Both incident parts consume the same ring count, orientation, named anatomical start direction and skeleton frame. Equal-count compatible rings can share topology; count changes need their own transition. Silent resampling would change correspondence. UV cuts remain face-varying texture boundaries rather than physical cuts.

[Stam's limit evaluation](../sources/stam-1998.md) shows that the surface near a cut depends on neighboring cage controls from both parts, so a part's isolated boundary appearance cannot establish the joined shape. [OpenSubdiv](../sources/opensubdiv-documentation.md) explains extraordinary-vertex transitions and their curvature effects; placing a transition away from a broad lit field reduces visible waviness without proving anatomical correctness.

## Branch patches

A branch region such as the axilla, groin, finger web or neck-shoulder needs one shared boundary per joined ring plus a cross-boundary derivative, the ribbon. Ruled or bilinearly blended bridges carry no derivative contract, so a crease or flat at their boundary is expected. Grade: inference.

Bicubically blended Coons patches need compatible twists at their corners. Gregory's rational blending removes that constraint (Gregory, "Smooth interpolation without twist constraints", in *Computer Aided Geometric Design*, Barnhill and Riesenfeld eds., Academic Press, 1974, pp. 71-87, not read), and the [C2 Gregory patch of Miura and Wang](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/egtp19911036) allows unrestricted cross-boundary first derivatives (abstract read). Transfinite n-sided patches interpolate a ribbon on every side with G1 continuity over convex polygonal domains (Várady, Rockwood and Salvi, CAD 43(11):1330-1340, 2011; Salvi, Várady and Rockwood, "Ribbon-based transfinite surfaces", CAGD 31(9):613-630, 2014; [composite ribbon precursor](https://diglib.eg.org/items/3539b73d-7c1a-4a27-8ff4-df6839bc64b0)); a concave domain needs a later variant. Only the precursor's abstract was read.

[Gabrielides et al. 2007](../sources/gabrielides-2007-branching-sections.md) build G1 branching surfaces from parallel cross sections, the situation of level trunk sections splitting into two thighs: cross tangents are estimated, the one-to-many case is skinned, trimmed and filled with G1 Gordon-Coons patches inside a C1 surrounding curve, and symmetric data stays symmetric.

The options trade exactness against the single cage. Gregory or ribbon patches give exact G1 to the joined rings but lie outside the subdivision cage, so sampling them into it reintroduces the [ring retention losses](subdivision-and-cage.md#feature-retention-through-a-ring). Curve-network subdivision keeps one cage with the curves exact but changes the rules near the curves ([Schaefer, Warren and Zorin](../sources/schaefer-2004-lofting-subdivision.md)). A plain cage with [poles placed by curvature sign](subdivision-and-cage.md#extraordinary-vertices-and-curvature-sign) and an interior faired to vanishing Laplacian of mean curvature (Schneider and Kobbelt, CAGD 18(4):359-379, 2001, not read) interpolates no curve exactly, and its cost is unmeasured. Isophote continuity across the bridge boundary under at least two light directions and the Gaussian curvature sign at each pole judge the result; see [surface interrogation](surface-interrogation.md). Grade: inference.
