# Hair cards

Cards can be generated directly from a semantic scalp when no strand groom is available. Roots need stable surface attachment; deterministic guide formulas can supply length, direction, sag and curl. Opacity textures approximate bundles rather than individual physical fibres.

[Scheuermann's hair rendering](../sources/scheuermann-2004-hair.md) uses layered polygon patches, opacity, tangent-based highlights and ordered transparency passes. [Unreal's card generator](../sources/unreal-hair-card-generator.md) groups strands into cards, adapts segmentation to curvature and packs texture islands. They support the card representation, not a claim that Human implements their full shading or ordering.

Root-to-tip widths, curl radius, pitch and scalp clearance need physical lengths; segmentation approximates those curves rather than defining their dimensions. Head weights suffice for short attached hair. Long hair reaching the neck or shoulders needs a different deformation premise than a rigid head attachment.

## Depth order and shading

Cards read as one hair mass only when their depth order follows the groom. Human gives every root nearly the same clearance and grows it with the length already grown, so a strand lies over the strands whose roots it passed; a random clearance of several millimetres per card breaks the mass into stepped planks with their own highlights. Side-by-side cards keep a sub-millimetre jitter so they never share a depth.

Shading normals come from the mass, not from each card: the outward normal of a smooth envelope over the draped head, and halfway to the body normal where hair rests on the body. The envelope over the head is a kernel power mean of the draped radii (spherical Gaussians 0.15 radian wide, order 16); a positive kernel cannot ring, whereas the earlier least-squares fit of a sum of Gaussians turned the normals up to 14 degrees either way at the Gaussians' spacing and broke the back and top into grey blotches. Below the head the envelope is an analytic vertical column. Lying cuts hang it from each azimuth's lowest draped point; a hanging cut (the bob) hangs it from the widest one, as a curtain falls, because following the head back in under the occiput, the ears and the jaw turned the normals down and shaded the hair black there. Roots start a layer above the envelope where it rises above the skin beside the auricle, since a root on the skin climbed that rise on its first step and stood off the head. A hanging strand also keeps out of the draped head itself, because the power mean runs up to 8 mm under the drape at the auricle, whose rim showed through the curtain. Lying hair ends where the drape, not the envelope, rises more than 5 mm over the skin: the envelope bridges the concave nape under the occiput by more than that, and strands tested on it ended there and bared the undercoat down the nape. The cards' highlight is isotropic and as wide as the lobe allows (roughness 1, keratin's index 1.55): a fibre's highlight is spread over every azimuth about its axis, and at roughness 0.38 the isotropic lobe gathered that spread into one round white spot on the smooth mass normals, which read as shiny bare scalp on f1's top and side; the narrow band along the strands needs an anisotropic lobe on a tangent continuous across cards. An anisotropic lobe stretches each highlight across the strands along the card's own heading, which turns a little from card to card; at strength 0.6 it switched the highlight on and off card by card and broke the lit side of every cut into grey planks. In a controlled render on f1, removing only that lobe removed the planks, while flattening the per-card shade or the cards' edge lean did not. Per-card leans of a few hundredths of a radian still separate locks; leans of tenths give every card its own highlight band. Curl waves share a phase that varies smoothly over the scalp, so neighbouring cards wave together instead of crossing.

## Ends

Ends need the same physical size on every card. The strand image reserves a root band, where strands start at scattered places and fade in, and a tip band, where strands end at uneven places, narrow and converge into clump points. Each card maps them onto its first 8 mm and last 30 mm (at most two fifths and a fifth of its length), and the card's width narrows by a further fifth over the tip. Each root's length also varies by up to 6 percent either way in locks and 2 percent per root, so ends never align along one cut, including a bob's level cut.

## Body contact

Hair below the head meets a height field of the body: the highest skin crossing of each vertical line, from every chain below the head and from the head chains' triangles below the menton, since those chains also loft the neck and its base over the shoulders. A strand reaching it settles at a clearance above it and slides down the surface under gravity without climbing; at steep places, including the step where the field drops from one surface to a lower one, it moves out horizontally instead of being lifted. A bob lays along the body the drop it still had to its level cut, at most 10 mm, so its back and sides do not slide out over the shoulders. A height field is single-valued, so an overhang or a surface facing down is not represented, and there is no collision between strands.

## Hairline

The boundary is assembled from zones built on the canonical side and reflected, each placed from the head's landmarks rather than from shares of the head: the frontal line from the trichion at the Farkas forehead height from the nasion (`docs/anatomy/hair.md`) to its apex on the exocanthion's sagittal plane, the temple down to the sideburn, the margin over the ear, the nape. Shares of the eye-to-vertex height put the hairline where the vault put it, not where the face does: m1's trichion stood 12 mm too high and m2's 7 mm too low, and a head change moved the hairline with the vertex. A hair-bearing boundary interpolated over azimuth and mirrored at the front and back midlines needs zero slope there, or the mirror folds it into a cusp, a notch at the parting.

The hairline is soft only when the cards there end in fine hair, and it reads as a hairline only when the hair is seen from the boundary on. Roots in the transition zone, 10 mm over the boundary, grow shorter cards the closer they lie, and their bundles and cards hold down to the zone's share of the full density (55 of 152.8 hairs per cm2) at the edge. Each card widens from a third over its root band, since square card roots of hair combed back at an angle line up as a saw edge. The root band is 8 mm at the boundary, where a short one compressed the scattered roots into a row of teeth, and 2 mm inside the mass: an 8 mm root band on every card made every strand start 8 mm from its root, which bared a 10 mm strip along f1's parting and a 15 mm spot at f2's whorl. Strands shorter than 4 mm get no card, since such a card shows neither band and reads as a dark fleck on the forehead.

Stacking an 8 mm feather, an 8 mm root band on every card and an opaque undercoat that started 16 mm inside the boundary left a band of bare forehead skin 10 to 16 mm wide along the whole hairline (27 to 67 percent covered in an orthographic front view): with the hairline already high, that band was most of the balding look. The undercoat is now a scalp layer whose edge is the boundary itself, blended by a coverage image: its alpha is the share of scalp real hair hides there, 1 - exp(-tau), tau accumulated from the guides lying within 20 mm of the skin, each standing for its root's share of the hairs spread over its card's width (`AutoMovieHumanHairScalpLayer`). It is opaque under the mass and fades to the skin at the hairline, a parting and a whorl as hair would, so it has no edge to show; the earlier opaque shell's edge had to be hidden 16 mm inside the boundary, since at 3 mm it drew a hard comb and at 6 mm dark flecks between the root bands. Short hair that meets the auricle's drape ends above the ear instead of stepping out and standing off the side of the head, and a bob is combed away from its parting along the coronal circle through each root (lateral beside the line, down at the sides), so the hair beside the parting runs along the frontal line and down over the temples. A backward part added to that comb lifted the front hair over the vault and bared a wedge of hair-bearing forehead under the bell's dome; it had been added to stop strands falling across the face, which came from blending the comb with a downward fall over the front of the head, and the two conventions hid each other. A crop's whorl turns its flow only near the whorl: a swirl at a constant share of the outward flow turned the temples' hair as much as the crown's and bared one temple, so the crop points straight away from its whorl.

## Representation and cost

Measured 2026-11-02 in Chrome, ANGLE D3D11 on an AMD Radeon 8060S, 1200 x 1200, WebGL2 with `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`, one person (f1, bob) unless stated, every variant from the same 1,698 guides. GPU milliseconds are the hair drawn alone at close, medium and distant views (camera 0.75, 2.6 and 8 m away with a 30 degree field, a 220 mm head about 660, 190 and 60 pixels high).

The whole frame took 19, 27 and 37 GPU ms at those views, of which the body's 400 to 930 draw calls (one per skin part) are nearly all; four people took 45 to 160 ms with the hair at 0.7 to 1.4 ms of it. One shadow-casting light raised the cards' own cost to 3.7 / 1.3 / 0.67 ms and doubled the draw calls, and broke the opaque planks' tone (mean difference 14-24) by self-shadowing. The strand image decodes to a 256 x 512 RGBA8 texture with mipmaps, 699,052 bytes.

## Limits

Card geometry and a procedural strand image alone establish neither attachment nor acceptable coverage and appearance. Anisotropy and double-sided material channels alone do not establish convincing hair or correct transparent fringes.
