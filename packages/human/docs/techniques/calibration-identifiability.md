## Identifiability

For a scalar shape coefficient regressed on independent dimensions, four complete identity rows provide at most four design directions including an intercept, or three centred variation directions. A four-column full-rank design is saturated: zero residual degrees of freedom does not establish predictive accuracy. Adding an interaction cannot create another independent identity direction. Holding one identity out leaves at most three training directions. Missing observations reduce these limits further.

This limit concerns between-identity dimension effects. Many sections can still constrain a shared geometric basis within a fixed identity, and shared station structure can reduce the unknowns. Each candidate must therefore state its measured anchors, shared geometric coefficients, dimension-response coefficients, constraints, spans and source-dependent quantities separately. Stored endpoint coordinates from a dense replay are not learned population coefficients.

The maintained scalar study tool reports complete training rows, missing observations, descriptive ranges and twice-reorthogonalized numerical rank with a declared cutoff. Each model centres its dimensions on its complete training rows, normalizes each column separately and forms the declared feature products. This normalization does not turn mixed native scalars and millimetres into a spatial metric. Rank is necessary for separating the selected design effects; it does not establish clinical correspondence, uncertainty, extrapolation quality or an acceptable surface. No scalar study replaces the actual interface-to-engine-to-viewer comparison.

## Next distinct-reference batch

Use a first expansion of twenty additional source identities: twelve for development and eight reserved before observation or fitting, making sixteen development identities including the four exposed seeds. This is an experimental batch size, not a claim of adequate population coverage. Reserve all members of one identity, including alternate poses, mirrored regions, sections and derived measurements, together. Keep the same reserve through a correction round.

For each regional family, declare a small candidate design before selection. Larger coefficient models need more distinct identities or stronger justified shared constraints. Unsupported families remain unsupported rather than borrowing a full-body sample count.

## Dimension response and complexity

For every supported independent dimension, evaluate the original value, admissible positive and negative changes, selected pair changes and the exact return. Choose changes inside the admitted domain and state their physical sizes. Verify the named measurement's response, retained independent measurements, neighbouring boundaries and generated topology through the actual engine consumer. A changed document value without a changed, correctly measured surface establishes no control. These generated variations establish the operator's behaviour, not new anatomical reference coverage.

Compare compact candidates with the same frozen observations, registration, holdout split and surface operator. Record geometric and dimension-response unknowns, spans, constraints and runtime alongside curve error, omitted and added surface error and localized defects. Choose a candidate only when the extra complexity improves reserved identity or geometric evidence without concealing a local regression. Keep the dense replay as an observation diagnostic. A compact family's formula is still provisional outside the measured domain, even when its numerical design has full rank.
