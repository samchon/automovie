# Parametric curve families

For a cubic Bezier span with controls `B0..B3` and original parameter width `h`, the equivalent Hermite data are `P0=B0`, `P1=B3`, `T0=3(B1-B0)/h` and `T1=3(B3-B2)/h`. The inverse controls are `B1=P0+h*T0/3` and `B2=P1-h*T1/3`. Spatial millimetres and parameter units remain distinct: a horizontal ring can have zero height difference and a positive parameter width. [MIT's shape-interrogation text](https://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/node12.html) describes Bezier derivatives. Rational curves and higher-degree polynomials are different representations; the cubic identity does not reduce them exactly.

Section breadth, depth and perimeter are separate observations. An ellipse couples all three. A superellipse `|x/a|^n+|y/b|^n=1` is convex for `n>=1` and cannot contain a smooth midline recess. [You et al. 2011](../sources/you-2011-trigonometric-sections.md) fitted trigonometric cross sections with 3 to 10 terms and used manually adjusted transition tangents; their error thresholds concern sections, not the intervening surface.

A profile-and-guide network has intersection, ordering and parameter-compatibility constraints. [TiGL's curve-network algorithm](https://dlr-sc.github.io/tigl/doc/3.3.0/classtigl_1_1CTiglInterpolateCurveNetwork.html) handles these explicitly. [OCCT's constrained filling documentation](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_geom_fill___constrained_filling.html) distinguishes position and tangency constraints. Equal endpoints alone do not establish compatible transverse derivatives, injectivity or anatomical correspondence.

Piecewise cubic interpolation can be C1 without being C2. The [interpolation literature note](../sources/shape-preserving-interpolation.md) distinguishes monotonicity-preserving slopes from Akima-style oscillation reduction. Modified Akima has no blanket monotonicity guarantee: its published rule applied to equally spaced values `[0,10,20,20.1,30.1,40.1]` gives endpoint derivatives about `6.032064` on `[2,3]`, value about `20.581` at `2.25`, and midpoint derivative about `-2.866`. This is an algebraic counterexample, not an observed body measurement.

The spacing of observations affects interpolation only through the chosen parameterization and operator. Refining evaluation samples of an unchanged fitted function changes approximation density rather than its shape. A contour birth, split, merge or contact changes correspondence and cannot be identified from equal loop counts or regular spacing alone.

[Ceres' covariance documentation](https://ceres-solver.readthedocs.io/latest/nnls_covariance.html) explains rank deficiency and gauge ambiguity. [Wang, Pottmann and Liu](https://i.cs.hku.hk/~wenping/SDM/TOG_SDM.pdf) analyze curve-distance fitting with point and tangent approximations. These numerical methods do not identify anatomical correspondence or establish population generalization from four modeled people.
