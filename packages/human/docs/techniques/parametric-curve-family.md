# Parametric curve families

A shared family of dimensioned sections can make envelopes, rounding and longitudinal guides consume one shape definition. This is a candidate method, not an implemented or accepted replacement for Human's current generators. Changing a curve basis alone does not resolve contradictory dimensions, incompatible boundaries or source detail lost before the displayed skin.

## Representation and correspondence

An ellipse couples breadth, depth and perimeter. Extra rounding degrees of freedom can preserve breadth and depth while solving a compatible girth; paired anterior masses or recesses may require additional semantic arcs. Front and side silhouettes do not uniquely determine those internal courses, as the author's [visual-hull explanation](https://cgvg.polito.it/vhull.html) shows.

A section family uses physical height in millimetres, dimensionless semantic phase, independent measurements and named shape coefficients. Horizontal anthropometric sections retain their declared planes rather than inheriting a transported sweep frame. Skin arclength, convex-hull tape perimeter and caliper breadth are different measurements; incompatible protocols cannot be repaired by fitting.

For a cubic span of width `du`, endpoint derivatives give controls `P1=P0+du*T0/3` and `P2=P3-du*T1/3`. Adjacent spans share endpoints and derivative definitions. The [MIT shape interrogation text](https://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/node12.html) gives these derivative and convex-hull properties. [OCCT's Bezier documentation](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_geom___bezier_curve.html) explains why rational weights represent exact conics and why closure alone does not ensure periodic derivatives. Existing Catmull-Rom interpolation waypoints retain their own meaning.

A Gordon network requires intersecting profiles and guides with compatible parameters. [TiGL's algorithm](https://dlr-sc.github.io/tigl/doc/3.3.0/classtigl_1_1CTiglInterpolateCurveNetwork.html) explicitly solves intersections, ordering and parameter compatibility. At a join, a shared boundary curve also needs compatible transverse derivatives and corner conditions; [OCCT constrained filling](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_geom_fill___constrained_filling.html) distinguishes position from tangency constraints. Neither source proves an organic surface injective or anatomically correct.

## Section shape

A superellipse `|x/a|^n + |y/b|^n = 1` is convex for every `n >= 1`, so one superellipse per half section cannot contain a midline recess, a spinal furrow or a saddle between two convex masses; no value of `n` changes that, and `n < 1` makes concave quadrants that end in cusp-like tips on the axes instead of a smooth recess. Grade: derived (convexity of the `p`-norm unit ball). Gielis' superformula adds lobes but ties them to polar angle rather than to independent anatomical positions. Grade: inference. [Trunk research](../anatomy/trunk.md) records the current two-half superellipse level fits, which inherit this limit.

Bezier spans between named section points carry paired masses and recesses locally: a recess is two spans meeting symmetrically at an inward midline point with a shared tangent (G1) or matched curvature (G2), and each mass is a convex span between named apex and border points. Rational spans keep exact elliptic arcs where a region is elliptic. Grade: conventional.

Trigonometric and elliptic Fourier sections are compact but global around the section, so a local recess needs many harmonics and its truncation ripples elsewhere, and their coefficients carry no anatomical names. [You et al. 2011](../sources/you-2011-trigonometric-sections.md) chose 3 to 10 terms per body section so that mean error stays within 1 % and maximum error within 5 %, a design criterion rather than a validation. The elliptic Fourier form is Kuhl and Giardina, "Elliptic Fourier features of a closed contour", CGIP 18(3):236-258, 1982 (not read). [Friess, Rohlf and Hsiao, SPIE 5302, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.526889) regressed 3D elliptic Fourier torso coefficients of 98 adults on stature, weight and hip circumference and used the outlines as NURBS templates (abstract read; harmonic counts and errors not read).

A regular, injective normal displacement remains a normal graph over its base chart and preserves that chart's topology. Large offsets can fold or self-intersect, so an unconstrained normal field does not prove the absence of undercuts. It cannot create a new free boundary or change topology without an additional construction operation. Ear rims and alar overhangs therefore need explicit base geometry when the required shape has no valid normal-graph representation. Grade: derived, conditional on the stated regularity.

## Station channels

Every station quantity that varies along the axis, measured dimensions, rounding exponents and feature centres, widths and amplitudes alike, is a channel `c(z)` evaluated between stations by one interpolant or fit. Channel plots can reveal a source of bands, but surface continuity also depends on the section operator, parameter correspondence and patch joins. Smooth scalar channels alone do not establish a regular or band-free surface; [surface interrogation](surface-interrogation.md) lists the band signatures.

A C2 interpolating spline can overshoot; uneven key spacing alone neither requires nor excludes overshoot. A tension policy can alter its size and curvature distribution, but its result depends on the actual operator and data. Fritsch-Carlson limiting, Fritsch-Butland `pchip` slopes and Steffen's method preserve monotonicity under their stated conditions ([source note](../sources/shape-preserving-interpolation.md)). Modified Akima can reduce visible oscillation but does not guarantee monotonicity: its [official derivative rule](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.16.1/reference/generated/scipy.interpolate.Akima1DInterpolator.html) can overshoot monotone input. These piecewise cubic methods guarantee C1, not generally C2, so second derivatives may jump at keys. A `pchip` local-extremum slope is zero and may flatten the vicinity of a girth peak. Grade: derived under each method's conditions.

Exact interpolation retains the key values. Alternating first divided differences require intervening extrema for a differentiable interpolant, but do not fix those extrema at the keys; alternating second differences alone imply neither extrema nor nonmonotonicity. For example, equally spaced values `[0,1,3,4,6]` remain increasing while their second differences alternate. Smoothing splines (Reinsch, Numer. Math. 10:177-183, 1967) and penalized B-splines (Eilers and Marx, Statistical Science 11(2):89-121, 1996) trade residual against a curvature or difference penalty (both not read; candidate literature). A constrained fit could preserve measured stations while regularizing inferred coefficients; it does not guarantee removal of bands, feasibility or anatomical correctness. Grade: proposed engineering inference. C2 shape-preserving candidates include rational splines (Delbourgo and Gregory, IMA J. Numer. Anal., 1985) and energy-minimizing monotone splines (Wolberg and Alfy, J. Comput. Appl. Math. 143(2):145-188, 2002); neither was read.

Compatible B-spline skinning merges degree and knot vectors and parameterizes across sections by averaged chord length (Piegl and Tiller, *The NURBS Book*, 2nd ed., 1997, section 10.3; Woodward, CAD 20(8):441-451, 1988; both not read); [Piegl and Tiller 2002](https://doi.org/10.1007/s003710100156) addresses skinning anomalies at the cost of more control points (abstract read). [Kaklis and Ginnis 1996](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-8396(95)00048-8) build C2 skinning surfaces through points on parallel planes that preserve the sign of sectional curvature, using splines of non-uniform degree (abstract read). Energy-faired lofting appears in [Kim 1998](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO199811920957801.pub?lang=en) and [Park and Kim 1999](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO199911920640124.pub?lang=en) (abstracts read); Park and Kim, "Smooth surface approximation to serial cross-sections", CAD 28(12), 1996, approximate many sections instead of interpolating them, and Park, "Lofted B-spline surface interpolation by linearly constrained energy minimization", CAD 35, 2003, adds linear constraints (both not read). A Gordon construction reproduces compatible declared guides, but behavior between guides also depends on profile interpolation, intersection parameters, correction terms and mixed derivatives. Band-free guides alone do not guarantee a band-free longitudinal surface. Grade: proposed method with unresolved compatibility and regularity.

No source read prescribes anatomical station spacing. Spacing shapes the surface only through an interpolant that responds to uneven intervals; once a channel is a fitted smooth function of `z`, adding or moving stations changes sampling, not shape. Grade: inference.

The absence of a general modified-Akima monotonicity guarantee follows directly from its published slope formula. Equally spaced increasing values `[0,10,20,20.1,30.1,40.1]` give both endpoint derivatives of the `[2,3]` interval approximately `6.032064`. Its Hermite value at `2.25` is approximately `20.581`, above the upper key `20.1`, and its midpoint derivative is approximately `-2.866`. This is an algebraic counterexample to a blanket guarantee, not a Human acceptance criterion or a claim about every modified-Akima curve.

## Fitting limits

Origin, axes, scale, mirror convention and semantic section heights fix the fitting gauge. Centre shift and anterior projection, or width and rounding, can otherwise become indistinguishable. [Ceres covariance documentation](https://ceres-solver.readthedocs.io/latest/nnls_covariance.html) explains rank deficiency and gauge ambiguity; its statistical assumptions are not established for selected reference meshes.

Connected contour distances with arclength weights preserve geometric meaning better than matching vertex indices or equal polar angles. [Wang, Pottmann and Liu](https://i.cs.hku.hk/~wenping/SDM/TOG_SDM.pdf) analyze curve-distance fitting and point/tangent approximations. Their planar-curve method does not establish anatomical correspondence. A fitted modeled reference is a shape prior, not population anatomy or independent validation of dimensions used to warp it.

The generating surface, sampled writer values, inverse-fitted cage, refined controls and displayed limit/relief skin are different stages. Pinned controls need not lie on the source curve; refined controls need not lie on the limit surface. [OpenSubdiv](https://opensubdiv.org/docs/subdivision_surfaces.html) distinguishes refinement from limit evaluation. Inflating an anatomical amplitude to compensate for lost source detail confounds shape with the sampling operator.

Unresolved limits include shoulder branching, derivative compatibility across part cuts, infeasible measurement triplets, parameter identifiability and preservation of the source through the displayed skin. Four selected adult references cannot establish age/body coverage or posed tissue behavior.
