# Garments

Basic underwear is generated by code from the body the generator emits: the skin of the covered chains is cut along hem lines drawn on body landmarks and inflated over the skin as a stiff, stretched sheet. The code is `packages/human/src/appearance/AutoMovieHumanGarment*.ts`; its JSDoc holds the formulas. A garment never changes the skin parts, so hiding it shows the body unchanged.

## Input and output

`appearance.underwear` chooses the garments: `bottom` is `briefs`, `boxerBriefs` or `none`, `top` is `bra` or `none`, and `color` is the fabric's sRGB colour. Each garment is one part, `human.garment.briefs`, `human.garment.boxerBriefs` or `human.garment.bra`, citing the material `human.garment.fabric`: roughness 0.9, specular intensity 0.35 and a sheen lobe, double-sided.

## Construction

1. **Body.** The generated `trunk`, `upper`, `leg.left` and `leg.right` skin parts are welded by position into one surface, the same closed manifold the lofter built, including the crotch contact ring. Landmarks are measured on that surface in height bins of a hundredth of the trunk: crotch and axilla (the trunk's branch events), waist (narrowest width in the middle half of the trunk), buttock (most posterior point below the waist), bust and underbust (most and least anterior point of the breast columns above the waist), the midline and the crotch centre. Reading the emitted skin rather than the calibration means a later body warp dresses correctly without a second shape authority.
2. **Pattern.** A garment is the covered skin where every hem distance is positive. Hems are smooth signed distances in landmark units, the crotch-to-waist span S:

   | Garment | Hem | Rule |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Briefs | Waistband | Plane 0.45 S above the crotch, 0.1 lower per unit forward; band 0.07 S |
   | Briefs | Leg openings | One plane per leg through the gusset edge (half-width 0.1 S, 0.05 S below the crotch), rising 0.35 per unit outward and 0.3 per unit forward; binding 0.025 S |
   | Boxer briefs | Waistband | Plane 0.55 S above the crotch, rake 0.08; band 0.1 S |
   | Boxer briefs | Leg openings | Level plane 0.33 S below the crotch; binding 0.03 S |
   | Bra | Underband | Level, 0.03 S below the inframammary fold; band 0.08 S |
   | Bra | Top edge | 0.15 S above the bust within 25 degrees of the front, falling by a smoothstep to 0.08 S under the axilla at 120 degrees and level across the back; binding 0.025 S |

   A brief's leg opening runs from the crotch up the inguinal fold to the hip and back down across the lower buttock, which a plane through the leg root tilted up outward and forward follows. The bra's top edge is a plateau rather than a cosine series, whose maximum would peak at the front, so it stays level over both breasts. A band lifts the fabric by 0.6 to 0.84 mm along its hem, the elastic binding that reads as the garment's edge.
3. **Cut.** Covered triangles are clipped to the zero line of the hem field by linear interpolation on their edges, after snapping skin vertices within 0.15 mm of a hem onto it, so the hem is as smooth as its field and no sliver triangle forms.
4. **Fit.** Each vertex starts 1.2 mm (plus band lift) above its skin point. Gauss-Seidel sweeps then move it toward the mean of its neighbours less 0.95 of their mean umbrella vector, damped by 0.6 and accelerated by 0.4 of the previous move, and push it out to its thickness above the tangent plane of its nearest skin vertex, found by a walk on the skin graph and a grid search every 100 sweeps. This is a sheet with tension and bending stiffness over the body as an obstacle: it hugs convex skin and spans the gluteal cleft, the inguinal folds, the vulva and the cleavage instead of following them. Hem vertices stay at their thickness, as an elastic binding grips the skin.
5. **Hem.** Each boundary loop gets a rounded lip: rings over a quarter circle of the local thickness to a ring 0.4 mm under the skin, outward of the cut line, so no gap shows at grazing angles and the edge shades round.

The fit's constants have reasons. The obstacle is the nearest skin vertex's tangent plane, not each vertex's own skin point's, because the membrane slides vertices along the skin and a vertex in a narrow hollow would otherwise cross the opposite wall; the grid search escapes walks stuck on the far wall. A pure membrane follows the vulva and the groin creases at convergence, so more sweeps cannot help; bending dominates tension at the hollows' scale, about ten edges, only when its share is near one, hence 0.95 with damping. Momentum 0.8 diverges.

## Display switch

The Human page and the PNG service take `garment=0` to hide every `human.garment.` part (see the [screenshot guide](../../../../.agents/skills/human/screenshot.md#free-view)). Garments show by default, because every image shown outside development wears them; body-shape work adds `garment=0`. Framing comes from the whole model, so frames with and without garments align pixel for pixel.

## Attachment

How a garment stays on the body when the body changes shape or moves decides whether it is regenerated, bound to the surface or skinned on its own. The options, from production tools and literature:

| Method | What is stored | Strength | Weakness for Human |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regenerate from the skin (current) | Nothing | Exact for every shape; hems follow landmarks | 3 to 4 s on f1; not per frame |
| Surface binding: [Blender Surface Deform](../sources/blender-surface-deform-shrinkwrap.md), [Maya wrap](../sources/maya-wrap-deformer.md) | Each vertex's place on the target surface and its offset | Follows any skin deformation, correctives included, with no weights | Triangle bindings break when the triangulation changes |
| Displacement on the body: [CAPE](../sources/ma-2020-cape.md) | One offset per body vertex, posed by the body's skinning | Same skinning as the body | Garment topology must equal the body's; detail bounded by body resolution |
| Skin weight transfer: [Abdrashitov et al.](../sources/abdrashitov-2023-skin-weight-transfer.md), Unreal `FTransferBoneWeights` | Bone weights copied from the closest body point, inpainted where untrusted | Standard for separate garment meshes | Copies skinning only; the body's pose-space corrections are not followed, so tight fabric crosses the skin at the crotch and axillae |
| Collision projection: [Shrinkwrap](../sources/blender-surface-deform-shrinkwrap.md) outside or above surface | An offset | Keeps a shell outside a target | Unreliable near sharp angles; no shape memory |

The skin's triangulation changes with every parameter set, because stations come from bisection at a chord tolerance, so triangle and vertex-index bindings do not survive a shape change, while the loft coordinates (chain, station, phase) address the same anatomical place on every body. A candidate binding therefore stores each garment vertex's nearest skin point in loft coordinates and its offset in that point's frame (normal, section tangent and station tangent), evaluates the garment on the posed loft and cleans it by a few obstacle sweeps of the fit above: the displacement-then-skin order of CAPE with the surface-binding evaluation of Surface Deform. Patterns and the fit would run once per body at rest, and only binding evaluation and the short obstacle pass per pose. The fit already records each garment vertex's nearest skin vertex. Weight transfer is the option for loose garments, which this note does not cover.

## Limits

- The sheet is not run to full convergence; the largest difference from a converged sheet is at the deepest midline bridging.
- Neighbour weights are uniform, so vertices slide a few millimetres where the loft's spacing varies; the obstacle keeps them outside.
- No folds, wrinkles, drape or fabric texture: the garment is a skin-tight prototype with plain knit shading.
- The front of the briefs shows a soft mons and the V between the thighs, as real briefs do; a lined gusset would soften it further.
