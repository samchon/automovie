# Section-chain lofting

The JSDoc of `packages/human/src/lofting/` and `src/generation/` holds the formulas and premises.

## Construction

The body surface is a tree of chains, each a family of horizontal sections indexed by world height in the registered frame (forward +X, up +Y, lateral +Z, mm). Horizontal sections match the saved Recovery loft, which slices the neutral A-pose body along Y, so every observed loop belongs to one chain without reslicing.

1. **Sections.** A section is a closed periodic uniform cubic B-spline in x(t) and z(t), with N control points chosen from the section's perimeter spacing. Local support keeps a sharp feature in one place. Observed samples are projected by penalised least squares (a P-spline): a second-difference penalty whose weight halves the response at a cutoff wavelength (20 mm on the body, 8 mm on the head and toes, 4 mm on the auricle), so sub-millimetre scan noise does not become ribs. The control-point mean is the section centre, which gives the chain's central axis. A correspondence rule assigns the phase t: polar angle for limb and digit shafts, and arclength from the front for contact parents such as palms.
2. **Layers.** Between two principal layers, coefficients follow the cubic Hermite polynomial of their values and height slopes. A section frame may tilt where a horizontal cut would graze the surface, such as under the chin: the origin follows a Hermite curve and the orientation a cubic Hermite curve on the rotation group, both with three-point derivatives of the neighbouring layers, so the plane turns with a continuous angular velocity. A point at distance x from the turning axis leaves the plane at x times that velocity, so a velocity jump at a layer prints a crease along it. Tessellation bisects each interval recursively and stops when the midpoint section lies within a physical chord tolerance.
3. **Branch events.** Where loops merge (the axillae, the crotch, the finger webs), the parent's bottom section is a contact of its children's sections. The parent ring is split along pinch-phase arcs, and the children inherit those exact vertices. Each child projects them onto its own end section for their phases, then zips them to its own phase set in its first band, so parent and children share one boundary without seam copies. Chain ends close at recovered critical points: the crown and the fingertips. Events closer than a usable chain share one ring, because a chain a fraction of a millimetre tall has end slopes that are its rows' noise over its height, and its two rings print a ridge. The fitters reconcile every event with one transition map: the contact ring's samples take the child's phases by projection on the child's own end section, and the two sides' height slopes are averaged only along the ring's in-plane normal, which alone fixes the tangent plane; each side keeps its own component along the ring, so the interval next to the ring needs no bend to meet it. Within 10 mm above a contact the parent's sections are matched to the section above them instead of taking the rule's phases, because the rule redistributes the outline's length as the pinch closes and every phase would slide along the ring; pinch phases are read in that matched parametrization.
4. **Addresses.** Every principal section is the curve `<chain>/<k>`, every axis is `<chain>/axis`, and every guide (the fixed-phase Hermite curve carrying the sections along the chain) is `<chain>/g<j>`. The skin is split per chain interval `<chain>/<k>`, so a viewer can select a curve and the skin it shapes. The surface also returns every vertex's loft coordinates (chain, station, phase), the parameters attachments such as garments bind to instead of vertex numbers.
5. **Parameter laws.** `AutoMovieHumanBodyLaw` maps the specimen calibration to a person's calibration before lofting, so skin, construction, apertures, globes, poles and the lip boundary keep one evaluator:
   - A shape basis per unit of `masculineFeminine`. The four reference meshes share one vertex order, so a barycentric address of the calibrated f1 surface names the same point on every specimen; the stature-normalized displacements of f2, m1 and m2 there are reduced by least squares through f1 to one direction (`fitBodyMorph.ts`). Section displacements live in each layer's frame, the out-of-plane part as the layer's `lift`.
   - A measurement law. Tape girths (hip, high hip, waist, underbust, chest, neck base, neck, thigh, knee, calf, biceps, elbow, forearm, wrist, hand) scale each section about its centre at the named stations, the head length and breadth scale the head, and the legs stretch so the crotch takes the inputs' inseam share of the stature, everything above moving up with it. Targets are the specimen's own geometry times the input ratios in stature units, divided by the body being warped, so the basis's own size change is not counted twice.
   - Branches move rigidly with their parent's map where they join and blend to it within 40 mm of the ring, so shared boundaries stay shared.
   - Both are the identity for the specimen's own inputs, which return the specimen calibration object itself.
   - Arm and leg lengths other than the inseam are not read; three specimens identify only the one basis direction. Stature then scales the body uniformly.

Horizontal slices do not separate toes lying on the floor, so the foot region cuts its own planes.
