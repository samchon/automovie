# Section-chain lofting

This is the current generator design for campaign #2925. The JSDoc of `packages/human/src/lofting/` and `src/generation/` holds the formulas and premises; this note keeps the overall plan and its status.

## Plan

The body surface is a tree of chains, each a family of horizontal sections indexed by world height in the registered frame (forward +X, up +Y, lateral +Z, mm). Horizontal sections match the saved Recovery loft, which slices the neutral A-pose body along Y, so every observed loop belongs to one chain without reslicing.

1. **Universal lofting logic.** A section is a closed periodic uniform cubic B-spline in x(t) and z(t), with N control points chosen from the section's perimeter spacing. Local support keeps a sharp feature in one place, where the earlier Fourier series rang along the whole loop. Observed samples are projected by penalised least squares (a P-spline): a second-difference penalty whose weight halves the response at a cutoff wavelength (20 mm on the body, 8 mm on the head and toes, 4 mm on the auricle), so sub-millimetre scan noise does not become ribs. The control-point mean is the section centre, which gives the chain's central axis. A correspondence rule assigns the phase t: polar angle for limb and digit shafts, arclength from the front for contact parents such as palms, and guide rails for the trunk and head. Each rail is a phase anchored at the front and back midline crossings, smoothed along height, with uniform arclength between rails, so a point keeps its anatomical place from section to section. Between two principal layers, coefficients follow the cubic Hermite polynomial of their values and height slopes. A section frame may tilt where a horizontal cut would graze the surface, such as under the chin: the origin follows a Hermite curve and the orientation a cubic Hermite curve on the rotation group, both with three-point derivatives of the neighbouring layers, so the plane turns with a continuous angular velocity. A point at distance x from the turning axis leaves the plane at x times that velocity, so the earlier slerp, whose velocity jumped at every layer, printed a crease of up to 4 degrees along each layer of the chin, nape and upper lip. Tessellation bisects each interval recursively and stops when the midpoint section lies within a physical chord tolerance.
2. **Body layers.** Each chain keeps only its principal layers. The shared layer fit solves the Hermite values and slopes by least squares over every observation, with a bending penalty, and bisects an interval wherever it misses a recovered section by more than the tolerance. Principal layers therefore fall where the shape changes. The head's skin chains separate smoothing from layer choice instead: a smoothing spline through every observed section, with a bending weight per control point set for one cutoff on the skin (8 mm, 4 mm on the face and the auricle, scaled by how far a station step moves that point when tilted planes crowd or spread), is the target, and the principal layers are the fewest whose Hermite interpolation stays within 0.1 mm of it. A tolerance against noisy rows had put a layer at nearly every row (80 to 90 per chain), and the layers carried the rows' facet noise as ripples. After branch events are reconciled, chains are fitted again with their reconciled end sections and end slopes held, so no interval bends to meet an end changed afterwards.
3. **Branch events.** Where loops merge (the axillae, the crotch, the finger webs), the parent's bottom section is a contact of its children's sections. The parent ring is split along pinch-phase arcs, and the children inherit those exact vertices. Each child projects them onto its own end section for their phases, then zips them to its own phase set in its first band, so parent and children share one boundary without seam copies. Chain ends close at recovered critical points: the crown and the fingertips. Events closer than a usable chain (the two upper helices, 0.7 mm apart for f1) share one ring, because a chain a fraction of a millimetre tall has end slopes that are its rows' noise over its height, and its two rings print a ridge. Both sides of a ring also carry the same section detail: a chain whose phases crowd into the auricles keeps the nominal cutoff in millimetres on its sparse stretches. The fitters reconcile every event with the same transition map: the contact ring's samples take the child's phases by projection on the child's own end section, and the two sides' height slopes are averaged only along the ring's in-plane normal, which alone fixes the tangent plane; each side keeps its own component along the ring, the sliding of its own correspondence, so the interval next to the ring needs no bend to meet it. Within 10 mm above a contact the parent's sections are matched to the section above them instead of taking the rule's phases, because the rule redistributes the outline's length as the pinch closes and every phase would slide along the ring; pinch phases are read in that matched parametrization.
4. **Addressable construction.** Every principal section is the curve `<chain>/<k>`, every axis is `<chain>/axis`, and every guide (the fixed-phase Hermite curve carrying the sections along the chain) is `<chain>/g<j>`. The skin is split per chain interval `<chain>/<k>`, so a viewer can select a curve and the skin it shapes (#2966). The surface also returns every vertex's loft coordinates (chain, station, phase), the parameters attachments such as garments bind to instead of vertex numbers.
5. **Parameter laws.** `AutoMovieHumanBodyLaw` maps the specimen calibration to a person's calibration before lofting, so skin, construction, apertures, globes, poles and the lip boundary keep one evaluator:
   - A shape basis per unit of `masculineFeminine`. The four reference meshes share one vertex order, so a barycentric address of the calibrated f1 surface names the same point on every specimen; the stature-normalized displacements of f2, m1 and m2 there are reduced by least squares through f1 to one direction (`fitBodyMorph.ts`). Section displacements live in each layer's frame, the out-of-plane part as the layer's `lift`.
   - A measurement law. Tape girths (hip, high hip, waist, underbust, chest, neck base, neck, thigh, knee, calf, biceps, elbow, forearm, wrist, hand) scale each section about its centre at the named stations, the head length and breadth scale the head, and the legs stretch so the crotch takes the inputs' inseam share of the stature, everything above moving up with it. Targets are the specimen's own geometry times the input ratios in stature units, divided by the body being warped, so the basis's own size change is not counted twice.
   - Branches move rigidly with their parent's map where they join and blend to it within 40 mm of the ring, so shared boundaries stay shared.
   - Both are the identity for the specimen's own inputs, which return the specimen calibration object itself.
   - Arm and leg lengths other than the inseam are not yet read; three specimens identify only the one basis direction.
6. **Progressive conquest.** The order is: whole-body outer form, then fingers (done), toes, ears, eyes and the mouth as further branch events, then the parameter laws, then f2, m1 and m2.

## Observation program

The fitters live in `test/src/executable/reference-experiments/human-calibration/` and share `HumanSectionFitting` (spline sections, rails, matched samples), `HumanLayerFitting` (the layer fit) and `HumanEventReconciliation` (event transition map and slopes). Each region writes one calibration under `src/generation/calibration/`, the shared coefficients the generator reads:

- **Torso** (trunk, limbs, hands, neck) reads the saved Recovery loft `test/.shots/<id>/hermite-curves.json.gz`. Chains and merge events come from the loft's own interval correspondences, and pinch phases come from the contact sections. The loft slices about every 10 mm, so dense sections cut from the reference mesh near the axillae, crotch and wrists (`observeBodySections`, 1 mm apart) are added to the chains they continue; there the sections change fastest, and interpolation alone left a notch along each saddle. It trims at the head region's base.
- **Foot** cuts the reference mesh on planes suited to the foot, so toes lying on the floor separate.
- **Head** (the whole head above the neck base, auricles included; the file keeps its historical `Ear` name) plans tilted section frames and observes them on the reference mesh. It records the eyelid apertures and the eyeball globes, and the nostril margins (the ends of the chords that close each plane across a nostril of the open exterior), which become apertures with a pocket behind them.

## f1 observations used

The saved f1 loft has closed loops at every height except 1338 to 1404 mm. In that band the loops cross the body and head mesh boundary and are saved as open pieces; the fitter joins them. The striping over the shoulders in the ruled reverse render comes from this band. Topology events are:

- the crotch contact at 804.9 mm;
- the axillary contacts at 1276.6 mm;
- the finger merges between 920 and 958 mm;
- the floor-contact toe pads below 3 mm;
- facial and ear branches between 1490 and 1610 mm.

Horizontal slices do not separate toes lying on the floor, so the foot region cuts its own planes.
