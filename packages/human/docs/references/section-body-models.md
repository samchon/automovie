# Section-based body models

Published body models built from cross sections or feature curves report measurement errors such as girths and lengths, not surface-form quality: none of those read reports banding, isophote behaviour or curvature between sections. Dimension error and surface-form error are distinct quantities. Grade: inference from the sources below.

Methods Programs Biomed.* 229:107292, 2023, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2022.107292) (abstract read) | Anatomically placed calf feature points, control points computed from them, one NURBS surface | 260 scanned calves | Average error 0.37 %, against about 5 % cited for earlier work | The error quantity was not read |
| [You et al. 2011](../sources/you-2011-trigonometric-sections.md) | Trigonometric series per section, hand-tuned transition surfaces | Scanned point clouds | Terms chosen for mean error within 1 % and maximum within 5 % | A design criterion, not a validation |
| Friess, Rohlf and Hsiao, Proc. SPIE 5302, 2004, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.526889) (abstract read) | 3D elliptic Fourier torso curves regressed on stature, weight and hip circumference, used as NURBS templates | 98 adults | Not read | |
| Baek and Lee, *Computer-Aided Design* 44(1):56-67, 2012, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2010.12.006) (abstract read) | Statistical whole-body mesh with regression from sizing parameters | 250 whole-body scans | "Reasonably accurate", no number in the abstract | A template mesh rather than a curve-generation method |
| Wuhrer and Shu, [arXiv 1109.1175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.1175) (summary read) | Statistical shape model fitted to 34 measurements | 50 held-out CAESAR bodies | Baseline methods 3.3 to 4.0 mm mean and 10.0 to 11.5 mm maximum error for foot length | The proposed method's near-zero error comes from optimizing the measurements themselves and says nothing about the surface between them |

Feature-curve mannequins for apparel use semantic cross sections and longitudinal feature curves. Wang C.C.L., "Parameterization and parametric design of mannequins", *Computer-Aided Design* 37(1):83-98, 2005, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2004.05.001), builds a feature wireframe from scans and synthesizes new bodies by numerical optimization from parameterized samples; its use of Gregory patches for a G1 surface through the wireframe is remembered from the abstract, which was not available to verify. Hsiao and Chen, "A study of surface reconstruction for 3D mannequins based on feature curves", *Computer-Aided Design* 45, 2013, names neck, shoulder, bust, waist and hip girths, side line, centre line and princess line as feature curves; its surface representation was not read. Wang S.X. et al., *Applied Mechanics and Materials* 365-366:121-124, 2013, [doi](https://doi.org/10.4028/www.scientific.net/AMM.365-366.121), interpolates key points of semantic cross sections with tensioned cardinal splines and joins parts on a shared boundary curve, reporting no accuracy (abstract read). A survey of the template-based alternatives is Cheng, Chen, Martin, Wu and Song, "Parametric modeling of 3D human body shape: a survey", *Computers & Graphics* 71:88-100, 2018, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cag.2017.11.008).
