# Read a refusal

What to do when the motion vocabulary refuses a composition.

A refusal is the vocabulary saying that a real person could not perform what was composed, from where they stand, with what they hold. It is not a crash to work around. It names who was to act, which act, which axis of it is at fault, the value given, what that axis accepts and what to change, both as text and as fields a program can read.

## Repair at the axis

Change the one thing the refusal names, then build the performance again:

| The refusal says | Change |
| --- | --- |
| A value is outside what the act accepts | That value, to one inside the range it states |
| A target is out of reach | Bring the performer nearer first, move the target, or aim at a region within reach |
| A prop has no such grip or point, or a hand is already full | Name one the refusal lists, or release the hand first |
| An act is cut where it declares no window | Cut inside a window it lists, or let the act end |
| A partner, an anchor or an answered act is missing | Add it to the scene, or place it before the act that reads it |
| The state the act starts from does not allow it | What precedes the act: where the performer stands, faces or sits |

Take the repair from the screenplay. When two repairs would plan, choose the one the beat describes, because a repair that only silences the refusal stages a different scene.

## What not to do

Catching the refusal and carrying on performs nothing, and the film shows a person standing still. Widening a value until the refusal stops moves the fault to the picture, where it is harder to find. Both hide what the vocabulary found.

## When the fix does not plan

A refusal whose stated fix does not make the composition plan, or that names no axis, is a defect of the vocabulary. Hand it to the user with the composition and the refusal's text under [a defect or a gap](../development/reuse.md#a-defect-or-a-gap).
