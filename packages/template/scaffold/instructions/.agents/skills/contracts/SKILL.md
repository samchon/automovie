# Contracts

This tree holds the questions Evidence asks. A respondent is whoever answers one: a declaration, a document or a record. Read only the family and contracts the calling task selects; this file is not an evidence target.

| Family | Respondents | Where they answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Development](development/SKILL.md) | Source declarations and tests | Their doc comments |
| [Story](story/SKILL.md) | Research studies, settings or brief, treatments, scripts and screenplay units and populations, discovery results and review records | Where [answer placement](../story/answers.md#answer-placement) puts them |

## Items and statements

Each H2 is one item, established by a separate answer. An item is one broad question with its reason: what to achieve, and what its recipient loses when it is missed.

An item's detail lives one level down, in a `statements` document beside its contract, under the item's anchor. Each statement is one assertion, with its exceptions, that one reading of the work finds met or failed. Statement documents are not evidence targets: no selector reaches them, and a citation names the item, never a statement.

A respondent uses the statements to question its own compliance:

1. Check the work against every statement before answering.
2. Repair what fails.
3. Write an answer true against all of them, naming each statement that does not apply with its reason, without reciting the list.

A source reviewer audits the answer against the same list; a story answer's truth belongs to its [evidence answerer](../story/answers.md). Unless an item says its statements are its whole boundary, another proven loss on the item's question is still a finding.

An enumeration in an item or a statement defines what to compare, not ingredients to add to the respondent. Items and statements cite no research or reference source, though a statement may link the skill document or sibling statement that owns a rule it applies.

## Outside the graph

An item asks only what its authored respondent can establish from its own content and the references it cites. What a rendered or generated picture shows, and what a sound is, can only be observed, so generation requests, prompts, control channels and delivered media have no contract family.

[Review](../review/SKILL.md) judges them on stills and through the user's ears, and records the observations in the [acceptance record](../review/delivery-review.md#acceptance-record). An answer that records such an observation in place of the work it describes adds paperwork, not evidence.
