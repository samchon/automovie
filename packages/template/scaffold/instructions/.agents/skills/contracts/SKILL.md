## Items and statements {#items-and-statements}

Each H2 is one item, and a separate answer establishes it. An item is one broad question: what to achieve, why its recipient needs it, and where too little and too much begin. Items stay few. A new concern on a theme an item already covers joins that item instead of becoming another, because every item costs every respondent an answer.

The detail lives one level down. An item that needs it has statements: single assertions that can each be checked, with their exceptions, listed under the item's anchor in a `statements` document beside its contract. Statements are how you question your own compliance. Check the work against every statement before answering and repair what fails. Then write an answer that is true against all of them and names each statement that does not apply with its reason; the answer does not recite the list. A reviewer audits the answer against the same list.

Statement documents are not evidence targets: a citation names the item, never a statement. An enumeration in an item or a statement defines what to compare, not ingredients to add to the respondent. Neither cites supporting sources, so the judgment, reasons and protections stay in their own text.

## What the graph does not hold

An item asks only what its authored respondent can establish from its own content and the references it cites. What a rendered or generated picture shows, and what a sound is, can only be observed: generation requests, prompts, control channels and delivered media therefore have no contract family. [Review](../review/SKILL.md) judges them on stills and through the user's ears, and records the observations in the [acceptance record](../review/delivery.md#acceptance-record). An answer that records such an observation in place of the work it describes adds paperwork, not evidence.
