# Contracts

This tree holds the questions Evidence asks. Read only the family and contracts the calling task selects. This file is not an evidence target.

A respondent is whoever answers an item: a declaration, a document or a record.

| Family | Respondents | Where they answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Development](development/SKILL.md) | Source declarations and tests | Their doc comments |
| [Story](story/SKILL.md) | Research studies, settings or brief, treatments, scripts and screenplay units and populations, discovery results and review records | Where [answer placement](../story/evidence/answer-placement.md) puts them |

## Items and statements {#items-and-statements}

Each H2 is one item, and a separate answer establishes it. An item is one broad question with its reason: what to achieve, and what its recipient loses when it is missed. Items stay few. A new concern on a theme an item already covers joins that item, because every item costs every respondent an answer.

The detail lives one level down. An item's statements are single assertions that can each be checked, with their exceptions, listed under the item's anchor in a `statements` document beside its contract.

Statements are how you question your own compliance:

1. Check the work against every statement before answering.
2. Repair what fails.
3. Write an answer that is true against all of them and names each statement that does not apply, with its reason. The answer does not recite the list.

A reviewer audits the answer against the same list. Unless an item says its statements are its whole boundary, another proven loss on the item's question is still a finding. For story, [narrative rounds](../review/narrative-rounds.md#findings-and-objections) set what proves a body finding.

Statement documents are not evidence targets: no selector reaches them, and a citation names the item, never a statement.

An enumeration in an item or a statement defines what to compare, not ingredients to add to the respondent.

An item and its statements cite no research or reference source; a statement may link the skill document or sibling statement that owns a rule it applies.

## What the graph does not hold

An item asks only what its authored respondent can establish from its own content and the references it cites. What a rendered or generated picture shows, and what a sound is, can only be observed, so generation requests, prompts, control channels and delivered media have no contract family.

[Review](../review/SKILL.md) judges them on stills and through the user's ears, and records the observations in the [acceptance record](../review/delivery-review.md#acceptance-record). An answer that records such an observation in place of the work it describes adds paperwork, not evidence.
