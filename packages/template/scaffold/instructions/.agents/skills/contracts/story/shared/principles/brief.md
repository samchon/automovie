# Brief principles

Each brief H2 answers as one facet of its subject, owning the anchored H3 decisions beneath it. It does not answer for every film or scene.

## Does the record settle a decision the films need? {#needed-decisions}

Does it name its subject and settle a value, scope and the conditions, limits or exceptions that affect use, specifically enough that a screenplay author or reviewer can hold the films to it? The brief records decisions: the fixed conditions (running time, frame, delivery, and the adopted representation with what it cannot show, such as faceless figures or a narrow vertical frame), the world and its rules, people's personality, values, motives, abilities and knowledge, relationships, the identity and use of places and objects, and the tone and look. A screenplay that must invent one of these, or write past the representation's limits, has no basis to hold to.

Write only what the planned films need. Filling a facet nothing uses or writing "not applicable" for an absent one fails, because it buries the decisions. A measurement belongs here only when it is itself a story decision; otherwise its source goes to [research](../../../../story/planning/research.md) and its value to the code that realizes it. A practiced activity records its level and style and cites the study that holds its vocabulary. An external fact the record asserts cites its source, and a declared [factual basis](../../../../story/planning/requirements.md#factual-basis) applies its source duties to the claims it covers.

## Is the record findable, owned once and kept current? {#record-and-currency}

Can a screenplay author find the definition, settled content, conditions and open matters in a consistent order? Each independently used subject has one file with its inseparable attributes together, one term per concept and one owner per fact; the record states facts in prose for readers and never describes code. It holds baseline states and change conditions and leaves events, action and reveal order to the screenplay. A decision a screenplay established that another scene or film must keep belongs here once established, citing the unit that established it; a decision kept only in one screenplay lets the next film contradict it.
