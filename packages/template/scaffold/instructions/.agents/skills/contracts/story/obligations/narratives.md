# Narrative population obligations

Answer once per completed treatment, script or screenplay population, across all units and boundaries. Read treatments in spans ending at changes of direction. Read scripts and screenplays by partition.

## Continuity and progression

Followed across every unit and boundary, do events, states and pursuits continue or change for a reason? Local passes cannot show an unsupported reversal, a pursuit that silently stops, or an opposition that stops threatening and so collapses the tension.

Identify what each unit changes in events, understanding, relationships, emotion, pressure, rhythm or form. Locate empty duplication and progressions that can stand apart. Preserve inseparable montage, parallel action and continuous action across scenes.

Test what moving a unit does to the sequence. Follow exits into entries by cause or by the adopted episodic or nonlinear form. Across each boundary, check time, place, knowledge, injury, costume, possessions, resources, status, relationships and promises.

Identify where the audience first recognizes the protagonist's or ensemble's pursuit, or another organizing pattern. Follow its development through later goals and events. Trace recurring agents' drives, beliefs, interpretations and choices, and the role of each opposing person, group or condition, through resolution or an earned withdrawal. Locate the earliest cause of an unsupported reversal or a threshold crossed without pressure or cost. Explain a threshold intentionally left unreached.

## Promises and distribution

Does the whole work keep its promises and vary timing, speech and recurrence with each span's pressure? Check for patterns that local passes miss.

Read the opening with only the knowledge the audience has. Establish when people, relationships, pressure, place and time become legible. Trace recurring rewards and payoffs to their setups and costs. Identify the longest waits, unearned rewards and returns that fail to sustain the wait.

Show which choices and costs led to the last event, what it settles and why the work stops. An open ending can earn its place without an epilogue.

Compare scene, compression, pause, omission and simultaneous action with the pressure of the span. Identify what habitual time, flashback, anticipation or retelling contributes when the work departs from forward sequence.

Compare openings, exits, speech lengths and tactics, objects of attention and image-sound motifs across all units. Treatments assign exchanges and later layers realize them. Locate passages that show what repetition, silence, a narrow world or deliberate frustration does to the experience. Counts can guide that reading.
