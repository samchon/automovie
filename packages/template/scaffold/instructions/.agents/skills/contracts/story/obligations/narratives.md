# Narrative population obligations

Answer once per completed treatment, script or screenplay population, across all units and boundaries. Read treatments in spans ending at changes of direction. Read scripts and screenplays by partition.

## Continuity and progression

Followed across every unit and boundary, do events, states and pursuits continue or change for a reason? Local passes cannot show an unsupported reversal, a pursuit that silently stops, or an opposition that stops threatening and so collapses the tension.

- Identify the event, understanding, relationship, emotion, pressure, rhythm or formal contribution of every unit. Locate empty duplication and separable progressions. Preserve inseparable montage, parallel or continuous action across scenes.
- Test what the order of movable units contributes. Connect exits to entries by cause or the adopted form, including episodic or nonlinear order. Check time, place, knowledge, injury, costume, possessions, resources, status, relationships and promises across boundaries.
- Trace recurring agents' drives, beliefs, interpretations and choices, and the agency of each opposing person, group or condition through resolution or an earned withdrawal. Locate unsupported reversals or thresholds crossed without pressure or cost at their earliest cause. Explain an intentionally unreached threshold.
- For a protagonist, ensemble or other organizing subject, identify where the audience first recognizes the pursuit or pattern and how subsequent goals and events develop it.

## Promises and distribution

Does the whole work keep its promises and vary timing, speech and recurrence with each span's pressure? Check for patterns that local passes miss.

- Read the opening without settings knowledge. Check when the audience can follow people, relationships, pressure, place and time. Trace recurring rewards, payoffs, their setups and costs. Identify the longest gaps, unearned rewards and returns that fail to sustain the wait.
- Show how the last event follows from prior choices and costs, what it settles and why the work ends there. An open ending needs grounds and may stop without an epilogue.
- Compare scene, compression, pause, omission, simultaneity, habitual time, flashback, anticipation and retelling with the pressure of the span. State what each departure from forward sequence contributes.
- Compare all openings, exits, speech lengths and tactics, objects of attention and image-sound motifs. Treatments assign exchanges and later layers realize them. Show how repetition, silence, a narrow world or deliberate frustration changes the experience. Counts locate passages to read.
