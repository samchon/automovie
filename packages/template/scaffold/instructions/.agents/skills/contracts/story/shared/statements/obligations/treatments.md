## Whole event design

1. The answer maps what the audience knows, does not know, is led to believe and waits to learn throughout the sequence.
2. Consequential reveals, reversals and decisive uses of powers or objects have earlier establishment.
3. Every setup or promise has a payoff, a transformation or an owned unresolved ending, and the answer reports unsupported payoffs and abandoned setups.
4. The answer locates the questions that open and resolve, how long they stay open, the oldest outstanding ones and any deferral of all satisfaction. Counts locate the burden; reading judges it.

Entry, middle and ending:

7. Parallel progressions have consequential order, intersections or contrasts, not alternating summaries.
8. Events convey emotional movement and tone instead of leaving a mood for scripts to invent, and opposing agents act from their own priorities where present.
9. The ending shows what closes and what remains open, with its aftermath or changed state.
10. Chance may begin a crisis, while an outside resolution needs an owned work premise.
