# Script principles

Each script H2 answers for an executable scene of one continuous place and time within the script's chosen partition. Scripts already contain full action, dialogue, staging and a viewing position.

## Executable scene

Can the scene be performed as written, from its opening through action, exchange and reaction to an observable exit state? Keep place, positions, view and sound executable and consistent.

- **Opening.** Begin with a playable action or condition. Establish used space, interior or exterior, story day and time, light, color, weather, participants, access and inherited pressure. Let visible and audible conditions carry mood.
- **Performance.** Write the preparation, action, transition, reaction and observable exit. Include movement, practical limits, objects and elapsed time where they affect the exchange. Give consequential questions, lies, refusals, bargains and silences the words, tactics and responses that change choice, knowledge, power or relationship. An observational, ritual or nonhuman scene can turn on a process.
- **Blocks and voices.** Break on meaningful changes of action, process, response, time, place, form or view. Use block length for urgency, duration, humor or stillness under [execution rhythm](../naturalness/scripts.md#execution-rhythm). Distinguish speakers, world sound, audience-only sound and consequential overlap. Put interruptions, silence and meaning-changing delivery beside the action they affect.
- **Staging.** Locate performers and give each block its subject, shot scale, angle, movement and focus. State a fixed view once. Preserve canonical appearance, possessions, state, sightlines, props, costume and lighting. Decide expression, gaze and sound as far as the form or commissioned generation can carry them. Show how each participant learns or misreads a cue.
- **Handoff.** Use the notation that serves the adopted form, including theatrical staging, captions, voiceover or inserts. Leave incidental acting, lenses and music composition open unless required. The screenplay owns final attention, transitions and experienced duration.
