# Settings population statements

Contract: [obligations/settings.md](../../obligations/settings.md).

## World and scope

Promise and scope:

1. The answering file declares the audience, the central experience, the questions or formal effects it develops, its recurring rewards and the title's promise.
2. User-fixed title wording is not a taste question.
3. The answering file establishes where delivery begins and ends, its inclusions and exclusions, its relation to a larger work, and its release units with their number and duration ranges.
4. Where the user delegated these, a provisional plan is derived from the promise, material and production limits, names its grounds and uncertainty, and is revised when those grounds change.
5. A series establishes what recurs, what closes per release and what develops across releases. Scripts choose the release boundaries.
6. Scale serves the audience and available material, not copied size rules or padding.
7. The adopted representation is declared with what it cannot show, such as faceless polygon men, so later layers stage only what it carries and, for a commissioned generated film, decide facial expressions in beat reactions, which the generation prompt carries.
8. A dimension the commission excludes is excluded once here, not in every record.

Ownership and depth:

9. Every world rule, power, institution, person, relationship, place and object a scene will rely on has one owner, at a depth that matches its consequence.
10. Central and recurring agents have their situation, drives, values, fears, abilities and limits, knowledge and errors, observable behavior, recognizable appearance and, when heard, voice.
11. Nonhuman forces have their inputs, operation and thresholds without invented psychology.
12. Every presence a scene shows, such as an official, partner or crowd, has behavior for its whole presence.
13. Consequential relationships are defined from both sides with their change conditions; treatments own the actual progression.
14. Places and objects carry the identity, layout and use that scenes stage.
15. The shared material, costume, visual and audible grammar distinguishes factions, regions, eras and classes where they matter.

Coherence:

16. No two decisions contradict:
    - world rules against what agents can do;
    - what each agent knows against how they learned it;
    - relationships seen from each side;
    - baseline states against their change conditions.
17. The records explain why an initial threat, mystery or injustice persists: who could end it, and what stops them.
18. A contradiction is repaired at its earliest owner.
19. Under a declared [factual basis](../../../../../story/planning/requirements.md#factual-basis), derived chronology, ages, distances, travel, money and capacities agree across records, and every consequential participant the source names is identified.
20. Fiction's own quantities belong to research and code.

Records:

21. Each record gives its definition, settled content, conditions and open matters in a consistent order for its kind.
22. Each independently used subject has one file with its inseparable attributes together, each concept one term and each fact one owner, and a shared fact links to its owner.
23. Records state facts in prose for readers and never describe code. Rendering and generation controls belong to the production's source.
24. Records establish world facts, baseline states and change conditions, and leave chosen events, causal progression, choices, results, setups, payoffs and reveal order to treatments.
25. Where a fixed historical result or time-specific status constrains treatments, no record prewrites how the film reaches or reveals it.
26. A lasting state a later layer established, such as a wound or a revealed relationship, is recorded in settings and cites the unit that established it.
27. The cinematography domain owns the film's look. Editing and audience access belong to production instructions or work contracts, and a character's voice remains a world property.
