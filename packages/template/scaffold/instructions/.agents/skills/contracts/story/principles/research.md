# Research principles

Each study answers for its own subject. The research index points readers to its sources.

## Depicted practice

Can the writer or maker use this study to depict the practice at the required level?

- Distinguish what rules permit from what practitioners do. Ground frequency, tempo and spacing in observed passages or measurements. Identify deliberate stylization and, for an invented practice, the real analogue and where it differs.
- For a skilled performance, trace a continuous exchange from cue to outcome. Include simultaneous choices, waits, recovery, supports and obstacles that affect it. This applies even when installed modules supply all the movements.
- Establish the time scale before measuring a replay. For distance, establish scale in the plane of movement and account for camera motion. An uncalibrated view may establish order. Identify what a cut or occlusion leaves unobserved.
- For action beyond the installed vocabulary, describe the law in the performer's frame, including the starting distance, phases, key poses, reach, contact, weight transfer and timing it needs. Describe filming and sound practice where the commission calls for them, with targets the output can verify.

## Traceable sources

Can a reader find the source and tell what it establishes?

Give the source and locator, including the interval for footage. Distinguish reported measurements, direct observations, inferences and production choices. Give units, ranges, method and uncertainty for measured values. Identify what still needs evidence.
