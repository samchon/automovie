# Research principles

Each study answers for its own subject. The research index points readers to its sources.

## Depicted practice

<!-- @reference [Kinovea](https://kinovea.readthedocs.io/en/latest/userinterface/glossary.html#calibration) defines frame rate and spatial calibration. -->

Which production decision does the study support, and under what conditions? For a subject's appearance or another static fact, identify the feature the production can rely on.

For skilled performance, trace a continuous passage at the depicted level from cue to outcome, including simultaneous choices, waits, recovery, supports, obstacles and what practitioners do from each position they reach, such as a clinch. Ground frequency, tempo and spacing in observations or measurements, including for actions supplied by installed modules. Distinguish what rules allow from what practitioners do. Identify deliberate stylization or where an invented practice differs from its real analogue.

For a timing measurement, establish the replay's time scale. For a distance, establish scale in the plane of movement and account for camera motion. An uncalibrated view may still establish event order. Identify what a cut or occlusion leaves unobserved.

For action beyond the installed vocabulary, describe the law in the performer's frame. Include the starting distance, phases, key poses, reach, contact, weight transfer and timing it needs. For a study of camera or sound practice, give targets the output can verify.

## Traceable sources

Can a reader find the source and tell what it establishes?

Give the source and locator, including the interval for footage. Distinguish reported measurements, direct observations, inferences and production choices. Give units, ranges, method and uncertainty for measured values. Identify what still needs evidence.
