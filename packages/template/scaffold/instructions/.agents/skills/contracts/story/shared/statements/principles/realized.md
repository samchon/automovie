# Realized speech statements

## Speaker plausibility

Speakers:

1. Age, status, situation, knowledge, relationship, vocabulary, address, register, rhythm, dialect, avoidance and silence make each consequential line plausible and distinguish the speakers. Exception: speakers may share an ordinary reply.
2. The judgment rests on the exchange itself, not on an explanation about the speaker.
3. Authoring jargon and procedural codes appear in speech only when they exist in the world and this speaker would use them.
4. The inherited exchange's purpose and outcome are preserved across naturalistic, theatrical, ritual and other adopted forms.
5. Social and regional signals are chosen deliberately and reduce no community to an unexamined stereotype.

Action and line order:

6. The order of action and speech makes their relationship legible.
7. Delivery notes, speaker cues and neighboring action clarify the exchange without explaining again what the line has already done.
