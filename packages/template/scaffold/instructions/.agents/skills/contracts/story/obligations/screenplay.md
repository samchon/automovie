# Screenplay population obligations

Answer each question once across the selected screenplay. For recurring forms, voices and rhythms, compare the passages involved. If there is no recurrence to compare, name the scope read.

## Complete realization

Where does the screenplay deliver each central promise, and can the whole fit its commissioned format and duration?

- Trace the long-route script into the final scenes and back. On a short, trace the brief and the screenplay's own decisions. Follow recurring subjects across cuts for changes in place, possession, relationships and knowledge.
- Locate the passage that delivers each promise and estimate how long it plays. Check choices, consequences, reveals and image-sound transitions. A setup followed by its aftermath can leave the promised event missing. Allow an agreed delay or off-screen event.
- At construction or a text-only handoff, identify the basis for timing estimates, any justified duration ceiling, placeholders and outstanding production work. Distinguish missing material from redundant repetition and preserve functional refrains.
- At film delivery, compare the [measured release units and assembled cut](../../../review/delivery-review.md#measurement-and-comparison) with the plan. Use recorded speech, performed action and edit timing to trace shortfalls to their scene, event or partition. Identify any commissioned material still missing.

## Recurrent frames

Does each recurring form serve the circumstances in which it appears? A pattern may flatten the work even when each occurrence reads naturally alone.

Compare sentence shapes, directions, endings, gestures, modifiers and reactions. Synonyms and different props can mask the same repeated operation. Counts locate passages to read. Look for translated syntax, awkward combinations, unnecessary pronouns or flattened registers in the work's language. Preserve adopted tense, voice, period language and dialect.

For a refrain, habit or formal regularity, compare a workable version without it and identify what it loses. Repair a recurring cause across the affected passages.

## Voice distribution

Do recurring speakers keep recognizable voices as relationships and situations change?

Compare exchanges for vocabulary, address, register, breath, pace, interruptions, avoidance, silence and how arguments begin or end. Locate an explanatory tactic, metaphor or tone that erases differences between speakers. Preserve a shared form of address or ritual when its use reveals the relationship.

## Execution distribution

Does the pattern of entry, action, speech, silence and exit vary with the pressure of the scenes?

Compare how scenes open and close, who takes the floor, how long actions hold, what the camera shows and when a response arrives. Repeated headings and position cues may serve production. Test a fixed view, ritual or sustained stillness by what it achieves. Preserve staging and partition when repairing formulaic passages.

## Published version

Can the recipient identify what changed in an existing public release and what notice it requires?

Give the version, scope, reason and effect of each correction, whether editorial, technical, factual or story-changing. Align affected text, source, media and review and follow applicable platform requirements. A change to canon needs authority. For an unpublished work, state its status.
