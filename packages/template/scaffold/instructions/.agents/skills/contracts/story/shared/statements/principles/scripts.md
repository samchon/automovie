# Script statements

Contract: [principles/scripts.md](../../principles/scripts.md).

## Executable scene {#executable-scene}

1. The scene starts in an executable block, not a planning summary.
2. It establishes time, space, participants or forces, access, inherited pressure and the initiating condition.
3. It performs action, process, exchange and reaction, and ends in an observable exit state that follows from the final operation without restating its meaning.
4. Blocks follow meaningful changes of action, response, process, time, space, form or view.
5. Speakers are separated, and interruption, silence and simultaneous action sit beside the operation they change.
6. Block length and division carry urgency, duration, humor or stillness; [execution rhythm](../../naturalness/scripts.md#execution-rhythm) judges their molds.
7. Consequential questions, lies, bargains, refusals, interruptions and silences have the actual words, tactics, actions and reactions that change choice, knowledge, power or relationship.
8. Delivery is specified where tone, volume, pace or interruption changes meaning.
9. Movement, objects, environment, physical limits and time that affect the exchange are included.
10. Preparation, interaction, response and resulting state are executable, with no isolated pose and no result the next author must invent.
11. An observational, ritual, nonhuman or formal process has its actual operation, not an invented conflict.
12. No exchange is reported, no transition or reaction omitted, and no redundant explanation or incidental instruction conceals the decisive operation.

## Audiovisual staging {#audiovisual-staging}

1. At the opening and each change of place, the scene establishes:
    - the used area, and interior or exterior;
    - story day and time;
    - light sources and color;
    - weather;
    - mood through visible and audible conditions.
2. At each block, participants and movement are located, and the view has its subject, scale, angle, movement and point of focus. Exception: a declared fixed view supplies that rule once.
3. These are decided where they carry the action, identity, reaction or meaning, within what the adopted representation can show:
    - performance, expression and gaze;
    - appearance, clothing and possessions;
    - nonverbal sound.
4. Canonical names and appearance terms are used, and unchanged state is carried forward instead of recited in every block.
5. The scene distinguishes who speaks, whether the speaker is visible, what is audible within the world or only to the audience, and consequential overlap with action or sound.
6. Space, sightlines, props, light, costume and sound fit settings, research and inherited state, leaving none of these:
    - a missing view;
    - an impossible position or sightline;
    - an unsupported core appearance;
    - a habitual composition that conceals the scene's changing force.
7. A change of mood is expressed through its conditions, never by an interpretive label alone.
8. Inner meaning has visible action, sound, or an explicitly adopted voiceover, caption or inserted form.
9. Exception to the two statements above: declared theatrical, experimental and fixed-view forms express these decisions through their own notation.
10. Each participant's knowledge, belief, doubt and error has an actual path such as speech, observation, document, silence or absence. A camera view grants a participant no knowledge.
11. Lens numbers, music composition and inconsequential acting detail are left to production unless the work requires them.
12. The final screenplay still owns finished realization, transitions, attention and duration.
