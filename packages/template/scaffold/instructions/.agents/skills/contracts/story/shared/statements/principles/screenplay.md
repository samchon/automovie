# Final screenplay statements

Contract: [principles/screenplay.md](../../principles/screenplay.md).

## Audiovisual realization

Realization:

1. Events and execution are inherited from the actual scripts in a long work, or decided here on the short route.
2. The used time and space, participants or forces, conditions, action, spoken words, response and resulting state are established to the extent the chosen form uses them.
3. Settled events, choices, relationships, consequences, world values and reveal order are preserved.
4. Important choices and exchanges occur instead of becoming synopsis. Exception: an already complete short utterance or inserted artifact may stay concise.

Performance:

5. Every required observable movement, interaction, reaction, expression the adopted representation can show or, for a commissioned generated film, the generation prompt carries, and sound is decided through its preparation, development and resulting state.
6. Where physical action matters, support, weight transfer, contact or separation, recovery and connecting movement are executable.
7. No named technique, isolated key pose or emotional label stands in for that executable action.
8. A specialist performance meets its study's density and distribution targets for the depicted level, with chained sequences, tempo changes and spatial strategy drawn from its research.
9. A recurring motif is varied and interleaved with other exchanges, not repeated as one shape.
10. Authored visible participants and audible events have their actual performances in the commissioned output. Writing review establishes the plan and delivery review its realization.

Orientation and access:

11. The audience can follow who, when, where, what is happening, what a reference means and what its access permits.
12. Orientation arrives when a name, relationship, world fact or spatial arrangement first matters, without a lecture that stops the action.
13. Across joins, consequential position, direction, gaze, object and bodily state, and the source and temporal relation of sound are preserved, or their deliberate change is legible.
14. Visible and off-screen speech, audience-only narration, screen text, and simultaneous or successive actions are distinguished where that changes meaning.
15. What the camera shows a character cannot see grants that character no knowledge. Changes of access have legible boundaries, and withholding has a reason within the declared form.
16. Deliberate ambiguity or fragmentation still lets the audience tell its rule from an accidental omission.
17. Concise connection and summary may serve the selected experience, while sustained tension, humor or loss receives the time needed to be felt.

Attention and duration:

18. Important exchanges and sustained moments occur in full, and an action that recurs is written each time it happens.
19. Duration estimates are marked provisional until the performed action and the measured file establish them.
20. Concrete details come from the inherited scene and change action, relationship, atmosphere, understanding or thematic force.
21. Emotion is realized through the context of those details before its effect is demanded.
22. None of these replaces the audience's experience:
    - a static appearance inventory;
    - a sensory checklist;
    - a generic mood label;
    - an unnecessary factual explanation;
    - an after-the-fact interpretation.
23. Restrained detail, nonemotional atmosphere and deliberate gaps work when their cues let the audience experience their function.
24. Inward meaning reaches the audience through performance, image, sound, or an adopted narration or caption form.
25. Performance and sensory emphasis, images, motifs and image-sound relations are created as the scene needs them: choices, not categories to fill.
26. Each action is written as its preparation, execution and result in the observable progression, not as an inventory of procedures.
27. Places, objects, appearance, capacities and other world values arrive through actual parents. Realization changes no fact, closes no open relationship and alters no cause or outcome. Exception: on a short, the screenplay decides what the [brief leaves to it](../../../../../story/layers/brief.md#what-it-holds).
28. Where a parent leaves no room for this layer, its owner is repaired and no length is manufactured.

Language:

29. Dialogue, narration and screen text read as idiomatic in the work's adopted language, variety, period and register, with no translated construction nobody would use there.
30. Action lines are judged for precision, who does what, where and with what result, and never for literary style.

## Beat decisions

The scene file:

1. The scene file's first section opens with the standing state, stated once: the place, the cast present, any person or object only this scene shows, the camera default and the sound bed.

What each beat decides:

2. The beat names who and what acts by their settings facts; on a short, by brief decisions, or by the standing state for a person or object only this scene shows.
3. Each participant has an intent.
4. The beat states its outcome and each body's reaction or consequence.
5. Position, camera and sound appear only where they change from the standing state as earlier beats left it. Exception: stillness the scene decides is stated where it matters.
6. The beats cover the whole running time with the continuous response of everyone present. No stretch is summarized, as in "they dance".

What the modules can perform:

7. Each action is one the installed motion vocabulary performs, alone or joined with others, or the one its cited research law describes.
8. Each outcome is one the cited outcome table permits for the written intents and the performers' settings. An outcome the table forbids, or one no table covers, is undecided, not dramatic license.
9. The beat asks nothing the adopted representation cannot show, such as a facial expression on a polygon man. Exception: for a commissioned generated film, a reaction states the facial expression it needs, which the generation prompt carries.

Measurements:

10. Every coordinate and offset is left to the research laws that determine it and the code that derives it.
11. A beat may state its start second within its section as a plan, marked provisional like a duration estimate; the code derives the exact timing from the research laws.
12. A true anchor is written exactly:
    - a line, lyric or music event the picture syncs to;
    - a count the audience perceives;
    - a key event the edit lands on.
13. No decision is expressed only as a number, because the number leaves its meaning undecided.

Addresses:

14. A section, beat or scene file removed after others cite it keeps its address as `OMITTED`, a removed scene file keeping only its H1, and no address is reused or renumbered.
