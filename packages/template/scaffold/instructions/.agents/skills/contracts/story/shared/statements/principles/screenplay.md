# Final screenplay statements

Contract: [principles/screenplay.md](../../principles/screenplay.md).

## Audiovisual realization {#audiovisual-realization}

Realization:

1. Events and execution are inherited from the actual scripts in a long work, or decided here from the brief on the short route.
2. The used time and space, participants or forces, conditions, action, spoken words, response and resulting state are established to the extent the chosen form uses them.
3. Settled events, choices, relationships, consequences, world values and reveal order are preserved.
4. Important choices and exchanges occur instead of becoming synopsis. Exception: an already complete short utterance or inserted artifact may stay concise.

Performance:

5. Every required observable movement, interaction, reaction, expression the adopted representation can show, and sound is decided through its preparation, development and resulting state.
6. Where physical action matters, support, weight transfer, contact or separation, recovery and connecting movement are executable. No named technique, isolated key pose or emotional label stands in for them.
7. A specialist performance reads as its depicted practice, with chained sequences, tempo changes and spatial strategy drawn from its research, not one exchange shape repeated through the scene.
8. Authored visible participants and audible events have their actual performances in the commissioned output. Writing review establishes the plan and delivery review its realization.

Orientation and access:

9. The audience can follow who, when, where, what is happening, what a reference means and what its access permits. Orientation arrives when a name, relationship, world fact or spatial arrangement first matters, without a lecture that stops the action.
10. Across joins, consequential position, direction, gaze, object and bodily state, and the source and temporal relation of sound are preserved, or their deliberate change is legible.
11. Visible and off-screen speech, audience-only narration, screen text, and simultaneous or successive actions are distinguished where that changes meaning.
12. What the camera shows a character cannot see grants that character no knowledge. Changes of access have legible boundaries, and withholding has a reason within the declared form.
13. Deliberate ambiguity or fragmentation still lets the audience tell its rule from an accidental omission.
14. Concise connection and summary may serve the selected experience, while sustained tension, humor or loss receives the time needed to be felt.

## Attention and duration {#attention-and-duration}

1. Important exchanges and sustained moments occur in full, and an action that recurs is written each time it happens, varied as its practice varies.
2. Duration estimates are marked provisional until the performed action and the measured file establish them.
3. Concrete details come from the inherited scene and change action, relationship, atmosphere, understanding or thematic force, and emotion is realized through their context before its effect is demanded.
4. No static appearance inventory, sensory checklist, generic mood label, unnecessary factual explanation or after-the-fact interpretation replaces the audience's experience. Exception: restrained detail, nonemotional atmosphere and deliberate gaps work when their cues let the audience experience their function.
5. Inward meaning reaches the audience through performance, image, sound, or an adopted narration or caption form.
6. Performance and sensory emphasis, images, motifs and image-sound relations are created as the scene needs them: choices, not categories to fill.
7. Each action is written as its preparation, execution and result in the observable progression, not as an inventory of procedures.
8. Places, objects, appearance, capacities and other world values arrive through actual parents. Realization changes no fact, closes no open relationship and alters no cause or outcome.
9. Where a parent leaves no room for this layer, its owner is repaired and no length is manufactured.

## Beat decisions {#beat-decisions}

What each beat decides:

1. The beat names its participants by their settings facts, or by brief decisions on a short.
2. Each participant has an intent.
3. The beat states its outcome, and the consequence for each body with its duration and the mark it leaves.
4. It states the change in where everyone stands among the set's zones and landmarks. Exception: stillness the scene decides.
5. It states the camera's size, angle and movement.
6. It states all five sound layers: dialogue, foley, ambience, effects and music with its usage. A layer with nothing in it says `none`.
7. It states the continuity state it leaves.
8. The beats cover the whole running time with the continuous response of everyone present. No stretch is summarized, as in "they dance".

What the modules can perform:

9. Each action is one the installed human motion vocabulary performs, or the one its cited research law describes.
10. Each outcome is one the cited outcome table permits for the written intents and the performers' settings. An outcome the table forbids, or one no table covers, is undecided, not dramatic license.
11. The beat asks nothing the adopted representation cannot show, such as a facial expression of a faceless figure.

Measurements:

12. Every measurement is left to the research laws that determine it and the code that derives it: no coordinate, offset or time between exchanges is written. Exception: a true anchor, namely a line, lyric or music event the picture syncs to, a count the audience perceives, or a key event the edit lands on.
13. No decision is expressed only as a number, because the number leaves its meaning undecided.

Breakdown and addresses:

14. The scene's breakdown names by settings or brief link every cast member, crowd, prop, set, wardrobe and makeup state, effect, sound, camera and lighting element its beats use, and nothing they do not.
15. A scene or beat removed after others cite it keeps its address as `OMITTED`, and no address is reused or renumbered.
