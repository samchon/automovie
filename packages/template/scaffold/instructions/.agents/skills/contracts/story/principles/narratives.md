# Narrative unit principles

Each treatment event, script scene and screenplay section answers for its own progression and what it inherits.

## Unit progression

Does the unit create a recognizable change in events, understanding, relationships, emotion, pressure, rhythm or form that gives the audience a reason to continue?

Follow the unit from entry through action to result, tracing the cause, response, reversal, question or formal relation that connects them. Track consequential changes in time, place, knowledge, injury, appearance, clothing, possession, resources, status, relationships and promises. Mark a departure into memory, anticipation, repetition or habitual time and the return.

When a person drives a change, establish the desires, beliefs, fears, values, alternatives, limits and costs that bear on the choice. Give new names, rules and relationships enough context to follow it. An observational or nonhuman process can progress through its operation.

Identify what the exit earns and what carries the audience onward. A recurrence, rest, completed unit or intentional pause may serve the work.

## Parent differentiation

Does the unit do the work its parent leaves to it? In long work, treatments decide events and reveal order, scripts shape them into first-draft scenes and exchanges, and the screenplay writes the finished dialogue, action, sound and cuts. On a short, the screenplay develops the brief directly.

Compare the unit with the decisions it inherits. Where the route requires independent classification, record for each paragraph whether its wording, imagery, explanatory labels, opening or execution comes from the parent. A change of vocabulary can leave the execution copied.

Check inherited names, address, quantities, times, places and consequential states. Shared event order, a concise connection or an already complete artifact may carry over. Preserve needed values. A paragraph may do its work without adding a sensation or decision.

Treat an unresolved first judgment as a repair candidate. Recreate a copied passage from the parent decisions. After the rewrite, fail it again only for clear copying.

A parent can consume the next layer's work, force a failure, block a promise or leave a necessary decision open. Refer that defect to the upstream item for [treatments](../upstream/treatments.md#settings-repair), [scripts](../upstream/scripts.md#script-upstream-repair) or [screenplay](../upstream/screenplay.md#screenplay-upstream-repair) and repair the affected descendants.
