# Narrative unit principles

Each treatment event, script scene and screenplay section answers for its own progression and what it inherits.

## Unit progression

Does the unit create a recognizable change in events, understanding, relationships, emotion, pressure, rhythm or form that gives the audience a reason to continue?

- Trace entry, operation and result through cause, response, reversal, question or the adopted formal relation. Follow changes in time, place, knowledge, injury, appearance, clothing, possession, resources, status, relationships and promises. Mark departures to memory, anticipation, repetition or habitual time and the return.
- When a person drives change, show desire, belief, fear, values, alternatives, limits and cost as they matter. Give new names, rules and relationships the context needed for this choice. An observational or nonhuman process can progress through its operation.
- Identify what carries the audience onward and what the exit earns. A recurrence, rest, completed unit or intentional pause can serve the work without a cliffhanger.

## Parent differentiation

Does the unit do the work its parent leaves to it? In long work, treatments decide events and reveal order, scripts shape them into first-draft scenes and exchanges, and the screenplay writes the finished dialogue, action, sound and cuts. On a short, the screenplay develops the brief directly.

- Compare the unit with its actual parent decisions. Where the route requires [independent classification](../../../story/correction/inspection.md#independent-classification), record for each paragraph whether it borrows wording, imagery, explanatory labels or its opening and execution from the parent. A change of vocabulary alone can leave the execution copied.
- Check inherited names, address, quantity, time, place and consequential state. Shared event order, concise connection or an already complete artifact may carry unchanged. Preserve needed values without an inventory. A paragraph need not add a sensation or decision.
- Treat an unresolved first judgment as a repair candidate. Recreate a copied passage from the parent decisions. After such a rewrite, fail it again only for clear copying.
- Separately return a parent that consumes the next layer's work, forces a failure, blocks a promise or leaves a necessary decision open to the corresponding upstream item for [treatments](../upstream/treatments.md#settings-repair), [scripts](../upstream/scripts.md#script-upstream-repair) or [screenplay](../upstream/screenplay.md#screenplay-upstream-repair) and repair its affected descendants.
