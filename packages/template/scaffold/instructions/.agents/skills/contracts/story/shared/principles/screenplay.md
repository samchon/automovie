# Final screenplay principles

Each final screenplay H2, a section of one scene file, answers for the finished audiovisual experience that unit specifies. A long work realizes already executable scripts; a short realizes its brief and research and owns its events and scene execution directly. Neither route replaces finished realization with planning or polished wording.

Statements: [statements/principles/screenplay.md](../statements/principles/screenplay.md).

## Audiovisual realization

Does the unit realize its established events and performance through deliberate image, sound, attention, duration, juxtaposition and transitions, giving each moment the time its importance and pressure need, so the audience can follow and feel it? Transcribed directions, a shot inventory, commentary, decorative expansion or a mood label cannot establish this; a named technique leaves the next recipient to invent the performance; and dialogue density, cuts or a word count cannot prove pace.

## Beat decisions

Does each beat decide everything motion, sound, generation and assembly will realize, in terms the production's modules can perform, so that code composes and never chooses? A beat that leaves an intent, an outcome or a reaction open hands that decision to whoever writes the scene, a beat that restates what has not changed buries the decisions that did, and a number written where a decision belongs freezes a value the code should derive.
