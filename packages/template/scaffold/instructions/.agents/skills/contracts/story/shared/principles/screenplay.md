# Final screenplay principles

Each final screenplay H2 answers for the finished audiovisual experience specified by that unit. In a long work it follows already executable scripts; a short realizes its brief and research, owning necessary events and executable scene decisions directly. Neither route replaces finished audiovisual realization with planning or polished wording.

Statements: [statements/principles/screenplay.md](../statements/principles/screenplay.md).

## Does it realize its material as a finished audiovisual experience? {#audiovisual-realization}

Does the unit realize its established action, performance, staging, view, speech, silence and result through deliberate images, sound, attention, duration, juxtaposition and transitions, so that the audience can follow and feel it? Transcribed directions, a shot inventory, commentary or decorative expansion cannot establish this, and a named technique or emotional label leaves the next recipient to invent the performance.

## Do selected detail and time earn their effect? {#attention-and-duration}

Do image, sound, performance, speech, silence and transition allocate attention and time according to the moment's importance, pressure and intended audience experience? Dialogue density, cuts or a matching word count cannot prove pace, and an inventory or a mood label cannot replace the audience's experience.

## Does every beat decide what production must execute? {#beat-decisions}

Does each beat decide everything motion, sound and assembly will realize, in terms the production's modules can perform, so that code composes and never chooses? A beat that leaves an intent, an outcome, a presence or a sound layer open hands that decision to whoever writes the scene, and a number written where a decision belongs freezes a value the code should derive.
