# Coarse forms

A coarse form is the few primitives that tell a reader of the hint what a subject is, how big it is and where its parts are. It serves every subject that is neither a person nor an object an installed archetype offers.

## Parts

Use two to four simple volumes in their real arrangement to distinguish the subject ([Biederman 1987](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.94.2.115)). Name it in the generation prompt.

Add only parts a scene interacts with or moves separately. Define their affordances under entity work.

Keep only the parts whose [form tier](crafting/construction.md#form-tiers) the scene's camera distance resolves. A part the scene interacts with or moves stays at any size.

## Size

Size the form from the subject's record.

## Primitives

Use primitives or straight extrusions with only the sides needed at the scene's camera distance. A correctly sized low-poly piece from [Sources](sources.md) also works.

Leave out bevels, surface relief, ornament, texture and any interior no camera sees. Entity work says how the facts a coarse form cannot show reach the film.

## Outline check

Render its outline from the actual scene cameras beside the polygon man. Confirm the silhouette identifies it without the screenplay and the scale is correct. Follow entity inspection and handoff.
