# Coarse forms

A coarse form is the few primitives that tell a reader of the hint what a subject is, how big it is and where its parts are. A person takes the polygon man under [entity work](../development/entities.md#people); this page covers every other subject.

## Parts

People recognize an object quickly once two or three of its simple volumes and their arrangement can be made out ([Biederman 1987](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.94.2.115)). Build the two to four volumes that make the subject itself, each where the real one sits: a cylinder with an arc on its side is a mug, and the same arc over the top makes it a bucket.

The generation prompt names the subject, so the form only has to be told apart from the other subjects in its scene.

Add a further part only when a scene uses it: one a body grips, sits on, strikes or opens, or one that moves on its own pivot, such as a door or a lid.

## Size

Take every dimension from the subject's record. The hint's outline and depth are rendered from the form, so a wrong size or bulk comes back wrong in the film.

## Primitives

Use boxes, cylinders, cones, spheres, wedges and flat outlines extruded straight, each with the fewest sides that keep its outline at the scene's camera distance. A low-polygon kit piece from [Sources](sources.md) serves instead when it already has these parts at the record's size.

Leave out bevels, surface relief, ornament, texture and any interior no camera sees. [Entity work](../development/entities.md#what-stays-out) says how the facts a coarse form cannot show reach the film.

## Check

Render the form's outline from the cameras its scenes use, beside the polygon man. Stop when someone who has not read the screenplay could name the subject from that outline and its size is right against the figure, then accept it under [entity work](../development/entities.md#inspect-and-hand-off).
