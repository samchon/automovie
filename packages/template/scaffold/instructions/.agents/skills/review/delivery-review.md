# Delivery review

Identify the promised output from [project context](../production/project-context.md) and the [commissioned outputs](../production/SKILL.md#commissioned-outputs), inspect that complete output yourself with the unfiltered validation reports, and disclose what was not produced.

## Narrative

Review narrative only when the [screenplay choice](../story/workflow/routes.md#who-writes-the-screenplay) calls for narrative passes. Check the required [passes and handoff](../story/workflow/routes.md#completion), and lock your first response as a recipient before reading plans or evidence.

Then read every current body and contract and the answers of every enabled stage, and compare the beginning with its consequences and ending, part boundaries, promises and duration. A pilot establishes only its slice, and closed work keeps its boundary under [closed passes](../story/correction/repair.md#closed-passes).

## Video

You judge a film on stills, in two passes that answer different questions:

| Pass | Stills come from | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | The film's source, drawn with the README's capture command | Pose, contact, staging, camera and timing at any second and from any view |
| Artifact | The delivered or generated file itself, extracted at matched seconds with the README's capture of an encoded file | What the audience receives: every scene present, the raster, the transitions, the encode, and for a generated film the picture itself |

Make both passes for every delivery claim, because an accepted source pass does not show that the file holds it.

Review first as an audience that has not read the plans, and lock that response before opening the screenplay or code, covering the opening, the ending, the middle and every join. Then inspect contacts, support, facing, reactions, occlusion and continuity through the motion. Every screenplay beat reaches the picture through the scene that cites it; a function name or a citation proves neither invocation nor visibility.

Judge each output by what it promises:

- a whole motion hint carries staging, identity, camera, action and timing at its adopted representation, which polygon men and coarse forms do, with its control channels checked at matched frames under [control channels](../motion/control-channels.md);
- a selected generator is judged on its actual accepted inputs and takes under [generation](../video/generation.md);
- a finished direct 3D or generated film is judged by its own picture for preserved action, identity, continuity and audience effect, not by its conditioning, and a direct 3D film also for what [direct film](../video/direct-film.md#what-to-look-for) lists.

Ask the user for what only playback shows, as [what you can observe](../../../AGENTS.md#what-you-can-observe) sets, with the film time of each note, and record each report against that time so its repair can find the frames.

## Motion judgment

Judge the complete performed action in catalog cases, segments and the assembled film:

- Are the complete forms correctly proportioned, oriented and attached beside the bodies and objects they meet, at the camera's use scale?
- Do preparation, support, body movement, contact and recovery connect into the intended action, including the counterpart's response? Deliberate stillness remains valid.
- Do blocking and camera preserve the required geography, participants, contacts and reactions through the action and its joins?
- Does the picture show the screenplay's outcome, rather than only an event named for it?

Use the [capture plan](../visual-debugging/capture.md#plan) for views, motion states and full-size observations. Answer from the pictures; successful checks, sheet counts and hashes establish no visual verdict.

Record the observed cells, failing requirement and repair owner. Trace the cause through authored input, composition, native output and drawing under [correction rounds](../visual-debugging/correction-rounds.md#causes).

## Measurement and comparison

Review the picture densely before any delivery claim, so a flaw anywhere in the film reaches you before its audience. Cut the film into review segments, each one capture of a stretch of the film, and inspect every segment on its contact sheets at the capture's default spacing. Around every contact, cut and join, add the film's own consecutive frames, as [capture](../visual-debugging/capture.md#times) requires; the README owns the window, the default spacing and the options that select exact frames. With motion already accepted in its [catalog cases](../motion/catalog-cases.md), this pass judges staging, camera and timing. Repeat it on every segment a fix touches.

Study what [when to study](../story/planning/research.md#when-to-study) assigns to delivery review. Measure the research's visible targets on the displayed bodies and camera in those stills, because the resolved schedule only plans them, and its audible targets on the decoded audio, confirmed by the user's listening.

Measure the delivered file against the [delivery profile](../production/project-context.md#the-delivery-profile) by reading the file itself: container, codec, dimensions, frame rate, frame count, duration, scan, the color primaries, transfer, matrix and range it is tagged with, and for a film with sound its audio tracks, sample rate and loudness by the profile's measurement method. An untagged file is played with a guessed color matrix, so stills extracted from it can match the source while the audience sees shifted color. An encoder's exit or a matching nominal duration is not a measurement, and runtime comes from performed and measured timing, never from page or word count.

Keep an accepted delivery's sheets and measures where [media records](../production/media-records.md#files) puts them. Every later delivery claim sets each segment's sheets and measures beside the previous delivery's at matched times under [comparison](../visual-debugging/comparison.md). A regression holds delivery until it is repaired or the owner accepts it.

## Acceptance record

Every acceptance records the revision, the media and output identities, the checks completed, the observations and the unverified limits. A film's record is `docs/acceptance/<film>.md`, one tracked file per film, so a later revision is accepted against the same list, and it also holds:

- technical file completion and the separate verdict on the depicted practice, with rejected requirements and explicitly adopted limitations;
- the checklist: every scene and beat seen, on-model entities, contacts, presences, camera and continuity;
- the [measures](#measurement-and-comparison);
- the views and passes reviewed;
- what the user watched or heard, and what nobody did.

The catalog film has its record too, `docs/acceptance/catalog.md`, with one row per case giving the revision and views at which it was accepted. A film composes only cases that record lists, and a module changed after its row was written is accepted again before a film relies on it.

The record holds review's observations and answers no contract item, because [the graph](../contracts/SKILL.md#outside-the-graph) cannot establish what a picture shows.

## Assets

Inspect a model or visual export through its intended consumer, required views and promised controls, and its shape, contact, deformation and motion by [form inspection](../3d-modeling/inspection.md) at the uses [entity work](../development/entities.md#inspection-and-handoff) sets. Have the user listen to standalone audio as delivered.
