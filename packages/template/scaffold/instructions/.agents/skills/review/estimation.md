# Estimation

Estimation is a read-only quality assessment, made only on request. Do not edit files, research external facts, prescribe repairs or change stages, evidence or reviews, and never feed the scores to an approval reviewer or present them as human judgment or approval.

## Scope

Set the requested scope and artifact role. Unless narrowed, assess every selected layer body in the [document layout](../story/document-layout.md#documents).

Use [diagnostic stills](../visual-debugging/capture.md#times) for action, staging and conditioning. Score pace and rhythm from [continuous viewing](delivery-review.md#video).

Before judging the screenplay or video, choose at most two central experience axes from the intended experience [project context](../production/project-context.md) records or links, or state that none was declared.

## Judges

Use at least two fresh judges on the author's model, each judging twice independently. A light request may use one disclosed judge. Give scope, method, intended experience and unannotated material. Withhold author identity, generation method, contracts, Evidence and other judgments.

The coordinator assigns and aggregates. Only judges score. Read each unit once per judgment, using disjoint chunks and an inventory if needed. Lock reactions before scoring per file for settings, brief and treatments, and per scene or sequence for scripts, screenplays and videos.

## Axes and anchors

A judge scores only the axes of a card that fit the layer's recipient.

### Settings or brief card

Its axes are world appeal, generative potential, credibility, agents, concrete realization, navigation, expressive quality and distinctiveness.

### Treatments card

Its axes are story force, structure, attention and tension, consequential choices, emotional progression, ending, distinctiveness, usable specificity and expressive quality.

### Scripts card

Its axes are unit composition, playable scene force, attention and tension, spoken language, agents, staging and distinctiveness.

### Final screenplay card

Its axes are comprehension and attention, engagement, structure, agents, emotion and aftermath, voice and rhythm, concrete realization and distinctiveness.

### Delivered video card

Score delivered video only when observed. Add intelligibility and continuity to the screenplay axes. Use listener reports for sound.

### Card across layers

Its axes are arrival of the intended experience, upstream handoff, whole-work rhythm and identity of the work.

### Anchors

Score each fitting axis against these anchors:

| Anchor | Experienced effect | Reference share of weak units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | Exceptional effect sustained | Almost none |
| 80 | Clear effect with a few weaknesses | At most one tenth |
| 60 | Functional but weak or uneven | Over one tenth through three tenths |
| 40 | Weakness dominates | Over three tenths through half |
| 20 | The experience or handoff fails | Over half |

An 80 or 100 needs two supported effects. A 20 or 40 needs two supported losses. Otherwise score 60. Count each cause once.

## Comparison and report

Compare editions in both orders. Treat an order-dependent result as similar. Judge improvement by the experienced effect.

Average judgments by axis, then axes by card. Keep a separate final-experience figure and report judges' ranges as disagreement. Leave unassessed and inapplicable entries unscored.

Report scope, editions, intended experience, axes, models, judge counts, reading inventory, unavailable checks and human judgment status. Per card, report reactions, score inputs and results, effects, losses and comparisons. Compare only matching models, scope, editions, axes and method.
