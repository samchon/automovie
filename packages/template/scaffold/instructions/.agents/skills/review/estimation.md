# Estimation

Estimation is a read-only quality assessment, made only on request. Do not edit files, research external facts, prescribe repairs or change stages, evidence or reviews, and never feed the scores to an approval reviewer or present them as human judgment or approval.

## Scope

Fix the requested scope and artifact role; without a narrower request, assess every selected layer under `docs/body` and its frozen edition under `docs/revision`.

A motion hint is assessed for its visible action, staging and conditioning information, not as an uncommissioned generated film, and media in scope is inspected as stills of the actual complete file, at the spacing [delivery review](delivery.md#measure-and-compare) gives.

Before judging the screenplay or video, choose at most two central experience axes from the declared promise, or state that none was declared.

## Judges

Use at least two fresh judges with the author's model, each making two independent judgments; a light request may use one and must say so. Give them the promise, scope, this method and annotation-free bodies or media, and withhold author identity, generation method, contracts, evidence, reviews and each other's results.

The coordinator assigns and aggregates without voting. Judges read every selected unit, in non-overlapping chunks with a read inventory when needed, and lock reactions per file for settings, brief and treatments and per scene or sequence for scripts, screenplay and video before scoring.

## Axes and anchors

| Card | Experience axes |
| --- | --- |
| Settings or brief | World appeal, generative potential, credibility, agents, concrete realization, navigation, expressive quality, distinctiveness |
| Treatments | Story force, structure, attention and tension, consequential choices, emotional progression, ending, distinctiveness, usable specificity, expressive quality |
| Scripts | Unit composition, playable scene force, attention and tension, spoken language, agents, staging, distinctiveness |
| Final screenplay | Comprehension and attention, engagement, structure, agents, emotion and aftermath, audiovisual voice and rhythm, concrete realization, distinctiveness |
| Delivered video, if observed | The screenplay axes as its stills show them, plus intelligibility and continuity; sound axes only from a listener's report |
| Across layers | Arrival of the promise, upstream handoff, whole-work rhythm, identity of the work |

Score only axes that fit the layer's recipient; an unwaived frozen construction marks expression axes not applicable.

| Anchor | Experienced effect | Reference share of weak units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | Exceptional effect sustained | Almost none |
| 80 | Clear effect with a few weaknesses | At most one tenth |
| 60 | Functional but weak or uneven | Over one tenth through three tenths |
| 40 | Weakness dominates | Over three tenths through half |
| 20 | The experience or handoff fails | Over half |

An 80 or 100 needs two supported effects and a 20 or 40 two supported losses; otherwise score 60. Count one cause once.

## Comparison and report

Compare a frozen or previous edition with the current one in both presentation orders; an order-dependent result is similar, and more edits or length do not establish improvement.

Average judgments per axis, then axes per card, keep a separate final-experience figure, and report each judge's range as disagreement. Unassessed and not-applicable entries never become zeros.

Return the scope and editions, promise and central axes, judge models and counts, read inventory, unavailable inspection, each card's reactions, scores, calculation inputs, strongest effects and losses and comparisons, and whether human judgment exists. Compare scores only under the same models, scope, editions, axes and method.
