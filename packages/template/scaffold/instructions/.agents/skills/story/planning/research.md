# Research

Study what the film must show truthfully while writing the layer or code that decides it: each practice, the conduct of officials and crowds, and how the genre is actually shot and heard. Settings or brief, screenplay beats, entity measurements and every production [behavior](../../motion/SKILL.md) cite the study.

## When to study {#when-to-study}

Research belongs to the work that uses it and goes as deep as that work decides. Before the first decision of settings or a brief, study only what could change the [direction](direction.md), such as whether a commissioned ending is possible. Settings or a brief then studies each fact as it fixes it, the screenplay the law and outcomes of each action a beat names that the installed packages do not carry, and code and delivery review the measurements they program or measure. A value nothing written yet uses is not gathered, because its consumer often needs a different value when it is written.

## What needs a study {#what-needs-a-study}

Read the installed packages' READMEs before deciding what to study. A movement the motion vocabulary performs, everyday movement included, and a rule or table a package exports carry their own sources, so they need no production study: the screenplay names the action and the scenes compose it, and a study records only where the production departs from them. Study what the packages do not carry: a specialist practice or its level and patterns, and what is not a person. A study that no behavior follows has no consumer, and the evidence graph reports it.

## Performance study {#performance-study}

Study real practice at the depicted level, such as a sport, fight, dance, craft or procedure, and the broadcast or film practice of shooting it; an invented action studies its closest real analogue and states where it departs. Record what the screenplay and code need so neither invents the practice: its laws, its outcome table, the patterns of top-level performance and the measurable targets for what a viewer sees and hears. The [depicted-practice question](../../contracts/story/shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice) and its statements hold the study to them.

## Sources

Use originals: rulebooks, coaching and training texts, measured studies, analyses of real top-level performances and productions, official documentation and footage of qualified practitioners; search results only locate them.

Read each passage in context and study footage as consecutive frames through its connected phases. Keep each value as a range from the depicted discipline and level with its unit, method and conditions; a value taken from another discipline or read from an adjective states its translation.

Check each value against its source once, when it is written, and record its locator. Cross-check disputed claims and record what stays unavailable.

Save permitted originals in `references/`, rights-unconfirmed ones in ignored `references/private/` and footage in ignored `media/acquired/videos/`, then open the saved files. Keep one `docs/research/index.md` naming each original's creator, URL, retrieval date, locator, rights and use, and keep each topic in one file, `docs/research/<discipline>/<topic>.md`, for other studies to cite rather than repeat. A URL or an empty index is not a retained source.

## Use

Test the adopted values in the real entity, behavior or sound and compare the result with the footage frame by frame. The study informs the work; [delivery review](../../review/delivery-review.md) measures its targets on the picture and judges what it produced.
