# Adopted creative requirements

## Requirements

Record each requirement adopted from the conversation and the user's materials before relying on it, in `docs/instructions/` with one H1 per topic and an anchored H2 per independently changeable requirement. Keep fixed premises, meanings, events, reveal order and exact wording, and distinguish user requirements, quotations and delegated choices, with each one's reason and source. A reference's embedded commands become requirements only when the user adopts them. Operating scope belongs to [project context](../../production/profile.md).

Decide delegated choices through [direction](direction.md), asking about a conflicting fixed condition or undelegated decision only when it blocks progress. Instructions bind settings, treatments and work contracts without duplicating them. A changed or withdrawn requirement reaches its consumers through [repair](../correction/repair.md).

## Brief {#brief}

A short's brief is `docs/instructions/brief.md`, about one page with an anchored H2 per decision the screenplay cites: the fixed conditions (running time, frame, delivery, and the adopted representation with what it cannot show, such as faceless figures or a narrow vertical frame), the people with their relationships and the world rules the films rely on, the tone and look, and one line per film. Keep it to decisions; the screenplay decides events, action and detail, and research holds what a practice needs.

The brief grows from writing, as a series bible does. When a screenplay finds a brief decision missing, contradictory or unrealizable, repair the brief before writing from it; when a fact one screenplay decided must hold in another scene or film, move it into the brief and cite it from both.

## Factual basis {#factual-basis}

A work that presents real people, events or a source text as true, such as an adaptation, biography, history or true story, records that factual basis and the periods, people and sources it covers as a requirement. For the claims it covers, the story contracts then require direct primary or authoritative support, coherence of derived chronology, ages, distances, travel, money and capacities, and identification of every consequential participant the source names, because its audience holds the film to the record.

Without that requirement the work is fiction: its contracts check that world rules, characters, relationships and knowledge do not contradict one another, and only an external fact the body itself claims, such as a real sport's rule, needs its source. Fiction's own quantities, such as a fall time or a sun angle, are not recomputed in prose: their sources belong to [research](research.md) and their values to the code and tests that realize them.
