# Scope and graph ownership

## Choose the participating work

Ordinary coding and standalone assets can retain the default engineering configuration. Add actual asset or delivery duties where their respondents can establish them; a blank project needs no narrative mode, stages, or gate.

For narrative work, long form retains settings → treatments → scripts → final screenplay. Scripts and screenplay each have construction and expression-revision passes; settings and treatments finish within construction. Short mode is the authorized direct-screenplay route and retains its expression and review duties. A disabled pass drafts its complete body; it does not waive the route's responsibilities. [Narrative completion](../narrative-completion/SKILL.md) owns when those stages open and close.

Complete work is the default scope. Select a first-part pilot only when the user commissions that slice, preserve work-wide settings and the exact part's inherited lineage, and identify which whole-work obligations it cannot establish. Restore complete scope and complete populations before claiming the whole work complete. Route an expansion or replacement of closed material through [upstream repair](../upstream-repair/SKILL.md).

## Bind claims to their owners

Use [contract adoption](../contracts/adoption.md) to decide the meaning and narrowest honest respondent of a new duty. Production-specific and language-specific questions belong in their corresponding story-contract families. Unit principles, whole-population obligations, discovery, upstream repair, expression revision, and independent review remain distinct responsibilities even when related questions share a checklist.

Adopt additional narrative claims through the production configuration with their actual layer and pass. Read the installed package's composition and ownership constraints rather than inventing parallel ownership records. A scene answers its own behavior; the designated population respondent answers a whole-work duty.

Engineering support duties stay outside creative bodies, frozen and working editions, creative evidence and review, narrative instructions, and story-contract inputs. Physical or logical aliases and regular-file hard links do not evade that boundary, including for inactive layers, disabled descendants, or work outside a selected pilot. A duty consuming protected narrative inputs needs an explicitly owned narrative work claim. Use the installed API's supported composition when changing these selections.

## Preserve actual relationships

Treatments consume settings; scripts and screenplay cover the treatments they realize. Event-to-scene coverage can be many-to-many when their partitions differ. Final screenplay preserves its actual script file and section lineage; rewriting working editions preserve the corresponding frozen construction identities. Keep source citations, selections, and respondent addresses consistent with those relationships.

For commissioned execution, keep formal coverage from final screenplay units to executable shots and from shots into the actual edit where an edit is part of the deliverable. Use the existing Evidence API for this many-to-many coverage when verifying implementation, rather than making a draft assert a future program exists. The handoff must establish which required units reached the requested hint or finished artifact. A source function alone cannot prove inclusion or visible effect.

Asset and delivery claims use builders or scoped observation and handoff reports that can establish the actual output. A coarse or stick-figure hint may require accurate motion without photorealism; a screenplay-only handoff cannot establish playback. [Delivery review](../delivery-review/SKILL.md) owns inspecting the commissioned output and recording what remains unobserved.
