# Composition and creative ownership

Use this resource to choose a narrative route, distinguish its layers, and adopt the work's form. [Production layout](../production/layout.md) owns file locations; [narrative completion](../narrative-completion/SKILL.md) owns pass order and handoffs.

## Four distinct kinds of work

A long film or series retains settings → treatments → scripts → final screenplay. Keep all four responsibilities even when one is brief; compress or regroup content rather than replacing a layer with a description of its parent.

| Layer | Owns | Hands onward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Settings](../settings/SKILL.md) | World facts, baseline states, constraints, characters and relationships, places, objects, knowledge boundaries, and sensory consistency | Usable facts with conditions, uncertainty, sources, and one semantic owner |
| [Treatments](../treatments/SKILL.md) | Events, causes, choices, exchanges, consequences, setup and payoff, information release, and the complete progression | A storyline another author can execute without inventing decisive events |
| [Scripts](../scripts/SKILL.md) | Part and episode partition; fully executed scenes with blocking, actual dialogue, reactions, meaningful performance, stage, light, sound, and viewing position | Complete playable scenes rather than an outline with scene headings |
| [Final screenplay](../screenplay/SKILL.md) | The audience's complete audiovisual experience: selected image and sound, access, attention, duration, juxtaposition, and transitions | Final production text traceable into executed shots and the edit |

Scripts already supply enough detail for a performer and filmmaker to execute each scene. Final screenplay makes independent decisions about what receives attention, what image and sound reveal together, which reaction holds, what silence changes, and how a transition carries the effect. Reformatting a script or appending shot numbers does not supply that realization. Preserve inherited events, decisions, results, fixed values, and required information order while choosing their audiovisual expression.

A baseline relationship belongs in settings; its change belongs to the event causing it. A historical outcome can constrain the work without settings owning its chosen dramatic path. Possible knowledge, the moment someone learns something, and the audience's access to evidence are distinct. Cite their owners rather than making each layer a second biography or encyclopedia.

## Form and scope

Use [instructions](../instructions/SKILL.md) to distinguish user constraints from author choices. Preserve audience, central experience, language, information access, format, and scope through their owners. Work contracts own production-specific form, rather than hiding it in settings prose or imposing a generic structure everywhere.

An explainer, demonstration, music video, abstract sequence, or contemplative film follows its chosen progression. The shared contracts do not require invented conflict, a protagonist, a twist, uniform scene lengths, or a cliffhanger. Declare the form's actual organizing effect where a common question depends on it.

Choose scope before multiplying scenes. Complete work needs a whole event progression supporting its promised duration and ending. A first-part pilot is an explicit commission: preserve work-wide settings and identify the slice and dependencies. It demonstrates selected material and its production path, not completion of the whole work.

## The short route

For a short, choose short mode and write the complete final screenplay under `docs/body/screenplay`. The request and screenplay header can provide a sufficient brief. Add useful supporting facts or event planning without fabricating empty upstream layers or false lineage.

Direct entry skips the first three layers, not discovery, selected screenplay contracts, complete construction, expressive rewriting, evidence, independent review, or the commissioned delivery. It retains fidelity, coherent screen action, continuity, and intelligibility. An asset-only commission needs no narrative pipeline.

## Construction and expression

Construction makes a complete layer; settings and treatments correct their documents within it. Scripts and final screenplay each also receive [expressive rewriting](../rewriting/SKILL.md), preserving construction decisions while replacing awkward dialogue, redundant instructions, mechanical rhythm, and habitual imagery. Final screenplay is not the scripts expression pass. Follow [narrative completion](../narrative-completion/SKILL.md) for current reviews and handoffs.

## From screenplay to the commissioned artifact

The final screenplay describes the intended work even when the commissioned implementation is a film-generation motion hint. Use [motion](../motion/SKILL.md) to program the main scenes' performance and staging, then assemble the whole video. Match simple figures, framing, and useful objects to the intended performance. Detailed representation is optional; the action must still be accurate. Fixed 2D images can support appearance or assets but cannot replace that motion video; acquisition follows [3D modeling](../3d-modeling/SKILL.md), not a new narrative layer.

Keep required revelations and transitions traceable into source and the actual edit. A separately commissioned screenplay can end at reviewed text; a film hint cannot end at stills or pilot clips. An explicitly requested finished direct 3D film retains its picture, sound, and whole-video requirements without diffusion. [Delivery review](../delivery-review/SKILL.md) checks the actual commissioned output, performed timing, and recipient's access. Finishing a motion hint does not claim that a finished diffusion film exists.
