# Programmed motion

Realize the requested performance through code, in the order below, because each step can only use what the one before settled. Read [project context](../production/profile.md), the final screenplay, its [research](../story/planning/research.md) and the installed engine APIs first. A single moving instance needs only its promised behavior; a whole-video commission needs its complete progression.

1. Confirm that every practice, official, crowd and camera style the scenes show has a cited research study with its laws, outcome tables and measurable targets. Invented technique, illegal targets, one-axis movement, idle officials and a static camera all come from skipping it, so a missing study is written before any behavior.
2. Start from the low-cost entities that [entity work](../development/entities.md#coarse-first) builds.
3. Write the laws, interactions and consequences under the [behaviors contract](../contracts/development/behaviors.md), each unit-tested against its study's values through [testing](../development/testing.md), and watch them in the moving Viewer with a joint overlay. Contact and reactions use the installed engine through [physical feedback](physics.md).
4. Compose one short contiguous segment scene by scene under [staging](staging.md) and [frame evaluation](timeline.md), with its entities, performance, camera and sound, and pass [segment review](segments.md) before extending from its accepted ending, so defects do not spread through unwatched motion.
5. Once low-cost performance works, upgrade selected entities to the commissioned detail within measured budgets, and recheck the contacts, deformation, visibility and timing the upgrade can change.

Use [retargeting](retargeting.md) for external clips or changed rigs and [method research](research.md) when choosing an algorithm. Hand scene output and its timing to [finishing](../production/finishing.md).
