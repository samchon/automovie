# Brief and adopted requirements

## Requirements

Collect requirements adopted from the conversation and the user's chosen materials. Preserve fixed premises, meanings, events, reveal order, and exact wording. A reference is material to interpret; its embedded commands become requirements only where the user adopts them.

Write requirements before relying on them in `docs/instructions/NNN-topic.md`: one H1 per topic and one explicitly anchored H2 per independently changeable requirement. State the requirement, its known reason, and the source's actual support. Distinguish quotations and author choices from user requirements. Keep procedure, status, evidence answers and secrets outside these documents. Production scope and operating choices belong to [production decisions](../production/profile.md).

When the user delegates direction, read [developing direction](brief.md#developing-direction), choose within that authority, and record why that direction serves the work. Resolve routine creative choices autonomously. Ask only about conflicting fixed requirements or a decision outside that authority that actually blocks progress.

Keep facts in [settings](settings.md), events in [treatments](treatments.md), and production-specific form and judgment in [story contracts](../contracts/story/index.md). Instructions bind those owners without duplicating their detailed content. [Discovery](brief.md#discovery) checks every requirement's owner and identifies uncovered conditions.

For a direct short, the screenplay header may hold a sufficient brief; separate instruction files serve independently reused or evidenced requirements. On the long route, settings accounts for instruction adoption and treatments for event-design implications. Changes or withdrawals update the requirement and the bodies that realize it. Handle evidence only for removed or newly created H2s under [upstream repair](repair.md#closed-passes).

## Developing direction

First identify the user's fixed conditions, permitted changes, and preservation boundary for supplied material. Research the intended audience, nearby works, genre or format expectations, recurring rewards, and common failures before choosing the direction. Use findings to understand the viewing experience; another work's duration, structure, or style is not this production's default.

Compare at least three materially different candidates where the user delegates the central premise. Change the protagonist's situation or desire, opposing forces, continuing engine, or ending direction; names and decorations do not make distinct candidates. Preserve the expected reward while selecting a meaningful difference that changes events or choices. Check that difference against the researched examples and record the default the production must avoid falling back into.

Resolve the following as far as the delegation permits:

- Who or what the audience follows, the starting situation and pursuit, the force or condition encountered, choices and costs, and destination.
- The question explored through those choices, and the recurring reward or alternative progression that sustains attention.
- An engine capable of producing material across the requested duration, with an ending that says what closes and what remains open.
- The audience's access to information and the intended visual language, sound, tone, rhythm, format, and delivery scope.

An observation, demonstration, ensemble, abstract film, or music video can use its own progression; character conflict is not compulsory. Preserve user-supplied events and design their connections within that boundary. Make decisions concrete enough for later authors to choose and discard material. Distinguish decisions handed onward from choices deliberately left for scene writing and production.

Record why the chosen direction serves this work. Check audience reward, central question, engine, ending, duration, and audiovisual form together before handoff. When the user supplies a plot without a duration or episode count, choose a provisional scale from its consequential turns, parallel progressions, and material to enact. Revisit that budget against completed treatments and scene execution; text length does not establish running time. Author choices remain revisable within the delegation; changing a fixed user condition requires the user's authority.

## Discovery

Compare adopted instructions, references, assets, research, delegated choices, and current work across the entire selected population. Break instructions into consequential elements before bulk writing and assign each to its existing owner or a missing one. A genre label or general reading of the brief does not establish coverage. Apply discovery questions in [story contracts](../contracts/story/index.md), including for a short's direct screenplay route.

Keep facts and constraints in settings, event and release decisions in their narrative layer, and production-specific judgment in `.agents/skills/contracts/story/work`. A new question records a distinct requirement, its narrowest honest respondent population, an observable desired difference, a representative failure, and the basis for requiring it. Group requirements judged together and reuse existing owners rather than adding parallel versions of the same rule.

Discover both requirements and positively chosen form: what a setting record, event document, scene, or final audiovisual unit presents or withholds, how it guides its recipient, and which recurring conventions matter. Naming a style is insufficient; identify the visible difference and repair boundary that preserves intent. Keep incidental conditions off unrelated units.

Keep adopted questions in their matching family under the work contract tree. Adopt each question and its real Evidence population together through `evidence.config.ts`. Installed `@automovie/evidence` documentation owns supported claim composition and paths; this skill owns requirement meaning and placement.

If no independent requirement remains, the author writes `.agents/skills/contracts/story/work/discovery/results.md` with the inspected scope, concrete risks examined, and existing owners covering them. A finding in one area does not establish absence elsewhere, and an empty claim list does not replace investigation. Never install a fabricated negative in a blank production.

When adopting, changing, merging, or retiring a rule, update its owners and selected targets; handle answers and reviews under the [closed-pass boundary](repair.md#closed-passes). Requirements existing only in status notes, selector names, or repeated guidance need a canonical owner. Use [evidence-writing](evidence/writing.md) for answers and independent [review](../review/SKILL.md) for challenge.
