# Upstream repair exposed by final screenplay

Each final screenplay H2 answers about its actual script parents and their treatment and settings causes. A short work authorized to begin at screenplay instead identifies the brief and creative constraints that serve as its actual parents; it does not claim nonexistent upstream stages. Repair follows the [closed-pass boundary](../../../../screenwriting/repair.md#closed-passes).

## What did audiovisual realization prove missing or unusable upstream? {#screenplay-upstream-repair}

Identify the exact earliest owner of any missing, contradictory, overdetermined, or unrealizable event, execution, access, relationship, knowledge, staging, world value, or form exposed while creating the final audiovisual experience. Trace a script defect to its treatment or settings cause where applicable, and inspect designs that force a named failure or prevent an adopted goal or promise: a recognition or lesson assigned to be said or experienced repeatedly; prewritten explanation, record, certainty note or anticipation assigned as narrator text; a list of arguments a person must recite; a setup with no available payoff; or a promised experience assigned nowhere. Recording a fact's certainty in treatments remains a legitimate design decision; the defect is assigning the note as an explanatory line the audience must receive. Correct the earliest owner's body locally yourself before writing from that repair. The answer names the owner, defect and corrected passage; reporting it while covering it downstream fails. Name future verification as pending rather than require unmade output to complete this writing stage. A polished transition cannot conceal unexecuted action or an unsupported result; direct settings evidence cannot replace an absent script decision. If no repair is needed, identify concrete actual parent decisions tested unchanged and how they survived realization. A short work tests its actual brief and screenplay-owned decisions without inventing skipped settings, treatments, or scripts.
