# Final screenplay principles

Each final screenplay H2 answers for the finished audiovisual experience specified by that unit. In a long work it follows already executable scripts; a short work authorized to start here realizes its actual brief and owns the necessary scene decisions directly. Neither path replaces finished audiovisual realization with planning or polished wording.

## Does it realize its material as a finished audiovisual experience? {#audiovisual-realization}

Does the unit transform its established action, performance, staging, view, speech, silence, and result through deliberate images, sound, attention, duration, juxtaposition, and transitions? Inherit these from actual scripts in a long work, or decide them here within the actual brief for an authorized short path. Preserve the settled events, choices, relationships, consequences, world values, and reveal order, while creating image and sound relationships, performance emphasis, motifs, and temporal experience. Merely transcribing script blocks, expanding their directions, listing shots, summarizing the story, or filling it with commentary fails. An already complete short utterance or inserted artifact may be preserved without decorative expansion; the layer's contribution is the resulting experience, not extra words.

Can the audience follow who, when, where, what is happening, what a reference means, and what information its chosen access permits? Supply usable orientation when a name, relationship, world fact, or spatial arrangement first matters, without a lecture that stops the action. The camera may show what a character cannot see; that does not grant the character knowledge. Changes of access need legible boundaries, and withholding needs a reason within the declared form rather than conveniently hiding an already visible fact. Deliberate ambiguity or fragmentation still gives the audience a way to distinguish its rule from an accidental omission.

## Do selected detail and time earn their effect? {#attention-and-duration}

Do image, sound, performance, speech, silence, and transition allocate attention and time according to the moment's importance, pressure, and intended audience experience? Let important exchanges and sustained moments occur, and compress or omit repetition that adds no experience while preserving required action and results. Mark duration estimates as provisional until actual performance, recorded sound, and playback establish them. Dialogue density, cuts, or a matching word count cannot prove pace.

Choose concrete details from the inherited scene that change action, relationship, atmosphere, understanding, or thematic force; realize emotion through their context before demanding its effect. A static appearance inventory, generic mood label, unnecessary factual explanation, or after-the-fact interpretation cannot replace the audience's experience. Inward meaning must reach the audience through performance, image, sound, or an adopted narration or caption form. New images and implications may realize an established scene but cannot silently create world facts, close an open relationship, or change its cause or outcome. If the script leaves no room for this layer, repair the earliest owner instead of manufacturing length.
