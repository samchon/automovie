# Research for production decisions

Use this method when a creative fact, appearance, physical behavior, sound or production technique needs outside evidence. Research serves a concrete authoring or implementation decision; existing [creative ownership](../screenwriting/workflow.md#creative-ownership) and production tasks keep their responsibilities.

## Define the question and sources

State what must be decided, what evidence could support it and what remains an authored choice. Select original research, authoritative textbooks, official documentation, standards, collections or qualified practitioners appropriate to that claim. Search results and summaries help locate originals. Cross-check consequential or disputed claims with independent authoritative evidence, preserving disagreements and differences in conditions. Verify the relevant edition, date and current version when rules, tools, rights or other changing facts affect the decision.

## Study a specialist performance before writing it {#performance-study}

When the work shows a practiced physical activity, such as a sport, fight, dance, craft, procedure or the conduct of officials and attendants, study real practitioners at the depicted level before settings and screenplay describe it. An adjective such as "highly trained" or a few named techniques cannot stand in for that practice, and invented movement written from general impressions becomes the motion the film shows.

Gather primary material in the reference archive `references/`: rules, coaching and training texts, measured studies and footage of qualified practitioners at the relevant level, with timestamps. Write the synthesis in `docs/research/` so the settings, screenplay and motion authors can use it without reopening every source:

- the repertoire: each action with its phases, typical timing, distances and the body's support and weight transfer;
- how actions chain: sequences, combinations, setups, feints and transitions, with typical intervals;
- spatial strategy: positions, angles, lines, routes, use of the boundary or space, and how participants change them;
- tempo: how activity alternates between probing, bursts and resets, and how it changes over the duration;
- responses: how partners, opponents, officials, bystanders or animals react, move and position themselves;
- skill markers: what distinguishes the depicted level from a beginner, including economy, timing and defense integrated with action;
- measurable values the motion must meet, with their method and uncertainty.

Mark which observations come from which source, what is inference and what is the film's own choice. The settings record owns the adopted facts; this study is their evidence.

## Inspect the original material

Read the relevant original passage with its surrounding argument, methods, captions and limitations. Open the actual images and figures; listen to sound and watch video where the claim depends on them. Inspect enough resolution and context to identify the feature being used, including scale, view, lighting, playback speed and any editing that affects interpretation.

For motion or physical behavior, inspect connected preparation, action, interaction and recovery, using available views to understand support, coordination and consequences. Posture is one example: study how body parts relate to the supporting surface and how the pose is entered and left. A still or projected angle supports only what it shows; a three-dimensional measurement needs suitable calibration or other supporting evidence.

Distinguish reported measurements, directly observed features, your inference and the production's staging choices. Keep units, coordinate conventions, measurement method, uncertainty and applicable conditions with quantitative claims. Quote only the necessary passage within applicable quotation limits. Record inaccessible material and unresolved gaps rather than filling them with a confident conclusion.

## Retain usable local references

Actually save the useful permitted originals and selected reference images, PDF pages, figures, recordings or clips in the [reference archive](layout.md#source-ownership) `references/`, then open the saved files to verify their content. Preserve enough surrounding pages or connected phases to interpret an excerpt. Keep originals identifiable and distinguish crops, annotations, conversions and other derivatives; retain the original locator and transformation used. A URL or an empty index is not a retained reference.

Maintain one current index in `docs/research/index.md` linking each local file to its creator, title, original URL, edition or retrieval date, page/figure/time locator, content identity, rights and required attribution. State the specific feature or claim it supports, its limits and the settings, asset, controller, sound or shot decision that uses it. Record unavailable downloads honestly and retain other permitted useful evidence. Keep restricted or rights-unconfirmed material in ignored `references/private/`; public access alone grants no redistribution rights.

## Apply and verify the decision

Keep a concise task-oriented synthesis in `docs/research/`, linked from its index and paired with selected local visual or other extracts. Explain the applicable principles, conditions, actionable constraints and meaningful variations well enough for an implementer to apply them without reopening the full source. Cite exact source locators and extracts, retaining the distinction between evidence, inference and authored choices and their unresolved limits.

Translate supported features into the meaningful constraints the task needs: model proportions and frames, rig axes and supported movement, material response, light, acoustic character or performance timing. Keep these reusable across the intended uses while naming the actual source conditions and any stylized departure. Use installed APIs for implementation and [execution traceability](../evidence/execution.md) to connect the chosen evidence to its actual consumer.

Test the resulting controls through the real model, rig, scene or audio host under comparable conditions, then judge the adopted presentation. [Asset inspection](assets.md#inspect-the-commissioned-use), [motion](../motion/SKILL.md), [audio acquisition](../audio/acquisition.md) and [delivery review](../review/delivery.md) own those observations. Retained still studies inform motion; they do not replace continuous normal-speed viewing, listening or complete-film review. Keep source support, implementation checks and observed output distinct in the current record.
