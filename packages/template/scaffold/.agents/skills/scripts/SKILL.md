# Scripts

Recreate reviewed treatments as scenes performers and filmmakers can execute: actual actions, reactions, exchanges, dialogue, physical staging, sound, atmosphere, and viewing position. The scripts questions in [story contracts](../contracts/story/index.md) judge this layer's complete execution. An event summary with scene headings is not a script.

After [discovery](../discovery/SKILL.md), settle part, episode, and meaningful-unit partition before bulk scene writing. Assign every treatment to its script consumers and every script to real treatment parents; relationships can be many-to-many. Preserve information release. Choose boundaries and titles for the viewing material and release format, rather than counting treatment files or inventing scenes to meet a duration. Report unresolved parents before fixing that partition.

Use `docs/body/scripts/NNN-part/NNN-episode.md` with one H1 and ordered explicitly anchored H2s. Each part has an `index.md` with its H1 and links to episode files. A continuous film can use one part and a suitable sequence division without pretending it is an episodic release. Hand identities and order to the final screenplay.

Write through what each participant tries, encounters, changes, says, and hears. Allocate readable blocks and duration to the exchange's weight; compress transit and repeated material that adds no experience. Give actual words and short performance directions where delivery changes meaning. The filmmaker should not need to invent core action, appearance, staging, light, sound, atmosphere, or viewing emphasis. Leave finishing choices open where they do not carry those decisions.

Inherit world facts and uncertainty through treatments. Cite every realized treatment rather than bypassing an incomplete parent with settings evidence. Repair missing or contradictory facts and events at their first owner. Use the [working notation](../../../docs/guides/storytelling.md#working-files) to make action, speech and changes of scene immediately readable. Before answering evidence, read the complete script as its recipient, follow the staged action and consecutive spoken exchanges, compare dispensable lines with a simpler alternative, and repair the work first.

Complete construction [evidence](../evidence-writing/SKILL.md) and independent [review](../review/SKILL.md), then [rewriting](../rewriting/SKILL.md) before [screenplay](../screenplay/SKILL.md) construction. Scripts is a complete scene-execution layer; the next layer owns a different, final audiovisual realization. Follow the [lifecycle](../../../docs/guides/narrative-lifecycle.md) for current parents and reviews.
