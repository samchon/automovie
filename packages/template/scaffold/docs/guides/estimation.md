# Read-only experience estimation

Use this guide with the [estimation skill](../../.agents/skills/estimation/SKILL.md) when the user asks for a quality assessment. It preserves independent recipient reactions and edition comparison separately from creative approval.

## Scope and independent judgments

Fix the requested scope. Without a narrower request, assess all existing selected narrative layers under `docs/body` and their frozen construction editions under `docs/revision`. Read configuration only to identify scope, and locate the declared audience and promise without using stage or approval status as evidence of quality. Before judging the final screenplay or delivered video, select at most two central experience axes from the promise, or state that none was declared.

Use at least two fresh judges with the author's model when it is known and available, each making two independent judgments. A light request can use one judge and one judgment; disclose that reduction and any unavailable model match. The coordinator assigns and aggregates rather than supplying its own confirming vote. Give judges the promise, scope, this method, and annotation-free bodies or actual media; withhold author identity, generation method, conversation, work memory, contracts, evidence, review records, and other judgments. Do not send their output to an approval reviewer.

Read every selected file and unit, using non-overlapping chunks and an explicit read inventory when the work exceeds a context. Lock reactions per file for settings and treatments, and per scene, sequence, or release unit for scripts, screenplay, and video, before scoring. Record effective and difficult passages, their positions and recipient effects; narrative reactions also track attention, tension, curiosity, surprise, and emotional movement. Do not infer a cause in an unread upstream layer. Missing qualified judges or an incomplete edition leaves the affected card unassessed.

## Axes and anchors

Assess only axes that apply to the layer's actual purpose. A setting document is read by its next author or designer, a treatment by the script writer, a script by performers and the final screenplay writer, and the final screenplay or video against the declared audience experience. Do not demand scene tension in reference documents or penalize a genre or ending for taste alone.

| Card | Experience axes |
| --- | --- |
| Settings | World appeal, generative potential, credibility, agents, concrete realization, navigation, expressive quality, distinctiveness |
| Treatments | Story force, structure, attention and tension design, consequential choices, emotional progression, ending, distinctiveness, usable specificity, expressive quality |
| Scripts | Unit composition, playable scene force, attention and tension, spoken language, agents, staging, distinctiveness |
| Final screenplay | Comprehension and attention, engagement, structure, agents, emotion and aftermath, audiovisual voice and rhythm, concrete realization, distinctiveness, continuation where relevant |
| Delivered video, if observed | The final screenplay axes as actually seen and heard, plus intelligibility and continuity |
| Across layers | Arrival of the promise, strength of upstream handoff, whole-work rhythm, identity of the work |

For an unwaived frozen construction, mark expression axes as not applicable before rewriting; do not deduct elsewhere for a problem expression alone could fix. If expression revision was explicitly waived, assess its current expression. An absent opportunity for any other axis is also not applicable. Distinguish scene decisions and their handoff from performed timing and audience effects that only actual media can establish. Across-layer judgments require reading those layers and do not deduct the same recipient loss twice.

Every score is provisional. Use these anchors before averaging; proportions locate the experience rather than decide quality automatically.

| Anchor | Experienced effect | Reference share of weak units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | Exceptional effect sustained | Almost none |
| 80 | Clear effect with a few weaknesses | At most one tenth |
| 60 | Functional but weak or repeatedly uneven | Over one tenth through three tenths |
| 40 | Weakness dominates and effects are rare | Over three tenths through half |
| 20 | The experience or usable handoff fails | Over half |

For 80 or 100, identify at least two supported positive effects; for 20 or 40, identify at least two supported losses. Otherwise use 60. Every score above 20 needs a positive ground, and every score below 100 needs a negative ground. Count one cause once while distinguishing genuinely different recipient losses. If human scores exist, show them separately and explain differences of at least 40 points rather than treating model agreement as human validation.

## Edition comparison and report

When a frozen or previous edition is available, compare it with the current edition in separate judgments after the individual cards. Have each judge compare both presentation orders, recording better, similar, or worse per axis with gains and losses. An order-dependent result is similar. Read frozen expression for this comparison even when it was inapplicable to its individual construction card. More edits, detail, or duration do not establish improvement.

Average independent anchor judgments per axis, then average applicable axes for a card. The overall figure pools all applicable axes of current recipient editions and the across-layer card, so disclose that cards with more axes contribute more. Keep a distinct final-experience figure for screenplay or observed video. Calculate each judge's figures by the same rule and report the minimum and maximum as disagreement; with one judgment it is unmeasured. Keep intermediate values unrounded and round only the report. Unassessed and not-applicable entries never become zero scores.

Return the exact scope and editions, declared promise and chosen central axes, actual judge models and counts, read inventory, exclusions and unavailable inspection, provisional status, and presence or absence of human judgment. Give every requested card its reactions, supported axis scores, calculation inputs, strongest effects and losses, and edition comparisons; include the cross-layer result and disagreement. Compare scores only under the same model family, scope, edition, axes, and method. Differences within disagreement do not establish improvement, and narrow disagreement does not establish validity.
