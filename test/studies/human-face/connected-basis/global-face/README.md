# Global facial morphology

The connected editor selects this revision, `mpfb-connected-head-2026-09-17-binocular-frame`. It adds four whole-head controls to the [regional basis](../whole-face/README.md), giving 84 shape and 52 expression controls. The previous basis, studies and research remain preserved. Saved documents require their exact basis revision.

## Source and coordinates

The pinned [CC0 source and license record](../README.md#sources-and-license) applies. MPFB's macro target service supplies age structure, sexual dimorphism, adiposity and muscularity endpoints. These are dimensionless authored morphological axes, not estimates of a person's age, sex, body mass or health. The source macro values are recorded in [extraction-receipt.json](extraction-receipt.json); the source population mixture remains unchanged.

Whole-body macro targets also translate the head when stature changes. Each sampled endpoint therefore subtracts the change in the centroid of the complete bilateral eye surface from every attached surface. This preserves relative displacements while keeping a common binocular reference frame. It applies the same translation to skin, eyes, brows, lashes, teeth and tongue; it does not resize individual parts or correct a particular subject.

The neutral positions, materials, UVs, connectivity and all 212 preceding endpoints are exact. All six surfaces recover their neutral coordinates exactly after source extraction. The eight new signed endpoints passed the public builder and Float32 GLB exporter. Direct inspection covered their 72 canonical colour/clay views. Age and dimorphism visibly change the cranium, jaw and surrounding tissues; adiposity and muscularity act mainly on the retained neck in this source asset. They do not supply a measured physiological model or guarantee arbitrary combinations.

## Joint input fitting

The nineteen studies use one bounded robust whole-face objective. Six semantic landmark groups (lips, eyes, brows, outline, nose and the remaining surface) contribute equal mean-square terms instead of letting the largest landmark population dominate the fit. Signed positive and negative endpoints meet at a derivative discontinuity at zero, so each subject is solved from three complete shape vectors, zero and uniform -0.2/+0.2, retaining the smallest identical robust objective. No subject-specific loss or anatomical parameter is hardcoded. [subject-receipt.json](subject-receipt.json) retains all three costs, convergence results, source identities, per-group residuals, committed shape and actual exported GLB hashes.

### Landmarks anchor on the surface they name

A landmark's template anchor is the frontmost point of the neutral mesh under its detected image position. That rule holds wherever the front surface is continuous, and fails at the mouth: the source neutral's lips part over part of their length, so eight of MediaPipe's twenty inner-lip landmarks cast between the lips and anchored on the oral cavity roughly ninety-six millimetres behind them, while five upper outer-ring landmarks anchored on skin above the vermilion border. Those eight carried the highest per-landmark weight in the objective and drove oral-cavity geometry to match photographed lip contours.

MediaPipe defines `FACEMESH_LIPS` as the two lip contours and the source mesh authors the lip as its own material region, so a lip landmark now anchors on a lip triangle: the ray-cast anchor is kept when it already does, and otherwise moved to the closest point of the front-facing lip surface in the image plane. The eight aperture anchors move under 0.27 mm in the image plane while returning to the lip surface, so the correspondence changes without moving the measured projection; the five vermilion anchors move at most 2.08 mm.

### The shape prior charges geometric commitment

A basis weight is dimensionless and its metres-per-unit spans a factor of about 160 across this basis, so a flat regularizer priced a millimetre of philtrum like a centimetre of skull. The shape regularizer is now 0.003 multiplied by the channel's own commitment (`measureHumanFaceBasisChannels(...).displacement` in `@automovie/human`) divided by the root-mean-square commitment over the basis's shape channels, which keeps the published 0.003 rate at a channel of typical commitment and redistributes the prior rather than strengthening it. Expression channels keep the flat 0.015 regularizer toward the detector's observed performance. These are numerical fitting choices, not clinical uncertainty estimates.

The consequence is that a channel's data-to-prior balance becomes the fraction of its motion the frontal landmarks can see. Across the nineteen subjects the fitted solution stopped claiming a specific head depth (mean -0.214 to +0.008), neck depth (-0.183 to -0.008), sexual dimorphism (-0.376 to -0.056) and age structure (-0.121 to -0.004) from a single frontal photograph. Committed shape fell from 20.29 mm to 16.09 mm per subject, and the part carried by channels whose in-plane sensitivity is under a tenth of their commitment fell from 1.28 mm to 0.70 mm. Chin projection and mouth depth moved the other way and are named here rather than hidden.

### Measured against one instrument

Repairing an anchor changes which mesh point a landmark is compared against, so a solution's own residual is not comparable across that change. Both the previously published solution and this one are therefore re-evaluated on the repaired anchors. The equal-group root mean falls from 0.015064158 to 0.012420141, a 17.6 percent reduction. Per group, lips improve for 18 of 19 subjects (median -7.1 percent), brows for 19 of 19 (-15.6), outline for 18 of 19 (-31.6), nose for 17 of 19 (-18.3) and the remaining points for 14 of 19 (-9.3).

Eyes are the exception: 10 of 19 improve, median -0.6 percent, and the three largest regressions (alan-rickman, emma-watson and generated-white-girl-01, each about +41 percent) are the three subjects whose outline residual fell most, between 54 and 57 percent. Their previous pose was wrong in a way that happened to place the eye anchors well. Eye anchors sit a mean 9.05 mm from the eyelash strip on the lid margin and a mean 4.79 mm from the eyeball surface, and unlike the lip the lid margin is not an authored material boundary, so the same repair does not apply to it. That correspondence is open work, not a solved part of this fit.

Two candidates were measured and rejected. Weakening expression regularity to 0.003 lowered central-feature residuals further but systematically removed mouth opening and tooth exposure from smiling inputs, so 0.015 stands. Calibrating the commitment-scaled shape prior at the median channel commitment rather than the root-mean-square multiplied the total prior mass by about four: it cut committed shape to 8.27 mm but raised the equal-group root mean to 0.013626, and the rendered result was indistinguishable from the published one. Both remain preserved research rather than published fits.

Direct review compared all nineteen subjects against their sources under each solution's own fitted weak-perspective pose on a real device. Heads that had been narrowed and flattened read fuller and closer to their sources, the generated-white-girl-01 cheek and mouth distortion is reduced, and open-mouthed sources recover more aperture and tooth exposure. A heavier brow and orbital shadow appears on several subjects. Generic head appearance, weak older tissue and absent scalp grooming still limit likeness. Registration uses estimated weak perspective, not recovered physical camera intrinsics. These studies remain editable candidates without whole-person likeness acceptance.
