### Landmark refit of shape and expression

The published documents had never used 57 of the basis's 141 shape channels (every canthal, epicanthal, lid-fold, cupid's bow, nasal dorsum and head-outline channel was zero), and their expression weights were the detector's blendshape scores copied onto channels of a different scale. `fit-face-landmarks.ts` refits both from each photograph's 468 mesh landmarks through the shared editor only:

- [landmark-anchors.json](population/landmark-anchors.json) binds every landmark to the neutral `Human` surface once per subject camera (`anchor-face-landmarks.ts`): the face oval is a silhouette, and a front-view anchor paired a turned photograph's far contour with the wrong surface point, which bent neck and ear channels in the first trial;
- shape channels enter through their sparse endpoint rows and expression channels through finite differences of the real builder, with the 2D similarity as joint Gauss-Newton variables, box constraints at each channel's range, a prior on the current document of strength (0.02 / 0.5)^2 x 468 / 68, a left-right symmetry prior, and a five times tighter prior on channels whose displacement field is not mirror symmetric (the mouth and nose lateral positions);
- every step is admitted by the builder and the final document with its hair.

Under the portrait lens (2 m, 8.84 degrees) the published documents measure a median landmark RMS of 0.036 inter-ocular with 11 of 17 renders nearest their own photograph; the refit reaches 0.0316 and 16 of 17 (median margin 0.018). The refit documents are not published. They express the photographs' smiles through lip roll, press and pucker with the jaw opened 0.24 to 0.37, where the detector reads the same photographs as smiling with a raised upper lip, closed lips over the teeth and no roll: the landmarks trace the lips but never the teeth, and on this basis a lip roll moves the lip seam the way a raised lip does, while the detector reads the basis neutral itself as a pucker of 0.69. The incisal-edge observations `fit-face-landmarks.ts` now takes did not separate the two on three subjects; the landmark anchors also bind about 1.6% of the points (lid margins, the inner lip seam, the silhouette) through a gap to a surface behind the one the camera sees. Both are open.

## Source and coordinates

The pinned [CC0 source and license record](../README.md#sources-and-license) applies. MPFB's macro target service supplies age structure, sexual dimorphism, adiposity and muscularity endpoints. These are dimensionless authored morphological axes, not estimates of a person's age, sex, body mass or health. The source macro values are recorded in [extraction-receipt.json](extraction-receipt.json); the source population mixture remains unchanged.

Whole-body macro targets also translate the head when stature changes. Each sampled endpoint therefore subtracts the change in the centroid of the complete bilateral eye surface from every attached surface. This preserves relative displacements while keeping a common binocular reference frame. It applies the same translation to skin, eyes, brows, lashes, teeth and tongue; it does not resize individual parts or correct a particular subject.

The neutral positions, materials, UVs, connectivity and all 212 preceding endpoints are exact. All six surfaces recover their neutral coordinates exactly after source extraction. The eight new signed endpoints passed the public builder and Float32 GLB exporter. Direct inspection covered their 72 canonical colour/clay views. Age and dimorphism visibly change the cranium, jaw and surrounding tissues; adiposity and muscularity act mainly on the retained neck in this source asset. They do not supply a measured physiological model or guarantee arbitrary combinations.

## Joint input fitting

The nineteen studies use one bounded robust whole-face objective. Six semantic landmark groups (lips, eyes, brows, outline, nose and the remaining surface) contribute equal mean-square terms instead of letting the largest landmark population dominate the fit. Signed positive and negative endpoints meet at a derivative discontinuity at zero, so each subject is solved from three complete shape vectors, zero and uniform -0.2/+0.2, retaining the smallest identical robust objective. No subject-specific loss or anatomical parameter is hardcoded. [subject-receipt.json](subject-receipt.json) retains all three costs, convergence results, source identities, per-group residuals, committed shape and actual exported GLB hashes.

### Landmarks anchor on the surface the camera sees

A landmark's template anchor is the frontmost point of the neutral mesh under its detected image position. The source head is one continuous skin surface whose only boundary is the neck clip at Y = -0.145 m, so the lids and the lips are folds that turn inward rather than holes. Where such a fold stands open the ray meets no outer skin at all, and the frontmost hit is the interior behind it. Eight of MediaPipe's twenty inner-lip landmarks anchored on the oral cavity about 96 mm behind the lips, and twelve of its thirty-two eye landmarks, the whole upper lid ring plus the outer corners, anchored on the orbital wall about 25 mm behind the lid margin. Those anchors carried their groups' full weight while driving interior geometry to match photographed contours.

The outer surface is now defined without naming any feature: take the mesh's frontmost triangle, then flood fill across shared edges through front-facing triangles only. A fold's back-facing band is never crossed, so the oral cavity and the orbit stay outside the component (21,019 of the 24,879 front-facing triangles remain inside it) while the face, lids, lips, nose and ears stay inside. A landmark whose ray misses that component anchors at the component point closest to it in the image plane, which is the aperture rim. Twenty-two anchors move: the eight lip anchors return to the lip surface while shifting at most 0.264 mm in the image plane, the twelve eye anchors return to the lid margin shifting at most 1.688 mm, and landmarks 58 and 288 stop falling back to a nearest vertex, so no landmark is down-weighted as a projection miss any more.

### Identity is symmetric, performance is not

The source neutral mirrors onto itself. Negating X and pairing every one of the 17,927 skin vertices with its mate leaves a root mean square deviation of 0.098 micrometres and a worst pairing of 0.366 micrometres, which is single-precision storage rather than real asymmetry; the pairing is a proper involution and 237 vertices sit on the midline and mate with themselves. Each right endpoint is the mirror of its left to within 0.0084 mm. Those figures are measured at publication and recorded in [subject-receipt.json](subject-receipt.json) under `basisMirror`, so a basis that is not symmetric cannot quietly inherit a fit that assumes it is. All of a fitted face's asymmetry is therefore something the fit chose, and it splits into an identity part with no evidence behind it and a performance part the detector reports per side. The total tracked the camera: subjects photographed square on departed from their own mirror by 0.3 to 1.0 mm, subjects photographed off axis by 1.2 to 3.0 mm, with a worst single vertex of 10.11 mm.

A missing camera degree of freedom was the first suspect and was measured and rejected. Giving the projection one bounded inverse distance, on the four subjects the experiment covered, finds physically plausible portrait distances of 0.62 to 1.38 m and lowers the reprojection residual by three to eight percent, but it does not reduce the mirror deviation: alan-rickman moves from 2.43 to 2.51 mm and emma-watson from 2.12 to 2.35, while generated-white-girl-01 falls from 2.99 to 2.46. The skew is not a perspective artifact, so a perspective term would not have fixed it. That candidate is preserved as research for its own topic.

The seventeen left/right shape pairs are now solved as one weight each. This removes a degree of freedom rather than penalizing one, so it carries no tuning constant, and because each right endpoint mirrors its left to within 0.0084 mm the resulting identity is symmetric to that same order: identity mirror deviation falls from a 0.994 mm mean to 0.009 mm, with 0.0125 mm the worst of the nineteen, which is the endpoints' own mirror residual rather than anything the fit did. Total mirror deviation falls from 1.260 mm to 0.650 mm and the worst single vertex from 10.11 mm to 5.75 mm.

What remains is performance. Expression channels stay free because the detector reports a separate score per side, and the fitted side gap follows the observed one rather than inventing it: 2.73 against 2.29 for alan-rickman, 3.12 against 2.60 for generated-white-girl-01, 0.49 against 0.42 for the frontal generated-korean-boy-01. One photograph cannot separate a genuinely asymmetric face from a symmetric one seen at an angle, so this study no longer claims identity asymmetry it cannot evidence. The editor still exposes both controls of every pair, because authoring asymmetry deliberately is a different act from inferring it from one photograph.

All nineteen were read against their sources at each solution's own fitted pose on a real device, and six off-axis subjects additionally at front, both three-quarters, both profiles and clay through the built editor. The two most oblique are visibly better: generated-white-girl-01 loses a pinched cheek and a skewed mouth, and alan-rickman's jaw stops leaning. The other seventeen are unchanged to the eye or nearly so, which is what the measurement predicts: the identity correction is a median of 0.822 mm per subject and under a millimetre for twelve of the nineteen, with generated-white-girl-01's 2.413 mm the largest. Nothing in the population regressed. Because a directional key light makes even an exactly symmetric mesh photograph asymmetric, the deviation itself was read as geometry rather than from the frames: every skin vertex plotted at its front-projected position and coloured by the distance to its mirrored mate. Under the previous solution the whole cheek and jaw carried four to eight millimetres; under this one that area is flat and what remains sits at the mouth corners, which is the smile.

The reprojection cost is real and is concentrated where an oblique silhouette lives. The equal-group root mean rises from 0.010889438 to 0.011024022, 1.2 percent. Lips, eyes and brows are unchanged to slightly better; outline rises 3.6 percent at the median with 3 of 19 improving, nose 6.1 percent with 4 of 19, and the remaining points 3.3 percent with none improving. The fit can no longer bend a face contour to match a skewed one, and that is the intended loss.

### The shape prior charges geometric commitment

A basis weight is dimensionless and its metres-per-unit spans a factor of about 160 across this basis, so a flat regularizer priced a millimetre of philtrum like a centimetre of skull. The shape regularizer is now 0.003 multiplied by the channel's own commitment (`measureHumanFaceBasisChannels(...).displacement` in `@automovie/human`) divided by the root-mean-square commitment over the basis's shape channels, which keeps the published 0.003 rate at a channel of typical commitment and redistributes the prior rather than strengthening it. Expression channels keep the flat 0.015 regularizer toward the detector's observed performance. These are numerical fitting choices, not clinical uncertainty estimates.

The consequence is that a channel's data-to-prior balance becomes the fraction of its motion the frontal landmarks can see. Committed shape falls from 20.29 mm to 14.91 mm per subject, and the part carried by channels whose in-plane sensitivity is under a tenth of their commitment falls from 1.28 mm to 0.47 mm. Averaged over the nineteen subjects, head depth returns to neutral (-0.214 to -0.005) and so do sexual dimorphism (-0.376 to -0.022) and age structure (-0.121 to -0.011), none of which a single frontal photograph can settle. Neck depth halves without reaching neutral (-0.183 to +0.091), and chin projection (+0.010 to +0.257) and mouth depth (+0.059 to +0.315) grow instead of shrinking; both are named here rather than hidden.

### Measured against one instrument

Repairing an anchor changes which mesh point a landmark is compared against, so a solution's own residual is not comparable across that change. Both the previously published solution and this one are therefore re-evaluated on the outer-shell anchors, the only anchor set that samples the outer surface at every landmark. The equal-group root mean falls from 0.014633742 to 0.010889438, a 25.6 percent reduction. Per group the subjects that improve, with the median change: eyes 19 of 19 (-27.7 percent), brows 19 of 19 (-14.0), nose 17 of 19 (-19.1), lips 18 of 19 (-11.5), outline 18 of 19 (-38.4) and the remaining points 15 of 19 (-10.1). The worst single regressions are nose +12.8 percent, other +10.2 and outline +5.4 on individual subjects.

Four candidates were measured and rejected, each preserved as research. Weakening expression regularity to 0.003 lowered central-feature residuals further but systematically removed mouth opening and tooth exposure from smiling inputs, so 0.015 stands. Calibrating the commitment-scaled prior at the median channel commitment rather than the root-mean-square multiplied its total mass by about four and made every group worse than this fit. Anchoring the lip landmarks on the authored lip material instead of on the outer shell repaired the lips but left the twelve eye anchors in the orbit, and its eyes group is about a third worse than this one. Re-mapping the detector's two lip rings onto the lip material's two border loops by arc length moved anchors that were already correct, the mouth corners by as much as 50 mm.

Direct review compared all nineteen subjects against their sources under each solution's own fitted weak-perspective pose on a real device. Heads that had been narrowed and flattened read fuller and closer to their sources, the generated-white-girl-01 cheek and mouth distortion is reduced, and open-mouthed sources recover more aperture and tooth exposure. A heavier brow and orbital shadow appears on several subjects.

Four subjects (alan-rickman, generated-white-girl-01, michael-gambon and kdy1) were then loaded into the built editor itself and captured at front, both three-quarters, both profiles, back and clay under both solutions, because the frontal view is the one the objective optimizes and therefore the least informative one to judge it by. What the other views showed is a silhouette the frontal fit had been free to get wrong. Measuring the cranial vault above the ears across all nineteen subjects, its width spanned 105.1 to 143.2 mm under the previous solution and spans 130.4 to 142.7 mm under this one, and the depth-to-width ratio tightened from 1.317 to 1.561 (standard deviation 0.0698) to 1.344 to 1.472 (0.0383). The two extremes were the two subjects whose frontal residual was worst: alan-rickman's vault was 108.8 mm wide and generated-white-girl-01's 105.1 mm, narrower than any adult skull, and both read in profile as a long head on a thin neck. Both now sit inside the population. The change is not uniformly in one direction. Ten of the nineteen ratios rose, michael-gambon's among them (1.320 to 1.432); what improved is that the population no longer contains impossible outliers, not that every subject moved toward the middle. Generic head appearance, weak older tissue and absent scalp grooming still limit likeness. Registration uses estimated weak perspective, not recovered physical camera intrinsics. These studies remain editable candidates without whole-person likeness acceptance.

### Single channels first

A single channel that already crosses says the shape itself is wrong, and no pair corrective would save it. On the neutral head six do: `jawOpen`, `jawLeft`, `jawRight`, `mouthFunnel` and `tongueOut` put the oral lining through the mandibular gum block as the jaw swings, and `mouthRollUpper` rolls the upper lip through the incisors. `repair-single-endpoints.ts` wears each at full weight, pushes what crosses clear by the standing orders below and folds the displacement into the endpoint itself: inside 3 mm where soft tissue over an arch gives that, and inside 8 mm where the floor of the mouth has to follow the mandible that swung out from under it, which the census measured as the authored floor lagging the gum block by up to that. `tongueOut` carries `jawOpen` whole, so `jawOpen`'s repair is applied to it and negated in `tongueOutJawOpenUnfold` as well, or the two together would apply it twice; `tongueOut` is then solved after, on the head that already has it. A channel still crossing at a fraction of its weight, which an endpoint cannot answer because a straight line has no middle, gets a single-driver in-between there, spanned to its neighbours. All six read clear at 1, ¾, ½ and ¼ afterwards.

### Pair correctives

Fifty-two expression channels make 1,326 pairs, and `enumerate-expression-pairs.ts neutral` wears the neutral head at every one of them. A pair counts only when it makes a pair of surfaces cross that neither channel alone makes cross; measured against the larger single instead, two channels on disjoint regions read as interacting, and shutting both eyes was the first false positive. On the single-repaired head 662 pairs interact past superposition and 109 invent a surface pair, up from 54 before the singles were repaired, because a crossing a single used to own is now the pair's.

Each of the 109 is solved by `solve-combination.ts`, one part pair at a time on one working head, with one of four answers by the shape of the crossing, each chosen because the others were measured to fail. A soft part inside a firm one is pushed clear by `push-out.ts` inside a 3 mm budget, along the side it sat on at rest, because a vertex inside a tooth is near every face of that tooth and the nearest face is as likely to lead into the mouth as out of it, and the push is relaxed over three rings so tissue moves rather than dents; the oral lining against the gum block gets the 8 mm the mandible took. Bone and the globe never yield, and between soft parts the enclosed one does, except that a tongue being put out is the agent of its pose and the lips part around it off its nearest face. A lip triangle through a skin triangle turned out, on every one of the 17 pairs, to be a fan folded over its own apex at the lip seam, both triangles sharing a vertex and the deeper corner 0.03 to 1.2 mm past the other's plane; `unfold.ts` draws each apex toward the centroid of its ring until the fans no longer cross beyond the four the source seam crosses at rest, inside 0.82 mm and twelve vertices, after pushing carried the fold along (16 to 63 vertices) and relaxing collapsed the lip's two layers (27 to 290). And a part that bone cannot let through and a budget cannot clear has its own motion taken back by `takeBack`, scaled by bisection until it is clear, not to rest but to the pose less the channel that did the crossing: a lip rolled over the incisors with the jaw thrust forward unrolls and the jaw stays thrust, and a tongue put out through sealed or puckered lips comes out less and keeps whatever the jaw did, because returning it to rest under teeth that had moved was measured to cross by more than the pose did.

The activation of a corrective is a product of clamped drivers, which is bilinear, so a corrective solved at full weight lands at a quarter of itself at half weights. A driver may therefore name the weight it peaks at and the weights on either side where it fades to nothing, and its factor becomes a tent that is one there and zero at its neighbours; `generate-combination-correctives.ts` wears each pair at (½, 1), (1, ½) and (½, ½) in turn, each with every earlier tier present, measures what crosses at that pose against that pose's own singles, and publishes what remains as such an in-between. The revision carries 134 correctives: the two authored earlier, three single-driver in-betweens from the repair step, 109 solved at full weight and 25 pair in-betweens, and not one of the 109 combinations stays beyond its budget.

The earlier population experiment stored per-subject vertex correctives. Issue #2533 rejects that representation: published documents now contain numerical shape/expression controls, material overrides and resource identities only. Legacy geometry is retained in Git history rather than replayed by the editor. Shared shape/expression interactions must be repaired and verified in the basis. The earlier per-document crossing result does not establish validity of these compact documents; their population verification is pending.

`verify-combination-correctives.ts` then wears the neutral head and every subject at every enumerated pair and four weights, with and without any pair corrective, and folds the result into [pair-corrective-receipt.json](pair-corrective-receipt.json) under `verification`, so the receipt says what the revision has been verified to do rather than what it was meant to do. Weights off the {½, 1} grid are the fine grid `--grid fine` measures separately.

## Articulated revision

The [reassessment](architecture-reassessment.md) asked for one shared articulated evaluator before any further personal tuning, and this revision is that evaluator's data. ### Attachments from the source

`test/scripts/face-review/extract-face-attachments.py` runs inside Blender 5.1 with the pinned MPFB revision `817587ceb2ea03ea17a5b47e04396cbb4ddfa2d5` enabled from source, the same session the body extraction uses: the default human, the CC0 `default` rig weights bound as vertex groups (the mandible as the `jaw` subtree, each globe as its `eye` bone) and one limit-surface Catmull-Clark round, which reproduces every one of the 16,942 `Human` vertices (16,758 exact twins, 184 clipped-edge points, worst match 1.6e-17 m). It reads the centroids of thirteen `joint-*` cubes in the face frame, recovers the MPFB target or macro node behind every one of the 274 published shape endpoints by measurement (worst residual 1.8e-14 m; the recipes were never tracked before), and publishes the cube motion of the 29 endpoints that move a cube as sparse landmark rows. [face-attachments.json.gz](face-attachments.json.gz) and its [receipt](face-attachments-receipt.json) hold the result; the neutral restores to the last bit.

The painted weights turned out to be the wrong attachment for this source: the jaw subtree reaches 0.92 at the chin and leaves a 4.19 mm rms residual against the source's own `jawOpen` after the rigid motion is removed, while the source's pure-translation mandibular endpoints (`jawForward`, `jawLeft`, `jawRight`) reveal a per-vertex weight directly (`E = w t` up to a 0.07 mm mean off-axis remainder; the two lateral channels agree to 0.06 rms) with a 2.85 mm rms residual. The revision attaches the skin and tongue with those measured weights (8,190 skin vertices, 60 at unit weight, 714 clamped by at most 0.0095; the whole tongue at unit weight), the mandibular arch and both globes at unit weight from the legacy rigid membership, and records the painted comparison in the receipt.

### Joints from the source and the literature

The opening endpoint's screw (21.155 degrees about +X, pivot (0, -15.7, 58.8) mm, slide 0) is decomposed under the in vivo coupling of condylar translation to rotation, Chen et al. 2021 (Dentomaxillofac Radiol 50:20190464): 14.3 mm forward and 3.5 mm down at 27.4 degrees, about 0.5 mm per degree. Under that coupling the same transform is a rotation about (0, 15.2, 60.6) mm plus a coupled translation of (0, -2.7, 11.0) mm, and that axis point sits 18.6 mm below the globe centres, where the condyle is; the source's low screw pivot is the equivalent instantaneous centre of a rotation plus a forward translation, which is also why the helical axis of habitual opening is never inside the condyle (Gallo et al. 1997). The axis point is published as an offset (0, 13.8, 4.1) mm from the `joint-mouth` cube and follows it through every shape channel. Protrusion (0, -1.5, 8.3) mm and laterotrusion (±6.6, -0.8, 4.3) mm are the source's authored translations. The summed sagittal translation of opening and protrusion is budgeted at Chen's 14.72 mm; a document past it is refused by name rather than clamped, which is what closes the bottom of Posselt's envelope.

Each globe turns about its `joint-l-eye`/`joint-r-eye` cube, 15.2 mm behind the corneal apex against the 13.5 to 15.3 mm the centre-of-rotation literature reports (Ohlendorf et al. 2022), by the angle each authored gaze endpoint fits (up 9.6, down 11.1, in 14.8, out 14.3 degrees) plus the eccentric translation the source authored with it (up to 1.85 mm, bounded at 3 mm), so the lids the source sculpted around its globe stay coherent. A blink turns no globe (Doane 1980).

### Residuals and what was retired

Every expression endpoint and corrective is rewritten as a rest-space residual over the articulation (`unpose(neutral + E) - neutral`), so a smile authored at rest travels with an open jaw, and every expression replays its authored pose at weight one to under a micrometre. `tongueOut` had carried `jawOpen` whole; it is now decomposed at the open-jaw state, so tongue plus open jaw replays the authored pose while `tongueOut` alone is the combination the source never authored and the census reports (the tongue through closed teeth). The teeth and globes publish no expression rows: `jawOpenMouthCloseEase` had lifted the mandibular arch 19.6 mm to dodge the closing lips, and that row is gone with the other 16 dropped rows the receipt lists; the 19 correctives that compensated the tongue's double count are removed, leaving `jawOpenMouthCloseEase` and the three opening in-betweens as skin and tongue residuals. All eighteen documents keep every numerical value and name the new revision.

### What the census says

`export-articulation-census.ts` and `capture-articulation.mjs` run fourteen coupled states per subject through the actual runtime and the engine's triangle-crossing census, then render them on a real GPU. Against the previous revision evaluated on the same states with its own rule, on the neutral and two subjects: rest, protrusion, laterotrusion and blink are identical; every gaze state and tongue-with-open-jaw replay their authored poses; full opening crosses the skin less (150 to 122 and 209 to 145 crossed triangles) and half opening slightly more (84 to 101, 20 to 46), because the teeth now travel the coupled arc while the half-open lips were authored to the old chord. Two combinations are now visibly unsupported instead of hidden: lips fully closed over a fully open jaw (0 to about 300 to 480 skin and teeth crossings, the lifted-teeth cheat gone) and the tongue out through closed teeth. Both are contact problems the articulation makes explicit and does not solve; the coupled contact evaluator is the next owner.

## Contact revision

The articulated revision left two combinations visibly unsupported, lips closed over an open jaw and the tongue through closed teeth, and the lining crossing the gums under opening. This revision is the coupled contact evaluator's data: the generic evaluation lives in `@automovie/human` (`measureHumanFaceAperture`, `evaluateHumanFacePassage`, `resolveHumanFaceContact` inside `createHumanFaceBasisBuilder`) on the engine's oriented sheet query; `prepare-contact-basis.ts` measured what this directory now carries and [contact-receipt.json](contact-receipt.json) records it.

### What the source's `mouthClose` is

MPFB ships `mouthClose` as the ARKit face unit "closes the lips while the jaw remains open": a delta authored over the open jaw. The articulated revision had decomposed it at the closed jaw, so at `jawOpen` 1 the delta rode the 21 degree turn of the mandible and the lower lip rolled 11.9 mm off the seam; `jawOpenMouthCloseEase` had been compensating exactly that (its largest row is the same 11.861 mm) and is retired. The rows are now the delta unposed through the per-vertex blend at `jawOpen` 1, and the builder applies them scaled by the ratio of the current interlabial aperture to the aperture `jawOpen` alone produces, both read between the vermilion seam vertices 15892 and 16040 (0.89 mm apart at rest) along the cranial vertical. On the neutral the closure seals the seam at full opening (ratio 1.000, 0.69 mm) and at half opening (ratio 0.500, 0.56 mm) instead of overshooting; `mouthClose` at a closed jaw does nothing, which is what the face unit means.

### Tongue passage and the crowns

The tongue is a constant-volume muscular hydrostat (Kier and Smith 1985); it cannot be pressed through closed incisors or sealed lips. A tongue past the posed lower incisal edge (vertices 3806 over 2593, 0.31 mm apart at rest) must be thinner over a 2 mm slab than both the interincisal and the interlabial aperture, or the document is refused naming the channel and the millimetres: `tongueOut` alone reads "puts a 9.6 mm tongue through a 0.3 mm gap; jawOpen must open it by at least 9.3 mm more", and it is the only single endpoint the preparation's probe refuses. The 32 crowns of the dental surface are open caps with twelve-vertex root rings; each ring is sealed with a fan wound against its rim (320 triangles over resident vertices), the two gum sheets (96 and 102 boundary vertices) stay open, and the sealed surface is admitted as an oriented sheet whose rim features report no side.

### The floor rule and its budgets

Every soft vertex keeps the clearance it has in its shape-only rest state: at rest the tongue's sides lie 1.48 mm inside the lower molars and the lining touches the gums, and both stay so; only tissue a pose pushes deeper is moved back to that floor along the nearest feature, seam copies together. The skin's budget is Holdaway's upper lip thickness, vermilion point to upper incisor, 13.41 mm (Saudi Dent J 2011, n=93); the tongue's is its own 20.8 mm height along the vertical, a push larger than the tissue being the tissue in the wrong place rather than a deformation. Beside them the receipt records what the source itself authored: the deepest excess the floor rule finds on any single expression endpoint is 6.1 mm on the skin (the vestibular lining under the swinging gums) and 1.76 mm on the tongue (the source's own `jawOpen` reshapes the tongue into the molars by that much). All eighteen documents build against the new block without their grooms rebuilt.

### Lids over the cornea

The same block carries the ocular contact. Each globe of `Human.low-poly` is open at its posterior pole by a ring of thirty-two vertices and is sealed like a crown (60 triangles, both globes closed and outward); the lashes join the soft surfaces with their own 14.3 mm height as budget. At rest the lids lie up to 2.29 mm inside the globes, as the source authored them, and that is the floor each lid vertex keeps. Measured on the neutral before this revision, lateral gaze carried 848 lid vertices deeper than their rest clearance by up to 1.83 mm and a blink 781 by up to 2.67 mm, while vertical gaze moved nothing beyond 0.13 mm because the source authored those rows on the globe; after it no vertex sits deeper than its rest clearance by more than 0.09 mm in any of the four states, and the vertices inside a globe fall from 1089 to 372 at lateral gaze and from 1195 to 477 at a blink. The triangle census reads more skin and globe crossings in those states (neutral blink 606/452 to 1445/726), because a lid held at its rest clearance runs along the globe's surface and every triangle that straddles it counts, where before whole regions lay entirely inside; the renders of blink and lateral gaze are unchanged to the eye. No lid rule exists: the floor rule gained a collider.

### What the census says

`export-articulation-census.ts` and `capture-articulation.mjs` again ran fourteen coupled states per subject on the neutral and two subjects. Against the articulated revision: every state that was authored replays it (rest, blink, gaze, protrusion and laterotrusion are identical to the triangle); `tongue-closed` is refused by name on all three heads instead of rendering a tongue through closed teeth; `open-close` closes the lips on the seam, and its lip and teeth crossings are zero as they were. The skin and teeth counts are unchanged (neutral open-close 70/237, full opening 34/48) because they are the vestibular lining below the modelled gum's open base, where the collider has no floor to give: the census shows them, the floor rule does not reach them, and a closed alveolar shell is a data follow-up. The tongue and teeth counts move by a few percent as the floor rule returns the tongue's sides to their rest clearance (neutral full opening 329/216 to 354/273), well inside the 487/410 the source authored at rest.

## Dental revision

The source hid the upper incisors at rest: the upper central incisal edge sat 0.56 mm above the upper lip's vermilion seam, so no smile showed the upper teeth and the population renders showed the lower arch and the tongue where the photographs show upper crowns. The upper lip itself is ordinary (20.5 mm from subnasale to stomion) and the incisors meet within the Ricketts norms (overbite 1.67 mm against 2.5 +- 2, overjet 0.97 mm against 2.5 +- 2.5), so the height of the upper dentition was what was out of place. The resting display of the upper central incisor is 1.91 mm in men and 3.40 mm in women (Vig and Brundo 1978; 2.5 and 3.8 mm in Misch's later sample); the basis carries no sex, so the target is the midpoint of the first study, 2.655 mm, and a sex, age or lip-length difference is left to the lip's own shape channels, which move the lip over a fixed dentition as tissue moves over bone.

`prepareDentalPosition` reads each incisal edge from the crown that holds the contact's incisal pair (the pair marks where the two crowns meet, not their edges) and lowers only the maxillary arch, the dentition vertices not bound to the mandible, as one rigid body. A first candidate lowered both arches and the tongue by 3.22 mm; `export-articulation-census.ts` found the lower gum's open base through the floor of the mouth at rest (0 to 26 crossed skin triangles on the neutral, 117 at full opening instead of 34), because the source's lower gum clears that lining by less than two millimetres, and lowering the maxillary arch alone with the mandibular arch lowered less still crossed. The mandibular arch and the tongue therefore stay seated on the mandible, and lowering the upper arch deepens the overbite by the same amount: the shift stops at the smaller of the display target and the upper normal overbite, 4.5 mm. On this basis the overbite binds, so the maxillary arch moves 2.83 mm and the display is 2.26 mm, between the two sexes' means.

Every vertex identity (contact pairs, colliders, attachments, budgets) and every endpoint row (they are displacements) is kept, and the real builder admits the neutral and all eighteen documents, which are restamped and otherwise unchanged. [dental-receipt.json](dental-receipt.json) records the measurements before and after, the bound that held, the citations and the input and output digests. The census of the neutral and two subjects against the ocular-contact revision reads no new skin or lip crossing at rest, in the smile or at half opening; at full opening the lowered upper incisors cross the upper lip's lining (26 lip triangles on the neutral, 9 and 1 on the subjects), and the tongue, which the source already authored through the teeth at rest (487 tongue triangles), crosses the lowered arch more (588; 760 with the jaw protruded). Both are inside the mouth; they are recorded, not solved. [landmark-anchors.json](population/landmark-anchors.json) was rebound on this revision's neutral renders.

## Eighteen-subject acceptance

Every published document was built through the actual connected runtime and rendered on a real GPU (ANGLE D3D11 on a Radeon 780M), and every one was put through the fourteen coupled states with the engine's triangle-crossing census. Nothing here is a separate asset: the views are what the editor produces from each document's own parameters.

**Views.** Eighteen subjects at rest with their hair, five views each (front, both profiles, back and a clay front): 90 frames. Build times run from 2.1 to 15.2 seconds with a median of 3.1, and hair meshes from 36k to 359k triangles, the longest curly hair costing the most of both. The scalp reads as hair, not skin, wherever the hair is darker than the skin. Those times are what the population costs after the signed query stopped allocating per triangle; before it the same eighteen builds took 751 seconds against 81.

**States.** Nineteen documents (the eighteen plus the bald neutral) across fourteen states, 266 entries. `tongue-closed` is refused by name on all nineteen, as the passage rule requires. Rest, both gazes, lateral gaze and blink cross the teeth on none of them but one, whose lining touches its own gums at rest as the source authored it. The lining crosses more as the jaw opens (101 triangles at half opening, 138 at full, 469 with the lips closed, medians), which is the vestibular lining below the modelled gum's open base where no collider exists; that is a data follow-up, stated rather than hidden. Lip against teeth stays at zero except under laterotrusion, where the arches slide as the census records.

**Hair against skin.** The states above are built without hair, so the coupled census does not read it; the view build does, and its own crossing counts are what the ribbon rules are held to. Summed over the population's hair layers, skin against hair fell from 11,888 triangles with the authored 4 mm ribbon to 9,957 with the derived width and the strand chord rule, having risen to 15,973 in between, when the ribbons were widened to cover the scalp before their strands were held to the integrator's step. The subjects that moved are the long-haired ones: emma-watson 1,014, then 1,842, then 553; generated-white-girl-01 1,138, then 2,026, then 526. What is left is about five hundred per thousand roots, which is the root fan itself: a hair root stands on the skin, so its first triangle touches it. The cost of both rules together was a population build of 751 seconds against 510, which the query's own repair then took to 81.

**Known findings.** An earlier reading of one subject's front view, a rectangular region where the hair in front of the face seemed not to be drawn, was wrong and is withdrawn. Compared pixel by pixel against the same model rendered without hair, that region is exactly where no ribbon stands in front of the face: the hair parts around it, its straight lower edge is the model's own neck cut and its straight sides are the curtains' inner edges. The hair alone, the skin alone and a render with the ribbons alpha-masked opaque instead of blended all agree, so no viewer artifact remains to chase. The renders are review evidence, not an anatomical or likeness acceptance.
