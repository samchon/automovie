# Global facial morphology

The connected editor selects this directory's basis, whose first revision `mpfb-connected-head-2026-09-17-binocular-frame` added four whole-head controls to the [regional basis](../whole-face/README.md), giving 84 shape and 52 expression controls; the [revisions](#revisions) that followed are listed below with their receipts. The previous basis, studies and research remain preserved. Saved documents require their exact basis revision.

The user's 2026-09-22 instruction requires [reassessment of the representation and evaluation architecture](architecture-reassessment.md) after repeated local corrections. That investigation compares current code with MetaHuman, FLAME, GNM and procedural hair references, includes a pinned GNM data audit, and distinguishes numerical migration from anatomical acceptance.

Issue [#2533](https://github.com/samchon/automovie/issues/2533) and Draft PR [#2534](https://github.com/samchon/automovie/pull/2534) started from `mpfb-connected-head-2026-09-21-single-repaired`, with 136 channels and 23 shared correctives. The currently published basis is the latest row of [the revisions](#revisions), with 144 shape channels (the three ancestry shares added by the population revision), 52 expression channels, 31 shared correctives and 29 simple groups; each revision's receipt and the published [subjects](subjects.json) name it. All 18 documents contain numerical facial, pigmentation and hair controls. They contain no personal vertex fields, bitmap references or groom resource keys. [Fine-basis preparation](preparation-receipt.json) and [numerical-hair migration](numerical-hair-migration.json) record their separate revisions and provenance. Earlier counts and crossing results below describe their named historical revisions. Numerical representation does not establish anatomical validity or accepted likeness.

### Measured through the product editor under a portrait lens

The receipts above drew each document with `capture-articulation.mjs`'s own two lights, no tone mapping, no shadows, opaque optics and hard alpha tests, at 0.62 m. That is enough for geometry but not for appearance, and a close camera also adds a perspective no portrait photograph has. From the dental revision on, the portrait image is the product face editor's own render (`capture-editor-views.mjs` on the playground dev server: ACES tone mapping, a shadowed key, fill and rim, alpha-to-coverage cut-outs), the camera stands 2 m away with an 8.84 degree field that keeps the framing ([poses-lens.json](population/poses-lens.json), its [plan](population/poses-lens.plan.json) and [frame](population/poses-lens-frame.json)), and the hair mask and frame stay geometric. The landmark anchors are rebound with the occluders a camera sees ([landmark-anchors.json](population/landmark-anchors.json) at 0.62 m and [landmark-anchors-lens.json](population/landmark-anchors-lens.json)): before, a lid-margin or inner-lip ray that slipped past the visible edge went through the globe or the teeth, and 195 of 544 eye-contour and about 100 inner-lip anchors lay on the orbit or the pharynx behind them; now 0 and 8 do.

### Documents derived by shared rules

The documents published until this revision were landmark fits: a bounded least-squares refit of up to 84 shape and 52 expression channels to each photograph's landmarks ([subject-receipt.json](subject-receipt.json)). Every channel was a shared, named one, but a fit that moves most of them until one photograph's residual is small sculpts that photograph in numbers: an 80-year-old's age axis read 25 years, every dimorphism axis sat near its midpoint, and the residual lived in whichever channels reduced it. The documents are now derived instead, each value a fact, a named proportion or a named expression unit carried into the one control that means it by a rule shared by every subject ([derivation-receipt.json](population/derivation-receipt.json)):

- **Population.** Age, sex and ancestry recorded for each subject ([subject-facts.json](population/subject-facts.json): age from the source file name, ancestry from its inventory folder) set the population controls by MakeHuman's own definitions (`facePopulationControls`: years to the age macro as `apps/human.py` maps them, female -1 and male 1, the recorded ancestry's share one). Lee Tae-ri was recorded female until 2026-09-26; he is a male actor (born Lee Min-ho, 1993) and his photograph shows a man, so every document and round derived before the correction carries the female population controls and norms for him.
- **Proportions.** Frontal anthropometric indices (`FACE_ANTHROPOMETRY_INDICES`; first fourteen, from Farkas: face height over bizygomatic width, intercanthal and palpebral widths, fissure height, nasal width and height, mouth width, both vermilion heights, upper lip over lower face, lower face and chin widths, chin height, brow height; the vertical ones read as the face's three segments over its width since 2026-09-26, see [Mental height](#mental-height); since then the canthal tilt, apertures, brow slope, Cupid's bow, lateral vermilion, cheek, nasal, temple, lid and nasal base indices and the mouth's states), read on the photograph through its instruments (the detector's landmarks, the jaw's outline from a skin segmentation, the vermilion's borders from the midline's colour; "Instrument" says which index each can measure) and, through the landmark anchors, on the model under the same lens, each set by one paired shape control in a square solve (`solveFaceAnthropometry`). A control that cannot reach its index is held at its bound and recorded; a basis that holds the same control for most subjects is a basis defect, which is how the brow seat and the lower face above were found. The solve is closed on the render: a second pass takes each index's target as the photograph's value plus one correction per index, the median over the subjects of what the anchored model said minus what the product render of the first pass showed through the same detector (`faceRenderCorrection`; until 2026-09-26 each subject took its own difference, which repaid one render's placement quirks one person at a time).
- **Expression.** The detector's ARKit scores on the photograph, over the scores the subject's own identity reads at rest, are carried into the same-named channels through a shared calibration on the reference head ([expression-calibration.json](population/expression-calibration.json)); a unit whose calibrated span is narrower than the spread the detector reports across the photographs is not carried (`faceExpressionTransfer`).
- **States read from the lips' own landmarks.** Two further indices read a state of the face rather than its form and write expression: `lipParting` (stomion superius to inferius over mouth width) sets the upper lip raiser and lower lip depressor pairs in the posed-smile ratio, and `cornerLift` (the corners' rise above the lip centre over mouth width) sets the smile pair, superseding the detector's transfer of those units ([Lip parting from the lips' own landmarks](#lip-parting-from-the-lips-own-landmarks), [Corner lift and the face frame](#corner-lift-and-the-face-frame)). The identity-only pass, whose renders are the expression transfer's rest reading, leaves them at zero.
- **Appearance.** Skin albedo from the measured cheek norms of the recorded ancestry and sex, and iris, brow, hair and lip colours from the photograph by the reflectance-ratio rule on that albedo ([Measured skin albedo](#measured-skin-albedo)); hair lengths from how far the photograph's hair falls and how much of its forehead it covers ([Hair shape from the photograph](#hair-shape-from-the-photograph)).

A document now sets 19 or 20 shape channels (83 before) and 6 to 12 expression channels; `kdy1`, without a photograph, carries its recorded ancestry only. Hair, iris and material values are unchanged. Measured through the product editor under the portrait lens ([editor-lens-principled-receipt.json](population/editor-lens-principled-receipt.json)): 13 of 17 renders are nearest their own photograph, as the fits were, the landmark RMS is 0.039 inter-ocular (0.035 for the fits) and the eye aperture error is -0.004 (+0.024). The landmark anchors were rebound on the brow-binding neutral ([landmark-anchors-lens.json](population/landmark-anchors-lens.json)). The remaining systematic index errors, the lower face and chin widths 3.5 and 6.9% wider in the renders, sit on contour landmarks and are not a basis defect by the norms (the neutral's bigonial width is within Farkas's), so they stay recorded, not corrected.

### Landmark refit of shape and expression

The published documents had never used 57 of the basis's 141 shape channels (every canthal, epicanthal, lid-fold, cupid's bow, nasal dorsum and head-outline channel was zero), and their expression weights were the detector's blendshape scores copied onto channels of a different scale. `fit-face-landmarks.ts` refits both from each photograph's 468 mesh landmarks through the shared editor only:

- [landmark-anchors.json](population/landmark-anchors.json) binds every landmark to the neutral `Human` surface once per subject camera (`anchor-face-landmarks.ts`): the face oval is a silhouette, and a front-view anchor paired a turned photograph's far contour with the wrong surface point, which bent neck and ear channels in the first trial;
- shape channels enter through their sparse endpoint rows and expression channels through finite differences of the real builder, with the 2D similarity as joint Gauss-Newton variables, box constraints at each channel's range, a prior on the current document of strength (0.02 / 0.5)^2 x 468 / 68, a left-right symmetry prior, and a five times tighter prior on channels whose displacement field is not mirror symmetric (the mouth and nose lateral positions);
- every step is admitted by the builder and the final document with its hair.

Under the portrait lens (2 m, 8.84 degrees) the published documents measure a median landmark RMS of 0.036 inter-ocular with 11 of 17 renders nearest their own photograph; the refit reaches 0.0316 and 16 of 17 (median margin 0.018). The refit documents are not published. They express the photographs' smiles through lip roll, press and pucker with the jaw opened 0.24 to 0.37, where the detector reads the same photographs as smiling with a raised upper lip, closed lips over the teeth and no roll: the landmarks trace the lips but never the teeth, and on this basis a lip roll moves the lip seam the way a raised lip does, while the detector reads the basis neutral itself as a pucker of 0.69. The incisal-edge observations `fit-face-landmarks.ts` now takes did not separate the two on three subjects; the landmark anchors also bind about 1.6% of the points (lid margins, the inner lip seam, the silhouette) through a gap to a surface behind the one the camera sees. Both are open.

## Source and coordinates

The [numerical hair migration](numerical-hair.md) records shared scalp provenance, preparation/export commands and remaining visual limitations. The personal guide archive has been removed from this directory. The current numerical study inputs are initial authored candidates whose runtime and visual results must be read separately from the historical studies below.

The pinned [CC0 source and license record](../README.md#sources-and-license) applies. MPFB's macro target service supplies age structure, sexual dimorphism, adiposity and muscularity endpoints. These are dimensionless authored morphological axes, not estimates of a person's age, sex, body mass or health. The source macro values are recorded in [extraction-receipt.json](extraction-receipt.json); the source population mixture remains unchanged.

Whole-body macro targets also translate the head when stature changes. Each sampled endpoint therefore subtracts the change in the centroid of the complete bilateral eye surface from every attached surface. This preserves relative displacements while keeping a common binocular reference frame. It applies the same translation to skin, eyes, brows, lashes, teeth and tongue; it does not resize individual parts or correct a particular subject.

The neutral positions, materials, UVs, connectivity and all 212 preceding endpoints are exact. All six surfaces recover their neutral coordinates exactly after source extraction. The eight new signed endpoints passed the public builder and Float32 GLB exporter. Direct inspection covered their 72 canonical colour/clay views. Age and dimorphism visibly change the cranium, jaw and surrounding tissues; adiposity and muscularity act mainly on the retained neck in this source asset. They do not supply a measured physiological model or guarantee arbitrary combinations.

## Joint input fitting

The nineteen studies use one bounded robust whole-face objective. Six semantic landmark groups (lips, eyes, brows, outline, nose and the remaining surface) contribute equal mean-square terms instead of letting the largest landmark population dominate the fit. Signed positive and negative endpoints meet at a derivative discontinuity at zero, so each subject is solved from three complete shape vectors, zero and uniform -0.2/+0.2, retaining the smallest identical robust objective. No subject-specific loss or anatomical parameter is hardcoded. [subject-receipt.json](subject-receipt.json) retains all three costs, convergence results, source identities, per-group residuals, committed shape and actual exported GLB hashes.

### Landmarks anchor on the surface the camera sees

A landmark's template anchor is the frontmost point of the neutral mesh under its detected image position. The source head is one continuous skin surface whose only boundary is the neck clip at Y = -0.145 m, so the lids and the lips are folds that turn inward rather than holes. Where such a fold stands open the ray meets no outer skin at all, and the frontmost hit is the interior behind it. Eight of MediaPipe's twenty inner-lip landmarks anchored on the oral cavity about 96 mm behind the lips, and twelve of its thirty-two eye landmarks, the whole upper lid ring plus the outer corners, anchored on the orbital wall about 25 mm behind the lid margin. Those anchors carried their groups' full weight while driving interior geometry to match photographed contours.

The outer surface is now defined without naming any feature: take the mesh's frontmost triangle, then flood fill across shared edges through front-facing triangles only. A fold's back-facing band is never crossed, so the oral cavity and the orbit stay outside the component (21,019 of the 24,879 front-facing triangles remain inside it) while the face, lids, lips, nose and ears stay inside. A landmark whose ray misses that component anchors at the component point closest to it in the image plane, which is the aperture rim. Twenty-two anchors move: the eight lip anchors return to the lip surface while shifting at most 0.264 mm in the image plane, the twelve eye anchors return to the lid margin shifting at most 1.688 mm, and landmarks 58 and 288 stop falling back to a nearest vertex, so no landmark is down-weighted as a projection miss any more.

### Identity is symmetric, performance is not

The source neutral mirrors onto itself. Negating X and pairing every one of the 17,927 skin vertices with its mate leaves a root mean square deviation of 0.098 micrometres and a worst pairing of 0.366 micrometres, which is single-precision storage rather than real asymmetry; the pairing is a proper involution and 237 vertices sit on the midline and mate with themselves. Each right endpoint is the mirror of its left to within 0.0084 mm. Those figures are measured at publication and recorded in [subject-receipt.json](subject-receipt.json) under `basisMirror`, so a basis that is not symmetric cannot quietly inherit a fit that assumes it is. All of a fitted face's asymmetry is therefore something the fit chose, and it splits into an identity part with no evidence behind it and a performance part the detector reports per side. The total tracked the camera: subjects photographed square on departed from their own mirror by 0.3 to 1.0 mm, subjects photographed off axis by 1.2 to 3.0 mm, with a worst single vertex of 10.11 mm.

A missing camera degree of freedom was the first suspect and was measured and rejected. Giving the projection one bounded inverse distance, on the four subjects the experiment covered, finds physically plausible portrait distances of 0.62 to 1.38 m and lowers the reprojection residual by three to eight percent, but it does not reduce the mirror deviation: alan-rickman moves from 2.43 to 2.51 mm and emma-watson from 2.12 to 2.35, while generated-white-girl-01 falls from 2.99 to 2.46. The skew is not a perspective artifact, so a perspective term would not have fixed it. That candidate is preserved as research for its own topic.

The seventeen left/right shape pairs are now solved as one weight each. This removes a degree of freedom rather than penalizing one, so it carries no tuning constant, and because each right endpoint mirrors its left to within 0.0084 mm the resulting identity is symmetric to that same order: identity mirror deviation falls from a 0.994 mm mean to 0.009 mm, with 0.0125 mm the worst of the nineteen, which is the endpoints' own mirror residual rather than anything the fit did. Total mirror deviation falls from 1.260 mm to 0.650 mm and the worst single vertex from 10.11 mm to 5.75 mm.

What remains is performance. Expression channels stay free because the detector reports a separate score per side, and the fitted side gap follows the observed one rather than inventing it: 2.73 against 2.29 for alan-rickman, 3.12 against 2.60 for generated-white-girl-01, 0.49 against 0.42 for the frontal generated-korean-boy-01. One photograph cannot separate a genuinely asymmetric face from a symmetric one seen at an angle, so this study no longer claims identity asymmetry it cannot evidence. The editor still exposes both controls of every pair, because authoring asymmetry deliberately is a different act from inferring it from one photograph.

All nineteen were read against their sources at each solution's own fitted pose on a real device, and six off-axis subjects additionally at front, both three-quarters, both profiles and clay through the built editor. The two most oblique are visibly better: generated-white-girl-01 loses a pinched cheek and a skewed mouth, and alan-rickman's jaw stops leaning. The other seventeen are unchanged to the eye or nearly so, which is what the measurement predicts: the identity correction is a median of 0.822 mm per subject and under a millimetre for twelve of the nineteen, with generated-white-girl-01's 2.413 mm the largest. Nothing in the population regressed. Because a directional key light makes even an exactly symmetric mesh photograph asymmetric, the deviation itself was read as geometry rather than from the frames: every skin vertex plotted at its front-projected position and coloured by the distance to its mirrored mate. Under the previous solution the whole cheek and jaw carried four to eight millimetres; under this one that area is flat and what remains sits at the mouth corners, which is the smile.

The reprojection cost is real and is concentrated where an oblique silhouette lives. The equal-group root mean rises from 0.010889438 to 0.011024022, 1.2 percent. Lips, eyes and brows are unchanged to slightly better; outline rises 3.6 percent at the median with 3 of 19 improving, nose 6.1 percent with 4 of 19, and the remaining points 3.3 percent with none improving. The fit can no longer bend a face contour to match a skewed one, and that is the intended loss.

### The shape prior charges geometric commitment

A basis weight is dimensionless and its metres-per-unit spans a factor of about 160 across this basis, so a flat regularizer priced a millimetre of philtrum like a centimetre of skull. The shape regularizer is now 0.003 multiplied by the channel's own commitment (`measureHumanFaceBasisChannels(...).displacement` in `@automovie/human`) divided by the root-mean-square commitment over the basis's shape channels, which keeps the published 0.003 rate at a channel of typical commitment and redistributes the prior rather than strengthening it. Expression channels keep the flat 0.015 regularizer toward the detector's observed performance. These are numerical fitting choices, not clinical uncertainty estimates.

The consequence is that a channel's data-to-prior balance becomes the fraction of its motion the frontal landmarks can see. Committed shape falls from 20.29 mm to 14.91 mm per subject, and the part carried by channels whose in-plane sensitivity is under a tenth of their commitment falls from 1.28 mm to 0.47 mm. Averaged over the nineteen subjects, head depth returns to neutral (-0.214 to -0.005) and so do sexual dimorphism (-0.376 to -0.022) and age structure (-0.121 to -0.011), none of which a single frontal photograph can settle. Neck depth halves without reaching neutral (-0.183 to +0.091), and chin projection (+0.010 to +0.257) and mouth depth (+0.059 to +0.315) grow instead of shrinking; both are named here rather than hidden.

### Measured against one instrument

Repairing an anchor changes which mesh point a landmark is compared against, so a solution's own residual is not comparable across that change. Both the previously published solution and this one are therefore re-evaluated on the outer-shell anchors, the only anchor set that samples the outer surface at every landmark. The equal-group root mean falls from 0.014633742 to 0.010889438, a 25.6 percent reduction. Per group the subjects that improve, with the median change: eyes 19 of 19 (-27.7 percent), brows 19 of 19 (-14.0), nose 17 of 19 (-19.1), lips 18 of 19 (-11.5), outline 18 of 19 (-38.4) and the remaining points 15 of 19 (-10.1). The worst single regressions are nose +12.8 percent, other +10.2 and outline +5.4 on individual subjects.

Four candidates were measured and rejected, each preserved as research. Weakening expression regularity to 0.003 lowered central-feature residuals further but systematically removed mouth opening and tooth exposure from smiling inputs, so 0.015 stands. Calibrating the commitment-scaled prior at the median channel commitment rather than the root-mean-square multiplied its total mass by about four and made every group worse than this fit. Anchoring the lip landmarks on the authored lip material instead of on the outer shell repaired the lips but left the twelve eye anchors in the orbit, and its eyes group is about a third worse than this one. Re-mapping the detector's two lip rings onto the lip material's two border loops by arc length moved anchors that were already correct, the mouth corners by as much as 50 mm.

Direct review compared all nineteen subjects against their sources under each solution's own fitted weak-perspective pose on a real device. Heads that had been narrowed and flattened read fuller and closer to their sources, the generated-white-girl-01 cheek and mouth distortion is reduced, and open-mouthed sources recover more aperture and tooth exposure. A heavier brow and orbital shadow appears on several subjects.

Four subjects (alan-rickman, generated-white-girl-01, michael-gambon and kdy1) were then loaded into the built editor itself and captured at front, both three-quarters, both profiles, back and clay under both solutions, because the frontal view is the one the objective optimizes and therefore the least informative one to judge it by. What the other views showed is a silhouette the frontal fit had been free to get wrong. Measuring the cranial vault above the ears across all nineteen subjects, its width spanned 105.1 to 143.2 mm under the previous solution and spans 130.4 to 142.7 mm under this one, and the depth-to-width ratio tightened from 1.317 to 1.561 (standard deviation 0.0698) to 1.344 to 1.472 (0.0383). The two extremes were the two subjects whose frontal residual was worst: alan-rickman's vault was 108.8 mm wide and generated-white-girl-01's 105.1 mm, narrower than any adult skull, and both read in profile as a long head on a thin neck. Both now sit inside the population. The change is not uniformly in one direction. Ten of the nineteen ratios rose, michael-gambon's among them (1.320 to 1.432); what improved is that the population no longer contains impossible outliers, not that every subject moved toward the middle. Generic head appearance, weak older tissue and absent scalp grooming still limit likeness. Registration uses estimated weak perspective, not recovered physical camera intrinsics. These studies remain editable candidates without whole-person likeness acceptance.

### Single channels first

A single channel that already crosses says the shape itself is wrong, and no pair corrective would save it. On the neutral head six do: `jawOpen`, `jawLeft`, `jawRight`, `mouthFunnel` and `tongueOut` put the oral lining through the mandibular gum block as the jaw swings, and `mouthRollUpper` rolls the upper lip through the incisors. `repair-single-endpoints.ts` wears each at full weight, pushes what crosses clear by the standing orders below and folds the displacement into the endpoint itself: inside 3 mm where soft tissue over an arch gives that, and inside 8 mm where the floor of the mouth has to follow the mandible that swung out from under it, which the census measured as the authored floor lagging the gum block by up to that. `tongueOut` carries `jawOpen` whole, so `jawOpen`'s repair is applied to it and negated in `tongueOutJawOpenUnfold` as well, or the two together would apply it twice; `tongueOut` is then solved after, on the head that already has it. A channel still crossing at a fraction of its weight, which an endpoint cannot answer because a straight line has no middle, gets a single-driver in-between there, spanned to its neighbours. All six read clear at 1, ¾, ½ and ¼ afterwards.

### Pair correctives

Fifty-two expression channels make 1,326 pairs, and `enumerate-expression-pairs.ts neutral` wears the neutral head at every one of them. A pair counts only when it makes a pair of surfaces cross that neither channel alone makes cross; measured against the larger single instead, two channels on disjoint regions read as interacting, and shutting both eyes was the first false positive. On the single-repaired head 662 pairs interact past superposition and 109 invent a surface pair, up from 54 before the singles were repaired, because a crossing a single used to own is now the pair's.

Each of the 109 is solved by `solve-combination.ts`, one part pair at a time on one working head, with one of four answers by the shape of the crossing, each chosen because the others were measured to fail. A soft part inside a firm one is pushed clear by `push-out.ts` inside a 3 mm budget, along the side it sat on at rest, because a vertex inside a tooth is near every face of that tooth and the nearest face is as likely to lead into the mouth as out of it, and the push is relaxed over three rings so tissue moves rather than dents; the oral lining against the gum block gets the 8 mm the mandible took. Bone and the globe never yield, and between soft parts the enclosed one does, except that a tongue being put out is the agent of its pose and the lips part around it off its nearest face. A lip triangle through a skin triangle turned out, on every one of the 17 pairs, to be a fan folded over its own apex at the lip seam, both triangles sharing a vertex and the deeper corner 0.03 to 1.2 mm past the other's plane; `unfold.ts` draws each apex toward the centroid of its ring until the fans no longer cross beyond the four the source seam crosses at rest, inside 0.82 mm and twelve vertices, after pushing carried the fold along (16 to 63 vertices) and relaxing collapsed the lip's two layers (27 to 290). And a part that bone cannot let through and a budget cannot clear has its own motion taken back by `takeBack`, scaled by bisection until it is clear, not to rest but to the pose less the channel that did the crossing: a lip rolled over the incisors with the jaw thrust forward unrolls and the jaw stays thrust, and a tongue put out through sealed or puckered lips comes out less and keeps whatever the jaw did, because returning it to rest under teeth that had moved was measured to cross by more than the pose did.

The activation of a corrective is a product of clamped drivers, which is bilinear, so a corrective solved at full weight lands at a quarter of itself at half weights. A driver may therefore name the weight it peaks at and the weights on either side where it fades to nothing, and its factor becomes a tent that is one there and zero at its neighbours; `generate-combination-correctives.ts` wears each pair at (½, 1), (1, ½) and (½, ½) in turn, each with every earlier tier present, measures what crosses at that pose against that pose's own singles, and publishes what remains as such an in-between. The revision carries 134 correctives: the two authored earlier, three single-driver in-betweens from the repair step, 109 solved at full weight and 25 pair in-betweens, and not one of the 109 combinations stays beyond its budget.

The earlier population experiment stored per-subject vertex correctives. Issue #2533 rejects that representation: published documents now contain numerical shape/expression controls, material overrides and resource identities only. Legacy geometry is retained in Git history rather than replayed by the editor. Shared shape/expression interactions must be repaired and verified in the basis. The earlier per-document crossing result does not establish validity of these compact documents; their population verification is pending.

`verify-combination-correctives.ts` then wears the neutral head and every subject at every enumerated pair and four weights, with and without any pair corrective, and folds the result into [pair-corrective-receipt.json](pair-corrective-receipt.json) under `verification`, so the receipt says what the revision has been verified to do rather than what it was meant to do. Weights off the {½, 1} grid are the fine grid `--grid fine` measures separately.

## Articulated revision

The [reassessment](architecture-reassessment.md) asked for one shared articulated evaluator before any further personal tuning, and this revision is that evaluator's data. The generic evaluation lives in `@automovie/human` (`createHumanFaceBasisBuilder`: rest layer, landmarks, `resolveHumanFaceArticulation`, `poseHumanFaceSurface`); this directory carries what it evaluates.

### Attachments from the source

`test/scripts/face-review/extract-face-attachments.py` runs inside Blender 5.1 with the pinned MPFB revision `817587ceb2ea03ea17a5b47e04396cbb4ddfa2d5` enabled from source, the same session the body extraction uses: the default human, the CC0 `default` rig weights bound as vertex groups (the mandible as the `jaw` subtree, each globe as its `eye` bone) and one limit-surface Catmull-Clark round, which reproduces every one of the 16,942 `Human` vertices (16,758 exact twins, 184 clipped-edge points, worst match 1.6e-17 m). It reads the centroids of thirteen `joint-*` cubes in the face frame, recovers the MPFB target or macro node behind every one of the 274 published shape endpoints by measurement (worst residual 1.8e-14 m; the recipes were never tracked before), and publishes the cube motion of the 29 endpoints that move a cube as sparse landmark rows. [face-attachments.json.gz](face-attachments.json.gz) and its [receipt](face-attachments-receipt.json) hold the result; the neutral restores to the last bit.

The painted weights turned out to be the wrong attachment for this source: the jaw subtree reaches 0.92 at the chin and leaves a 4.19 mm rms residual against the source's own `jawOpen` after the rigid motion is removed, while the source's pure-translation mandibular endpoints (`jawForward`, `jawLeft`, `jawRight`) reveal a per-vertex weight directly (`E = w t` up to a 0.07 mm mean off-axis remainder; the two lateral channels agree to 0.06 rms) with a 2.85 mm rms residual. The revision attaches the skin and tongue with those measured weights (8,190 skin vertices, 60 at unit weight, 714 clamped by at most 0.0095; the whole tongue at unit weight), the mandibular arch and both globes at unit weight from the legacy rigid membership, and records the painted comparison in the receipt.

### Joints from the source and the literature

The opening endpoint's screw (21.155 degrees about +X, pivot (0, -15.7, 58.8) mm, slide 0) is decomposed under the in vivo coupling of condylar translation to rotation, Chen et al. 2021 (Dentomaxillofac Radiol 50:20190464): 14.3 mm forward and 3.5 mm down at 27.4 degrees, about 0.5 mm per degree. Under that coupling the same transform is a rotation about (0, 15.2, 60.6) mm plus a coupled translation of (0, -2.7, 11.0) mm, and that axis point sits 18.6 mm below the globe centres, where the condyle is; the source's low screw pivot is the equivalent instantaneous centre of a rotation plus a forward translation, which is also why the helical axis of habitual opening is never inside the condyle (Gallo et al. 1997). The axis point is published as an offset (0, 13.8, 4.1) mm from the `joint-mouth` cube and follows it through every shape channel. Protrusion (0, -1.5, 8.3) mm and laterotrusion (±6.6, -0.8, 4.3) mm are the source's authored translations. The summed sagittal translation of opening and protrusion is budgeted at Chen's 14.72 mm; a document past it is refused by name rather than clamped, which is what closes the bottom of Posselt's envelope.

Each globe turns about its `joint-l-eye`/`joint-r-eye` cube, 15.2 mm behind the corneal apex against the 13.5 to 15.3 mm the centre-of-rotation literature reports (Ohlendorf et al. 2022), by the angle each authored gaze endpoint fits (up 9.6, down 11.1, in 14.8, out 14.3 degrees) plus the eccentric translation the source authored with it (up to 1.85 mm, bounded at 3 mm), so the lids the source sculpted around its globe stay coherent. A blink turns no globe (Doane 1980).

### Residuals and what was retired

Every expression endpoint and corrective is rewritten as a rest-space residual over the articulation (`unpose(neutral + E) - neutral`), so a smile authored at rest travels with an open jaw, and every expression replays its authored pose at weight one to under a micrometre. `tongueOut` had carried `jawOpen` whole; it is now decomposed at the open-jaw state, so tongue plus open jaw replays the authored pose while `tongueOut` alone is the combination the source never authored and the census reports (the tongue through closed teeth). The teeth and globes publish no expression rows: `jawOpenMouthCloseEase` had lifted the mandibular arch 19.6 mm to dodge the closing lips, and that row is gone with the other 16 dropped rows the receipt lists; the 19 correctives that compensated the tongue's double count are removed, leaving `jawOpenMouthCloseEase` and the three opening in-betweens as skin and tongue residuals. All eighteen documents keep every numerical value and name the new revision.

### What the census says

`export-articulation-census.ts` and `capture-articulation.mjs` run fourteen coupled states per subject through the actual runtime and the engine's triangle-crossing census, then render them on a real GPU. Against the previous revision evaluated on the same states with its own rule, on the neutral and two subjects: rest, protrusion, laterotrusion and blink are identical; every gaze state and tongue-with-open-jaw replay their authored poses; full opening crosses the skin less (150 to 122 and 209 to 145 crossed triangles) and half opening slightly more (84 to 101, 20 to 46), because the teeth now travel the coupled arc while the half-open lips were authored to the old chord. Two combinations are now visibly unsupported instead of hidden: lips fully closed over a fully open jaw (0 to about 300 to 480 skin and teeth crossings, the lifted-teeth cheat gone) and the tongue out through closed teeth. Both are contact problems the articulation makes explicit and does not solve; the coupled contact evaluator is the next owner.

## Contact revision

The articulated revision left two combinations visibly unsupported, lips closed over an open jaw and the tongue through closed teeth, and the lining crossing the gums under opening. This revision is the coupled contact evaluator's data: the generic evaluation lives in `@automovie/human` (`measureHumanFaceAperture`, `evaluateHumanFacePassage`, `resolveHumanFaceContact` inside `createHumanFaceBasisBuilder`) on the engine's oriented sheet query; `prepare-contact-basis.ts` measured what this directory now carries and [contact-receipt.json](contact-receipt.json) records it.

### What the source's `mouthClose` is

MPFB ships `mouthClose` as the ARKit face unit "closes the lips while the jaw remains open": a delta authored over the open jaw. The articulated revision had decomposed it at the closed jaw, so at `jawOpen` 1 the delta rode the 21 degree turn of the mandible and the lower lip rolled 11.9 mm off the seam; `jawOpenMouthCloseEase` had been compensating exactly that (its largest row is the same 11.861 mm) and is retired. The rows are now the delta unposed through the per-vertex blend at `jawOpen` 1, and the builder applies them scaled by the ratio of the current interlabial aperture to the aperture `jawOpen` alone produces, both read between the vermilion seam vertices 15892 and 16040 (0.89 mm apart at rest) along the cranial vertical. On the neutral the closure seals the seam at full opening (ratio 1.000, 0.69 mm) and at half opening (ratio 0.500, 0.56 mm) instead of overshooting; `mouthClose` at a closed jaw does nothing, which is what the face unit means.

### Tongue passage and the crowns

The tongue is a constant-volume muscular hydrostat (Kier and Smith 1985); it cannot be pressed through closed incisors or sealed lips. A tongue past the posed lower incisal edge (vertices 3806 over 2593, 0.31 mm apart at rest) must be thinner over a 2 mm slab than both the interincisal and the interlabial aperture, or the document is refused naming the channel and the millimetres: `tongueOut` alone reads "puts a 9.6 mm tongue through a 0.3 mm gap; jawOpen must open it by at least 9.3 mm more", and it is the only single endpoint the preparation's probe refuses. The 32 crowns of the dental surface are open caps with twelve-vertex root rings; each ring is sealed with a fan wound against its rim (320 triangles over resident vertices), the two gum sheets (96 and 102 boundary vertices) stay open, and the sealed surface is admitted as an oriented sheet whose rim features report no side.

### The floor rule and its budgets

Every soft vertex keeps the clearance it has in its shape-only rest state: at rest the tongue's sides lie 1.48 mm inside the lower molars and the lining touches the gums, and both stay so; only tissue a pose pushes deeper is moved back to that floor along the nearest feature, seam copies together. The skin's budget is Holdaway's upper lip thickness, vermilion point to upper incisor, 13.41 mm (Saudi Dent J 2011, n=93); the tongue's is its own 20.8 mm height along the vertical, a push larger than the tissue being the tissue in the wrong place rather than a deformation. Beside them the receipt records what the source itself authored: the deepest excess the floor rule finds on any single expression endpoint is 6.1 mm on the skin (the vestibular lining under the swinging gums) and 1.76 mm on the tongue (the source's own `jawOpen` reshapes the tongue into the molars by that much). All eighteen documents build against the new block without their grooms rebuilt.

### Lids over the cornea

The same block carries the ocular contact. Each globe of `Human.low-poly` is open at its posterior pole by a ring of thirty-two vertices and is sealed like a crown (60 triangles, both globes closed and outward); the lashes join the soft surfaces with their own 14.3 mm height as budget. At rest the lids lie up to 2.29 mm inside the globes, as the source authored them, and that is the floor each lid vertex keeps. Measured on the neutral before this revision, lateral gaze carried 848 lid vertices deeper than their rest clearance by up to 1.83 mm and a blink 781 by up to 2.67 mm, while vertical gaze moved nothing beyond 0.13 mm because the source authored those rows on the globe; after it no vertex sits deeper than its rest clearance by more than 0.09 mm in any of the four states, and the vertices inside a globe fall from 1089 to 372 at lateral gaze and from 1195 to 477 at a blink. The triangle census reads more skin and globe crossings in those states (neutral blink 606/452 to 1445/726), because a lid held at its rest clearance runs along the globe's surface and every triangle that straddles it counts, where before whole regions lay entirely inside; the renders of blink and lateral gaze are unchanged to the eye. No lid rule exists: the floor rule gained a collider.

### What the census says

`export-articulation-census.ts` and `capture-articulation.mjs` again ran fourteen coupled states per subject on the neutral and two subjects. Against the articulated revision: every state that was authored replays it (rest, blink, gaze, protrusion and laterotrusion are identical to the triangle); `tongue-closed` is refused by name on all three heads instead of rendering a tongue through closed teeth; `open-close` closes the lips on the seam, and its lip and teeth crossings are zero as they were. The skin and teeth counts are unchanged (neutral open-close 70/237, full opening 34/48) because they are the vestibular lining below the modelled gum's open base, where the collider has no floor to give: the census shows them, the floor rule does not reach them, and a closed alveolar shell is a data follow-up. The tongue and teeth counts move by a few percent as the floor rule returns the tongue's sides to their rest clearance (neutral full opening 329/216 to 354/273), well inside the 487/410 the source authored at rest.

## Dental revision

The source hid the upper incisors at rest: the upper central incisal edge sat 0.56 mm above the upper lip's vermilion seam, so no smile showed the upper teeth and the population renders showed the lower arch and the tongue where the photographs show upper crowns. The upper lip itself is ordinary (20.5 mm from subnasale to stomion) and the incisors meet within the Ricketts norms (overbite 1.67 mm against 2.5 +- 2, overjet 0.97 mm against 2.5 +- 2.5), so the height of the upper dentition was what was out of place. The resting display of the upper central incisor is 1.91 mm in men and 3.40 mm in women (Vig and Brundo 1978; 2.5 and 3.8 mm in Misch's later sample); the basis carries no sex, so the target is the midpoint of the first study, 2.655 mm, and a sex, age or lip-length difference is left to the lip's own shape channels, which move the lip over a fixed dentition as tissue moves over bone.

`prepareDentalPosition` reads each incisal edge from the crown that holds the contact's incisal pair (the pair marks where the two crowns meet, not their edges) and lowers only the maxillary arch, the dentition vertices not bound to the mandible, as one rigid body. A first candidate lowered both arches and the tongue by 3.22 mm; `export-articulation-census.ts` found the lower gum's open base through the floor of the mouth at rest (0 to 26 crossed skin triangles on the neutral, 117 at full opening instead of 34), because the source's lower gum clears that lining by less than two millimetres, and lowering the maxillary arch alone with the mandibular arch lowered less still crossed. The mandibular arch and the tongue therefore stay seated on the mandible, and lowering the upper arch deepens the overbite by the same amount: the shift stops at the smaller of the display target and the upper normal overbite, 4.5 mm. On this basis the overbite binds, so the maxillary arch moves 2.83 mm and the display is 2.26 mm, between the two sexes' means.

Every vertex identity (contact pairs, colliders, attachments, budgets) and every endpoint row (they are displacements) is kept, and the real builder admits the neutral and all eighteen documents, which are restamped and otherwise unchanged. [dental-receipt.json](dental-receipt.json) records the measurements before and after, the bound that held, the citations and the input and output digests. The census of the neutral and two subjects against the ocular-contact revision reads no new skin or lip crossing at rest, in the smile or at half opening; at full opening the lowered upper incisors cross the upper lip's lining (26 lip triangles on the neutral, 9 and 1 on the subjects), and the tongue, which the source already authored through the teeth at rest (487 tongue triangles), crosses the lowered arch more (588; 760 with the jaw protruded). Both are inside the mouth; they are recorded, not solved. The landmark anchors were rebound on this revision's neutral renders, and again on the appearance revision's with occluders.

## Appearance revision

Two shared material facts of the source were wrong for a portrait. The globe material had roughness 0.55, which spreads a light's reflection over the whole eye: no catchlight formed and a dark iris read grey. The ocular surface is covered by the tear film, an optically smooth layer, so the revision gives every articulated eye's globe material roughness 0 and leaves the highlight of the editor's point lights to the renderer's own roughness floor. The crowns' enamel was a grey near L* 59.5, a* 1.9, b* 1.5; spectrophotometric samples of maxillary central incisors read L* 73.5, a* 2.2, b* 11.9 (vital enamel, PMC9381640) and L* 73.02, a* -0.54, b* 14.50 (Chinese adults, PMC10155942), so the texels of the 32 crowns the contact seals as colliders are shifted to their mean, L* 73.26, a* 0.83, b* 13.20, keeping the texture's own variation (603,824 texels; the gums are untouched). `prepareAppearanceBasis` finds both surfaces by geometry, never by an asset name, restamps the eighteen documents and has the real builder admit them.

The sclera's albedo stays as the source painted it. The ratio of the bright sclera (90th percentile inside the lid aperture, outside 1.15 iris radii) to the cheek spreads from 0.5 to 3.2 across the seventeen photographs with lid shade, narrow apertures and speculars, and no literature value for in-vivo scleral reflectance was found, so nothing here fixes it.

The same publication writes each document's brow pigment. `fit-face-brows.ts` reads the darkest tenth of each photograph's brow outline, hair excluded, against its cheek, and times that linear ratio by the document's skin albedo (the iris fit's rule) into the brow card's `pigment`, painted by the shared fibre rule. Sixteen documents carry one; Oh Seung-yoon's fringe covers his brows, and `kdy1` has no photograph.

## Population revision

The source builds a human from macro axes by a multilinear blend: each `race-gender-age` target of `macrodetails` is weighted by the product of the race share, the gender component and the age component, and each `universal-gender-age-muscle-weight` target by the product of its four (MPFB `TargetService.calculate_target_stack_from_macro_info_dict`). The basis carried the dimorphism and age axes as linear channels sampled one at a time, so a combination of them lacked the products, and it carried no ancestry at all. Summed linearly, a pure ancestry with a sex and an age is off by 1.0 to 2.6 mm rms over the face; an ancestry alone moves it 1.9 to 3.8 mm, as much as the whole dimorphism axis (3.8 mm).

`extract-face-population.py` samples the model in Blender 5.1 with the pinned MPFB at every corner the channels span: race (the equal mixture or one pure ancestry) x gender {0, 0.5, 1} x age {0.25, 0.5, 1}, on all six surfaces. The skin goes through the committed limit-surface correspondence; the five proxies are the system-asset pack's (every file matching [its receipt](../system-assets-receipt.json)) fitted by MPFB's own `fit_clothes_to_human` and subdivided as the basis has them (eyes twice and the tongue once on the limit surface, the rest not at all), each reproducing the pre-dental basis exactly at the neutral. The same path reproduces the published dimorphism and age endpoints within 0.4 micrometres on every surface, which is what makes its new samples trustworthy. `population_rows.py` removes each state's eye-centroid change (the frame of the original extraction) and expands the multilinear function into its corner terms: one endpoint per ancestry share, a product term for every pair of axes and one for the triple, 28 correctives of 1.1 to 2.0 mm rms each. The builder evaluates a corrective as the product of its clamped drivers, which is that expansion, so `verify-population-basis.ts` replays all 36 sampled corners through the real builder within 2.35 micrometres (the rows are rounded to one).

The three shares (`africanAncestry`, `asianAncestry`, `europeanAncestry`) each replace the source's equal mixture; shares of one document sum to at most one. Documents that set none of the population channels build exactly as before, and the eighteen were restamped unchanged. The landmark anchors were bound on the appearance revision's neutral, which this revision leaves untouched.

## Brow rest revision

The source seated its brow card on the upper lid's sulcus: on the pupil's vertical its hair rows lay 7 to 9 mm above the pupil, while the skin's own brow prominence, the soft tissue over the supraorbital rim, crests 16 to 18 mm above it, and the brow overlies the orbital rim. The resting brow height, from the central inferior corneal limbus to the first row of mature brow hairs in primary gaze, was 12.9 mm on the neutral against 19.4 mm (men) and 19.7 mm (women) in 213 subjects (Cole, Winn and Putterman, Ophthalmic Plast Reconstr Surg 2010;26:443-447; no significant sex difference). Three other readings agreed: the photographs, through the same detector, put the brow apex about 5 mm further above the lid than any published render could reach; 14 of 17 principled documents held the brow control at its bound; and the detector read the neutral as a lowered brow (browDown 0.42 and 0.33).

`prepareBrowRestBasis` therefore moves only the card. Every card vertex is lifted by one height and keeps its clearance from the skin along the view axis, read at its old and new heights on the skin's front-most surface at its own x, and the height is the one that brings the mean of both sides to 19.55 mm: 6.62 mm. The skin, globes and every endpoint row are untouched (the rows are displacements), and the eighteen documents build unchanged. A first attempt scaled the source's frontalis field (brow raise) to the same height; it stretched the card vertically and creased the forehead and was discarded.

## Lower face revision

## Brow binding revision

The brow rest revision reseated the card but kept the rows authored for its old seat on the lid sulcus. On the brow prominence the source's frontalis lifts the skin by up to 5.3 mm, and the card, following its stale rows, rose 1.8 mm at its lower edge and 3.5 mm at its upper edge in a full brow raise: it lagged the skin and stretched. MPFB binds a proxy card to the body, each vertex a weighted sum of body vertices, so hair moves with the skin it grows from. `prepareBrowBindingBasis` does the same at the card's present seat: each card vertex attaches to the front-most skin triangle at its own x and y, and every skin target (177 of them: shapes, expressions, correctives and population rows) is written to the card as the barycentric blend of that triangle's rows. A full raise now lifts the card 4.7 to 5.0 mm on the pupil's vertical without stretching it; healthy adults raise the brow a median 7.2 mm (interquartile 5.9 to 10.5), so the source's frontalis stays on the small side and is recorded, not scaled.

## Lower lash revision

The source lash card draws both lids from one texture at one density: its lower pieces cover 124.5 mm² of card, 78% of the upper pieces' 160.3 mm², so in every render the lower lashes read as a dark fringe as full as the upper ones. The lower lid carries 75 to 80 lashes and the upper 90 to 160 (Aumond and Bitton, J Optom 2018;11:211-222), a count ratio of 0.62 at each range's middle. A card's drawn strands cover an area proportional to their count times their length, and a card's strand length is its area over its span across the eye (7.0 mm upper, 5.8 mm lower, as the card draws them); keeping those lengths, the lower card should cover 160.3 × 0.62 × 5.8 / 7.0 = 82.0 mm². `prepareLowerLashBasis` moves the pieces lying wholly below their eye's corneal apex to a copy of the lash material and clears whole drawn clumps from that copy's texture at an even spacing along the lid, keeping the most that do not exceed the target: 21 of 30 clumps, 72.7 mm². Covered area counts only texels passing the material's alpha cutoff, since a masked card is either drawn or not; the first attempt, scaling the copy's alpha by the count ratio, left every strand above the cutoff and changed nothing on screen. No published per-lid lash length was found (the review gives only that lashes rarely exceed 12 mm), so the card's own lengths stand.

The documents were derived again on this revision by the shared rules, with the anchors rebound on its neutral and the expression calibration rendered on it ([editor-lens-principled-receipt.json](population/editor-lens-principled-receipt.json)): the median landmark RMS falls from 0.0388 to 0.0363 inter-ocular (mean 0.0469 to 0.0455), 12 of 17 renders are nearest their own photograph (13 before), and the eye aperture error is -0.006 (-0.004).

## Lip seal revision

The source's `mouthSmile` pair lifts the lower lip's midline 6.0 mm and the upper lip's 3.2 mm, so the smiling lips cross: the midline vermilion seam goes from 0.88 mm open at rest to 2.01 mm overlapped, and 94 upper-lip triangles pass through lower-lip ones (22 for one side alone). A crossing census of part pairs does not count it because both lips lie on one surface. Whatever a smile does to the corners, the lower lip cannot pass through the upper one; a posed smile in fact parts them (Banditsaowapak and Cheng, J Dent Sci 2025;20:2219-2230: the upper lip's lower edge rises 4.76 +- 2.69 mm and the lower lip's upper edge falls 3.28 +- 2.02 mm in 41 Class I adults). `prepareLipSealBasis` adds to each side of the smile the multiple of the same side's `mouthLowerDown` endpoint, the source's own lower lip depressor, that returns the bilateral smile's midline seam to its rest aperture: 0.725, inside the channel's envelope. The smile no longer crosses, bilateral or one-sided, and nothing else moves. A sealed closed-lip smile is the least a smile can do to the seam, not a fit to the posed-smile norm; the rest of a posed smile's opening belongs to `mouthUpperUp` and `mouthLowerDown`.

The documents were derived again on this revision with two further shared changes: the expression calibration renders a unit with its partner on the other side (`faceExpressionPartner`: the same muscle, the conjugate gaze for the eyes, none for the directional mouth and jaw units), because the detector reads a side acting alone weaker than the same side acting with its partner; and the hair ribbons face away from the scalp (`buildHumanFaceHairMesh`), which had read their own shadow on the key-lit side. Measured ([editor-lens-principled-receipt.json](population/editor-lens-principled-receipt.json)): 14 of 17 renders are nearest their own photograph (12 before), the median landmark RMS over the sixteen subjects both runs measured is 0.0367 inter-ocular (0.0363), the head-region hair IoU 0.573 (0.525) and the hair colour difference 9.1 (12.8). The mouth corner lift reads -0.041 (-0.017): with its partner the smile's calibration spans more of the detector's scale, so each photograph asks for less of it, and the eye squint pair no longer reaches the photographs' spread, while the detector reads a squint as part of a smile.

## Measured skin albedo

Every document's `materials.skin.color` had been authored per subject, and every photograph ratio rule (iris, brow, hair and now lips) multiplies it. A photograph cannot give the albedo itself: exposure scales the whole image. The authored values ran from 1.2 to 2.7 times the measured cheek norms (0.97 red for one European subject, whose group averages 0.45). `derive-skin-albedo-norms.py` integrates the International Skin Spectra Archive's cheek spectra (Lu et al., Sci Data 2025;12:487, doi:10.1038/s41597-025-04857-5; 1,954 subjects measured with di:8 spectrophotometers, specular included; CC BY 4.0) with the CIE 1931 2 degree observer under D65 into linear sRGB, averages each subject's readings, and reports each group's mean and deviation overall and by sex ([skin-albedo-norms.json](population/skin-albedo-norms.json)); the group means reproduce the paper's lightness (Caucasian L* 61.6 against 61.2 over all sites, African 40.7 against 39.6). `faceSkinAlbedo` gives a subject the mean of the groups measuring its recorded ancestry and sex (East Asian is the Chinese and Japanese groups together, the archive having no Korean one), and `fit-face-skin.ts` writes it; an individual's deviation from its group is not recoverable from an uncalibrated photograph, so the group mean is the estimate. The iris, brow, hair and lip fits then run again on it, the lips from their own new sample of each photograph's vermilion (`faceLikenessLipColour`, `fit-face-lips.ts`).

The fits' record is [appearance-fit.txt](population/appearance-fit.txt). Measured ([editor-lens-principled-receipt.json](population/editor-lens-principled-receipt.json)): the skin over sclera luminance error, the one colour signal in which exposure cancels, falls from 1.75 to 0.97; the mean landmark RMS over the sixteen subjects both runs measured falls from 0.0467 to 0.0440 and the median rises from 0.0372 to 0.0379, with 12 of 17 renders nearest their own photograph (14). The cheek colour difference rises from 18.4 to 24.8 CIELAB units, because the photographs are exposed for the face while the editor's exposure is fixed, so a skin at its measured albedo reads darker than a photograph's; that is the render's exposure, not the skin's albedo. The SCI readings include the surface's specular reflection, which the renderer adds again from its own dielectric term; the documented difference between specular-included and excluded skin readings is small against the group deviations and is left as a stated limit.

## Lip parting from the lips' own landmarks

The detector's score for the upper lip raiser spans less on the basis than across the photographs (`faceExpressionObservable`), so the toothy smiles of seven photographs never reached their documents, whose lips stayed sealed over the teeth. The lips' own landmarks measure the same state directly: `lipParting`, stomion superius to inferius (landmarks 13 and 14) over mouth width, is an anthropometric index like the others, except that it reads a state of the face rather than its form, so its control is written as expression and is left at zero in the identity-only pass whose renders are the expression transfer's rest reading. Over closed teeth two muscles part the lips, the upper lip raiser (`mouthUpperUp`) and the lower lip depressor (`mouthLowerDown`), and a posed smile moves both: the upper lip's lower edge rises 4.76 mm and the lower lip's upper edge falls 3.28 mm, from a 1.8 to a 10.5 mm gap (Banditsaowapak and Cheng 2025). The control sets both pairs together; at full weight the source's pairs part the seam from 0.9 to about 9.8 mm. The lip heights now run to each lip's own inner edge (ls to stomion superius, stomion inferius to li, subnasale to stomion superius), as posed-smile studies measure them (Ren et al., Korean J Orthod 2026;56:34-44), so parted lips lend neither vermilion half their gap.

Stomion superius and inferius are now the basis's own vermilion seam pair (`contact.lips`), and every other inner lip landmark is cast onto its own lip, the lower lip being the vertices the mandible carries (a full opening moves them at least half as far as the lower seam vertex), holding a hidden one on its lip's nearest vertex.

## Corner lift and the face frame

The smile's defining action is the mouth corner's rise (the zygomaticus major; 6.2 mm in a posed smile, Banditsaowapak and Cheng 2025). The detector's smile unit also reads the eye squint of a genuine smile, and once the calibration rendered each unit with its partner the documents' smiles under-read their photographs (mouth corner lift error -0.041). `cornerLift`, the corners' (61, 291) rise above the lip centre (13, 14) over mouth width, now pairs with the smile pair as an expression index and supersedes the detector's smile transfer; the measurement's corner lift is therefore a derivation input rather than an independent check.

Every earlier index is an unsigned ratio and was unaffected; the turn now puts the midline's first landmark (the brow's) above its last (the chin's).

## Hair shape from the photograph

The hair lengths had been authored per subject, and the renders showed it: Maggie Smith's short hair hung 0.97 inter-ocular below her chin, Park Eun-bin's fringe covered 0.73 of her forehead and eyes against her photograph's 0.14. `fit-face-hair-shape.ts` reads two indices on the photograph's head hair (the segmenter's components that reach above the brows, so a collar or a beard does not count) and on the document's built model under the same camera, from the hair the camera sees (`faceHairVisibleMask`: skin and hair rasterized with depth at the view's width over three viewport heights, so a model's hair hanging below the frame still ends), and pairs each with one control ([hair-shape.json](population/hair-shape.json)):

- the drop (`faceHairDropIndex`), chin to lowest hair over inter-ocular, with a common factor on each layer's hanging lengths (left, right, nape and back);
- the fringe (`faceHairFringeCoverage`), the share of the forehead and eyes between the eye corners and above the lower lids that hair covers, with a factor on the front length. The region reaches through the palpebral fissures: ending at the upper lids, it saturated once a fringe reached them and read Miriam Margolyes's render, curls over both eyes, like her photograph, whose eyes are clear.

The crown's length lies over the scalp and keeps its authored value. Each factor is solved by a secant within [0.2, 3], drop first; a document already within tolerance keeps its lengths. An index the photograph cannot give keeps them too: hair cut by the frame (one photograph) and hair falling further below the chin than the shoulders sit, where it lies on shoulders a head model does not have: the acromion's drop below menton in ANSUR II ([shoulder-drop-norms.json](population/shoulder-drop-norms.json), `derive-shoulder-drop-norms.py`: 79.1 ± 18.3 mm in 4082 men, 71.3 ± 15.7 mm in 1986 women, the subject-weighted mean when sex is unrecorded), in the model's own inter-ocular units from its anchored eye corners (Emma Watson's hair falls 1.72 against her shoulders' 0.86). So does one its control cannot reach: Michael Gambon's hair combs back rather than down, and the generated Black girl's front locks hang over her face, where her photograph's are parted off it, so her lowest hair is set by their direction, not their length. Twelve drops and two fringes move (Maggie Smith's hanging lengths by 0.35 to her photograph's 0.28 above the chin, Kim Min-jung's front by 1.83 to her fringe's 0.41). Where a drop factor sits at its lower bound (Kim Min-jung, Rupert Grint and the short curly head 0.20) the photograph's hair ends near the temples, and a side hairline or flow authored lower than the photograph's may share the difference; the index cannot tell them apart and that is its stated limit.

## Hair shadows by fibre transmittance

The editor's key light cast an opaque shadow from every hair card, so every card behind the outermost layer went dark whatever the hair's colour: a white head (Miriam Margolyes, fitted albedo 1) rendered at L* 33 against 54.5 with the shadows off, while dark heads barely changed (Kim Min-jung 9.8 against 10.1). Across the population the render's hair-to-cheek luminance ratio fell against the photograph's for light hair (log ratio -2.28, -0.87, -0.71) and rose for dark hair (+0.92, +0.44). Light hair is bright throughout because its fibres absorb little and pass light on to the fibres behind them; dark hair stops it.

A hair-card mesh now casts through a depth material (`createHairCardShadowMaterial`) that keeps each covered shadow-map texel with the probability its fibre stops light (`hairFibreShadowOpacity`). That probability combines Chiang, Bitterli, Tappan and Burley's map from the hair's colour to its fibre absorption (Computer Graphics Forum 2016, at pbrt's azimuthal roughness 0.3), the straight transmission exp(-2 sigma sqrt(1 - h^2)) averaged over the impact offset, and keratin's two surface reflections (index 1.55), taken as luminance. Interleaved gradient noise on the map's texel grid sets the threshold, so the soft-shadow filter averages it into a partial shadow (stochastic transparency, Enderton et al. 2010). Miriam Margolyes's hair renders at L* 52.3; the four darker heads measured move by at most 0.5.

## Face cover

## Canthal tilt

The eye's slant had no index, so a photograph's slanted or drooping eye could not reach the model. `canthalTilt` reads the palpebral fissure's inclination (Hall et al., Am J Med Genet A 2009;149A:29-39: the en-ex line against the horizontal) as the exocanthion's rise above endocanthion over the fissure's length, signed, and pairs it with the lateral canthus elevation pair, which turns the fissure about 3.7 degrees per unit on the basis and moves no other index by more than 2.4 percent.

Read on the source itself, with each fissure corner taken where the globe rather than the lids is front-most (a 0.1 mm frontal depth buffer of lids and globe, each corner the centroid of the fissure's last millimetre), the European corners tilt 1.7 degrees in men and 3.7 in women and the African 1.7 and 3.5, within the White and African-American norms (Farkas 1994: 2.1 and 4.1; Price et al. 2009: 3.6 and 5.8, 3.9 and 6.0; Gao et al. 2025 in three dimensions: 0.8 and 2.4), but the Asian corners tilt 7.35 in both sexes. Korean, Japanese and Chinese adults show women 0.9, 1.18 and 1.12 degrees above men (Park et al. 2008; Takahagi et al. 2008; Gao et al. 2025). `prepareCanthalTiltBasis` ([canthal-tilt-receipt.json](canthal-tilt-receipt.json)) moves the Asian dimorphism correctives, the male one by -a and the female by +a of the source's lateral canthus field, until the Asian difference is their mean, 1.07 degrees: the men 6.79 and the women 7.86 (the three samples' means 6.46 and 7.54). A sex-neutral document keeps its tilt.

## Lashes from lash anatomy

The source's lash texture is a cosmetic one: clumped strands three to six texture pixels wide, 0.2 to 0.4 mm on the card where a lash is 61 to 72 um across, and a lower lid drawn nearly as full as the upper; rendered, it gave the eyes spider legs. `prepareEyelashBasis` ([eyelash-receipt.json](eyelash-receipt.json)) redraws each lid's cards. A card is a triangulated grid whose root row is the long row by the lid's skin; each lash is rooted along it and runs across the card, its length read as arc length on the card's own curved surface, so the card's curl is the lash's. The upper lid holds 160 lashes (Na et al. 2006: about 147 in Asian and 172 in Caucasian women), 7.4 mm long at the centre (Kikuchi et al. 2015) and 0.844 and 0.891 of that at the medial and lateral ends (Adekanmbi 2019), 66 um across; the lower lid 0.44 of the upper's lashes (Kikuchi 2015), 5.27 mm long, 0.77 of the upper's diameter (Elder 1997). Lash follicles cycle out of step and a lash grows steadily through its growing phase (Thibaut et al. 2010), so the resting lashes stand at full length and the growing ones (41 percent upper, 15 lower: Elder 1997) anywhere up to it. Each texel's alpha is the fibres' coverage, and the lash materials blend it; so do the brows, whose mask at 0.15 had made a solid bar of their fading edges. In the editor, blended fibre cards cast the partial shadow their coverage and their fibres' transmittance give, as the hair cards do.

## Brow density from the outline's coverage

Rendered through the source's mask the brows covered their outline nearly whole (0.8 to 0.98 of it) where the photographs show 0.3 to 0.9 (a sparse ginger brow 0.38, a grey one none), so every brow read as a dark bar: against the cheek the render's brow tone sat 0.62 log units below the photograph's. An outline's tone is its fibres and the skin between them mixed by coverage, `tone = c fibre + (1 - c)` in luminance over the cheek's, which cancels exposure, so the coverage is `(1 - tone) / (1 - fibre)` wherever the fibres differ from the skin by a tenth or more (`faceLikenessBrowCoverage`, the fibre sample being the outline's darkest tenth and the tone its median). The fibre rule already scales a card's coverage by a document `density`; `fit-face-brows.ts` sets it to the current density times the photograph's coverage over the render's (`fitFaceLikenessBrowDensity`), held to the rule's [0, 4].

Measured renders of documents derived with the fitted densities have not been compared yet; the method stands on its own derivation, and its effect on the brow tone is left to the next likeness round.

## Arch width by regression

The source's `mouthWidth` carried the whole dentition with the commissures and scaled it further than the mouth itself: the mouth 52.9 to 61.5 mm (+16.4 percent) at +1 and to 44.8 (-15.2) at -1, the arch 59.5 to 70.8 and 48.2 (plus and minus 19 percent), so a wide mouth had wide incisors and a narrow one thin ones. With the mouth open, a wide mouth now shows more buccal corridor around teeth of the same size, and a narrow one fills its opening with them.

## Eye depth

How deep-set an eye is, the corneal apex's distance ahead of the lateral orbital rim, differs by millimetres between adults (exophthalmometry: white men 16.5 and women 15.4 mm, black men 18.5 and women 17.8, Migliori and Gladstone 1984; Han Chinese adults 15.7, Sci Rep 2015), and the source has no target for it: its eye targets scale, raise, spread and fold the eye, and none moves the globe in its orbit. `prepareEyeDepthBasis` ([eye-depth-receipt.json](eye-depth-receipt.json)) adds `leftEyeDepth` and `rightEyeDepth`, one unit moving the globe 5 mm (two standard deviations of exophthalmometry) along its orbit axis, the eye's pivot to its aim, with the pivot and aim landmarks so gaze turns about the moved eye. Nothing else is authored by hand:

- the lids are the skin the blink moves at least half as far as it moves any (the mobile lid); within the globe's silhouette about the axis they move with it whole;
- toward the canthi, which the canthal tendons tie to the rim, the lids belong to a membrane (each vertex's displacement the mean of its neighbours'), except that none is left inside the globe: a lid the membrane would leave there is pushed out to the globe's normal displacement and held, and the membrane solved again (13 lid vertices per eye);
- the membrane reaches the socket's lining back to the globe's rear and ends a centimetre outside the orbital aperture (40 by 35 mm), where the skin is anchored beyond the rim; with the end at the aperture itself, the change ran over the 5 mm between the lateral canthus and the rim, a hard dark slope lateral of the eye from 2.5 mm forward;
- the lashes and brows move as the skin vertex nearest them.

The first attempts are the evidence for each rule: all the mobile lid moved whole left that dark slope; only the lid within the silhouette left the globe through the lateral lower lid; the lid sliding along the globe bared the sclera all round. ## Instrument

The identity solve moves each index's control until the anchored model has the photograph's value, which presumes the detector reads a control's change as the anchors do. It does not always. Each index's control was set to its envelope's ends on the reference head (the neutral, and once Kim Min-jung's derived face), rendered through the product editor under a subject's camera, and read both ways: the detector's change over the anchored model's change is the index's gain. Under the frontal cameras of Daniel Radcliffe and Emma Watson, fourteen of the thirty-two shape indices have gains under 0.2: lower vermilion, chin width and height, medial aperture, both Cupid's bow indices, lateral lower vermilion, cheek prominence, upper and middle nasal widths, temple width, nasal tip and nostril heights, and the infraorbital height; the upper lid index reads with the wrong sign (-0.55, -0.88; its landmarks are the lash line's top and the iris's, a lash band, not the lid fold), and the epicanthal fold does not move the medial lower lid slope's anchored reading at all. The renders do change: chin width 1 against -1 broadens the chin's outline visibly while the detector's chin contour points stay within a pixel or two, and so do the eye bag's height and the nasal tip's. The other indices read with gains of 0.26 to 0.98 (mouth width 0.91 to 0.98, face height 0.76 to 0.80, nasal width 0.29 to 0.45).

That the detector holds its place on a render and not on a photograph was tested directly: the image content at each index's landmarks was moved by 2 percent of the face width (a Gaussian displacement of radius 5 percent) on the seventeen photographs and on nineteen renders, and the detector followed by the same fraction on both, within about a tenth for every landmark (chin width 0.06 and 0.01, nasal tip 0.35 and 0.31, mouth width 0.65 and 0.70). The blindness is the detector's: where the skin gives it no edge of its own it places a landmark where the rest of the face predicts it, and on a photograph that index reports the detector's expectation for the face. A control fitted to it fits that expectation, which is why chin width, chin height and nostril height were the fine documents' persistent misses.

What the detector sees also depends on the view: under Alan Rickman's camera (yaw -31) the chin's height reads with a gain of 0.49, its outline being visible in a turned face. So `derive-face-documents.ts instrument-study` renders every index's control at its envelope's ends on the reference head under every subject's camera, `instrument` reads the gains (`faceInstrumentGains`), and an index is observed under a camera when its gain there lies within [bound, 1 / bound] (0.25: the detector sees at least a quarter of the change) and its control's envelope changes the detector's reading by at least twice the reading's own deviation under half a pixel of error in every point (`faceInstrumentResolution`; the epicanthal fold moves the medial lower lid's slope, a rise over a run of a few pixels, by less than that jitter, which had made its gain a ratio of noise). A control too short to reach every photograph is still observed: it goes as far as it reaches and the bound is recorded. `identity --instrument` fits only the observed indices; an unobserved index's control keeps its start, or takes its population's norm where one stands in for it (below). With the jaw outline and the vermilion borders below, over the seventeen cameras nineteen shape and expression indices are observed everywhere or nearly (face height, intercanthal width, fissure length and height, canthal tilt, nasal height and width, mouth width, both vermilion heights, the upper lip, the lower face's and chin's widths, the chin's height, brow height and slope, eye level, lateral aperture, lateral upper vermilion, and the mouth's states), and thirteen at none or one: medial aperture, both Cupid's bow indices, lateral lower vermilion, cheek prominence, upper and middle nasal widths, temple width, the medial lower lid, the upper lid, the infraorbital height, the nasal tip's and the nostrils' heights.

The gain admits; it is not applied. Read through its gain (the photograph's departure from the start over the gain), each target also carried the photograph-vs-render offset, which no render calibration can identify, multiplied: derived that way from the ff5 inputs, 134 controls ended at a bound (the upper vermilion's on every subject) and the median landmark RMS rose to 0.0432. Admitted alone, 64 ended at a bound against 125 before and 60 indices missed their targets against 108; the median landmark RMS was 0.0376 against 0.0346 (the metric reads the same detector, whose expectation the blind indices had been fitted to), the renders nearest their own photograph 15 of 17 against 13, and the eye aperture error 0.015 against 0.020.

Solving the detector's own linear response instead (the calibration renders give every index's change under every control, per camera) is ill-conditioned: on Daniel Radcliffe's photograph it asked for controls of -15.9 and 27.5.

A state of the face is calibrated where it reads. The incisal edges read only where the lips part over the teeth, which the reference head at rest does not show: with the upper lip raised the upper edge rests on the lower teeth, and at the envelope's closed end the jaw's gap reads nothing, so `upperDisplay` and `incisalGap` read at neither end under any camera, the jaw and the upper lip raiser were never fitted, and the lips' gap was left to the lower lip's depressor alone (held at its bound on 13 of 17 documents of round j13). The states of the face are now stepped over five values of their envelope and each gain taken between the widest pair of steps both instruments read (`faceInstrumentPair`); the upper lip raiser's index, whose edge shows only with the teeth apart, is stepped again with the jaw at each of its steps and read at the most closed that shows it (`uncoveredBy`, `faceInstrumentState`). Under Daniel Radcliffe's and Kim Min-jung's cameras the jaw's gap now reads with gains of 1.00 and 0.95 (from a quarter open) and the upper display with 0.83 and 0.60 (the jaw a quarter open), both observed.

## Jaw outline

The detector does not see the chin: rendered with chin width at its two ends the chin's outline broadens visibly while its chin contour points (176, 400) move 1 percent, and with the chin's height changed its menton (152) stays put. The face's outline against the neck is nonetheless in the image, and MediaPipe's multiclass selfie segmenter finds it: its face-skin class ends at the jaw where the neck's body skin begins. Rendered at the same ends, its outline three quarters of the way from the mouth line to menton widens from 0.48 to 0.60 of the face's width (chin width), its lowest midline point drops from 0.62 to 1.05 of the eye-to-stomion height (chin height), and its width at the mouth line grows from 0.78 to 0.95 (lower face width), where the detector's own lower face contour (136, 365) moved from 0.70 to 0.76.

`detect-face-likeness.py` runs the segmenter (`selfie_multiclass_256x256.tflite`) on a square crop about each detected face (1.5 times the landmarks' larger extent: the model sees 256 pixels) and writes the face-skin mask; a monochrome image gets none, its skin having no colour to be told by (on Michael Gambon's greyscale portrait it took white hair for face skin). `faceLikenessJawOutline` reads, in the face's frame (the eye line and its perpendicular, from stomion), on the mask's region holding the nasal tip: soft-tissue menton, the region's end on the midline found from below so the open mouth does not end it; and the outline's two points at the mouth line and at the chin's level, found from outside in. These become landmarks 470 to 474. The chin's level is half the eyes' height above stomion below it, fixed by the eyes and the mouth: first taken three quarters of the way from the mouth line to menton, it rose as a chin shortened into a wider part of the jaw and read the chin wider for being shorter, which the anchors, staying on their surface points, could not follow. Half the eyes' height is where the three-quarter level falls on the photographs (0.40 to 0.59, median 0.51); on the neutral's renders it falls at 0.62, the source's chin being longer. At that level the photographs' chins read 0.28 to 0.62 of the face's width and the neutral's 0.645, and most of the difference is the chin's length: the level meets a long chin high, where the jaw is still wide. `chinWidth` does not move the outline there at all (its target shapes the chin's front), and a taper of the skin the mandible carries narrows it by a tenth at most before the chin points. On the model they are anchored per view like the detector's (`anchor-face-outlines.ts`: the outline of the neutral's editor render under each subject's camera cast onto the surface, 0 to 4.6 px on reprojection). The lower face width, the chin's width and the chin's height are read on them, and soft-tissue menton replaces the detector's in the face's height.

## Vermilion borders

The detector's lower lip border (17) moves by a twentieth of what the lower lip's height does to the rendered lip. The border is in the image as the edge between the vermilion's colour and the skin's, and `faceLikenessVermilion` reads it on the midline: a profile perpendicular to the eye line, each sample the CIELAB median of columns 0.01 to 0.03 inter-ocular to either side of it; the skin's colour 0.15 to 0.35 inter-ocular beyond each outer lip landmark (the philtrum, the chin), each lip's the middle half of its vermilion; each border the first sample from the skin's side, within 0.08 inter-ocular of the detector's outer lip landmark, more than halfway in chroma from the skin's to the lip's. Chroma alone (a*, b*), because the shadow below the lower lip and the philtrum's shading change lightness, not redness: with lightness counted, those shadows were read as lip on four photographs. A lip within 4 CIELAB units of its skin's chroma is not read (Michael Gambon's greyscale portrait), nor a border the window does not hold (Maggie Smith's reddish philtrum, Alan Rickman's reddish chin). Both borders read on 13 of the 17 photographs; the lower border lies 0.01 to 0.05 of the mouth's width above the detector's 17 on most. They become landmarks 475 and 476, anchored per view like the jaw's outline, and both vermilion heights are read on them. Where the lower border is not read, or the instrument does not observe it under the subject's camera, the lower vermilion's population norm stands in (sto-li over ch-ch by ancestry and sex: 3D Facial Norms Europeans, Nairobi students, Hong Kong Chinese and Korean women; spread 0.036), read on the rest skin as the lip envelope revision reads it.

## Mental height

Read on the jaw's outline, the photographs' chins are shorter than the source's: stomion to soft-tissue menton is 0.36 to 0.42 of the face's height on them, and with `chinHeight` at its end eleven documents still read longer. Past that end the source's target lifts the chin off the neck and opens the junction: the connected head's neck ends 16 mm below menton, too little to absorb it. `prepareMentalHeightBasis` ([mental-height-receipt.json](mental-height-receipt.json)) adds `mentalHeight`, the height of the soft tissue below the labiomental fold, which lies on the mandible's symphysis: one unit scales the skin below the fold (supramentale on the neutral's midsagittal profile, -50.8 mm) vertically toward it so that menton (-81.1 mm) moves 5 mm, each vertex in proportion to the mandible's carry of it (how far the jaw's full opening moves it over how far it moves the lower lip's seam, capped at one). The lips, the teeth and everything above the fold stay, and the neck's base, which the mandible does not carry, stays; nothing is weighted by hand. Rendered through the editor from the front and in profile, 10 mm shorter and taller read as a chin, 15 mm shorter creases the skin along the jaw's lower border, so the envelope runs from -2 to 2; no triangle turns over and no new crossing appears to plus and minus 3, and every document builds.

The face's vertical proportions are its three segments since 2026-09-26, each over the face's width: the nose n-sn (`noseHeight`), the upper lip sn-stoms (`mouthElevation`) and the lower lip and chin stomi-me' (`mentalHeight`), the lips' gap between them (`lipParting`). The face's height is their sum and is no longer an index. Read as n-me' over the face's width with the head's vertical scale, and the nose over n-me', the upper lip over sn-me' and the chin over n-me' besides, the vertical indices were one too many: stomion to menton over n-me' equals (1 - noseHeight) (1 - upperLip) - lipParting mouthWidth / (2 faceHeight), on every photograph and model reading of round j13, so `mentalHeight` sat at -1.5 to -2 on documents whose every index fit and, where the lips could not part as far as the photograph's, shortened the chin by up to 10 mm to make up the gap. Dropping the chin instead (round j14) moved the fault: the head's vertical scale took up the face's height (-0.5 to -0.8 on 13 of 16 documents), shrinking a forehead no index reads, and the nose's length sat at its end on 9. The head's scale is now unpaired.

## Vermilion height

Read on the midline's colour, the photographs' upper vermilions are thinner than the source's `upperLipHeight` reaches: at its valid end (-1.5; past it the thinned lip's border crumples) seven documents still read fuller, and six lower vermilions held `lowerLipHeight` at its valid end (-1.25) on round j13. `prepareVermilionHeightBasis` ([vermilion-height-receipt.json](vermilion-height-receipt.json)) adds `upperVermilionHeight` and `lowerVermilionHeight`. The lips' region is both lips' surface, each vermilion wrapping over its lip's free margin into the lining that faces the mouth; the lips part at the mouth's slit and join only at the commissures, so each lip is the part of the region nearer its own meeting point along the surface, and its vermilion the part joined to its most prominent point without the lining (which faces back and toward the other lip). Where the slit parts the lips, each column's vermilion (1.5 mm across) scales about its free margin, 15 percent of its height per unit; toward the commissures, where the margin joins the other lip, the vermilion follows as the skin does. The source's slit ends 21 mm from the midline while the vermilion's outline runs on to the commissure's tip at 28 mm, the upper lip wrapping down around it, so past the slit's end the lips are one tissue and the other lip's vermilion there follows too. The cutaneous lip follows as a membrane up to subnasale above and the labiomental fold below, within a centimetre beyond the lip's widest point (the modiolus); the linings, the other lip within the slit and the skin outside stay.

Four first forms failed on the render or the fault census and are why it is built this way: dividing the lips by the mandible's carry put the lateral lower lip into the upper (the corners hardly move when the jaw opens); taking the vermilion as what lies in front of the lips' seam left the everted lower lip's inferior half, which lies behind the seam, in place; scaling the columns where the margin is the commissure itself turned 70 triangles over at the upper lip's fuller end; and holding the other lip's part past the slit left the tip hooked below a thinned lower lip. Each channel's envelope follows the lip envelope revision's rule: it reaches the populations' reference interval of its vermilion height over mouth width (the union of each sampled population's mean plus and minus two standard deviations, as [lip-envelope-receipt.json](lip-envelope-receipt.json) records it, read by the same `faceVermilionRatios`). The channels move the vermilion only vertically, so the measure is linear in them, and the ends are where it crosses the interval's edges: `upperVermilionHeight` from -4.2 to 3.3 (0.081 to 0.310 against the neutral's 0.210) and `lowerVermilionHeight` from -4.44 to 1.66 (0.091 to 0.317 against 0.256). At every end no triangle the channel moves turns over or crosses (the preparation refuses otherwise), every document builds, and rendered through the editor from the front, in profile and at the commissure each end reads as a lip; at the lower lip's fuller end its border stands 1 mm above the labiomental fold, the fold deepening beneath it.

## Jaw taper

Read on the jaw's outline at the chin's level (half the eyes' height below stomion), the photographs' chins are 0.27 to 0.62 of the face's width and the neutral's 0.645; the renders of round j13 were 46 percent wider at the median, and the source's `chinWidth`, which shapes the chin's front, moves that outline by under one percent over its envelope, so it sat at its end for most documents. The outline there is the mandible's body with its soft tissue, running from the gonial angle, lateral at the mouth's line, down to the symphysis, and how steeply it converges is the jaw's taper. `prepareJawTaperBasis` ([jaw-taper-receipt.json](jaw-taper-receipt.json)) adds `jawTaper`: one unit narrows the skin the mandible carries (its jaw-opening travel over the lower lip seam's, as for the mental height) toward the midline by a fraction of its distance from it that grows linearly from nothing at the mouth's line (the cheilia's height, -32.5 mm, where the lower face's width is read, which it leaves alone) to a tenth at soft-tissue menton (-81.1 mm) and stays there below it, each vertex weighted by how far its surface faces sideways. It is the mandible's body that converges, its outer face turned sideways; the chin's front faces forward and keeps its own width. Narrowed uniformly, the chin's front curved ever tighter and folded into a ridge down the midline from -4; weighted by the lateral facing, -4 reads as a tapered jaw with a rounded chin, and -5 turns four triangles over, so the envelope runs from -4 to 3. The chin width index now pairs with `jawTaper`.

## Eighteen-subject acceptance

Every published document was built through the actual connected runtime and rendered on a real GPU (ANGLE D3D11 on a Radeon 780M), and every one was put through the fourteen coupled states with the engine's triangle-crossing census. Nothing here is a separate asset: the views are what the editor produces from each document's own parameters.

**Views.** Eighteen subjects at rest with their hair, five views each (front, both profiles, back and a clay front): 90 frames. Build times run from 2.1 to 15.2 seconds with a median of 3.1, and hair meshes from 36k to 359k triangles, the longest curly hair costing the most of both. The scalp reads as hair, not skin, wherever the hair is darker than the skin. Those times are what the population costs after the signed query stopped allocating per triangle; before it the same eighteen builds took 751 seconds against 81.

**States.** Nineteen documents (the eighteen plus the bald neutral) across fourteen states, 266 entries. `tongue-closed` is refused by name on all nineteen, as the passage rule requires. Rest, both gazes, lateral gaze and blink cross the teeth on none of them but one, whose lining touches its own gums at rest as the source authored it. The lining crosses more as the jaw opens (101 triangles at half opening, 138 at full, 469 with the lips closed, medians), which is the vestibular lining below the modelled gum's open base where no collider exists; that is a data follow-up, stated rather than hidden. Lip against teeth stays at zero except under laterotrusion, where the arches slide as the census records.

**Hair against skin.** The states above are built without hair, so the coupled census does not read it; the view build does, and its own crossing counts are what the ribbon rules are held to. Summed over the population's hair layers, skin against hair fell from 11,888 triangles with the authored 4 mm ribbon to 9,957 with the derived width and the strand chord rule, having risen to 15,973 in between, when the ribbons were widened to cover the scalp before their strands were held to the integrator's step. The subjects that moved are the long-haired ones: emma-watson 1,014, then 1,842, then 553; generated-white-girl-01 1,138, then 2,026, then 526. What is left is about five hundred per thousand roots, which is the root fan itself: a hair root stands on the skin, so its first triangle touches it. The cost of both rules together was a population build of 751 seconds against 510, which the query's own repair then took to 81.

**Known findings.** An earlier reading of one subject's front view, a rectangular region where the hair in front of the face seemed not to be drawn, was wrong and is withdrawn. Compared pixel by pixel against the same model rendered without hair, that region is exactly where no ribbon stands in front of the face: the hair parts around it, its straight lower edge is the model's own neck cut and its straight sides are the curtains' inner edges. The hair alone, the skin alone and a render with the ribbons alpha-masked opaque instead of blended all agree, so no viewer artifact remains to chase. The renders are review evidence, not an anatomical or likeness acceptance.
