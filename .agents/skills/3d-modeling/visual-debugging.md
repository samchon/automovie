# Visual Debugging

Readiness or numerical success proves no appearance; only an observed render does. Work in this order:

1. Recover a broken viewer first: do no work that needs visual judgment until a displayed model and working controls confirm recovery, and protect other owners' processes meanwhile.
2. Inspect the actual consumer with current source on a hardware renderer, recording source, input, renderer, and view.
3. Inspect from every direction, back quarters and high and low obliques included, because front and side alone hide oblique sections and junctions. Aim a free camera at each defect and frame its close-up on the affected region from a camera outside the model. Inspect assembled neighbors, distance, rest, intermediate states, supported extremes, return, and shape-motion combinations.
4. When a surface is generated from curves, such as lofted sections, a curve network, or a subdivision cage, judge it only with that net: every set of views, whole sweeps and each defect's close-up alike, shows the same cameras as surface, net overlaid on the surface, and net alone. A field over the surface, such as displacement relief, puts its defining curves in the net too. The surface shows the symptom; the net shows the curve or section that causes it.
5. Judge form under raking light with fine material detail such as normal maps off, and materials and lighting in beauty; use structural passes only for the quantities they document.
6. Compare the render with the engine result to separate calculation from display faults, and compare before and after under matching conditions.
7. After every correction, render again and look yourself. Stale, blank, or unobserved output stays unverified.
